# Chapter 4: Superconductivity, Meissner–London–BCS Theory, Josephson Junctions & High-$T_c$ Superconductors

## 1. Phenomenology, Meissner–Ochsenfeld Effect & Thermodynamics

Ordinary metals retain a finite residual electrical resistivity even at $0\text{ K}$ due to impurity and defect scattering. In 1911, Heike Kamerlingh Onnes discovered that cooling solid mercury below a razor-sharp **critical temperature $T_c = 4.15\text{ K}$** causes its DC electrical resistance to plunge abruptly to **strictly zero** ($\rho < 10^{-25}\,\Omega\text{ m}$).

Superconductivity is **not** merely "infinite classical conductivity." It is a genuinely new macroscopic quantum phase of matter in which billions of paired electrons lock into a single phase-coherent wavefunction, simultaneously delivering zero DC dissipation, active magnetic flux expulsion, magnetic flux quantization, and macroscopic Josephson tunneling.

### 1.1 Persistent Currents, Critical Field $H_c(T)$ & Silsbee's Rule

1. **Zero DC Resistance & Persistent Currents:** Inducing an electric current around a closed superconducting ring establishes a **persistent supercurrent** that circulates indefinitely without a battery; experimental decay bounds yield an $L/R$ decay time exceeding $10^5$ years! *(Note: at non-zero AC frequencies, normal unpaired electrons in the surface layer still absorb tiny power, and photons with $h\nu > 2\Delta$ break Cooper pairs directly.)*
2. **Critical Magnetic Field $H_c(T)$:** Applying a sufficiently strong magnetic field destroys superconductivity and restores the normal resistive phase. Across virtually all Type-I superconductors, the critical field follows a clean empirical **parabolic law**:

$$\boxed{H_c(T) = H_c(0)\left[1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2\right], \qquad B_c(T) = B_c(0)\left[1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2\right]}$$

where $H_c(0)$ is the maximum critical field at $0\text{ K}$ (e.g., $\text{Al}: T_c = 1.18\text{ K}, B_c(0) = 0.0105\text{ T}$; $\text{Sn}: 3.72\text{ K}, 0.0305\text{ T}$; $\text{Pb}: 7.20\text{ K}, 0.0803\text{ T}$).

3. **Silsbee's Rule (Critical Current $I_c$):** Even in zero external magnetic field, passing a transport current $I$ along a cylindrical superconducting wire of radius $r$ generates a circumferential self-field $H_{\text{self}} = \frac{I}{2\pi r}$ at its outer surface. Francis Silsbee (1916) showed that superconductivity collapses as soon as the total surface magnetic field reaches $H_c(T)$:

$$\boxed{I_c = 2\pi r\,H_c(T) \quad (\text{zero external field})}$$

*(If an external transverse field $H_{\text{ext}}$ is simultaneously applied to a long cylinder, flux expulsion doubles the equatorial surface field to $2H_{\text{ext}}$, reducing the critical current to $I_c = 2\pi r[H_c(T) - 2H_{\text{ext}}]$.)*

### 1.2 The Meissner–Ochsenfeld Effect — Perfect Diamagnetism ($\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$) vs. Ideal Conductor ($\dot{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{0}$)

Understanding why **perfect diamagnetism is an independent, defining property of superconductivity**—not a mere corollary of $\rho = 0$—is the single most tested conceptual distinction in UPSC Paper II.

Consider a hypothetical **ideal (perfect) conductor** having $\rho = 0$ below $T_c$. By Ohm's law $\mathbf{E} = \rho\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{0}$, Faraday's induction law forces:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{B} = \text{constant in time (frozen flux!)}$$

Consequently, an ideal conductor would be **path-dependent (irreversible)**:
- **Zero-Field Cooled (ZFC):** Cool below $T_c$ in $H = 0$, then apply $H$ $\implies$ eddy currents keep $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$ inside.
- **Field Cooled (FC):** Apply $H$ while normal ($T > T_c$, so flux penetrates), then cool below $T_c$ $\implies$ magnetic flux remains **permanently trapped** inside ($\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{H} \ne \mathbf{0}$), and even switching $H$ off afterward would leave a permanently magnetized bar!

In 1933, Walther Meissner and Robert Ochsenfeld tested single-crystal tin and lead cylinders along the **Field-Cooled** path and discovered a dramatic shock: the instant $T$ crosses below $T_c$ (for $H < H_c$), the crystal **actively expels all magnetic flux lines from its bulk interior**!

$$\boxed{\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0} \quad \text{inside bulk superconductor (both ZFC and FC paths)}}$$

Inserting $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$ into the SI constitutive relation $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})$ immediately proves **perfect diamagnetism**:

$$\mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M}) = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{M} = -\mathbf{H} \implies \boxed{\chi = \frac{M}{H} = -1, \qquad \mu_r = 1 + \chi = 0}$$

**Profound Thermodynamic Consequence:** Because the final state ($\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$) is strictly **independent of cooling history** (reversible!), the superconducting phase is a **true thermodynamic equilibrium state** uniquely specified by state variables $(T, H)$.

### 1.3 Thermodynamics of the Superconducting Phase Transition (Unabridged SI Derivation)

Because the Meissner effect establishes thermodynamic reversibility, we apply classical thermodynamics to the magnetic **Gibbs Free Energy $G(T, H)$** (at constant pressure), whose exact differential for a specimen of volume $V$ is:

$$dG = -S\,dT - \mu_0 V M\,dH$$

#### Step 1 — Free Energy Stabilization (Condensation Energy)

Integrate $dG$ isothermally at fixed temperature $T < T_c$ from zero field to field $H$:

$$G_s(T, H) - G_s(T, 0) = -\mu_0 V \int_0^H M(H')\,dH'$$

Inside the Meissner state ($H' < H_c$), perfect diamagnetism sets $M = -H'$, giving:

$$G_s(T, H) = G_s(T, 0) + \mu_0 V \int_0^H H'\,dH' = G_s(T, 0) + \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 V H^2$$

In the normal metallic state, susceptibility is negligible ($|\chi_n| \sim 10^{-5} \ll 1$, so $M_n \approx 0$), leaving $G_n(T, H) \approx G_n(T, 0)$ independent of $H$. Right on the phase-coexistence boundary $H = H_c(T)$, both phases share equal Gibbs free energy: $G_s(T, H_c) = G_n(T, H_c) = G_n(T, 0)$. Hence the **superconducting condensation energy** (zero-field free energy lowering) is:

$$\boxed{\Delta G(T) \equiv G_n(T, 0) - G_s(T, 0) = \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 H_c^2(T)\,V > 0}$$

#### Step 2 — Entropy Ordering ($S_s < S_n$) & Latent Heat (1st-Order Transition at $H > 0$)

Differentiate $\Delta G$ with respect to $T$ using $S = -\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial T}\right)_H$:

$$S_s(T) - S_n(T) = -\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(G_s(T,0) - G_n(T,0)\right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu_0 V H_c^2(T)\right)$$

$$\boxed{S_s - S_n = \mu_0 V H_c(T)\frac{dH_c}{dT}}$$

Because $H_c(T)$ decreases monotonically from $H_c(0)$ to $0$ as $T$ rises toward $T_c$, **$\frac{dH_c}{dT} < 0$** throughout $0 < T \le T_c$:
- **Order Parameter Insight:** **$S_s < S_n$** strictly for all $0 < T < T_c$! The superconducting phase has **lower entropy**—proving thermodynamically that electrons condense into a **more ordered state** (Cooper pairs).
- **Third Law Check ($T \to 0$):** As $T \to 0$, $S_s - S_n \to 0$ demands $\left.\frac{dH_c}{dT}\right|_{T=0} = 0$ (matching the horizontal slope of the parabolic $H_c(T)$ curve!).
- **Transition in Non-Zero Field ($H = H_c > 0$, $0 < T < T_c$):** Because $S_n - S_s > 0$, crossing from superconducting to normal absorbs a finite **latent heat**:
  $$L = T(S_n - S_s) = -\mu_0 V T H_c \frac{dH_c}{dT} > 0$$
  Therefore, for **$H > 0$**, the transition is a **First-Order Phase Transition** (discontinuous entropy, finite latent heat).
- **Transition in Zero Field ($H = 0, T = T_c$):** Since $H_c(T_c) = 0$, **$S_s(T_c) = S_n(T_c)$** and **$L = 0$** (zero latent heat!).

#### Step 3 — Specific Heat Jump & Rutgers' Formula (2nd-Order Transition at $H = 0$)

Differentiate the entropy difference with respect to $T$ using $C = T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\right)$:

$$C_s - C_n = T\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(S_s - S_n\right) = \mu_0 V T\left[\left(\frac{dH_c}{dT}\right)^2 + H_c\frac{d^2 H_c}{dT^2}\right]$$

Evaluate right at the zero-field transition $T = T_c$, where $H_c(T_c) = 0$ while $\left.\frac{dH_c}{dT}\right|_{T_c} = -\frac{2 H_c(0)}{T_c} \ne 0$:

$$\boxed{\Delta C\big|_{T_c} \equiv \left(C_s - C_n\right)_{T_c} = \mu_0 V T_c \left(\frac{dH_c}{dT}\right)^2_{T_c} > 0 \qquad \text{(Rutgers' Formula)}}$$

At $H = 0, T = T_c$: Gibbs energy $G$ and first derivative $S$ are **continuous** ($L = 0$), while the second derivative ($C = -T\partial^2 G/\partial T^2$) undergoes a **finite upward jump $\Delta C > 0$**. Thus the zero-field superconducting transition is a textbook **Second-Order Phase Transition**!

```
   S ^                       C_e ^
     |          /  S_n           |        *  C_s (jumps UP at Tc!)
     |        /                  |       /|
     |      /  . S_s             |      / |---*  C_n = γT
     |    /  .'                  |     /  :  /
     |  /  .'  (S_s < S_n)       |   .'   : /  (exp(-Δ/kBT) at low T)
     |/_.-'                      |__/_____:/
   0 +--------+-----> T        0 +--------+-----> T
              Tc                          Tc
```

> **Problem:** Type-I Lead has critical temperature $T_c = 7.20\text{ K}$ and zero-temperature critical field $B_c(0) = \mu_0 H_c(0) = 0.0803\text{ T}$. Assuming the parabolic critical-field law:

>

> $$B_c(4.20\text{ K}) = B_c(0)\left[1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2\right] = 0.0803\left[1 - \left(\frac{4.20}{7.20}\right)^2\right] = 0.0803\left(1 - \frac{49}{144}\right)$$
> $$B_c(4.20\text{ K}) = 0.0803 \times 0.65972 = \boxed{0.0530\text{ T} \;(53.0\text{ mT})}$$
> $$H_c(4.20\text{ K}) = \frac{B_c}{\mu_0} = \frac{0.05298}{4\pi \times 10^{-7}} = \boxed{4.22 \times 10^4\text{ A m}^{-1}}$$
>

> $$I_c = 2\pi r H_c(4.20\text{ K}) = 2\pi(1.0 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m})(4.216 \times 10^4\text{ A m}^{-1}) = \boxed{265\text{ A}}$$
>

>    $$\frac{\Delta G(0)}{V} = \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 H_c^2(0) = \frac{B_c^2(0)}{2\mu_0} = \frac{(0.0803\text{ T})^2}{2(1.2566 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T m A}^{-1})} = \boxed{2.57 \times 10^3\text{ J m}^{-3}}$$

>    *(Sanity Check: Multiplying by Lead's molar volume $1.827 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$ gives $\Delta C_m = 0.0521\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$, matching experimental $0.053\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$ to within $2\%$!)*

## 2. Electrodynamics (London Two-Fluid Model) & Type-I vs. Type-II Superconductors

### 2.1 The Two-Fluid Model & Unabridged Derivation of London Equations

To build an electrodynamics capturing *both* zero resistance and the Meissner effect simultaneously, Fritz and Heinz London (1935) adopted the Gorter–Casimir **Two-Fluid Model**. Below $T_c$, the total conduction electron density $n$ separates into two interpenetrating, non-interacting fluids:

$$n = n_s(T) + n_n(T)$$

- **Normal Fluid ($n_n$):** Ordinary resistive electrons experiencing lattice scattering ($\mathbf{J}_n = \sigma_n\mathbf{E}$); $n_n(0) = 0$ and $n_n(T_c) = n$.
- **Superfluid ($n_s$):** Condensed, frictionless **superelectrons** carrying dissipationless supercurrent $\mathbf{J}_s$ that completely shorts out $\mathbf{J}_n$ in DC; $n_s(0) = n$ and $n_s(T_c) = 0$.

Empirically (Gorter–Casimir thermodynamics), the superfluid fraction varies with temperature as:

$$\boxed{\frac{n_s(T)}{n} = 1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^4}$$

#### Step 1 — First London Equation (Acceleration Without Resistance)

A superelectron of mass $m$ and charge $-e$ moves with zero friction ($\tau \to \infty$), obeying Newton's second law directly under an electric field $\mathbf{E}$:

$$m\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_s}{\partial t} = -e\mathbf{E}$$

Expressing the supercurrent density as $\mathbf{J}_s = -n_s e \mathbf{v}_s$ and differentiating with respect to time gives the **First London Equation**:

$$\frac{\partial\mathbf{J}_s}{\partial t} = -n_s e\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_s}{\partial t} = \frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{E} \implies \boxed{\mathbf{E} = \frac{m}{n_s e^2}\frac{\partial\mathbf{J}_s}{\partial t} = \mu_0\lambda_L^2\frac{\partial\mathbf{J}_s}{\partial t}}$$

*(In steady DC flow, $\frac{\partial\mathbf{J}_s}{\partial t} = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$ even for finite $\mathbf{J}_s$, encoding $\rho = 0$. Note: substituting Cooper-pair parameters $q^* = -2e$, $m^* = 2m$, and pair density $n_s^* = n_s/2$ leaves $\frac{n_s^*(q^*)^2}{m^*} = \frac{(n_s/2)(4e^2)}{2m} = \frac{n_s e^2}{m}$ strictly invariant!)*

#### Step 2 — Second London Equation (Enforcing the Meissner Effect)

Take the curl of the First London Equation and substitute Faraday's law $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$:

$$\frac{m}{n_s e^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s\right) = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t} \implies \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s + \frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{B}\right) = \mathbf{0}$$

Integrating over time yields $\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s + \frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$, where a non-zero integration constant $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ would merely preserve initial trapped fields (an ideal conductor!). To enforce the **Meissner effect** ($\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$ inside the bulk independent of history), the London brothers **postulated $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) \equiv \mathbf{0}$**, yielding the **Second London Equation**:

$$\boxed{\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s = -\frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{B} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0\lambda_L^2}\mathbf{B}}$$

*(Microscopic Quantum Justification: In London gauge $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = 0$, the macroscopic condensate has zero canonical momentum $\langle\mathbf{p}\rangle = m\mathbf{v}_s - e\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{0}$, immediately fixing $\mathbf{J}_s = -\frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{A}$, whose curl is identically the Second London Equation!)*

#### Step 3 — Magnetic Field Screening & London Penetration Depth $\lambda_L$

Take the curl of Ampère's magnetostatic law $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}_s$ and substitute the Second London Equation:

$$\nabla\times\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}\right) = \mu_0\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s\right) = -\frac{\mu_0 n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{B}$$

Apply the vector identity $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}) = \nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{B}$ together with Gauss's magnetic law $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$:

$$\boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mathbf{B}}{\lambda_L^2}, \qquad \lambda_L(T) = \sqrt{\frac{m}{\mu_0 n_s(T) e^2}}}$$

*(By the exact same steps, screening supercurrents obey $\nabla^2\mathbf{J}_s = \mathbf{J}_s / \lambda_L^2$.)*

**1D Semi-Infinite Slab Solution:** Let a superconductor occupy $x \ge 0$ with an external field $\mathbf{B}_0 = B_0\hat{z}$ parallel to the boundary plane $x = 0$. Solving $\frac{d^2 B_z}{dx^2} = \frac{B_z}{\lambda_L^2}$ and discarding the unphysical exponentially growing $e^{+x/\lambda_L}$ mode yields:

$$\boxed{B_z(x) = B_0\,e^{-x/\lambda_L}, \qquad J_{s,y}(x) = \frac{B_0}{\mu_0\lambda_L}\,e^{-x/\lambda_L}}$$

Magnetic flux does **not** vanish discontinuously at the surface—instead, lossless diamagnetic **Meissner screening currents** flow within a thin surface layer of thickness **$\lambda_L \sim 20\text{–}100\text{ nm}$**, canceling $\mathbf{B}$ exponentially into the bulk!

**Inverting for Superelectron Density $n_s(T)$ & Temperature Scaling:**
From $\lambda_L = \sqrt{m/(\mu_0 n_s e^2)}$ and $n_s(T) = n[1 - (T/T_c)^4]$:

$$\boxed{n_s(T) = \frac{m}{\mu_0 e^2 \lambda_L^2(T)}, \qquad \lambda_L(T) = \frac{\lambda_L(0)}{\sqrt{1 - (T/T_c)^4}}}$$

*Dimensional & Limiting Checks:* $[\lambda_L] = \sqrt{\frac{\text{kg}}{(\text{kg m C}^{-2})(\text{m}^{-3})(\text{C}^2)}} = \text{m}$ ✓. As $T \to T_c^-$, $n_s(T) \to 0 \implies \lambda_L(T) \to \infty$, so the external magnetic field floods the entire interior smoothly at $T_c$!

### 2.2 Pippard Coherence Length $\xi$, Ginzburg–Landau $\kappa$ & Type-I vs. Type-II Superconductors

Superconductivity is governed by **two fundamental length scales**:
1. **London Penetration Depth $\lambda_L$:** Characteristic decay length of magnetic fields and screening currents ($\sim 30\text{–}100\text{ nm}$).
2. **Coherence Length $\xi$ (Pippard / BCS):** Minimum spatial distance over which the superconducting order parameter $\Psi(\mathbf{r})$ (superelectron density $n_s$) can vary significantly—physically equal to the **spatial diameter of a Cooper pair**! From Heisenberg's uncertainty principle for electrons within energy $\sim 2\Delta(0)$ of $E_F$ ($\Delta p \approx 2\Delta/v_F$):
   $$\xi_0 = \frac{\hbar v_F}{\pi\Delta(0)} \sim 100\text{–}1600\text{ nm} \quad (\text{pure elemental metals})$$
   Alloying or adding impurities shortens the electron mean free path $\ell \ll \xi_0$, shrinking the effective coherence length to $\xi_{\text{eff}} \approx \sqrt{\xi_0 \ell}$ while enlarging penetration depth to $\lambda_{\text{eff}} \approx \lambda_L\sqrt{\xi_0/\ell}$.

**Surface Energy Sign & The Ginzburg–Landau Parameter $\kappa = \lambda_L / \xi$:**
At a planar boundary separating a Normal ($N$) and Superconducting ($S$) region at $H = H_c$:
- Losing superconducting order over thickness $\xi$ **costs** condensation energy $+\frac{1}{2}\mu_0 H_c^2 \xi$ per unit area.
- Letting magnetic field penetrate thickness $\lambda_L$ **saves** diamagnetic expulsion energy $-\frac{1}{2}\mu_0 H_c^2 \lambda_L$ per unit area.
- Net $N\text{–}S$ interface energy per unit area scales as $\sigma_{NS} \approx \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 H_c^2(\xi - \lambda_L)$!

Exact Ginzburg–Landau theory classifies **every superconductor in nature** via the dimensionless **Ginzburg–Landau parameter $\kappa \equiv \lambda_L / \xi$**:

- **Type-I Superconductors ($\kappa < \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \sigma_{NS} > 0$):** Positive $N\text{–}S$ wall energy suppresses internal normal/superconducting mixing. The crystal stays in the complete Meissner state ($-M = H$) up to a single sharp critical field **$H_c$**, then collapses abruptly into the normal state.
- **Type-II Superconductors ($\kappa > \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \sigma_{NS} < 0$):** Negative wall energy makes it thermodynamically favorable to create maximum $N\text{–}S$ interface area! Above a **lower critical field $H_{c1}$**, magnetic flux penetrates cleanly in a triangular lattice of microscopic **Abrikosov Flux Vortices** (**Mixed / Shubnikov State**):
  - Each vortex has a **normal-metal cylindrical core** of radius $\sim \xi$ surrounded by circulating supercurrents out to radius $\sim \lambda_L$, carrying **strictly one magnetic flux quantum**:
    $$\boxed{\Phi_0 = \frac{h}{2e} = 2.0678 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}}$$
  - As $H$ increases from $H_{c1}$ toward the **upper critical field $H_{c2}$**, vortex density grows until normal cores touch and overlap at:
    $$\boxed{B_{c2} = \mu_0 H_{c2} = \frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi\xi^2} = \sqrt{2}\,\kappa\,B_c}$$
    Because $\xi$ can be only a few nanometers in alloys and cuprates, $B_{c2}$ reaches **$15\text{–}100+\text{ T}$**!
  - **Flux Pinning:** Under transport current density $\mathbf{J}$, vortices feel a transverse Lorentz force $\mathbf{f}_L = \mathbf{J}\times\boldsymbol{\Phi}_0$. Free vortex motion dissipates energy (**flux-flow resistance**). Engineering nanoscale defects/precipitates **pins** vortices rigidly, sustaining lossless high-field superconducting magnets (MRI, LHC dipoles, fusion tokamaks).

> **Solution:**

> 1. Superelectron density at $0\text{ K}$ from $\lambda_L(0) = \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{\mu_0 n_s(0) e^2}}$:

## 3. BCS Microscopic Theory — Isotope Effect, Cooper Pairs & Energy Gap

For 46 years after Kamerlingh Onnes's discovery, the microscopic origin of superconductivity defeated every physicist because two electrons strongly repel via Coulomb's law. In 1957, John Bardeen, Leon Cooper, and Robert Schrieffer (Nobel Prize 1972) solved the mystery completely by demonstrating how **crystal lattice vibrations (phonons)** bind electrons into a coherent quantum condensate.

### 3.1 The Isotope Effect — Experimental Proof of Phonon Mediation

In 1950, Emanuel Maxwell and C. A. Reynolds measured $T_c$ across isotopically enriched samples of mercury ($^{198}\text{Hg}$ to $^{204}\text{Hg}$), tin, and lead, uncovering a striking universal power law:

$$\boxed{T_c\,M^\alpha = \text{constant} \qquad (\alpha \approx 0.50 \text{ for conventional BCS metals})}$$

where $M$ is the isotopic nuclear mass (e.g., $\text{Hg}: \alpha = 0.50$, $\text{Sn}: 0.47$, $\text{Pb}: 0.48$). Because distinct isotopes possess identical nuclear charge $Z$ and identical electronic band structure, **$T_c$ can depend on $M$ only if lattice ion motion participates directly in the pairing mechanism**! Since lattice Debye frequency scales identically as $\omega_D \propto \sqrt{C/M} \propto M^{-1/2}$, observing $T_c \propto M^{-1/2} \propto \omega_D$ provided the decisive clue pointing straight to **electron–phonon coupling**. *(Paradoxically, excellent room-temperature conductors like $\text{Cu, Ag, Au}$ fail to superconduct precisely because their electron–phonon coupling is too weak!)*

### 3.2 Electron–Phonon–Electron Cooper Pairs & Macroscopic Quantum Coherence

**How Two Repelling Electrons Attract (Retarded Lattice Polarization):**
Imagine Electron 1 racing past positive metal ions at Fermi speed $v_F \sim 10^6\text{ m s}^{-1}$. Its negative charge tugs nearby positive ions slightly inward toward its trajectory. Because heavy ions respond sluggishly (on timescale $\tau_{\text{ion}} \sim 2\pi/\omega_D \sim 10^{-13}\text{ s}$), Electron 1 has already moved $\sim v_F \tau_{\text{ion}} \sim 100\text{ nm}$ downstream by the time the positive ion cloud reaches peak compression! A second electron (Electron 2) entering this lingering **positively charged wake** feels a net attractive potential—overcoming screened Coulomb repulsion because the two electrons are separated in time and never sit on top of one another! In quantum field language, the electrons attract by exchanging **virtual acoustic phonons** of energy $\hbar\omega \lesssim \hbar\omega_D$.

**Cooper's Theorem (1956):** Leon Cooper proved that because all states below $E_F$ are Pauli-blocked by the Fermi sea, **any arbitrarily weak attractive interaction $V > 0$** inside a thin shell $[E_F, E_F + \hbar\omega_D]$ renders the normal Fermi sea unstable, binding pairs of electrons into **Cooper Pairs** with **strictly opposite wavevectors and antiparallel spins**:

$$\boxed{\left(\mathbf{k}\uparrow, \; -\mathbf{k}\downarrow\right), \qquad \mathbf{P}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{0}, \qquad S_{\text{total}} = 0 \text{ (Spin Singlet)}}$$

- Zero center-of-mass momentum ($\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2 = \mathbf{0}$) maximizes the number of phase-space scattering transitions around the entire Fermi sphere; spin singlet ($\uparrow\downarrow$) enforces a symmetric spatial wavefunction maximizing attractive overlap.
- Because a Cooper pair spans a huge diameter $\xi_0 \sim 100\text{–}1000\text{ nm}$, roughly $10^6$ other Cooper pairs overlap inside the volume of a single pair! Rather than isolated molecules, all pairs condense coherently into a **single macroscopic quantum wavefunction**:
  $$\Psi(\mathbf{r}) = \sqrt{n_s^*(\mathbf{r})}\,e^{i\theta(\mathbf{r})}$$
- **Flux Quantization Proof ($\Phi_0 = h/2e$):** Deep inside a thick superconducting ring ($\mathbf{J}_s = 0$), the gauge-invariant supercurrent velocity $\mathbf{v}_s = \frac{1}{m^*}(\hbar\nabla\theta - q^*\mathbf{A}) = \mathbf{0}$ forces $\hbar\nabla\theta = 2e\mathbf{A}$ (with pair charge $q^* = 2e$). Requiring $\Psi(\mathbf{r})$ to be single-valued around the closed loop demands $\oint \nabla\theta\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2\pi n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$):
  $$\frac{2e}{\hbar}\oint \mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2\pi n \implies \boxed{\Phi = \iint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = n\left(\frac{h}{2e}\right) = n\Phi_0, \qquad \Phi_0 = 2.0678 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}}$$
  Direct measurement of $\Phi_0 = h/2e$ (Deaver & Fairbank; Doll & Näbauer, 1961) confirmed experimentally that superconducting charge carriers carry exact charge **$2e$**!

### 3.3 The BCS Energy Gap $2\Delta(0)$, Critical Temperature & Exponential Specific Heat

Breaking one Cooper pair out of the coherent BCS ground state into two single-electron quasiparticle excitations costs a minimum **superconducting energy gap $E_g(T) = 2\Delta(T)$** centered symmetrically around the Fermi energy $E_F$ (unlike an insulator band gap fixed to Brillouin zone boundaries, the BCS gap moves *with* the Fermi surface when current flows!).

Weak-coupling BCS theory predicts four landmark quantitative results verified across elemental superconductors:

1. **Critical Temperature $T_c$:**
   $$\boxed{k_B T_c = 1.13\,\hbar\omega_D\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{N(E_F)\,V}\right)}$$
   where $N(E_F)$ is the single-spin electronic density of states at $E_F$ and $V$ is the attractive electron–phonon matrix element. Since $\omega_D \propto M^{-1/2}$, this immediately derives the **Isotope Effect ($\alpha = 0.5$)** and reveals the essential non-analytic singularity $e^{-1/N(E_F)V}$ explaining why ordinary perturbation theory could never capture superconductivity!
2. **Universal Zero-Temperature Energy Gap:**
   $$\boxed{E_g(0) = 2\Delta(0) = 3.53\,k_B T_c \qquad \left(\Delta(0) = 1.764\,k_B T_c\right)}$$
   Near $T_c$, the gap closes continuously as $\Delta(T) \approx 1.74\,\Delta(0)\sqrt{1 - T/T_c} \to 0$. Directly measured by **far-infrared/microwave absorption onset** ($h\nu_{\min} = 2\Delta(0)$) and **Giaever S–I–N tunneling** ($eV_{\text{onset}} = \Delta(0)$).
3. **Exponential Electronic Specific Heat ($T \ll T_c$):** Because quasiparticles must be thermally excited across energy gap $\Delta(0)$, low-temperature electronic specific heat decays exponentially instead of linearly:
   $$\boxed{C_{es}(T) \propto \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta(0)}{k_B T}\right), \qquad \left.\frac{C_{es} - C_{en}}{C_{en}}\right|_{T_c} = 1.43}$$
4. **Why Zero Resistance Persists:** To scatter a flowing Cooper pair of net velocity $\mathbf{v}_s$ off an impurity requires breaking the pair across the energy gap $2\Delta$; as long as drift kinetic energy stays below the depairing threshold ($v_s < \Delta/\hbar k_F$), elastic impurity scattering is **energetically forbidden**!

## 4. Josephson Junctions (DC, AC & DC SQUID) & High-$T_c$ Superconductors

### 4.1 Feynman's Coupled-Wavefunction Derivation of DC & AC Josephson Effects

Place two superconductors $S_1$ and $S_2$ on either side of an ultrathin insulating oxide barrier ($d \sim 1\text{–}2\text{ nm}$, an **S–I–S Josephson Junction**). Ordinary single-electron Giaever tunneling requires applied voltage $V > 2\Delta/e$ to break pairs. In 1962, Brian Josephson (Nobel Prize 1973) predicted that **intact Cooper pairs tunnel coherently** through the thin barrier via overlap of their macroscopic wavefunctions—even at **strictly zero voltage**! *(Notably, the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized John Clarke, Michel Devoret, and John Martinis for demonstrating **macroscopic quantum tunnelling and energy quantization** of the collective phase variable in Josephson junction circuits.)*

**Unabridged Derivation via Feynman's Two-State Macroscopic Wavefunctions:**

Describe the two weakly coupled condensates by macroscopic wavefunctions $\psi_1 = \sqrt{n_1}\,e^{i\theta_1}$ and $\psi_2 = \sqrt{n_2}\,e^{i\theta_2}$, where $n_1, n_2$ are Cooper-pair densities and $\theta_1, \theta_2$ are macroscopic phases. Weak barrier tunneling couples them with real coupling energy $K$ via coupled Schrödinger equations:

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi_1}{\partial t} = U_1\psi_1 + K\psi_2, \qquad i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi_2}{\partial t} = U_2\psi_2 + K\psi_1$$

Apply a constant DC potential difference $V_0$ across the junction. Because each Cooper pair carries charge $q^* = 2e$, the potential energy difference between the two sides is $U_2 - U_1 = 2eV_0$. Selecting the symmetric energy midpoint sets $U_1 = -eV_0$ and $U_2 = +eV_0$.

**Step 1 — Substitute Polar Wavefunctions & Separate Real/Imaginary Parts:**
Differentiate $\psi_1(t) = \sqrt{n_1}\,e^{i\theta_1}$ with respect to time:
$$\frac{\partial\psi_1}{\partial t} = \left(\frac{\dot{n}_1}{2\sqrt{n_1}} + i\sqrt{n_1}\dot{\theta}_1\right)e^{i\theta_1}$$

Insert into the first coupled equation, divide both sides by $e^{i\theta_1}$, and define the **gauge-invariant phase difference** $\delta \equiv \theta_2 - \theta_1$:

$$i\hbar\frac{\dot{n}_1}{2\sqrt{n_1}} - \hbar\sqrt{n_1}\dot{\theta}_1 = -eV_0\sqrt{n_1} + K\sqrt{n_2}\left(\cos\delta + i\sin\delta\right)$$

Equate Imaginary and Real parts independently:
- **Imaginary part:** $\dfrac{\hbar\dot{n}_1}{2\sqrt{n_1}} = K\sqrt{n_2}\sin\delta \implies \dot{n}_1 = \dfrac{2K}{\hbar}\sqrt{n_1 n_2}\sin\delta$
- **Real part:** $-\hbar\sqrt{n_1}\dot{\theta}_1 = -eV_0\sqrt{n_1} + K\sqrt{n_2}\cos\delta \implies \dot{\theta}_1 = +\dfrac{eV_0}{\hbar} - \dfrac{K}{\hbar}\sqrt{\dfrac{n_2}{n_1}}\cos\delta$

Performing identical algebra on the second equation ($\psi_2$, where $e^{i(\theta_1-\theta_2)} = \cos\delta - i\sin\delta$) yields:
- **Imaginary part:** $\dot{n}_2 = -\dfrac{2K}{\hbar}\sqrt{n_1 n_2}\sin\delta = -\dot{n}_1$ *(exact pair conservation!)*
- **Real part:** $\dot{\theta}_2 = -\dfrac{eV_0}{\hbar} - \dfrac{K}{\hbar}\sqrt{\dfrac{n_1}{n_2}}\cos\delta$

**Step 2 — The Two Josephson Equations:**
For identical superconductors maintained at equal pair density $n_1 = n_2 = n_s^*$ by the external current source:
1. **First Josephson Equation (Current–Phase Relation):** Since the tunneling supercurrent $I$ is directly proportional to the pair transfer rate $\dot{n}_1$ ($I = 2e\,\mathcal{V}\dot{n}_1$):
   $$\boxed{I = I_c\sin\delta}, \qquad I_c \equiv \frac{4e K n_s^* \mathcal{V}}{\hbar}$$
   where $I_c$ is the junction's maximum **critical supercurrent** (determined by barrier thickness and temperature).
2. **Second Josephson Equation (Voltage–Phase Relation):** Subtracting $\dot{\theta}_1$ from $\dot{\theta}_2$ cancels the $K\cos\delta$ terms **identically**:
   $$\boxed{\frac{d\delta}{dt} = \dot{\theta}_2 - \dot{\theta}_1 = -\frac{2e V_0}{\hbar}} \qquad \left(\text{or }\left|\frac{d\delta}{dt}\right| = \frac{2e V_0}{\hbar}\right)$$

#### 1. DC Josephson Effect ($V_0 = 0$)
Setting $V_0 = 0$ forces $\frac{d\delta}{dt} = 0 \implies \delta = \delta_0 = \text{constant}$. A **steady, zero-resistance DC supercurrent** $I = I_c\sin\delta_0$ (anywhere in $[-I_c, +I_c]$) tunnels effortlessly across the insulating barrier with **zero voltage drop**!

#### 2. AC Josephson Effect ($V_0 = \text{const} \ne 0$) & Primary Voltage Standard
Maintaining a constant DC voltage $V_0$ across the junction makes the phase wind linearly in time, $\delta(t) = \delta_0 - \omega_J t$, driving a **high-frequency alternating supercurrent** across the barrier:

$$\boxed{I(t) = I_c\sin\left(\delta_0 - \frac{2eV_0}{\hbar}t\right), \qquad \nu_J = \frac{\omega_J}{2\pi} = \frac{2e}{h}V_0 = \frac{V_0}{\Phi_0}}$$

Evaluating the exact universal **Josephson Constant $K_J = 2e/h$**:

$$\boxed{K_J = \frac{2e}{h} = \frac{2(1.602176634 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C})}{6.62607015 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s}} = 483.5979\text{ GHz mV}^{-1} \approx 483.6\text{ GHz mV}^{-1}}$$

- **Energy Conservation Check:** Each Cooper pair (charge $2e$) tunneling down voltage $V_0$ releases energy $2eV_0$, emitted directly as a microwave/THz photon of energy $h\nu_J = 2eV_0$!
- **Shapiro Steps & International Volt Standard:** Irradiating a Josephson junction with microwave radiation of known frequency $\nu$ phase-locks the AC supercurrent onto razor-sharp constant-voltage plateaus (**Shapiro steps**) at $V_n = n\frac{h\nu}{2e}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$). Since frequency $\nu$ is measurable to $1$ part in $10^{15}$ by atomic clocks, series arrays of thousands of Josephson junctions define the **SI primary standard of the Volt** worldwide!

### 4.2 Macroscopic Quantum Interference — The DC SQUID

A **DC SQUID (Superconducting Quantum Interference Device)** pairs **two identical Josephson junctions** $a$ and $b$ in parallel around a miniature superconducting ring enclosing total magnetic flux $\Phi = \iint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \oint\mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$.

```
                         I_tot
                           |
                    +------+------+
                    |             |
               Junction a    Junction b
                 [ X ]   Φ     [ X ]
                  (δa)  (⊙)     (δb)
                    |             |
                    +------+------+
                           |
                         I_tot
```

**Unabridged Derivation of $I_{\max}(\Phi)$:**
1. **Flux–Phase Linking Around the Loop:** Deep inside the two superconducting branches ($\mathbf{J}_s = 0$), $\nabla\theta = \frac{2e}{\hbar}\mathbf{A}$. Integrating $\nabla\theta$ around the closed loop crossing junction $a$ (phase jump $+\delta_a$) and returning across junction $b$ (phase jump $-\delta_b$), single-valuedness of $\Psi$ demands a total phase winding of $2\pi n$:
   $$(\delta_b - \delta_a) + \frac{2e}{\hbar}\oint\mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2\pi n \implies \delta_b - \delta_a = 2\pi\frac{\Phi}{\Phi_0} \pmod{2\pi}$$
   where $\Phi_0 = \frac{h}{2e} = 2.0678 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}$.
2. **Symmetric Phase Parameterization:** Write $\delta_a = \delta_0 - \frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}$ and $\delta_b = \delta_0 + \frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}$.
3. **Total Parallel Supercurrent:** Sum the currents through both junctions and apply $\sin(A-B) + \sin(A+B) = 2\sin A\cos B$:
   $$I_{\text{tot}} = I_c\sin\delta_a + I_c\sin\delta_b = I_c\left[\sin\left(\delta_0 - \frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right) + \sin\left(\delta_0 + \frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right)\right] = 2I_c\sin\delta_0\cos\left(\frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right)$$
4. **Maximum Critical Current Modulation:** Varying the free overall phase $\delta_0$ so $|\sin\delta_0| = 1$ yields the iconic **DC SQUID Quantum Interference Formula**:

$$\boxed{I_{\max}(\Phi) = 2 I_c \left|\cos\left(\frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right)\right|}$$

**Physical Analogy & Applications:**
- $I_{\max}(\Phi)$ oscillates periodically between $2I_c$ (constructive interference at $\Phi = n\Phi_0$) and $0$ (destructive interference at $\Phi = (n + \frac{1}{2})\Phi_0$)—a **macroscopic superconducting Young's double-slit interferometer**!
- Because $\Phi_0 \approx 2.07 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}$ is astronomically tiny, modern flux-locked DC SQUIDs detect field variations down to **$\sim 1\text{ fT}$ ($10^{-15}\text{ T}$, $10^{11}\times$ weaker than Earth's magnetic field!)**, powering **Magnetoencephalography (MEG)** of human brain currents, biomagnetism, geophysical surveying, ultra-low-noise cryogenic amplifiers, and **superconducting transmon qubits** for quantum computing.

### 4.3 Elementary Ideas of High-Temperature Superconductors (HTS Cuprates)

Until 1986, all known superconductors were conventional metallic elements or intermetallics capped at $T_c = 23.2\text{ K}$ ($\text{Nb}_3\text{Ge}$), requiring expensive liquid helium ($4.2\text{ K}$). In 1986, Georg Bednorz and Alex Müller (Nobel Prize 1987) broke this ceiling with ceramic **cuprate** $\text{La}_{2-x}\text{Ba}_x\text{CuO}_4$ ($T_c \approx 35\text{ K}$). Within months (1987), Paul Chu and Maw-Kuen Wu discovered **$\text{YBa}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{7-\delta}$ (YBCO)** with **$T_c \approx 92\text{–}93\text{ K}$**—surpassing the **$77\text{ K}$ boiling point of cheap, abundant liquid nitrogen**!

Later cuprate families pushed ambient-pressure $T_c$ even higher: **BSCCO** ($\text{Bi}_2\text{Sr}_2\text{Ca}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{10}$, $110\text{ K}$), **TBCCO** ($\text{Tl}_2\text{Ba}_2\text{Ca}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{10}$, $125\text{ K}$), and **$\text{HgBa}_2\text{Ca}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{8+\delta}$** ($133\text{ K}$ ambient; $\sim 164\text{ K}$ under high pressure), alongside iron-based pnictide HTS discovered in 2008 ($T_c \sim 55\text{ K}$).

#### Crystal Structure & Physics of $\text{YBa}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{7-\delta}$ (YBCO)

1. **Layered Oxygen-Deficient Triple Perovskite:** YBCO consists of three stacked defective perovskite cubes along the elongated $c$-axis ($a = 3.82\text{ Å}, b = 3.89\text{ Å}, c = 11.68\text{ Å}$).
2. **2D $\text{CuO}_2$ Superconducting Planes & 1D $\text{Cu–O}$ Charge-Reservoir Chains:** Superconductivity lives almost exclusively inside parallel two-dimensional **$\text{CuO}_2$ sheets** separated by $\text{Y}^{3+}$ ions. The intervening $\text{Cu–O}$ chains (between $\text{BaO}$ planes) act as **charge reservoirs**: varying oxygen deficiency $\delta$ tunes the concentration of mobile **holes** doped into the $\text{CuO}_2$ planes:
   - At $\delta \approx 1$ ($\text{YBa}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_6$, tetragonal): zero doped holes $\implies$ **antiferromagnetic Mott insulator**!
   - At optimal doping $\delta \approx 0.07$ ($\text{YBa}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{6.93}$, orthorhombic): $T_c$ peaks at **$93\text{ K}$**!

#### Four Core Features Distinguishing High-$T_c$ Cuprates from Conventional BCS Metals

1. **Extreme Type-II with Ultra-Short Anisotropic Coherence Length:** In-plane coherence length is only $\xi_{ab} \approx 1.5\text{–}2\text{ nm}$ and $c$-axis coherence length $\xi_c \approx 0.2\text{–}0.4\text{ nm}$ (smaller than a single $c$-axis unit cell!), while $\lambda_{ab} \approx 150\text{ nm}$ and $\lambda_c \approx 700\text{ nm}$. This produces gigantic $\kappa \sim 100$ and upper critical fields exceeding **$B_{c2} > 100\text{ T}$**.
2. **Pronounced 2D Anisotropy:** Critical currents and conductivity are orders of magnitude higher along the $ab$ $\text{CuO}_2$ planes than along the perpendicular $c$-axis (where transport proceeds via intrinsic weak Josephson tunneling across insulating spacer layers).
3. **Unconventional $d$-Wave Pairing & Non-BCS Mechanism Debates:** While Cooper pairs still carry charge $2e$ (verified by $\Phi_0 = h/2e$ and Josephson steps!), cuprates break weak-coupling BCS rules dramatically:
   - **Order-Parameter Symmetry:** Isotropic nodeless **$s$-wave** gap in BCS metals vs. anisotropic sign-changing **$d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave** gap with four zero-gap **nodes** along the $\text{CuO}_2$ diagonals!
   - **Gap Ratio & Isotope Effect:** Large strong-coupling ratio $2\Delta(0)/k_B T_c \approx 5\text{–}8$ (vs. $3.53$), nearly vanishing oxygen isotope exponent ($\alpha \approx 0.02\text{–}0.1$ at optimal doping), and an anomalous **pseudogap** opening above $T_c$ in underdoped samples.
   - **Pairing Glue Debate:** Pure electron–phonon BCS attraction appears insufficient; leading theories invoke **antiferromagnetic spin fluctuations** (strong on-site electron–electron correlations modeled by the 2D Hubbard / $t\text{–}J$ Hamiltonian) mediating $d$-wave pairing directly from the parent antiferromagnetic Mott insulator.
4. **Engineering & Flux-Pinning Hurdles:**
   - **Ceramic Brittleness & Grain-Boundary Weak Links:** Brittle oxides cannot be extruded like ductile $\text{NbTi}$; because $\xi \sim 1.5\text{ nm}$ spans only a few atoms, misaligned grain boundaries sever supercurrents unless textured epitaxially within $<5^\circ$ (Second-Generation REBCO coated-conductor tapes).
   - **Thermal Flux Creep & Pancake Vortices:** At $77\text{ K}$, high thermal fluctuations ($k_B T$) combined with 2D layering slice Abrikosov flux tubes into weakly coupled **2D pancake vortices** prone to thermal depinning (**vortex-lattice melting** and **flux-creep resistance**), requiring deliberate nanoscale artificial pinning centers (e.g., $\text{BaZrO}_3$ nanorods) to maintain high $J_c$ in strong fields!

>    $$E_g(0) = 2\Delta(0) = 3.53\,k_B T_c = 3.53(8.6173 \times 10^{-5}\text{ eV K}^{-1})(3.72\text{ K}) = 1.132 \times 10^{-3}\text{ eV} = \boxed{1.13\text{ meV}}$$
> 2. Minimum photon frequency & maximum wavelength ($h\nu_{\min} = E_g(0)$):
>    $$\nu_{\min} = \frac{E_g(0)}{h} = \frac{1.1316 \times 10^{-3}\text{ eV}}{4.1357 \times 10^{-15}\text{ eV s}} = \boxed{2.74 \times 10^{11}\text{ Hz} \;(274\text{ GHz})}$$
>    $$\lambda_{\max} = \frac{c}{\nu_{\min}} = \frac{2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}}{2.7362 \times 10^{11}\text{ Hz}} = \boxed{1.10 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} \;(1.10\text{ mm})}$$

>
> **(b) AC Josephson Radiation at $V_0 = 25.0\,\mu\text{V} = 2.50 \times 10^{-5}\text{ V}$:**
> $$\nu_J = \frac{2e V_0}{h} = \frac{V_0}{\Phi_0} = \frac{2.50 \times 10^{-5}\text{ V}}{2.0678 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}} = \boxed{1.21 \times 10^{10}\text{ Hz} \;(12.1\text{ GHz})}$$

> $$\lambda_J = \frac{c}{\nu_J} = \frac{2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}}{1.2090 \times 10^{10}\text{ Hz}} = \boxed{2.48\text{ cm}} \quad (\text{X-band microwave})$$
>
> **(c) DC SQUID Magnetic Field Period ($A = 0.50\text{ mm}^2 = 5.0 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}^2$):**

> $$\Delta B = \frac{\Phi_0}{A} = \frac{2.0678 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}}{5.0 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}^2} = \boxed{4.14 \times 10^{-9}\text{ T} \;(4.14\text{ nT})}$$

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **Confusing Ideal Conductor ($\dot{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{0}$) with Meissner Effect ($\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$):** Setting $\rho = 0$ in Ohm's law only proves $\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t = \mathbf{0}$ (trapped flux in field cooling!). Never claim the Meissner effect follows from zero resistance—always show **both ZFC and FC paths** and explicitly point out where the London brothers set the time-integration constant to zero to enforce $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$.
> 2. **Forgetting the Factor of $2e$ (Cooper-Pair Charge) in $\Phi_0$ and $\nu_J$:** Writing $\Phi_0 = h/e$ or $\nu_J = eV_0/h$ instead of **$\Phi_0 = \frac{h}{2e} = 2.068 \times 10^{-15}\text{ Wb}$** and **$\nu_J = \frac{2eV_0}{h}$** cuts both results in half and completely destroys the central experimental proof of Cooper pairing!
> 3. **Missing Explicit $\mu_0$ in SI London & Thermodynamic Equations:** Keep explicit $\mu_0$ everywhere: $\lambda_L = \sqrt{\frac{m}{\mu_0 n_s e^2}}$ (NOT CGS $\sqrt{\frac{mc^2}{4\pi n_s e^2}}$), $\Delta G = \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 H_c^2 V = \frac{B_c^2 V}{2\mu_0}$, and Rutgers' jump $\Delta C = \mu_0 V T_c (dH_c/dT)^2 = \frac{V T_c}{\mu_0}(dB_c/dT)^2$.
> 4. **Confusing $2\Delta(0)$ vs. $\Delta(0)$ in BCS Energy Gap:** Total pair-breaking energy (photon absorption threshold & S–I–S tunneling onset) is **$E_g(0) = 2\Delta(0) = 3.53\,k_B T_c$**, whereas the single-quasiparticle excitation gap entering S–I–N tunneling and exponential specific heat $C_{es} \propto e^{-\Delta(0)/k_B T}$ is **$\Delta(0) = 1.764\,k_B T_c$**.
> 5. **Mixing up First-Order ($H > 0$) vs. Second-Order ($H = 0$) Superconducting Transitions:** At $H = 0, T = T_c$, latent heat vanishes ($L = 0$) and $C$ jumps upward $\implies$ **2nd-order**. At $H = H_c(T) > 0, T < T_c$, $S_s < S_n$ produces nonzero latent heat $L = T(S_n - S_s) > 0$ $\implies$ **1st-order**!
> 6. **Misreading the Period of $|\cos(\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)|$ in a DC SQUID:** Because of the **absolute value** bars around $\cos(\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)$, $I_{\max}(\Phi)$ repeats every **$\Delta\Phi = \Phi_0$** (NOT $2\Phi_0$!).

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Answering "Why Superconductors Must Be Diamagnetic" Superficially (2023 Trap):** Writing merely "because $\chi = -1$" loses half the marks! Full step-marks demand proving **why** $\rho = 0$ alone yields only $\dot{\mathbf{B}} = 0$ (history-dependent trapped flux), contrasting ZFC vs. FC trajectories, and showing that $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M}) = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{M} = -\mathbf{H} \implies \chi = -1$ makes the superconducting phase a true reversible equilibrium state!
- **Skipping the Integration-Constant Step in the London Derivation (2011/2026 Trap):** Jumping blindly from $\mathbf{E} = \frac{m}{n_s e^2}\dot{\mathbf{J}}_s$ to $\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s = -\frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{B}$ without explicitly showing $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla\times\mathbf{J}_s + \frac{n_s e^2}{m}\mathbf{B}) = 0$ and stating why the Londons set the arbitrary constant to zero costs 3–4 marks; in 2026-style two-fluid prompts, **explicitly invert** $\lambda_L(T)$ to display $n_s(T) = \frac{m}{\mu_0 e^2 \lambda_L^2(T)} = n[1 - (T/T_c)^4]$.
- **Asserting $\nu = 2eV/h$ Without Feynman's Coupled Equations (2003/2010 Trap):** Quoting $h\nu = 2eV$ purely by energy conservation without writing the coupled macroscopic wavefunctions $i\hbar\dot{\psi}_1 = U_1\psi_1 + K\psi_2$ and $i\hbar\dot{\psi}_2 = U_2\psi_2 + K\psi_1$, separating real and imaginary parts, and canceling the $\cos\delta$ terms leaves 10–15 marks on the table in a 20–30 mark question.
- **Thinning Out High-$T_c$ Answers (2003 Trap):** Naming only YBCO ($92\text{ K}$) without citing a second family (BSCCO $110\text{ K}$ or $\text{HgBa}_2\text{Ca}_2\text{Cu}_3\text{O}_{8+\delta}$ $133\text{ K}$), layered **2D $\text{CuO}_2$ planes**, ultra-short anisotropic $\xi \sim 1.5\text{ nm}$, **$d$-wave pairing**, and $77\text{ K}$ **flux-creep / grain-boundary hurdles** restricts marks to average textbook credit.

### 3. Unseen Conceptual & Analytical Twists

1. **Superconducting Thin Film Parallel to $\mathbf{B}_0$ ($d \lesssim \lambda_L$):** Solve the 1D London equation $\frac{d^2 B_z}{dx^2} = \frac{B_z}{\lambda_L^2}$ inside a thin superconducting film spanning $-d/2 \le x \le +d/2$ with symmetric boundary conditions $B_z(\pm d/2) = B_0$.
