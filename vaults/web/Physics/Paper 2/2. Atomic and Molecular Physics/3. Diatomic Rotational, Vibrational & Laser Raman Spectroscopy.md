# Chapter 3: Diatomic Rotational, Vibrational & Laser Raman Spectroscopy

## 1. The Born–Oppenheimer Approximation & Molecular Energy Scales

Unlike isolated atoms, which possess only electronic degrees of freedom, even the simplest diatomic molecule simultaneously rotates end-over-end, vibrates along its internuclear bond, and undergoes electronic excitations. Solving the coupled multi-particle Schrödinger equation for both nuclei and electrons directly is intractable—yet nature hands us a decisive small parameter: the **electron-to-nuclear mass ratio**, $m_e / M \lesssim 5 \times 10^{-4}$.

Because nuclei are $2000\text{–}10^5$ times heavier than electrons, electrons move $\sim \sqrt{M/m_e} \sim 50\text{–}300$ times faster and instantaneously readjust their quantum cloud to any nuclear separation $R$. This clean timescale separation is the **Born–Oppenheimer (Adiabatic) Approximation** (1927).

### 1.1 Wavefunction Factorization & Competing Energy-Scale Hierarchy

1. **Exact Molecular Hamiltonian:**
   For nuclei of masses $M_\alpha$ at positions $\mathbf{R}$ and electrons of mass $m_e$ at positions $\mathbf{r}$:
   $$\hat{H} = \hat{T}_N(\mathbf{R}) + \hat{T}_e(\mathbf{r}) + V_{NN}(\mathbf{R}) + V_{ee}(\mathbf{r}) + V_{eN}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{R}).$$

2. **Two-Step Adiabatic Decoupling:**
   First clamp the nuclei at fixed separation $R$ ($\hat{T}_N \to 0$) and solve the electronic Schrödinger equation:
   $$\left[\hat{T}_e + V_{ee}(\mathbf{r}) + V_{eN}(\mathbf{r}, R)\right]\psi_{\text{el}}(\mathbf{r}; R) = E_{\text{el}}(R)\,\psi_{\text{el}}(\mathbf{r}; R).$$
   The electronic eigenvalue plus nuclear repulsion, $V(R) = E_{\text{el}}(R) + V_{NN}(R)$, then serves as the **effective potential energy curve** governing nuclear vibration and rotation! Neglecting tiny off-diagonal nuclear-kinetic derivative couplings ($\propto (m_e/M)^{3/4}$), the total molecular wavefunction factorizes cleanly and the total energy adds linearly:
   $$\boxed{\Psi(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{R}) = \psi_{\text{el}}(\mathbf{r}; R)\,\psi_{\text{vib}}(R)\,\psi_{\text{rot}}(\theta, \phi), \qquad E_{\text{tot}} = E_{\text{el}} + E_{\text{vib}} + E_{\text{rot}}}$$
   In spectroscopic wavenumber term values ($T = E / hc$ in $\text{cm}^{-1}$ or $\text{m}^{-1}$):
   $$T_{\text{tot}} = T_e + G(v) + F(J).$$

3. **Order-of-Magnitude Energy Hierarchy ($\Delta E_{\text{el}} \gg \Delta E_{\text{vib}} \gg \Delta E_{\text{rot}}$):**
   Let $a \sim a_0$ denote the characteristic molecular bond length and $\mu$ the nuclear reduced mass:
   - **Electronic scale:** $\Delta E_{\text{el}} \sim \frac{\hbar^2}{m_e a^2} \sim 1\text{–}10\text{ eV}$ ($10^4\text{–}10^5\text{ cm}^{-1}$, **Visible / UV**).
   - **Vibrational scale:** Since the bond spring constant scales as $k \sim \frac{\Delta E_{\text{el}}}{a^2} \sim \frac{\hbar^2}{m_e a^4}$, the vibrational quantum is:
     $$\Delta E_{\text{vib}} = \hbar\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}} \sim \left(\frac{m_e}{\mu}\right)^{1/2} \Delta E_{\text{el}} \sim 10^{-2}\text{–}0.5\text{ eV} \quad (10^2\text{–}4000\text{ cm}^{-1},\ \textbf{Infrared}).$$
   - **Rotational scale:** With moment of inertia $I = \mu a^2$:
     $$\Delta E_{\text{rot}} \sim \frac{\hbar^2}{I} = \frac{\hbar^2}{\mu a^2} \sim \left(\frac{m_e}{\mu}\right) \Delta E_{\text{el}} \sim 10^{-5}\text{–}10^{-2}\text{ eV} \quad (0.1\text{–}100\text{ cm}^{-1},\ \textbf{Microwave / Far-IR}).$$
   - **Canonical Ratio:**
     $$\boxed{\Delta E_{\text{el}} : \Delta E_{\text{vib}} : \Delta E_{\text{rot}} \approx 1 : \left(\frac{m_e}{\mu}\right)^{1/2} : \left(\frac{m_e}{\mu}\right) \approx 1 : 10^{-2} : 10^{-4}}$$
   Because these three energy tiers sit two orders of magnitude apart, **microwave photons** excite pure rotation, **infrared photons** excite simultaneous vibration–rotation bands, and **visible/UV photons** excite electronic transitions adorned with vibrational coarse structure and rotational fine structure!

## 2. Pure Rotational (Microwave) Spectroscopy of Diatomic Molecules

To absorb or emit a microwave photon directly via pure rotation, a molecule must present a **rotating electric dipole** to the oscillating electromagnetic field. That imposes the **gross selection rule** of pure rotational spectroscopy: the molecule must possess a **permanent electric dipole moment ($\boldsymbol{\mu}_0 \neq 0$)**. Heteronuclear diatomics ($\text{HF, HCl, CO, NO}$) are microwave-active; homonuclear diatomics ($\text{H}_2, \text{N}_2, \text{O}_2$) and centrosymmetric linear molecules ($\text{CO}_2$) are completely **microwave-silent**.

### 2.1 The Rigid Rotator - Energy Levels, Bond Length & Isotopic Substitution

#### Derivation: Rigid-Rotator Term Values & Uniform $2B$ Line Spacing

1. **Physical Model & Hamiltonian:**
   Model a diatomic molecule as two point nuclei of masses $m_1, m_2$ fixed at equilibrium separation $r_0$ rotating freely about their center of mass. Transforming to center-of-mass coordinates yields reduced mass $\mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ and moment of inertia $I = \mu r_0^2$. The rotational Hamiltonian is:
   $$\hat{H}_{\text{rot}} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{J}}^2}{2I},$$
   whose exact eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics $Y_J^{M_J}(\theta, \phi)$ with eigenvalues:
   $$E_J = \frac{\hbar^2}{2I} J(J+1) = \frac{h^2}{8\pi^2 I} J(J+1), \qquad J = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$
   Each level $J$ is **$(2J + 1)$-fold degenerate** ($M_J = -J, -J+1, \dots, +J$).

2. **Rotational Constant $B$ & Wavenumber Term Value $F(J)$:**
   Dividing $E_J$ by $hc$ converts energy to **wavenumber** (strict SI unit: $\text{m}^{-1}$; spectroscopic convention: divide by $100$ for $\text{cm}^{-1}$!):
   $$\boxed{F(J) = \frac{E_J}{hc} = B J(J+1), \qquad B = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 I c}\ (\text{m}^{-1}) = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 I c \times 100}\ (\text{cm}^{-1})}$$
   *(Calculator shortcut in $\text{cm}^{-1}$: $B\ (\text{cm}^{-1}) = \frac{2.7993 \times 10^{-46}}{I\ (\text{kg m}^2)}$; in frequency units: $\tilde{B}_{\text{Hz}} = c B = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 I}$ in $\text{Hz}$ or $\text{GHz}$.)*

3. **Specific Selection Rule & Spectral Line Positions:**
   Evaluating the electric-dipole transition integral $\langle J', M' | \mu_0 \cos\theta | J, M \rangle$ via spherical-harmonic recurrence relations restricts transitions strictly to **$\Delta J = \pm 1$** ($+1$ for absorption, $-1$ for emission). For absorption $J \to J + 1$:
   $$\bar{\nu}_{J \to J+1} = F(J+1) - F(J) = B(J+1)(J+2) - BJ(J+1) = B(J+1)\left[(J+2) - J\right],$$
   $$\boxed{\bar{\nu}_{J \to J+1} = 2B(J+1), \qquad J = 0, 1, 2, \dots}$$
   The pure rotational spectrum consists of a picket fence of **equidistant lines** at $2B, 4B, 6B, 8B, \dots$ with **constant adjacent separation $\Delta\bar{\nu} = 2B$**! Measuring $\Delta\bar{\nu}$ immediately yields $B = \Delta\bar{\nu}/2$, hence $I = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 B c}$, and finally the **equilibrium bond length** $r_0 = \sqrt{I / \mu}$ to $\pm 0.001\text{ Å}$ precision!

4. **Dimensional & Limiting-Case Sanity Checks:**
   - *Dimensions of $B$:* $[h / (I c)] = \frac{\text{J s}}{(\text{kg m}^2)(\text{m s}^{-1})} = \frac{\text{kg m}^2 \text{ s}^{-1}}{\text{kg m}^3 \text{ s}^{-1}} = \text{m}^{-1}$. Exact!
   - *Heavy-molecule limit ($I \to \infty$):* $B \to 0$, rotational levels collapse into a classical continuum.

#### Effect of Isotopic Substitution
Within the Born–Oppenheimer approximation, replacing one nucleus by a heavier isotope leaves the electronic cloud—and therefore the equilibrium bond length **$r_0$**—strictly **unchanged**, while increasing reduced mass $\mu' > \mu$ and moment of inertia $I' = \mu' r_0^2 > I$. Consequently, the rotational constant shrinks and all rotational lines shift toward lower wavenumber:
$$\boxed{\frac{B'}{B} = \frac{I}{I'} = \frac{\mu}{\mu'}}$$
Isotopic substitution serves two critical analytical purposes: *(i)* measuring ultra-precise atomic mass ratios (e.g., $^{13}\text{C}/^{12}\text{C}$ from $\text{CO}$), and *(ii)* extracting two independent bond lengths in linear triatomics like $\text{O}=\text{C}=\text{S}$ (where a single $I$ cannot solve two unknowns $r_{\text{CO}}$ and $r_{\text{CS}}$, but substituting $^{18}\text{O}$ or $^{34}\text{S}$ supplies a second independent moment of inertia!).

### 2.2 Thermal Population Distribution & Peak Rotational Level $J_{\max}$

Why is the lowest rotational transition **$J = 0 \to 1$ almost never the most intense line** at room temperature? Because the line intensity tracks the lower-state population $N_J$, which pits the linearly rising **$(2J + 1)$-fold spatial degeneracy** against the exponentially decaying **Boltzmann factor**:
$$\frac{N_J}{N_0} = (2J + 1)\exp\left(-\frac{E_J}{k_B T}\right) = (2J + 1)\exp\left(-\frac{hc B J(J+1)}{k_B T}\right).$$
At low $J$, degeneracy $(2J+1)$ wins and $N_J$ rises; at high $J$, the quadratic exponential $e^{-\beta B J^2}$ wins and $N_J$ plummets, producing a characteristic asymmetric peak at $J_{\max}$!

#### Derivation: Exact $J_{\max}$ & High-Temperature Scaling
Differentiate $f(J) = (2J + 1)\exp\left[-\frac{hc B}{k_B T}J(J+1)\right]$ with respect to $J$ and set $\frac{df}{dJ} = 0$:
$$\left[2 - (2J+1)\cdot\frac{hc B}{k_B T}(2J+1)\right]\exp\left(-\frac{hc B J(J+1)}{k_B T}\right) = 0 \implies (2J_{\max} + 1)^2 = \frac{2k_B T}{hc B},$$
$$\boxed{J_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{k_B T}{2 hc B}} - \frac{1}{2}} \quad (\text{rounded to nearest integer})$$
- **High-Temperature Scaling (UPSC CSE 2025):** When $k_B T \gg hcB$, the $-\frac{1}{2}$ offset is negligible so $J_{\max} \propto \sqrt{T}$. Thus, **doubling the absolute temperature ($T' = 2T$) increases $J_{\max}$ by a factor of $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$** (more accurately, $J_{\max}' = \sqrt{2}\left(J_{\max} + \frac{1}{2}\right) - \frac{1}{2}$).

### 2.3 The Non-Rigid Rotator - Centrifugal Distortion & Kratzer's Relation

A real chemical bond is an elastic spring of force constant $k$, not a rigid rod. At high rotational quantum numbers $J$, centrifugal force stretches the bond by $\Delta r = r - r_0 > 0$, increasing $I(J)$ and pulling rotational levels slightly **downward**!

#### Derivation: Centrifugal Distortion Constant $D$ & Kratzer's Relation
1. **Mechanical Force Balance:**
   Equate Hooke's restoring force $k\,\Delta r$ to the centrifugal force $\frac{L^2}{\mu r^3} \approx \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{\mu r_0^3}$:
   $$\Delta r = \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{k\mu r_0^3}.$$
2. **Total Rotational + Elastic Potential Energy:**
   Expanding $(r_0 + \Delta r)^{-2} \approx r_0^{-2}\left(1 - \frac{2\Delta r}{r_0}\right)$:
   $$E_J = \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{2\mu (r_0 + \Delta r)^2} + \frac{1}{2}k(\Delta r)^2 \approx \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{2\mu r_0^2} - \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{\mu r_0^3}\Delta r + \frac{1}{2}k(\Delta r)^2.$$
   Substituting $\frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{\mu r_0^3} = k\,\Delta r$ cancels half the cross-term cleanly:
   $$E_J = \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{2I} - \frac{1}{2}k(\Delta r)^2 = \frac{\hbar^2 J(J+1)}{2I} - \frac{\hbar^4}{2 k \mu^2 r_0^6} J^2(J+1)^2.$$
3. **Non-Rigid Term Value, Kratzer Relation & Line Positions:**
   Dividing by $hc$ yields the non-rigid term value $F(J) = B J(J+1) - D J^2(J+1)^2$, where $D = \frac{h^3}{32\pi^4 I^2 r_0^2 k c}$. Substituting $k = 4\pi^2 c^2 \mu \bar{\omega}_e^2$ and $B = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 I c}$ simplifies $D$ into **Kratzer's Relation**:
   $$\boxed{F(J) = B J(J+1) - D J^2(J+1)^2, \qquad D = \frac{4 B^3}{\bar{\omega}_e^2}}$$
   For absorption $J \to J + 1$, factor $(J+1)^2(J+2)^2 - J^2(J+1)^2 = (J+1)^2[(J+2-J)(J+2+J)] = 4(J+1)^3$:
   $$\boxed{\bar{\nu}_{J \to J+1} = 2B(J+1) - 4D(J+1)^3}$$
   *Diagnostic signature:* Because $D / B \sim 4(B/\bar{\omega}_e)^2 \sim 10^{-5}$, line spacing shrinks progressively at high $J$. Plotting $\frac{\bar{\nu}}{J+1}$ against $(J+1)^2$ yields a straight line with intercept $2B$ and slope $-4D$, directly estimating the bond vibrational frequency $\bar{\omega}_e$ from pure microwave data!

> [!example]- Worked Archetype 1: Pure Rotational Spectrum of $\text{HF}$ (UPSC CSE 2025 Archetype), Isotopic $\text{DF}$ & $J_{\max}$
> **Problem:**
> From the pure rotational absorption spectrum of hydrogen fluoride ($\text{HF}$), the wavenumber difference between consecutive rotational lines is found to be $\Delta\bar{\nu} = 4050\text{ m}^{-1}$. Given $M_H = 1\text{ u}$, $M_D = 2\text{ u}$, $M_F = 19\text{ u}$ ($1\text{ u} = 1.6605 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$):
> *(a)* Calculate: (i) rotational constant $B$ (in $\text{m}^{-1}$ and $\text{cm}^{-1}$), (ii) moment of inertia $I$, and (iii) equilibrium bond length $r_0$ of $\text{HF}$.
> *(b)* Predict the rotational constant $B_{\text{DF}}$ and line spacing upon deuteration ($\text{DF}$).
> *(c)* Find the most intensely populated rotational state $J_{\max}$ of $\text{HF}$ at $T = 300\text{ K}$ and at $T = 600\text{ K}$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a) - Constants & Bond Length of $\text{HF}$:**
> 1. *Rotational constant $B$:*
>    $$2B = 4050\text{ m}^{-1} \implies \boxed{B = 2025\text{ m}^{-1} = 20.25\text{ cm}^{-1}}.$$
> 2. *Moment of inertia $I$ (strict SI: keep $B$ in $\text{m}^{-1}$ and $c = 2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$!):*
>    $$I = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 B c} = \frac{6.6261 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s}}{8\pi^2 (2025\text{ m}^{-1})(2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1})} = \frac{6.6261 \times 10^{-34}}{4.7933 \times 10^{13}} = \boxed{1.382 \times 10^{-47}\text{ kg m}^2}.$$
>    *(Shortcut check: $I = \frac{2.7993 \times 10^{-46}}{20.25} = 1.3824 \times 10^{-47}\text{ kg m}^2$. Exact!)*
> 3. *Reduced mass $\mu$ and bond length $r_0$:*
>    $$\mu_{\text{HF}} = \frac{1 \times 19}{1 + 19}\text{ u} = 0.9500 \times 1.6605 \times 10^{-27} = 1.5775 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}.$$
>    $$r_0 = \sqrt{\frac{I}{\mu_{\text{HF}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.3824 \times 10^{-47}}{1.5775 \times 10^{-27}}} = \sqrt{8.763 \times 10^{-21}} = \boxed{9.36 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m} = 0.936\text{ Å}}.$$
>
> **Part (b) - Isotopic Substitution ($\text{HF} \to \text{DF}$):**
> $$\mu_{\text{DF}} = \frac{2 \times 19}{2 + 19}\text{ u} = 1.8095\text{ u} \implies B_{\text{DF}} = B_{\text{HF}}\frac{\mu_{\text{HF}}}{\mu_{\text{DF}}} = 20.25 \times \frac{0.9500}{1.8095} = \boxed{10.63\text{ cm}^{-1}}.$$
> Adjacent rotational line spacing shrinks nearly in half to $2B_{\text{DF}} = \boxed{21.26\text{ cm}^{-1}\ (2126\text{ m}^{-1})}$.
>
> **Part (c) - Most Populated Level $J_{\max}$ at $300\text{ K}$ vs. $600\text{ K}$:**
> At $T = 300\text{ K}$, $\frac{k_B T}{hc} = \frac{(1.3806 \times 10^{-23})(300)}{(6.6261 \times 10^{-34})(2.9979 \times 10^{10}\text{ cm s}^{-1})} = 208.5\text{ cm}^{-1}$:
> - At $300\text{ K}$: $J_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{208.5}{2(20.25)}} - \frac{1}{2} = \sqrt{5.148} - 0.5 = 2.27 - 0.5 = 1.77 \implies \boxed{J_{\max} = 2}$.
> - At $600\text{ K}$: $J_{\max} = \sqrt{2 \times 5.148} - \frac{1}{2} = 3.21 - 0.5 = 2.71 \implies \boxed{J_{\max} = 3}$.

## 3. Vibrational–Rotational (Infrared) Spectra of Diatomic Molecules

For a molecular vibration to absorb or emit infrared radiation, the **electric dipole moment must oscillate during the vibration**—giving the **IR gross selection rule**:
$$\left(\frac{d\mu}{dr}\right)_{r_e} \neq 0.$$
Hence heteronuclear diatomics ($\text{HCl, CO, HF, NO}$) are IR-active, whereas homonuclear diatomics ($\text{H}_2, \text{N}_2, \text{O}_2$) have $\mu(r) \equiv 0$ at every bond extension and are **strictly IR-inactive**.

### 3.1 Harmonic Oscillator vs. Morse Anharmonic Oscillator

1. **Harmonic Approximation ($V(r) = \frac{1}{2}k(r - r_e)^2$):**
   Quantum mechanics yields equidistant vibrational levels $E_v = \left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right)h\nu_e$, or in wavenumbers:
   $$G(v) = \bar{\omega}_e\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right), \qquad \bar{\omega}_e = \frac{1}{2\pi c}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}, \qquad v = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$
   Strict harmonic selection rule **$\Delta v = \pm 1$** predicts **a single IR line** at $\bar{\nu} = \bar{\omega}_e$ and an unphysical infinite dissociation energy!

2. **Real Molecules - The Morse Anharmonic Potential (1929):**
   A real chemical bond resists sharp compression as nuclei approach ($r \to 0$) and breaks cleanly into neutral atoms as $r \to \infty$. Philip Morse captured both limits with an analytically solvable potential:
   $$V(r) = D_e\left[1 - e^{-\beta(r - r_e)}\right]^2, \qquad \beta = \sqrt{\frac{k}{2D_e}},$$
   where $D_e$ is the **equilibrium dissociation energy** measured from the potential minimum. Solving the 1D Schrödinger equation yields the **exact anharmonic vibrational term values**:
   $$\boxed{G(v) = \bar{\omega}_e\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right) - \bar{\omega}_e x_e\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right)^2}$$
   where $x_e = \frac{hc\bar{\omega}_e}{4D_e}$ ($\sim 10^{-3}\text{–}10^{-2}$) is the dimensionless **anharmonicity constant**!
   - *Limiting check ($x_e \to 0$, i.e., $D_e \to \infty$):* Recovers $G(v) = \bar{\omega}_e(v + \frac{1}{2})$ identically.
   - *Zero-point energy:* $G(0) = \frac{1}{2}\bar{\omega}_e - \frac{1}{4}\bar{\omega}_e x_e$.

3. **Fundamental, Overtones & Hot Bands ($\Delta v = \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots$):**
   Anharmonicity (both mechanical anharmonicity in $V(r)$ and electrical anharmonicity $\frac{d^2\mu}{dr^2} \neq 0$) relaxes the selection rule to **$\Delta v = \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots$** with rapidly diminishing intensity:
   - **Fundamental ($v = 0 \to 1$, strongest):** $\bar{\nu}_{0\to 1} = G(1) - G(0) = \bar{\omega}_e(1 - 2x_e)$.
   - **First Overtone ($v = 0 \to 2$, weak):** $\bar{\nu}_{0\to 2} = G(2) - G(0) = 2\bar{\omega}_e(1 - 3x_e)$.
   - **Second Overtone ($v = 0 \to 3$, very weak):** $\bar{\nu}_{0\to 3} = G(3) - G(0) = 3\bar{\omega}_e(1 - 4x_e)$.
     *(General $0 \to v$ formula: $\bar{\nu}_{0\to v} = v\bar{\omega}_e[1 - (v+1)x_e]$.)*
   - **Hot Bands ($v = 1 \to 2$, $2 \to 3$, ...):**
     $$\bar{\nu}_{1\to 2} = G(2) - G(1) = \bar{\omega}_e(1 - 4x_e).$$
     Because room-temperature thermal energy ($k_B T / hc \approx 208.5\text{ cm}^{-1}$) is far smaller than $\bar{\omega}_e$ ($\sim 1500\text{–}4000\text{ cm}^{-1}$), the population ratio $N_1/N_0 = e^{-hc\bar{\nu}_{01}/k_B T}$ is tiny at $300\text{ K}$ ($\sim 10^{-4}\text{–}10^{-8}$). Heating the gas thermally populates $v = 1$, making the $1 \to 2$ absorption line (positioned $2\bar{\omega}_e x_e$ *below* the fundamental) grow sharply in intensity—earning the name **hot band**!

4. **Dissociation Energy ($D_e, D_0$) & Birge–Sponer Extrapolation:**
   Adjacent level spacing shrinks linearly with $v$:
   $$\Delta G\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right) = G(v+1) - G(v) = \bar{\omega}_e - 2\bar{\omega}_e x_e(v + 1).$$
   Levels converge to the dissociation continuum when $\frac{dG}{dv} = 0$, i.e., $v_{\max} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2x_e}$. Evaluating $G(v_{\max})$ gives the equilibrium well depth $D_e$ and the experimentally measurable **ground-state dissociation energy $D_0$**:
   $$\boxed{D_e = \frac{\bar{\omega}_e}{4x_e}\ (\text{cm}^{-1}), \qquad D_0 = D_e - G(0) = \frac{\bar{\omega}_e(1 - x_e)^2}{4x_e}}$$
   Plotting observed $\Delta G(v + \frac{1}{2})$ against $(v + \frac{1}{2})$ gives a **Birge–Sponer plot**, whose total enclosed area equals $D_0 = \sum_{v=0}^{v_{\max}} \Delta G$. *(Because real potentials flatten faster than Morse at very large $r$, linear Birge–Sponer extrapolation slightly overestimates true $D_0$ by $\sim 10\text{–}20\%$.)*

### 3.2 Simultaneous Vibrational–Rotational Spectra - P, R & Missing Q Branches

Because a gas-phase diatomic molecule rotates freely *while* vibrating, every IR vibrational band breaks under high resolution into a rich fine-structure manifold of **vibrational–rotational lines**:
$$S(v, J) = G(v) + B_v J(J+1), \qquad B_v = B_e - \alpha_e\left(v + \frac{1}{2}\right).$$

#### Selection Rules & Branch Anatomy ($\bar{\nu}_0 = G(1) - G(0)$)
For every closed-shell diatomic molecule whose ground electronic state has zero electronic orbital angular momentum along the bond axis ($\Lambda = 0$, i.e., $^1\Sigma$ states such as $\text{HCl, HF, CO}$), electric-dipole selection rules forbid $\Delta J = 0$:
$$\Delta v = +1\ (+2, \dots), \qquad \Delta J = \pm 1 \quad (\text{NO Q-branch!}).$$
*Why is $\Delta J = 0$ forbidden?* A photon carries intrinsic angular momentum $1\hbar$; with zero electronic angular momentum along the internuclear axis ($\Lambda = 0$), conservation of total angular momentum and parity forbids absorbing a photon without altering the nuclear rotation $J$. Consequently, the pure vibrational **band origin $\bar{\nu}_0$ is a missing line!**

1. **Rigid-Rotor Approximation ($B_1 = B_0 = B$):**
   - **R-Branch ($\Delta J = +1$: $J'' \to J' = J'' + 1$, $J'' = 0, 1, 2, \dots$):**
     $$\boxed{\bar{\nu}_R(J'') = \bar{\nu}_0 + 2B(J'' + 1) \qquad (J'' = 0, 1, 2, \dots)}$$
     Lines sit at $\bar{\nu}_0 + 2B,\ \bar{\nu}_0 + 4B,\ \bar{\nu}_0 + 6B, \dots$ (high-wavenumber wing).
   - **P-Branch ($\Delta J = -1$: $J'' \to J' = J'' - 1$, $J'' = 1, 2, 3, \dots$):**
     $$\boxed{\bar{\nu}_P(J'') = \bar{\nu}_0 - 2B J'' \qquad (J'' = 1, 2, 3, \dots)}$$
     Lines sit at $\bar{\nu}_0 - 2B,\ \bar{\nu}_0 - 4B,\ \bar{\nu}_0 - 6B, \dots$ (low-wavenumber wing; $J'' = 0$ cannot step down to $-1$!).
   - **Diagnostic Zero-Gap:** The separation between the innermost lines $R(0)$ and $P(1)$ is **$\mathbf{4B}$**—exactly twice the $2B$ line spacing within each wing—providing an unmistakable signature of the missing band origin $\bar{\nu}_0$!

2. **Vibration–Rotation Interaction ($B_1 < B_0$):**
   Because the asymmetric Morse well expands average bond length in $v = 1$, $I_1 > I_0 \implies B_1 < B_0$. Writing both branches via a single integer $m$ ($m = J'' + 1 = +1, +2, \dots$ for R-branch; $m = -J'' = -1, -2, \dots$ for P-branch):
   $$\boxed{\bar{\nu}(m) = \bar{\nu}_0 + (B_1 + B_0)m + (B_1 - B_0)m^2 \qquad (m = \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots)}$$
   Since $(B_1 - B_0) < 0$, **R-branch lines progressively crowd closer together** (even turning back into a band head at extreme $J$), whereas **P-branch lines spread progressively farther apart**!

3. **When DOES a Q-Branch ($\Delta J = 0$) Appear?**
   - In paramagnetic diatomics with **$\Lambda \neq 0$** (non-zero electronic orbital angular momentum along the bond axis)—most famously **Nitric Oxide ($\text{NO}$, ground state $^2\Pi$, $\Lambda = 1$)**—where the photon's angular momentum is absorbed by precessing the electronic axis without changing $J$, yielding a sharp central **Q-branch** at $\bar{\nu}_Q(J) = \bar{\nu}_0 + (B_1 - B_0)J(J+1)$ right at the band origin!
   - In **perpendicular bending vibrations** of linear polyatomic molecules (e.g., $\text{CO}_2$ bending mode $\nu_2$ at $667\text{ cm}^{-1}$), where degenerate bending creates vibrational angular momentum along the molecular axis.

## 4. The Raman Effect, Molecular Structure & Laser Raman Spectroscopy

Microwave and infrared absorption spectroscopies face a glaring blind spot: **homonuclear diatomics** ($\text{H}_2, \text{N}_2, \text{O}_2$) and symmetric vibrations of centrosymmetric molecules ($\text{CO}_2$) carry zero oscillating electric dipole moment and are completely invisible! In 1928, C. V. Raman and K. S. Krishnan discovered an **inelastic light-scattering** mechanism that operates in the convenient visible/near-IR optical band and probes **polarizability** rather than permanent dipole moments—unlocking rotational and vibrational structure across the entire molecular world!

### 4.1 Classical Polarizability Theory vs. Quantum Virtual-State Theory

When monochromatic radiation of frequency $\nu_0$ ($\bar{\nu}_0$) illuminates a molecular sample, almost all scattered light emerges at unchanged frequency $\nu_0$ (**elastic Rayleigh scattering**). A tiny fraction ($\sim 10^{-6}\text{–}10^{-8}$) scatters **inelastically** at displaced frequencies:
- **Stokes Lines ($\nu_S = \nu_0 - \Delta\nu$):** Lower frequency (red-shifted); photon transfers energy $h\Delta\nu$ to excite the molecule.
- **Anti-Stokes Lines ($\nu_{AS} = \nu_0 + \Delta\nu$):** Higher frequency (blue-shifted); already-excited molecule transfers energy $h\Delta\nu$ to the photon.
- **Key Property:** The **Raman shift** $\Delta\bar{\nu} = |\bar{\nu}_0 - \bar{\nu}_{\text{Raman}}|$ depends **exclusively on the molecular energy spacing** and is strictly independent of the exciting wavelength $\lambda_0$!

#### 1. Classical Derivation (Modulated Polarizability Tensor)
An incident optical field $E(t) = E_0 \cos(2\pi\nu_0 t)$ distorts the molecular electron cloud, inducing an electric dipole moment $p(t) = \alpha E(t)$, where $\alpha$ is the **molecular polarizability**. Expand $\alpha$ along a molecular normal vibration coordinate $q(t) = q_0 \cos(2\pi\nu_m t)$:
$$\alpha(t) = \alpha_0 + \left(\frac{d\alpha}{dq}\right)_0 q_0 \cos(2\pi\nu_m t) = \alpha_0 + \Delta\alpha \cos(2\pi\nu_m t).$$
Substituting into $p(t) = \alpha(t)E(t)$ and applying $2\cos A\cos B = \cos(A - B) + \cos(A + B)$:
$$p(t) = \underbrace{\alpha_0 E_0 \cos(2\pi\nu_0 t)}_{\text{Rayleigh } (\nu_0)} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\Delta\alpha\, E_0 \cos\left[2\pi(\nu_0 - \nu_m)t\right]}_{\text{Stokes } (\nu_0 - \nu_m)} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\Delta\alpha\, E_0 \cos\left[2\pi(\nu_0 + \nu_m)t\right]}_{\text{Anti-Stokes } (\nu_0 + \nu_m)}.$$
- **Raman Gross Selection Rule:** A vibration is Raman-active if and only if **$\left(\frac{d\alpha}{dq}\right)_0 \neq 0$** (the polarizability changes during vibration!).
- **Fatal Flaw of Classical Theory:** Predicts equal dipole amplitudes for Stokes and Anti-Stokes—which, via dipole radiation power $I \propto \nu^4$, would make Anti-Stokes *brighter* than Stokes! Experiment shows **Stokes is overwhelmingly brighter than Anti-Stokes**.

#### 2. Quantum Theory (Virtual Intermediate States & Boltzmann Intensity Ratio)
In the quantum picture (Smekal–Kramers–Heisenberg), an incident photon $h\nu_0$ collides with a molecule in initial state $|i\rangle$, momentarily polarizing it into a non-stationary **virtual state** (a superposition of all excited electronic eigenstates lasting $\Delta t \sim \hbar / \Delta E \sim 10^{-15}\text{ s}$—*not* a real stationary energy level!) before instantaneously re-emitting a scattered photon $h\nu'$ and dropping into final state $|f\rangle$:
- **Stokes:** $|i\rangle = |v=0\rangle \to \text{virtual} \to |f\rangle = |v=1\rangle$ ($h\nu_S = h\nu_0 - \Delta E$). Starts from heavily populated ground state!
- **Anti-Stokes:** $|i\rangle = |v=1\rangle \to \text{virtual} \to |f\rangle = |v=0\rangle$ ($h\nu_{AS} = h\nu_0 + \Delta E$). Requires thermal pre-population of $|v=1\rangle$!

Combining dipole $\nu^4$ scaling with the Boltzmann population ratio $N_1 / N_0 = e^{-h\Delta\nu / k_B T}$ gives the exact **Anti-Stokes-to-Stokes Intensity Ratio**:
$$\boxed{\frac{I_{\text{AS}}}{I_{\text{S}}} = \left(\frac{\nu_0 + \Delta\nu}{\nu_0 - \Delta\nu}\right)^4 \exp\left(-\frac{h\Delta\nu}{k_B T}\right) \approx \exp\left(-\frac{hc\Delta\bar{\nu}}{k_B T}\right)}$$
At $300\text{ K}$ for $\Delta\bar{\nu} = 1000\text{ cm}^{-1}$, $I_{\text{AS}}/I_{\text{S}} \approx e^{-4.80} \approx 8.2 \times 10^{-3}$ (Stokes is $> 120\times$ brighter!). Measuring $I_{\text{AS}}/I_{\text{S}}$ directly determines local gas/plasma temperature $T$ non-invasively!

#### 3. Polarization of Raman Lines Under Unpolarized Excitation (Depolarization Ratio $\rho$)
Even when exciting light is completely **unpolarized**, Raman lines viewed at $90^\circ$ exhibit partial **plane polarization**! Decompose the symmetric rank-2 polarizability derivative tensor $\boldsymbol{\alpha}'$ into a spherically symmetric **isotropic mean part** $\bar{\alpha}'$ and a traceless **anisotropic part** $\gamma'$:
- For **totally symmetric breathing vibrations** (e.g., $\text{CCl}_4$ $459\text{ cm}^{-1}$, $\gamma' \approx 0$), the induced dipole oscillates strictly parallel to the incident electric vector $\mathbf{E}$ (which lies in the plane perpendicular to the incident beam), radiating strongly **plane-polarized** light perpendicular to the scattering plane!
- For **non-totally symmetric vibrations** and **pure rotational Raman lines** ($\bar{\alpha}' = 0$), random molecular orientations depolarize the scattered field up to the theoretical maximum **depolarization ratio** $\rho = I_\perp / I_\parallel$:
  $$\rho_{\text{unpol}} = \frac{6(\gamma')^2}{45(\bar{\alpha}')^2 + 7(\gamma')^2} \le \frac{6}{7}, \qquad \rho_{\text{lin-pol laser}} = \frac{3(\gamma')^2}{45(\bar{\alpha}')^2 + 4(\gamma')^2} \le \frac{3}{4}.$$
  Any line with $\rho < \frac{6}{7}$ (or $< \frac{3}{4}$ with a polarized laser) is unambiguously diagnosed as a **totally symmetric vibrational mode**!

### 4.2 Pure Rotational Raman ($4B$ Spacing, $6B$ First Line!) & Nuclear Spin Alternation

1. **Gross & Specific Selection Rules ($\Delta J = 0, \pm 2$):**
   For a rotating molecule to modulate $\alpha$ as viewed from the lab frame, it must exhibit **anisotropic polarizability** ($\alpha_\parallel \neq \alpha_\perp$). **Every linear and diatomic molecule**—including homonuclear $\text{H}_2, \text{N}_2, \text{O}_2$ and linear $\text{CO}_2$—satisfies $\alpha_\parallel > \alpha_\perp$ and is **rotationally Raman-active**! *(Only spherical tops like $\text{CH}_4, \text{SF}_6$ are rotationally Raman-silent.)*
   Because rotating a linear molecule by **$180^\circ$ ($\pi$ radians)** restores its identical polarizability ellipsoid, $\alpha$ oscillates at **twice** the rotational frequency ($2\nu_{\text{rot}}$). Quantum-mechanically, this two-photon process obeys the strict parity-conserving selection rule:
   $$\Delta J = 0\ (\text{Rayleigh}), \quad \pm 2\ (\text{Raman}).$$

2. **Exact Rotational Raman Line Positions (CRITICAL: $6B, 10B, 14B, 18B, \dots$):**
   For Stokes ($J \to J + 2$, $J = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) and Anti-Stokes ($J + 2 \to J$, $J = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) transitions:
   $$|\Delta\bar{\nu}_{\text{rot}}| = F(J+2) - F(J) = B(J+2)(J+3) - B J(J+1) = B\left[(J^2 + 5J + 6) - (J^2 + J)\right],$$
   $$\boxed{\bar{\nu}_{\text{Raman}} = \bar{\nu}_0 \mp B(4J + 6), \qquad J = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots}$$
   - **First Stokes / Anti-Stokes line ($J = 0$):** located at **$\pm 6B$** from the Rayleigh line!
   - **Successive lines ($J = 1, 2, 3, \dots$):** located at **$\pm 10B,\ \pm 14B,\ \pm 18B, \dots$**
   - **Uniform adjacent line spacing:** **$\Delta\bar{\nu} = 4B$** (double the IR/microwave $2B$ spacing!).
   - **Gap between first Stokes and first Anti-Stokes lines:** **$12B$**!
   - *Vibrational–Rotational Raman ($\Delta v = +1$, $\Delta J = 0, \pm 2$):* Produces three branches—an **O-branch** ($\Delta J = -2$, $\bar{\nu}_0 - B(4J-2)$ for $J \ge 2$), an intense central **Q-branch** ($\Delta J = 0$, $\bar{\nu} \approx \bar{\nu}_0$, always active in Raman even for $\Sigma$ states!), and an **S-branch** ($\Delta J = +2$, $\bar{\nu}_0 + B(4J+6)$ for $J \ge 0$).

3. **Nuclear Spin Statistics - Ortho / Para Intensity Alternation in Homonuclear Diatomics:**
   Exchanging the two identical nuclei of spin $I$ in a homonuclear molecule must leave the total wavefunction $\Psi_{\text{tot}} = \psi_{\text{el}}\psi_{\text{vib}}\psi_{\text{rot}}\psi_{\text{ns}}$ **antisymmetric** for **fermionic nuclei** (half-integer $I$) and **symmetric** for **bosonic nuclei** (integer $I$).
   - Under nuclear exchange, $\psi_{\text{vib}}$ is always symmetric, while $\psi_{\text{rot}}$ carries parity $(-1)^J$ (**even $J$: symmetric**; **odd $J$: antisymmetric**).
   - Out of $(2I+1)^2$ nuclear spin states, **$(I+1)(2I+1)$ are symmetric (ortho)** and **$I(2I+1)$ are antisymmetric (para)**, yielding the statistical weight ratio:
     $$\frac{g_{\text{sym}}}{g_{\text{antisym}}} = \frac{I + 1}{I}.$$
   - **Landmark Spectroscopic Signatures:**
     - **$\text{H}_2$ ($^1\text{H}$: $I = \frac{1}{2}$ fermions; $^1\Sigma_g^+$ symmetric):** Total $\Psi$ must be **antisymmetric**! Thus symmetric **ortho-$\text{H}_2$** ($g = 3$) pairs exclusively with **ODD $J = 1, 3, 5, \dots$**, while antisymmetric **para-$\text{H}_2$** ($g = 1$) pairs with **EVEN $J = 0, 2, 4, \dots$**. Rotational Raman lines alternate cleanly in intensity: **$\text{Odd } J : \text{Even } J = 3 : 1$** ($10B, 18B, \dots$ are $3\times$ stronger than $6B, 14B, \dots$)!
     - **$^{14}\text{N}_2$ ($I = 1$ bosons; $^1\Sigma_g^+$ symmetric):** Total $\Psi$ must be **symmetric** $\implies$ symmetric spin ($g=6$) pairs with **EVEN $J$**, antisymmetric spin ($g=3$) pairs with **ODD $J$** $\implies$ **$\text{Even } J : \text{Odd } J = 2 : 1$**!
     - **$^{12}\text{C}^{16}\text{O}_2$ ($^{16}\text{O}$: $I = 0$ bosons; $^1\Sigma_g^+$ symmetric):** $g_{\text{antisym}} = 0 \implies$ **all ODD $J$ states vanish identically!** Lines appear only at $6B, 14B, 22B, \dots$ with doubled spacing **$8B$**!
     - **$^{16}\text{O}_2$ ($I = 0$ bosons; $^3\Sigma_g^-$ ANTISYMMETRIC electronic ground state!):** To restore symmetric total $\Psi$, **all EVEN $J$ states vanish identically!** Lines appear only at $10B, 18B, 26B, \dots$ with spacing **$8B$**!

### 4.3 Rule of Mutual Exclusion & IR vs. Raman Structural Diagnostics Matrix

**Rule of Mutual Exclusion:** *If a molecule possesses a **center of inversion symmetry ($i$)**, no normal mode of vibration can be simultaneously IR-active and Raman-active—its IR and Raman vibrational bands are strictly **mutually exclusive (complementary)**!*

*Symmetry proof:* Under spatial inversion ($\mathbf{r} \to -\mathbf{r}$), the dipole moment vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ reverses sign (**ungerade / odd, $u$**), whereas every component of the polarizability tensor $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ transforms like quadratic coordinate products ($x^2, xy$) and stays invariant (**gerade / even, $g$**). In a centrosymmetric molecule, every normal mode is purely $g$ (Raman-active, IR-silent) or purely $u$ (IR-active, Raman-silent)—never both!

### 4.4 Laser Raman Spectroscopy & Modern Enhanced Techniques

For three decades after Raman's discovery, Raman spectroscopy remained a slow, heroic laboratory craft. Because spontaneous Raman cross-sections are minuscule ($\sim 10^{-6}\text{–}10^{-8}$ of incident intensity), mercury-arc lamps ($4358\text{ Å}$) demanded huge sample volumes, multi-hour photographic exposures, and suffered severe contamination from broad lamp linewidths and fluorescence. The invention of the **laser** revolutionized Raman spectroscopy into one of modern physics' sharpest analytical tools:

1. **Six Decisive Advantages of Laser Excitation (CW $\text{Ar}^+\ 514.5\text{ nm}$, He–Ne $632.8\text{ nm}$, Nd:YAG $532\text{ nm}/1064\text{ nm}$, Diode $785\text{ nm}$):**
   - **Immense Spectral Brightness & Speed:** Concentrating watts of optical power into a single line boosts Raman photon flux by orders of magnitude, shrinking integration times from hours to **milliseconds** and allowing micro-liter / nanogram sampling.
   - **Ultra-Narrow Monochromaticity ($\Delta\bar{\nu} < 10^{-3}\text{ cm}^{-1}$):** Eliminates overlapping lamp satellite lines and, paired with holographic **notch/edge filters** that block the $10^6\times$ brighter Rayleigh line, resolves pure rotational and low-frequency lattice-phonon modes within $< 5\text{ cm}^{-1}$ of $\bar{\nu}_0$.
   - **Diffraction-Limited Spatial Collimation:** Enables **Confocal Laser Raman Microscopy** ($\sim 0.5\ \mu\text{m}$ spatial mapping of graphene layers, semiconductor stress, and live biological cells) and fiber-optic remote sensing.
   - **Intrinsic Linear Polarization:** Permits instant, high-precision **depolarization ratio ($\rho = I_\perp / I_\parallel$)** measurements to identify totally symmetric modes.
   - **Wavelength Tunability & Fluorescence Rejection:** Shifting excitation into the near-infrared ($785\text{ nm}$ or $1064\text{ nm}$ FT-Raman) stays below electronic absorption thresholds, cleanly eliminating overwhelming fluorescence backgrounds.
   - **Unlocks Resonant, Plasmonic & Nonlinear Enhancements:** High peak laser intensities power three transformative modern variants:

2. **Modern High-Sensitivity Laser Raman Modalities:**
   - **Resonance Raman Spectroscopy (RRS):** Tuning the laser frequency $\nu_0$ to coincide with an allowed electronic absorption band transforms the virtual state into a resonant excited electronic state, amplifying Raman intensity of vibrational modes coupled to that chromophore by **$10^3\text{–}10^6\times$** (selectively probing active heme sites inside giant hemoglobin proteins at micromolar concentration!).
   - **Surface-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS):** Adsorbing target molecules onto roughened or nanostructured $\text{Au}/\text{Ag}$ metallic surfaces excites **Localized Surface Plasmon Resonances (LSPR)**. Electromagnetic local-field amplification ($I_{\text{SERS}} \propto |E_{\text{loc}}/E_0|^4$) plus metal–molecule charge transfer boosts Raman signals by **$10^6\text{–}10^{10}\times$**, reaching **single-molecule trace detection** (explosives, narcotics, pathogens, food contaminants)!
   - **Stimulated Raman Scattering (SRS) & Coherent Anti-Stokes Raman Scattering (CARS):** Intense pulsed pump ($\nu_p$) and Stokes ($\nu_s$) lasers tuned so $\nu_p - \nu_s = \nu_m$ coherently drive macroscopic molecular oscillations via third-order susceptibility $\chi^{(3)}$, generating an intense, directional, fluorescence-free coherent anti-Stokes beam at $\nu_{\text{CARS}} = 2\nu_p - \nu_s$ used in jet-engine combustion thermometry and label-free real-time biomedical imaging!

Consequently, lines originating from **odd $J$** ($607.8, 1094.1\text{ cm}^{-1}$) exhibit a **$3 : 1$ intensity enhancement** over adjacent **even $J$** lines ($364.7, 850.9\text{ cm}^{-1}$) (modulated by the thermal Boltzmann factor)!

> **Part (a) - Vibrational Raman of $\text{HCl}$:**
> 1. *Unit conversion (CGS $\to$ SI trap!):*
>    $$1\text{ dyne cm}^{-1} = \frac{10^{-5}\text{ N}}{10^{-2}\text{ m}} = 10^{-3}\text{ N m}^{-1} \implies k = 4.80 \times 10^5 \times 10^{-3} = 480\text{ N m}^{-1}.$$
> 2. *Exciting wavenumber & vibrational Raman shift:*
>    $$\bar{\nu}_0 = \frac{1}{4358 \times 10^{-8}\text{ cm}} = 22{,}946.3\text{ cm}^{-1}.$$
>    $$\Delta\bar{\nu}_{\text{vib}} = \frac{1}{2\pi c}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}} = \frac{1}{2\pi (2.9979 \times 10^{10}\text{ cm s}^{-1})}\sqrt{\frac{480}{1.61 \times 10^{-27}}} = \frac{5.4602 \times 10^{14}}{1.8837 \times 10^{11}} = 2898.7\text{ cm}^{-1}.$$
> 3. *Stokes & Anti-Stokes positions:*
>    - **Stokes:** $\bar{\nu}_S = 22{,}946.3 - 2898.7 = \boxed{20{,}047.6\text{ cm}^{-1}} \implies \lambda_S = \boxed{4988.1\text{ Å}}$.
>    - **Anti-Stokes:** $\bar{\nu}_{AS} = 22{,}946.3 + 2898.7 = \boxed{25{,}845.0\text{ cm}^{-1}} \implies \lambda_{AS} = \boxed{3869.2\text{ Å}}$.
> 4. *Intensity ratio at $300\text{ K}$ ($k_B T / hc = 208.5\text{ cm}^{-1}$):*
>    $$\frac{I_{\text{AS}}}{I_{\text{S}}} \approx \exp\left(-\frac{2898.7}{208.5}\right) = e^{-13.90} = \boxed{9.2 \times 10^{-7}}\quad (\text{Stokes is }\sim 10^6\times\text{ brighter!}).$$
>
> **Part (b) - Linear Triatomic Rotational Raman ($4.86, 8.14, 11.36\text{ cm}^{-1}$):**
> Check consecutive spacings: $8.14 - 4.86 = 3.28\text{ cm}^{-1}$ and $11.36 - 8.14 = 3.22\text{ cm}^{-1}$ (average spacing $= 3.25\text{ cm}^{-1}$). Check the ratio of the first line to the spacing: $4.86 / 3.25 = 1.495 \approx \mathbf{1.5} = \frac{6B}{4B}$! Thus the three shifts correspond strictly to $6B, 10B, 14B$:
> $$B_1 = \frac{4.86}{6} = 0.810\text{ cm}^{-1}, \qquad B_2 = \frac{8.14}{10} = 0.814\text{ cm}^{-1}, \qquad B_3 = \frac{11.36}{14} = 0.811\text{ cm}^{-1}.$$
> Mean rotational constant: $\boxed{B = 0.812\text{ cm}^{-1} = 81.2\text{ m}^{-1}}$.
> Moment of inertia:
> $$I = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 B c} = \frac{2.7993 \times 10^{-46}}{0.812} = \boxed{3.45 \times 10^{-46}\text{ kg m}^2}.$$

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **The Classic Raman $6B$ vs. $4B$ Trap (Biggest Single Marks-Killer!):** Pure rotational Raman lines sit at **$6B, 10B, 14B, 18B, \dots$** (first shift $= \mathbf{6B}$, spacing $= \mathbf{4B}$, central Stokes–Anti-Stokes gap $= \mathbf{12B}$). Candidates routinely write $4B, 8B, 12B$ or even microwave's $2B, 4B, 6B$ (causing a devastating factor-of-3 error on $\text{H}_2$ in UPSC 2026 and on linear triatomics in UPSC 2013!). Always re-derive $B(J+2)(J+3) - BJ(J+1) = B(4J+6)$ in two lines on the answer sheet!
> 2. **$B$ in $\text{m}^{-1}$ vs. $\text{cm}^{-1}$ vs. $\text{J}$:** The formula $B = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 I c}$ with $c = 3.00 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$ gives $B$ in **$\text{m}^{-1}$**; dividing by $100$ gives $\text{cm}^{-1}$! Never plug $B$ in $\text{cm}^{-1}$ directly into $I = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 B c}$ with $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$—that causes an instant **$100\times$ error in $I$** and a **$10\times$ error in bond length $r_0$**!
> 3. **CGS Force-Constant Trap ($\text{dyne cm}^{-1} \to \text{N m}^{-1}$):** UPSC loves giving $k$ in $\text{dyne cm}^{-1}$ (e.g., $4.8 \times 10^5\text{ dyne cm}^{-1}$ in 2025). Remember **$1\text{ dyne cm}^{-1} = 10^{-3}\text{ N m}^{-1}$** (NOT $10^{-5}$!), so $4.8 \times 10^5\text{ dyne cm}^{-1} = 480\text{ N m}^{-1}$.
> 4. **Missing Lines in $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ ($I = 0$ Nuclei):** For $\text{CO}_2$ ($^1\Sigma_g^+$) only *even* $J$ survive ($6B, 14B, 22B$, spacing $8B$); for $^{16}\text{O}_2$ ($^3\Sigma_g^-$ antisymmetric electronic state!) only *odd* $J$ survive ($10B, 18B, 26B$, spacing $8B$). Never assume every homonuclear molecule has spacing $4B$!
> 5. **Anharmonic $D_e$ vs. $D_0$ Confusion:** $D_e = \frac{\bar{\omega}_e}{4x_e}$ is measured from the *bottom* of the Morse well, whereas the experimentally measured spectroscopic bond dissociation energy is **$D_0 = D_e - G(0) = D_e - \left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{\omega}_e - \frac{1}{4}\bar{\omega}_e x_e\right)$**!
> 6. **Missing $P(0)$ Line in IR Vib–Rot Spectra:** In the P-branch ($\Delta J = -1$), the lowest possible initial level is **$J'' = 1 \to J' = 0$** ($P(1)$ at $\bar{\nu}_0 - 2B$). There is NO $P(0)$ line!

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Rotational Raman $6B$ First Line & $4B$ Spacing (2013, 2017, 2026):** This is UPSC's single most lethal molecular-spectroscopy filter. Dividing the first Raman line $4.86\text{ cm}^{-1}$ by $4$ (or $2$) instead of **$6$**, or listing $\text{H}_2$ lines at $2B, 4B, 6B, 8B$ instead of **$6B, 10B, 14B, 18B$**, wipes out numerical credit immediately.
- **Exact SI vs. $\text{cm}^{-1}$ Discipline (2013, 2025, 2026):** Examiners deduct heavily when candidates substitute $B$ in $\text{cm}^{-1}$ alongside $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$ (a $100\times$ error in $I$), or misconvert $\text{dyne cm}^{-1} \to \text{N m}^{-1}$ (forgetting $1\text{ dyne cm}^{-1} = 10^{-3}\text{ N m}^{-1}$). Always show the explicit unit-cancellation line before writing the final number.
- **Complementarity Proof for $\text{CO}_2$ (2013, 2019):** Listing the three modes is only half the score; full marks require stating the **Rule of Mutual Exclusion** explicitly and proving it via inversion parity (**$\boldsymbol{\mu}$ is ungerade ($u$)** while **$\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ is gerade ($g$)**) accompanied by neat normal-mode arrow sketches.
- **Thermal $J_{\max}$ & Nuclear Spin Precision (2025, 2026):** For $J_{\max}$, explicitly differentiate $(2J+1)e^{-hcBJ(J+1)/k_BT}$ rather than quoting the formula blindly, and note the high-$T$ $\sqrt{2}$ scaling factor clearly. For $\text{H}_2$ nuclear spin, explicitly link **fermionic $I = \frac{1}{2}$ $\implies$ total antisymmetry $\implies$ ortho (odd $J$, weight 3) : para (even $J$, weight 1) $= 3 : 1$**!
