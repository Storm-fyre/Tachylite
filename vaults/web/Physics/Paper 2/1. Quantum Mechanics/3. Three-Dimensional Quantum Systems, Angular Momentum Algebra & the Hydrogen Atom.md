# Chapter 3: Three-Dimensional Quantum Systems, Angular Momentum Algebra & the Hydrogen Atom

## 1. Particle in a 3D Box, Density of States and Free Electron Theory of Metals

Stepping from one dimension into three introduces two defining features of real physical matter: **energy degeneracy** and **statistical state counting**. Whereas every one-dimensional bound state is strictly non-degenerate, a particle trapped inside a symmetric three-dimensional enclosure can partition its kinetic energy across $(x, y, z)$ in multiple distinct standing-wave modes sharing the identical total energy.

Scaling that three-dimensional box up to a macroscopic metallic crystal transforms a single-particle quantum exercise into Arnold Sommerfeld's **Free Electron Theory of Metals**—explaining the enormous Fermi velocity of conduction electrons, electron degeneracy pressure, and the century-old riddle of why metals exhibit a tiny, linearly temperature-dependent electronic specific heat.

### 1.1 Separation of Variables in a 3D Box and Degeneracy

#### Physical Setup and Separation of the 3D TISE

Consider a particle of mass $m$ confined inside a three-dimensional rectangular rigid box of sides $L_x, L_y, L_z$ (volume $V = L_x L_y L_z$):

$$V(x, y, z) = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 < x < L_x,\; 0 < y < L_y,\; 0 < z < L_z \\ \infty, & \text{elsewhere} \end{cases}$$

Inside the box, the 3D Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation (TISE) is:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial z^2}\right) = E\psi(x, y, z)$$

subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions $\psi = 0$ on all six walls. Because the Hamiltonian is a sum of three independent single-coordinate operators ($\hat{H} = \hat{H}_x + \hat{H}_y + \hat{H}_z$), seek a product solution $\psi(x, y, z) = X(x)Y(y)Z(z)$. Dividing through by $XYZ$ separates the PDE cleanly:

$$\underbrace{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{X''}{X}}_{=\,E_x} + \underbrace{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{Y''}{Y}}_{=\,E_y} + \underbrace{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{Z''}{Z}}_{=\,E_z} = E$$

Since $x, y, z$ vary independently, each term equals a separate 1D infinite-box constant with $E = E_x + E_y + E_z$. Solving each 1D equation yields the complete orthonormal eigenfunctions and quantized energy levels:

$$\boxed{\psi_{n_x, n_y, n_z}(x, y, z) = \sqrt{\frac{8}{L_x L_y L_z}}\sin\!\left(\frac{n_x\pi x}{L_x}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n_y\pi y}{L_y}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n_z\pi z}{L_z}\right)}$$

$$\boxed{E_{n_x, n_y, n_z} = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{n_x^2}{L_x^2} + \frac{n_y^2}{L_y^2} + \frac{n_z^2}{L_z^2}\right) \qquad (n_x, n_y, n_z = 1, 2, 3, \dots)}$$

#### Symmetry-Induced vs. Accidental Degeneracy in a Cubical Box

For an **isotropic cubical box** ($L_x = L_y = L_z = L$, $V = L^3$), the energy simplifies to:

$$E_{n_x, n_y, n_z} = E_0\left(n_x^2 + n_y^2 + n_z^2\right) \equiv E_0 N^2, \qquad E_0 \equiv \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$$

When multiple distinct triplets $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$ produce the same energy, the level is **degenerate**, and its **degeneracy $g$** is the number of linearly independent eigenstates sharing that energy. Two fundamentally distinct mechanisms create degeneracy:

1. **Symmetry-Induced (Essential) Degeneracy:** Permuting the indices of $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$ yields distinct wave functions with the same $N^2$ because the cubical Hamiltonian is invariant under rotations and reflections exchanging the $x, y, z$ axes (e.g., $(2,1,1), (1,2,1), (1,1,2)$ give $g = 3$; $(3,2,1)$ permutations give $g = 3! = 6$). Breaking the cube into an asymmetric rectangular box ($L_x \neq L_y \neq L_z$) immediately lifts this degeneracy.
2. **Accidental Degeneracy:** Distinct triplets that are *not* permutations of one another nevertheless yield the identical sum of squares—arising from number-theoretic coincidences rather than obvious geometric symmetries. The lowest accidental degeneracy in a 3D cubical box occurs at **$N^2 = 27$**, where $(5,1,1)$ ($3$ permutations) and $(3,3,3)$ ($1$ state) collide to produce total degeneracy $g = 3 + 1 = 4$. Subsequent examples occur at $N^2 = 38$ ($(6,1,1)$ and $(5,3,2)$, $g = 3 + 6 = 9$) and $N^2 = 41$ ($(6,2,1)$ and $(4,4,3)$, $g = 6 + 3 = 9$).

### 1.2 Exact $k$-Space Derivation of the 3D Density of States $g(E)\,dE$

For a macroscopic metallic cube ($L \sim 1\text{ cm}$), the level spacing $E_0 \sim 10^{-15}\text{ eV}$ is unimaginably tiny, turning the discrete triplets into a quasi-continuum. Rather than tracking individual $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$, we count how many allowed quantum states occupy an infinitesimal energy slice $[E, E + dE]$—defining the **Density of States** $g(E)$.

#### Step-by-Step $k$-Space State Counting

Each standing-wave eigenstate corresponds to a wave vector $\mathbf{k} = (k_x, k_y, k_z)$ with positive quantized components:

$$k_x = \frac{n_x\pi}{L}, \qquad k_y = \frac{n_y\pi}{L}, \qquad k_z = \frac{n_z\pi}{L} \qquad (n_x, n_y, n_z = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$

Adjacent allowed points in $\mathbf{k}$-space form a cubic lattice of spacing $\Delta k = \pi/L$, so each spatial orbital occupies an elementary $k$-space cell volume:

$$v_k = \left(\frac{\pi}{L}\right)^3 = \frac{\pi^3}{V}$$

All states having wave-vector magnitude $|\mathbf{k}| \le k$ lie inside the **positive octant** ($k_x, k_y, k_z > 0$) of a sphere of radius $k$, whose $k$-space volume is $\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi k^3\right) = \frac{\pi k^3}{6}$. Dividing this octant volume by $v_k$ and multiplying by the **spin degeneracy factor** $g_s = 2$ (for spin-$1/2$ electrons with $m_s = \pm 1/2$) gives the total number of quantum states $N(k)$ with $|\mathbf{k}| \le k$:

$$N(k) = g_s \cdot \frac{\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi k^3\right)}{\pi^3 / V} = 2 \cdot \frac{V k^3}{6\pi^2} = \frac{V k^3}{3\pi^2}$$

*(Equivalence Check — Born–von Kármán Periodic Boundary Conditions: Requiring $\psi(x + L, y, z) = \psi(x, y, z)$ yields $k_i = \frac{2\pi m_i}{L}$ with $m_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ spanning the full sphere (not just one octant), and cell volume $\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^3 = \frac{8\pi^3}{V}$. Thus $N(k) = 2 \cdot \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi k^3}{8\pi^3/V} = \frac{V k^3}{3\pi^2}$—identical!)*

Differentiating with respect to $k$ gives the number of states in the spherical shell $[k, k + dk]$:

$$dN = g(k)\,dk = \frac{V}{\pi^2}k^2\,dk$$

For non-relativistic free electrons, the dispersion relation is $E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}$, giving:

$$k = \left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/2}E^{1/2}, \qquad dk = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/2}E^{-1/2}\,dE$$

Substituting $k^2$ and $dk$ into $g(E)\,dE = g(k)\,dk$:

$$g(E)\,dE = \frac{V}{\pi^2}\left(\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}\right)\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/2}E^{-1/2}\,dE\right]$$

$$\boxed{g(E)\,dE = \frac{V}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}E^{1/2}\,dE = \frac{4\pi V(2m)^{3/2}}{h^3}E^{1/2}\,dE}$$

- **Dimensional Check:** $\left[\frac{V}{\hbar^3}m^{3/2}E^{1/2}dE\right] = \frac{\text{m}^3}{(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-1})^3}\,\text{kg}^{3/2}\left(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-2}\right)^{3/2} = \frac{\text{kg}^3\text{m}^6\text{s}^{-3}}{\text{kg}^3\text{m}^6\text{s}^{-3}} = 1$ (pure count of states).

#### Dimensionality Scaling (3D, 2D, 1D, 0D) and Extreme Relativistic Limit

1. **Lower-Dimensional Nanostructures ($g_s = 2$, $E = \hbar^2 k^2/2m$):**
   - **3D Bulk ($V$):** $N(k) = \frac{Vk^3}{3\pi^2} \implies g_{\text{3D}}(E) = \frac{V}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}E^{1/2} \propto E^{1/2}$.
   - **2D Quantum Well (area $A$):** $N(k) = 2\frac{\pi k^2}{(2\pi)^2/A} = \frac{Ak^2}{2\pi} \implies g_{\text{2D}}(E) = \frac{mA}{\pi\hbar^2} \propto E^0$ (energy-independent step function!).
   - **1D Quantum Wire (length $L$):** $N(k) = 2\frac{2k}{2\pi/L} = \frac{2Lk}{\pi} \implies g_{\text{1D}}(E) = \frac{L}{\pi\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2m}{E}} \propto E^{-1/2}$.
   - **0D Quantum Dot:** Quantized along all three axes into discrete atomic-like levels $E_i$, yielding Dirac delta spikes $g_{\text{0D}}(E) = 2\sum_i \delta(E - E_i)$.
2. **Extreme Relativistic Particle ($E \gg \mu c^2 \implies E \approx pc = \hbar ck$):**
   Substituting $k = E/(\hbar c)$ and $dk = dE/(\hbar c)$ into $g(k)\,dk$ gives:
   - For spinless particles ($g_s = 1$): $g(E) = \frac{V}{2\pi^2}k^2\frac{dk}{dE} = \frac{V}{2\pi^2\hbar^3 c^3}E^2 = \frac{4\pi V}{h^3 c^3}E^2$.
   - For spin-$1/2$ fermions ($g_s = 2$): $g(E) = \frac{V}{\pi^2\hbar^3 c^3}E^2$.

### 1.3 Sommerfeld Free Electron Theory of Metals

In classical Drude–Lorentz theory, $N$ free conduction electrons treated as a Maxwell–Boltzmann gas should obey equipartition and contribute $C_{V,e} = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B = \frac{3}{2}R$ to a metal's molar heat capacity. Experimentally, metals at room temperature obey Dulong–Petit ($C_V \approx 3R$ from lattice ions alone), with the electronic share roughly $100\times$ smaller than classical predictions and scaling linearly with $T$.

Sommerfeld resolved this paradox by recognizing that conduction electrons are **fermions** governed by the Pauli exclusion principle and the **Fermi–Dirac distribution**:

$$f(E, T) = \frac{1}{\exp\!\left(\frac{E - \mu(T)}{k_B T}\right) + 1}$$

#### Fermi Energy $E_F$, Ground-State Energy $U_0$, and Degeneracy Pressure at $T = 0\text{ K}$

At $T = 0\text{ K}$, $f(E, 0)$ becomes a sharp step function: $f = 1$ for $E \le E_F$ and $f = 0$ for $E > E_F$. Electrons stack into the lowest available quantum states (two per spatial orbital) up to the **Fermi Energy** $E_F \equiv \mu(0)$. Equating the integrated number of occupied states to total electron count $N$:

$$N = \int_0^{E_F} g(E)\,dE = \frac{V}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\int_0^{E_F} E^{1/2}\,dE = \frac{V}{3\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}E_F^{3/2}$$

Inverting in terms of the conduction electron number density $n \equiv N/V$ yields the **Fermi wave vector $k_F$**, **Fermi energy $E_F$**, **Fermi speed $v_F$**, and **Fermi temperature $T_F$**:

$$\boxed{E_F = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(3\pi^2 n\right)^{2/3}}, \qquad \boxed{k_F = (3\pi^2 n)^{1/3}}, \qquad \boxed{v_F = \frac{\hbar k_F}{m}}, \qquad \boxed{T_F = \frac{E_F}{k_B}}$$

Notice $E_F$ is an **intensive** property depending solely on $n$, not on sample size $V$. For ordinary metals ($n \sim 10^{28}\text{–}10^{29}\text{ m}^{-3}$), $E_F \approx 3\text{–}12\text{ eV}$ and $T_F \approx 3.5 \times 10^4\text{–}1.4 \times 10^5\text{ K} \gg 300\text{ K}$, meaning room-temperature conduction electrons form a **deeply degenerate quantum gas**.

The total ground-state kinetic energy $U_0$ and average energy per electron $\bar{E}_0$ at $T = 0\text{ K}$ are:

$$U_0 = \int_0^{E_F} E\,g(E)\,dE = \frac{V}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\int_0^{E_F} E^{3/2}\,dE = \frac{V}{5\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}E_F^{5/2} = \frac{3}{5}N E_F$$

$$\boxed{\bar{E}_0 = \frac{U_0}{N} = \frac{3}{5}E_F}, \qquad \boxed{P_0 = -\left(\frac{\partial U_0}{\partial V}\right)_N = \frac{2}{3}\frac{U_0}{V} = \frac{2}{5}n E_F}$$

*(Pressure derivation check: Since $E_F \propto V^{-2/3}$, $U_0 = \frac{3}{5}N E_F \propto V^{-2/3}$, hence $-\partial U_0/\partial V = +\frac{2}{3}U_0/V = \frac{2}{5}n E_F \sim 10^{10}\text{ Pa}$—this quantum **electron degeneracy pressure** accounts for the low compressibility of metals and supports white dwarf stars against gravitational collapse!)*

#### Linear Electronic Specific Heat ($T \ll T_F$)

At finite temperature $T \ll T_F$, thermal photons/phonons supply energy packets of order $k_B T \ll E_F$. Electrons buried deep inside the Fermi sea ($E \lesssim E_F - k_B T$) cannot absorb $k_B T$ because all neighboring higher states are already filled (**Pauli blocking**). Only a thin thermal crust of width $\sim k_B T$ near $E_F$—representing a tiny fraction $\sim T/T_F$ of the $N$ electrons—can be excited into empty states above $E_F$, each gaining energy $\sim k_B T$.

More rigorously, applying the **Sommerfeld expansion** to $N = \int_0^\infty g(E)f(E,T)\,dE$ and $U(T) = \int_0^\infty E\,g(E)f(E,T)\,dE$ using $\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{x^2 e^x}{(e^x + 1)^2}dx = \frac{\pi^2}{3}$ gives, to leading order in $(k_B T/E_F)^2$:

$$\mu(T) \approx E_F\left[1 - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\left(\frac{k_B T}{E_F}\right)^2\right], \qquad U(T) \approx U_0 + \frac{\pi^2}{6}g(E_F)(k_B T)^2$$

Note from $N = \frac{2}{3}E_F g(E_F)$ that $g(E_F) = \frac{3N}{2E_F}$. Differentiating $U(T)$ with respect to $T$ at constant volume gives Sommerfeld's celebrated **linear electronic specific heat**:

$$\boxed{C_{V,e} = \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V = \frac{\pi^2}{3}g(E_F)k_B^2 T = \frac{\pi^2}{2}N k_B\left(\frac{T}{T_F}\right) \equiv \gamma T}$$

- **Limiting-Case Checks:** As $T \to 0$, $C_{V,e} \to 0$ (satisfying the Third Law of Thermodynamics). At $T = 300\text{ K}$, $C_{V,e}/(\frac{3}{2}Nk_B) = \frac{\pi^2}{3}(T/T_F) \approx 0.01\text{–}0.03$, explaining why electrons barely affect room-temperature heat capacity. At cryogenic temperatures ($T \ll \Theta_D$), total metallic heat capacity is $C_V = \gamma T + \beta T^3$ (linear electronic term dominates over cubic Debye phonon term as $T \to 0$!).

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Fermi Energy, Fermi Speed, $T_F$ and Electronic Specific Heat of Sodium
> **Problem:** Using the free electron theory of metals, calculate for sodium (1 free electron per atom, density $\rho = 0.97\text{ g/cm}^3$, atomic mass $M = 22.99\text{ g/mol}$): (a) the free-electron number density $n$, (b) the Fermi energy $E_F$ (in $\text{eV}$) at $T = 0\text{ K}$, (c) the Fermi temperature $T_F$ and Fermi speed $v_F$, and (d) the molar electronic specific heat $C_{V,e}$ at $T = 300\text{ K}$ compared to the classical equipartition value $\frac{3}{2}R$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Conduction electron number density $n$:**
> Converting to SI units ($\rho = 970\text{ kg/m}^3$, $M = 22.99 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg/mol}$, $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$):
> $$n = \frac{\rho N_A}{M} = \frac{(970\text{ kg/m}^3)(6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1})}{22.99 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg/mol}} = \boxed{2.541 \times 10^{28}\text{ m}^{-3}}$$
>
> **(b) Fermi wave vector $k_F$ and Fermi energy $E_F$:**
> $$k_F = (3\pi^2 n)^{1/3} = \left[3\pi^2(2.541 \times 10^{28})\right]^{1/3} = \left(7.524 \times 10^{29}\right)^{1/3} = 9.095 \times 10^{9}\text{ m}^{-1}$$
> $$E_F = \frac{\hbar^2 k_F^2}{2m_e} = \frac{(1.0546 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s})^2(9.095 \times 10^{9}\text{ m}^{-1})^2}{2(9.109 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg})} = \frac{9.199 \times 10^{-49}}{1.8218 \times 10^{-30}} = 5.049 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$$
> Converting to electron-volts ($1\text{ eV} = 1.6022 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$):
> $$E_F = \frac{5.049 \times 10^{-19}}{1.6022 \times 10^{-19}} = \boxed{3.15\text{ eV}}$$
>
> **(c) Fermi temperature $T_F$ and Fermi speed $v_F$:**
> $$T_F = \frac{E_F}{k_B} = \frac{5.049 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}}{1.3806 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J/K}} = \boxed{3.66 \times 10^{4}\text{ K}}$$
> $$v_F = \frac{\hbar k_F}{m_e} = \frac{(1.0546 \times 10^{-34})(9.095 \times 10^{9})}{9.109 \times 10^{-31}} = \boxed{1.05 \times 10^{6}\text{ m/s}}$$
>
> **(d) Molar electronic specific heat at $T = 300\text{ K}$ ($R = 8.314\text{ J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$):**
> $$C_{V,e} = \frac{\pi^2}{2}R\left(\frac{T}{T_F}\right) = \frac{\pi^2}{2}(8.314)\left(\frac{300}{3.657 \times 10^{4}}\right) = \boxed{0.337\text{ J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}}$$
> Compared to the classical value $\frac{3}{2}R = 12.47\text{ J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$, Pauli blocking suppresses $C_{V,e}$ by a factor of $37$!

## 2. Quantum Theory of Angular Momentum

In classical mechanics, all three Cartesian components of orbital angular momentum $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$ can be specified simultaneously, fixing the rotational axis sharply in space. In quantum mechanics, promoting $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{p}$ to non-commuting operators shatters that classical picture: **no two components of $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$ commute with each other**.

This single non-commutativity dictates the entire rotational architecture of atoms, molecules, and nuclei—quantizing both the magnitude and the spatial orientation of angular momentum, producing discrete cones of allowed alignment (**space quantization**), and unexpectedly revealing the algebraic door through which half-integer intrinsic spin enters physics.

### 2.1 Fundamental Commutators $[\hat{L}_i, \hat{L}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_k$ and $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$

#### Orbital Angular Momentum Operators

Promoting $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$ via $\hat{\mathbf{p}} = -i\hbar\nabla$ defines the Cartesian orbital angular momentum operators:

$$\hat{L}_x = \hat{y}\hat{p}_z - \hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \qquad \hat{L}_y = \hat{z}\hat{p}_x - \hat{x}\hat{p}_z, \qquad \hat{L}_z = \hat{x}\hat{p}_y - \hat{y}\hat{p}_x$$

or compactly using the Levi-Civita tensor $\epsilon_{ijk}$: $\hat{L}_i = \epsilon_{ijk}\hat{r}_j\hat{p}_k$. Because distinct coordinates and momenta commute ($[\hat{y}, \hat{p}_z] = 0$), every component is strictly **Hermitian**: $\hat{L}_x^\dagger = \hat{p}_z^\dagger\hat{y}^\dagger - \hat{p}_y^\dagger\hat{z}^\dagger = \hat{y}\hat{p}_z - \hat{z}\hat{p}_y = \hat{L}_x$.

#### Unabridged Proof of $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$

Expand the commutator directly using canonical relations $[\hat{r}_j, \hat{p}_k] = i\hbar\delta_{jk}$, $[\hat{r}_j, \hat{r}_k] = 0$, and $[\hat{p}_j, \hat{p}_k] = 0$:

$$[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = [\hat{y}\hat{p}_z - \hat{z}\hat{p}_y,\; \hat{z}\hat{p}_x - \hat{x}\hat{p}_z]$$

$$= [\hat{y}\hat{p}_z,\, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x] - [\hat{y}\hat{p}_z,\, \hat{x}\hat{p}_z] - [\hat{z}\hat{p}_y,\, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x] + [\hat{z}\hat{p}_y,\, \hat{x}\hat{p}_z]$$

Evaluate each of the four brackets term by term:
1. $[\hat{y}\hat{p}_z, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x] = \hat{y}[\hat{p}_z, \hat{z}]\hat{p}_x = \hat{y}(-i\hbar)\hat{p}_x = -i\hbar\hat{y}\hat{p}_x$
2. $[\hat{y}\hat{p}_z, \hat{x}\hat{p}_z] = 0$ (all operators commute)
3. $[\hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x] = 0$ (all operators commute)
4. $[\hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \hat{x}\hat{p}_z] = \hat{x}[\hat{z}, \hat{p}_z]\hat{p}_y = \hat{x}(+i\hbar)\hat{p}_y = +i\hbar\hat{x}\hat{p}_y$

Summing all four contributions yields:

$$[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar(\hat{x}\hat{p}_y - \hat{y}\hat{p}_x) = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$$

By cyclic permutation of $(x, y, z)$, we obtain the **fundamental Lie algebra of angular momentum**:

$$\boxed{[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z, \qquad [\hat{L}_y, \hat{L}_z] = i\hbar\hat{L}_x, \qquad [\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_x] = i\hbar\hat{L}_y}$$

or compactly in index and vector cross-product notation:

$$\boxed{[\hat{L}_i, \hat{L}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_k} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \boxed{\hat{\mathbf{L}} \times \hat{\mathbf{L}} = i\hbar\hat{\mathbf{L}}}$$

*(Verify the $z$-component of $\hat{\mathbf{L}} \times \hat{\mathbf{L}}$: $(\hat{\mathbf{L}} \times \hat{\mathbf{L}})_z = \hat{L}_x\hat{L}_y - \hat{L}_y\hat{L}_x = [\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$—non-vanishing precisely because vector operator components do not commute!)*

#### Unabridged Proof of $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$

Define the squared total angular momentum operator $\hat{L}^2 \equiv \hat{L}_x^2 + \hat{L}_y^2 + \hat{L}_z^2$:

$$[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = [\hat{L}_x^2, \hat{L}_z] + [\hat{L}_y^2, \hat{L}_z] + \underbrace{[\hat{L}_z^2, \hat{L}_z]}_{=\,0}$$

Apply the Leibniz product identity $[\hat{A}\hat{B}, \hat{C}] = \hat{A}[\hat{B}, \hat{C}] + [\hat{A}, \hat{C}]\hat{B}$:

$$[\hat{L}_x^2, \hat{L}_z] = \hat{L}_x[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_z] + [\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_z]\hat{L}_x = \hat{L}_x(-i\hbar\hat{L}_y) + (-i\hbar\hat{L}_y)\hat{L}_x = -i\hbar(\hat{L}_x\hat{L}_y + \hat{L}_y\hat{L}_x)$$

$$[\hat{L}_y^2, \hat{L}_z] = \hat{L}_y[\hat{L}_y, \hat{L}_z] + [\hat{L}_y, \hat{L}_z]\hat{L}_y = \hat{L}_y(+i\hbar\hat{L}_x) + (+i\hbar\hat{L}_x)\hat{L}_y = +i\hbar(\hat{L}_y\hat{L}_x + \hat{L}_x\hat{L}_y)$$

Adding both lines produces exact cancellation:

$$\boxed{[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0, \qquad [\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_x] = 0, \qquad [\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_y] = 0}$$

**Physical Consequence:** Because $\hat{L}^2$ commutes with $\hat{L}_z$, the squared magnitude and *one* component (conventionally $\hat{L}_z$) share a complete set of simultaneous eigenstates and can be measured sharply together. However, because $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z \neq 0$, once $\hat{L}_z$ is sharply fixed (for $m \neq 0$), $\hat{L}_x$ and $\hat{L}_y$ cannot simultaneously be sharp, strictly obeying $\Delta L_x\,\Delta L_y \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}|\langle\hat{L}_z\rangle|$.

### 2.2 Algebraic Ladder Operators $\hat{J}_\pm$ (and $\hat{L}_\pm$) - Eigenvalues and Half-Integers

Define **generalized angular momentum** $\hat{\mathbf{J}}$ as *any* trio of Hermitian operators obeying $[\hat{J}_i, \hat{J}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{J}_k$ (covering orbital $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$, intrinsic spin $\hat{\mathbf{S}}$, and total $\hat{\mathbf{J}} = \hat{\mathbf{L}} + \hat{\mathbf{S}}$ simultaneously!).

#### Step 1: Ladder Operators and Core Algebraic Identities

Define the non-Hermitian, mutually adjoint **raising ($\hat{J}_+$)** and **lowering ($\hat{J}_-$)** operators:

$$\boxed{\hat{J}_+ \equiv \hat{J}_x + i\hat{J}_y, \qquad \hat{J}_- \equiv \hat{J}_x - i\hat{J}_y = (\hat{J}_+)^\dagger}$$

Compute their three foundational identities directly from $[\hat{J}_i, \hat{J}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{J}_k$:

1. **Commutator with $\hat{J}_z$:**
   $$[\hat{J}_z, \hat{J}_\pm] = [\hat{J}_z, \hat{J}_x] \pm i[\hat{J}_z, \hat{J}_y] = i\hbar\hat{J}_y \pm i(-i\hbar\hat{J}_x) = \pm\hbar(\hat{J}_x \pm i\hat{J}_y) = \boxed{\pm\hbar\hat{J}_\pm}$$
2. **Commutator with $\hat{J}^2$ and mutual commutator:**
   $$[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_\pm] = [\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_x] \pm i[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_y] = 0$$
   $$[\hat{J}_+, \hat{J}_-] = [\hat{J}_x + i\hat{J}_y,\, \hat{J}_x - i\hat{J}_y] = -i[\hat{J}_x, \hat{J}_y] + i[\hat{J}_y, \hat{J}_x] = -2i(i\hbar\hat{J}_z) = 2\hbar\hat{J}_z$$
3. **Products $\hat{J}_-\hat{J}_+$ and $\hat{J}_+\hat{J}_-$ in terms of $\hat{J}^2$ and $\hat{J}_z$:**
   $$\hat{J}_-\hat{J}_+ = (\hat{J}_x - i\hat{J}_y)(\hat{J}_x + i\hat{J}_y) = \hat{J}_x^2 + \hat{J}_y^2 + i[\hat{J}_x, \hat{J}_y] = \hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 - \hbar\hat{J}_z \tag{1}$$
   $$\hat{J}_+\hat{J}_- = (\hat{J}_x + i\hat{J}_y)(\hat{J}_x - i\hat{J}_y) = \hat{J}_x^2 + \hat{J}_y^2 - i[\hat{J}_x, \hat{J}_y] = \hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 + \hbar\hat{J}_z \tag{2}$$

#### Step 2: Eigenvalue Derivation and Proof of Half-Integer Quantization

Let $|\lambda, m\rangle$ be a normalized simultaneous eigenket of the commuting Hermitian operators $\hat{J}^2$ and $\hat{J}_z$:

$$\hat{J}^2|\lambda, m\rangle = \lambda\hbar^2|\lambda, m\rangle, \qquad \hat{J}_z|\lambda, m\rangle = m\hbar|\lambda, m\rangle \qquad (\lambda, m \in \mathbb{R})$$

1. **Boundedness of $m$:** Because $\hat{J}_x$ and $\hat{J}_y$ are Hermitian, $\hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 = \hat{J}_x^2 + \hat{J}_y^2$ has strictly non-negative expectation value:
   $$\langle\lambda, m|(\hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2)|\lambda, m\rangle = (\lambda - m^2)\hbar^2 = \|\hat{J}_x|\lambda, m\rangle\|^2 + \|\hat{J}_y|\lambda, m\rangle\|^2 \ge 0 \implies -\sqrt{\lambda} \le m \le +\sqrt{\lambda}$$
   Thus, for any fixed $\lambda$, the ladder of $m$-values **must terminate** at a finite maximum rung $m_{\max} \equiv j$ and a finite minimum rung $m_{\min} \equiv j'$.

2. **Ladder Action:** Applying $\hat{J}^2$ and $\hat{J}_z$ to $(\hat{J}_\pm|\lambda, m\rangle)$:
   $$\hat{J}^2(\hat{J}_\pm|\lambda, m\rangle) = \hat{J}_\pm\hat{J}^2|\lambda, m\rangle = \lambda\hbar^2(\hat{J}_\pm|\lambda, m\rangle)$$
   $$\hat{J}_z(\hat{J}_\pm|\lambda, m\rangle) = (\hat{J}_\pm\hat{J}_z \pm \hbar\hat{J}_\pm)|\lambda, m\rangle = (m \pm 1)\hbar(\hat{J}_\pm|\lambda, m\rangle)$$
   Thus $\hat{J}_\pm$ leaves $\lambda$ unchanged while raising/lowering $m$ in exact steps of $\pm 1$.

3. **Top and Bottom Termination Conditions:**
   - At the highest rung ($m = j$), $\hat{J}_+|\lambda, j\rangle = 0$. Acting with $\hat{J}_-$ and substituting Equation $(1)$:
     $$0 = \hat{J}_-\hat{J}_+|\lambda, j\rangle = (\hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 - \hbar\hat{J}_z)|\lambda, j\rangle = \left[\lambda - j^2 - j\right]\hbar^2|\lambda, j\rangle \implies \lambda = j(j + 1)$$
   - At the lowest rung ($m = j'$), $\hat{J}_-|\lambda, j'\rangle = 0$. Acting with $\hat{J}_+$ and substituting Equation $(2)$:
     $$0 = \hat{J}_+\hat{J}_-|\lambda, j'\rangle = (\hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 + \hbar\hat{J}_z)|\lambda, j'\rangle = \left[\lambda - j'^2 + j'\right]\hbar^2|\lambda, j'\rangle \implies \lambda = j'(j' - 1)$$
   - Equating both expressions for $\lambda$:
     $$j(j + 1) = j'(j' - 1) \implies (j + j')(j - j' + 1) = 0$$
     Because $j \ge j'$, the factor $(j - j' + 1) \ge 1$ cannot vanish, forcing strictly **$j' = -j$**.

4. **Why General $j$ Admits Half-Integers:** Climbing from the lowest rung $m_{\min} = -j$ to the highest rung $m_{\max} = +j$ via $\hat{J}_+$ takes a non-negative integer number $N \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ of unit steps:
   $$j - (-j) = N \implies 2j = N \implies \boxed{j = 0, \frac{1}{2}, 1, \frac{3}{2}, 2, \dots}$$
   with exactly $2j + 1$ allowed magnetic quantum numbers $m = -j, -j + 1, \dots, j - 1, j$, and universal eigenvalues:
   $$\boxed{\hat{J}^2|j, m\rangle = j(j + 1)\hbar^2|j, m\rangle, \qquad \hat{J}_z|j, m\rangle = m\hbar|j, m\rangle}$$

5. **Exact Ladder Matrix Elements:** Using Equations $(1)$ and $(2)$ to compute the squared norm $\|\hat{J}_\pm|j, m\rangle\|^2 = \langle j, m|\hat{J}_\mp\hat{J}_\pm|j, m\rangle$:
   $$\|\hat{J}_\pm|j, m\rangle\|^2 = \langle j, m|(\hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 \mp \hbar\hat{J}_z)|j, m\rangle = \left[j(j + 1) - m(m \pm 1)\right]\hbar^2$$
   Taking the standard Condon–Shortley positive square root:
   $$\boxed{\hat{J}_\pm|j, m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{j(j + 1) - m(m \pm 1)}\,|j, m \pm 1\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{(j \mp m)(j \pm m + 1)}\,|j, m \pm 1\rangle}$$
   *(Sanity check: Setting $m = +j$ gives $\hat{J}_+|j, j\rangle = 0$; setting $m = -j$ gives $\hat{J}_-|j, -j\rangle = 0$.)*

### 2.3 Spherical Harmonics $Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$, Parity $(-1)^l$ and Space Quantization

Converting $\hat{\mathbf{L}} = -i\hbar(\mathbf{r} \times \nabla)$ into spherical polar coordinates $(r, \theta, \phi)$ ($x = r\sin\theta\cos\phi$, $y = r\sin\theta\sin\phi$, $z = r\cos\theta$) yields differential operators that act purely on angles $(\theta, \phi)$, independent of $r$:

$$\boxed{\hat{L}_z = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}}, \qquad \hat{L}_\pm = \hbar e^{\pm i\phi}\left(\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta} + i\cot\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right)$$

$$\boxed{\hat{L}^2 = -\hbar^2\left[\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right) + \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2}\right]}$$

#### Why Orbital Angular Momentum $l$ Must Be an Integer Only

Solving the $\hat{L}_z$ eigenproblem $\hat{L}_z\Phi_m(\phi) = -i\hbar\frac{d\Phi_m}{d\phi} = m\hbar\Phi_m$ gives $\Phi_m(\phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{im\phi}$. Because $\phi$ and $\phi + 2\pi$ represent the exact same physical point in 3D space, single-valuedness of the coordinate wave function requires:

$$\Phi_m(\phi + 2\pi) = \Phi_m(\phi) \implies e^{i2\pi m} = 1 \implies m \in \{0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots\}$$

Since $m$ must be an integer, $l = m_{\max}$ is strictly restricted to non-negative integers ($l = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) for orbital angular momentum. Half-integer values ($s = \frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{2}, \dots$) are excluded from coordinate-space orbital motion and survive exclusively for **intrinsic spin**, which possesses no $(\theta, \phi)$ wave function!

#### Spherical Harmonics $Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)$ and Exact Parity $(-1)^l$

The normalized simultaneous eigenfunctions of $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$ on the unit sphere are the **Spherical Harmonics** $Y_l^m(\theta, \phi) = \langle\theta,\phi|l, m\rangle$:

$$\hat{L}^2 Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = l(l + 1)\hbar^2 Y_l^m(\theta,\phi), \qquad \hat{L}_z Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = m\hbar Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$$

Separating $Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = \Theta_l^m(\theta)\Phi_m(\phi)$ yields the Associated Legendre Equation in $u = \cos\theta$, solved by the Associated Legendre Polynomials $P_l^m(\cos\theta) = (1 - u^2)^{m/2}\frac{d^m}{du^m}P_l(u)$:

$$\boxed{Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = (-1)^m\sqrt{\frac{2l + 1}{4\pi}\frac{(l - m)!}{(l + m)!}}\,P_l^m(\cos\theta)\,e^{im\phi} \quad (m \ge 0)}, \qquad Y_l^{-m}(\theta,\phi) = (-1)^m\left[Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\right]^*$$

They satisfy completeness and orthonormality on the unit sphere: $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi (Y_{l'}^{m'})^* Y_l^m\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi = \delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}$. Explicit low-$l$ forms essential for exam calculations:

| $l$ | $m = 0$ | $m = \pm 1$ | $m = \pm 2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$0$ ($s$)** | $Y_0^0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}$ | — | — |
| **$1$ ($p$)** | $Y_1^0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4\pi}}\cos\theta$ | $Y_1^{\pm 1} = \mp\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{8\pi}}\sin\theta\,e^{\pm i\phi}$ | — |
| **$2$ ($d$)** | $Y_2^0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{16\pi}}(3\cos^2\theta - 1)$ | $Y_2^{\pm 1} = \mp\sqrt{\dfrac{15}{8\pi}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\,e^{\pm i\phi}$ | $Y_2^{\pm 2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{15}{32\pi}}\sin^2\theta\,e^{\pm 2i\phi}$ |

**Proof of Parity $\hat{\Pi}Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = (-1)^l Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$:** Under spatial inversion $\mathbf{r} \to -\mathbf{r}$, spherical coordinates transform as $(r, \theta, \phi) \to (r, \pi - \theta, \phi + \pi)$. Then $e^{im(\phi + \pi)} = (-1)^m e^{im\phi}$ and $P_l^m(\cos(\pi - \theta)) = P_l^m(-\cos\theta) = (-1)^{l - m}P_l^m(\cos\theta)$ (since $P_l(-u) = (-1)^l P_l(u)$ and each derivative flips parity once, while $(1 - u^2)^{m/2}$ is even). Multiplying both factors cancels $m$:

$$\boxed{\hat{\Pi}Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = (-1)^{l - m}(-1)^m Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = (-1)^l Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)}$$

Thus $s, d, g$ orbitals ($l$ even) have **even parity ($+1$)** and $p, f, h$ orbitals ($l$ odd) have **odd parity ($-1$)**—directly explaining Laporte's electric-dipole selection rule $\Delta l = \pm 1$!

#### Vector Cone Model of Space Quantization

In eigenstate $|l, m\rangle$, the angular momentum vector has exact length $|\mathbf{L}| = \sqrt{l(l + 1)}\,\hbar$ and quantized $z$-projection $L_z = m\hbar$, restricting it to $2l + 1$ discrete cones making polar half-angles:

$$\boxed{\cos\theta_m = \frac{L_z}{|\mathbf{L}|} = \frac{m}{\sqrt{l(l + 1)}} \qquad (m = -l, \dots, +l)}$$

Notice that for every $l > 0$, even the maximally aligned state $m = +l$ satisfies $\cos\theta_{\min} = \frac{l}{\sqrt{l(l + 1)}} < 1$ (e.g., for $l = 1$, $\theta_{\min} = \arccos(1/\sqrt{2}) = 45^\circ$; for $l = 2$, $\theta_{\min} = \arccos(2/\sqrt{6}) = 35.3^\circ$). The angular momentum vector can **never align strictly along the $z$-axis**, because complete alignment would simultaneously sharpen $L_x = 0$, $L_y = 0$, and $L_z = |\mathbf{L}| > 0$, violating $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$! Instead, $\langle L_x\rangle = \langle L_y\rangle = 0$ while the irreducible transverse zero-point fluctuations obey $\langle L_x^2\rangle = \langle L_y^2\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left[l(l + 1) - m^2\right]\hbar^2 > 0$.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Spherical Harmonic Expansion, Ladder Operators and Uncertainty Saturation
> **Problem:** The angular wave function of a particle moving in a central potential is:
> $$\psi(\theta, \phi) = A\left(2\cos\theta - \sin\theta\,e^{i\phi} + \sin\theta\,e^{-i\phi}\right)$$
> (a) Express $\psi(\theta, \phi)$ in terms of normalized spherical harmonics $Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)$ and find the real positive normalization constant $A$.
> (b) Determine all possible measurement outcomes and probabilities for $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$, and calculate $\langle\hat{L}_z\rangle$ and $\langle\hat{L}_z^2\rangle$.
> (c) Using ladder operators $\hat{L}_\pm$, show that $|\psi\rangle$ is an exact eigenstate of $\hat{L}_x$, compute $\langle\hat{L}_x\rangle$, $\langle\hat{L}_x^2\rangle$, and $\langle\hat{L}_y^2\rangle$, and check $\Delta L_y\,\Delta L_z$ against the Robertson uncertainty bound.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Expansion in $Y_l^m$ and normalization:**
> From the $l = 1$ spherical harmonic table:
> $$\cos\theta = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}Y_1^0, \qquad \sin\theta\,e^{i\phi} = -\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}}Y_1^1, \qquad \sin\theta\,e^{-i\phi} = +\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}}Y_1^{-1}$$
> Substituting into $\psi(\theta,\phi)$:
> $$\psi(\theta,\phi) = A\left[2\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}Y_1^0 + \sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}}Y_1^1 + \sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}}Y_1^{-1}\right] = A\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}}\left(Y_1^1 + \sqrt{2}\,Y_1^0 + Y_1^{-1}\right)$$
> By orthonormality of $Y_l^m$, normalization requires:
> $$1 = A^2\left(\frac{8\pi}{3}\right)\left(1^2 + (\sqrt{2})^2 + 1^2\right) = A^2\left(\frac{32\pi}{3}\right) \implies \boxed{A = \sqrt{\frac{3}{32\pi}}}$$
> In Dirac ket notation, the normalized state is:
> $$|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{2}|1, 1\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1, 0\rangle + \frac{1}{2}|1, -1\rangle$$
>
> **(b) Outcomes of $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$:**
> - Only $l = 1$ states appear, so measuring $\hat{L}^2$ yields **$2\hbar^2$ with certainty ($P = 1$)**.
> - Measuring $\hat{L}_z$ yields $+\hbar$ ($P = 1/4$), $0$ ($P = 1/2$), or $-\hbar$ ($P = 1/4$).
> - Expectation values:
>   $$\langle\hat{L}_z\rangle = \frac{1}{4}(+\hbar) + \frac{1}{2}(0) + \frac{1}{4}(-\hbar) = \boxed{0}, \qquad \langle\hat{L}_z^2\rangle = \frac{1}{4}\hbar^2 + 0 + \frac{1}{4}\hbar^2 = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}\hbar^2}$$
>
> **(c) Action of $\hat{L}_x = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{L}_+ + \hat{L}_-)$ and uncertainty saturation:**
> For $l = 1$, $\hat{L}_\pm|1, m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{2 - m(m \pm 1)}\,|1, m \pm 1\rangle$ gives $\hat{L}_-|1, 1\rangle = \sqrt{2}\hbar|1, 0\rangle$, $\hat{L}_+|1, -1\rangle = \sqrt{2}\hbar|1, 0\rangle$, $\hat{L}_\pm|1, 0\rangle = \sqrt{2}\hbar|1, \pm 1\rangle$, and $\hat{L}_+|1, 1\rangle = \hat{L}_-|1, -1\rangle = 0$. Applying $\hat{L}_x$ to $|\psi\rangle$:
> $$\hat{L}_x|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}\hbar|1, 0\rangle) + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sqrt{2}\hbar|1, 1\rangle + \sqrt{2}\hbar|1, -1\rangle) + \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}\hbar|1, 0\rangle)\right]$$
> $$= \hbar\left[\frac{1}{2}|1, 1\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1, 0\rangle + \frac{1}{2}|1, -1\rangle\right] = +\hbar|\psi\rangle$$
> Thus $|\psi\rangle$ is an **exact eigenstate of $\hat{L}_x$ with eigenvalue $+\hbar$**! Immediately $\langle\hat{L}_x\rangle = \hbar$, $\langle\hat{L}_x^2\rangle = \hbar^2$ ($\Delta L_x = 0$), and from $\hat{L}^2 = \hat{L}_x^2 + \hat{L}_y^2 + \hat{L}_z^2$:
> $$\langle\hat{L}_y^2\rangle = \langle\hat{L}^2\rangle - \langle\hat{L}_x^2\rangle - \langle\hat{L}_z^2\rangle = 2\hbar^2 - \hbar^2 - \frac{1}{2}\hbar^2 = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}\hbar^2}$$
> Since $\langle\hat{L}_y\rangle = \langle\hat{L}_z\rangle = 0$, we obtain $\Delta L_y = \Delta L_z = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $\Delta L_y\,\Delta L_z = \frac{\hbar^2}{2} = \frac{\hbar}{2}|\langle\hat{L}_x\rangle|$—**exact saturation** of the Robertson angular momentum uncertainty relation!

## 3. Exact Solution of the Hydrogen Atom

The Hydrogen atom—a single electron bound to a proton by the inverse-square SI Coulomb attraction—is the ultimate validation of non-relativistic quantum mechanics. Solving its three-dimensional Schrödinger equation from first principles predicts the Balmer–Lyman spectral series, the exact Bohr radius $a_0$, the $n^2$-fold shell degeneracy underpinning the periodic table, and the directed spatial geometries of $s, p, d$ chemical orbitals without a single ad hoc postulate.

### 3.1 Central-Field Separation and the Centrifugal Barrier

#### Physical Setup and Reduced Mass

Consider a hydrogenic atom (nuclear charge $+Ze$, mass $M$; electron charge $-e$, mass $m_e$, with $Z = 1$ for Hydrogen) interacting via the SI Coulomb potential:

$$V(r) = -\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$$

Transforming to center-of-mass coordinate $\mathbf{R}$ and relative separation $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_e - \mathbf{r}_N$ eliminates free center-of-mass translation and reduces the two-body problem exactly to a single particle of **reduced mass** $\mu$ moving in the fixed central potential $V(r)$:

$$\mu \equiv \frac{m_e M}{m_e + M} \qquad (\text{for } {}^1\text{H: } \mu = 0.999456\,m_e)$$

#### Spherical Laplacian and Radial Schrödinger Equation

Writing the 3D Laplacian in spherical coordinates cleanly exposes the orbital angular momentum operator $\hat{L}^2$:

$$\nabla^2 = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\left[\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right) + \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2}\right] = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right) - \frac{\hat{L}^2}{\hbar^2 r^2}$$

Inserting into the 3D TISE yields the central-field Schrödinger equation:

$$\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{\hat{L}^2}{2\mu r^2} + V(r)\right]\psi(r,\theta,\phi) = E\psi(r,\theta,\phi)$$

Because $V(r)$ depends solely on $r$, $\hat{H}$ commutes with $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$ ($[\hat{H}, \hat{L}^2] = [\hat{H}, \hat{L}_z] = 0$). Seeking a simultaneous eigenstate $\psi_{nlm}(r,\theta,\phi) = R_{nl}(r)Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$ replaces $\hat{L}^2 \to l(l + 1)\hbar^2$ and divides out $Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$, isolating the **Radial Equation**:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \left[-\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} + \frac{l(l + 1)\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}\right]R(r) = E R(r)$$

Define the **reduced radial wave function** $u(r) \equiv rR(r)$. Using $\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{d(u/r)}{dr}\right) = \frac{u''(r)}{r}$ and multiplying through by $r$ converts the 3D radial problem into an exact 1D-like TISE on $r \in [0, \infty)$:

$$\boxed{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2 u}{dr^2} + \underbrace{\left[-\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} + \frac{l(l + 1)\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}\right]}_{V_{\text{eff}}(r)}u(r) = E u(r)}$$

with boundary condition $u(0) = 0$ (required so $R = u/r$ stays non-singular enough for $\int_0^\infty |R|^2 r^2 dr = \int_0^\infty |u|^2 dr = 1$) and $u(\infty) = 0$. The positive repulsive term $+\frac{l(l + 1)\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}$ is the **centrifugal barrier**—it grows as $1/r^2$ near the origin, overwhelming the $-1/r$ Coulomb pull for all $l \ge 1$ and forcing $p, d, f$ wave functions to vanish at the nucleus!

---

### 3.2 Asymptotic Behavior, Associated Laguerre Polynomials, $E_n$ and $n^2$ Degeneracy

#### Step 1: Dimensionless Transformation

For bound states ($E < 0$), define positive constants $\kappa$, dimensionless radius $\rho$, and dimensionless coupling $\lambda$:

$$\kappa \equiv \frac{\sqrt{-2\mu E}}{\hbar} > 0, \qquad \rho \equiv 2\kappa r, \qquad \lambda \equiv \frac{\mu Z e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar^2 \kappa}$$

Rewriting the reduced radial equation as $u''(r) + \left[\frac{2\mu Z e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2 r} - \frac{l(l + 1)}{r^2} - \kappa^2\right]u(r) = 0$, transforming $\frac{d^2}{dr^2} = 4\kappa^2\frac{d^2}{d\rho^2}$, and dividing through by $4\kappa^2$ yields:

$$\boxed{\frac{d^2 u}{d\rho^2} + \left[\frac{\lambda}{\rho} - \frac{l(l + 1)}{\rho^2} - \frac{1}{4}\right]u(\rho) = 0}$$

#### Step 2: Dual Asymptotic Analysis ($\rho \to \infty$ and $\rho \to 0$)

1. **Large radius ($\rho \to \infty$):** Both $1/\rho$ and $1/\rho^2$ vanish, leaving $u'' \approx \frac{1}{4}u \implies u \sim e^{\pm\rho/2}$. Normalizability forces **$u(\rho) \sim e^{-\rho/2}$**.
2. **Small radius ($\rho \to 0$):** The centrifugal term dominates: $u'' \approx \frac{l(l + 1)}{\rho^2}u$. Substituting $u \sim \rho^s$ gives the indicial equation $s(s - 1) = l(l + 1) \implies s = l + 1$ or $s = -l$. Because $u(0) = 0$ forbids $\rho^{-l}$, physical regularity selects **$u(\rho) \sim \rho^{l + 1}$** (which means **$R(r) = u/r \sim r^l$** near the nucleus—proving only $l = 0$ ($s$-states) have non-zero probability density at $r = 0$!).

#### Step 3: Associated Laguerre Equation and Power-Series Termination

Factor out both exact asymptotic envelopes by writing $u(\rho) = \rho^{l + 1}e^{-\rho/2}v(\rho)$. Differentiating twice:

$$u' = \rho^{l + 1}e^{-\rho/2}\left[v' + \left(\frac{l + 1}{\rho} - \frac{1}{2}\right)v\right]$$

$$u'' = \rho^{l + 1}e^{-\rho/2}\left[v'' + 2\left(\frac{l + 1}{\rho} - \frac{1}{2}\right)v' + \left(\frac{l(l + 1)}{\rho^2} - \frac{l + 1}{\rho} + \frac{1}{4}\right)v\right]$$

Substitute into the dimensionless radial equation and cancel $\rho^{l+1}e^{-\rho/2}$—both $\frac{l(l+1)}{\rho^2}$ and $-\frac{1}{4}$ cancel identically! Multiplying by $\rho$ gives the **Associated Laguerre Differential Equation**:

$$\boxed{\rho v''(\rho) + \left[2(l + 1) - \rho\right]v'(\rho) + \left[\lambda - (l + 1)\right]v(\rho) = 0}$$

Expand $v(\rho) = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} c_j \rho^j$ ($c_0 \neq 0$) and substitute term by term:

$$\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[j(j + 1)c_{j+1} + 2(l + 1)(j + 1)c_{j+1} - j c_j + (\lambda - l - 1)c_j\right]\rho^j = 0$$

Equating every power $\rho^j$ to zero yields the two-term **recurrence relation**:

$$\boxed{c_{j+1} = \frac{j + l + 1 - \lambda}{(j + 1)(j + 2l + 2)}\,c_j \qquad (j = 0, 1, 2, \dots)}$$

**Normalizability Divergence Proof:** If the series never terminates, then as $j \to \infty$, $\frac{c_{j+1}}{c_j} \to \frac{1}{j}$, identical to the Taylor series of $e^{+\rho} = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \frac{\rho^j}{j!}$. Consequently $v(\rho) \sim e^{+\rho}$, forcing $u(\rho) = \rho^{l+1}e^{-\rho/2}v(\rho) \sim \rho^{l+1}e^{+\rho/2} \to \infty$ (unnormalizable!).

To preserve square-integrability, the series **must terminate** at some non-negative **radial quantum number** $n_r \equiv j_{\max} \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ where the numerator vanishes:

$$n_r + l + 1 - \lambda = 0 \implies \boxed{\lambda = n_r + l + 1 \equiv n \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)}$$

Because $n_r = n - l - 1 \ge 0$, the orbital quantum number is automatically constrained to:

$$\boxed{l = 0, 1, 2, \dots, n - 1}$$

#### Step 4: Quantized Energy Levels, Bohr Radius and $n^2$ Accidental Coulomb Degeneracy

Setting $\lambda = \frac{\mu Z e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2 \kappa} = n$ determines $\kappa_n$ and defines the **Bohr radius** $a_0$:

$$\kappa_n = \frac{\mu Z e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2 n} = \frac{Z}{n a_0}, \qquad \boxed{a_0 \equiv \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{\mu e^2} = 0.5292 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m} = 0.5292\text{ \AA}}$$

*(Note $\rho = 2\kappa_n r = \frac{2Zr}{na_0}$, confirming the exact exponential decay tail $e^{-\rho/2} = e^{-Zr/(na_0)}$.)* Substituting $\kappa_n$ into $E_n = -\frac{\hbar^2\kappa_n^2}{2\mu}$ produces the **Hydrogenic Energy Spectrum**:

$$\boxed{E_n = -\frac{\mu Z^2 e^4}{2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2 n^2} = -\frac{Z^2(13.60\text{ eV})}{n^2} \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)}$$

- **Dimensional & Limiting Checks:** $\left[\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 a_0}\right] = \text{J}$, and $E_n = -\frac{Z^2}{2n^2}\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 a_0}\right) = -\frac{Z^2(27.2\text{ eV})}{2n^2}$. As $n \to \infty$, $E_n \to 0^-$ (ionization continuum threshold); for $n = 1, Z = 1$, $E_1 = -13.60\text{ eV}$ matches the Lyman ionization limit exactly!
- **Origin of $n^2$ Degeneracy:** Notice $E_n$ depends *strictly* on $n$ alone—independent of both $m_l$ (expected from $SO(3)$ rotational invariance of any central potential) and $l$ (**accidental Coulomb degeneracy**, unique to the exact $1/r$ potential due to hidden $SO(4)$ symmetry generated by the conserved Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector!). Summing the $(2l + 1)$ magnetic substates over all $l = 0, \dots, n - 1$:
  $$\boxed{g_n = \sum_{l=0}^{n-1} (2l + 1) = n^2} \qquad (\text{or } 2n^2 \text{ including electron spin } m_s = \pm 1/2)$$

#### Normalized Hydrogen Radial Wave Functions ($1s, 2s, 2p$)

The terminating polynomial $v(\rho)$ is the **Associated Laguerre Polynomial** $L_{n - l - 1}^{2l + 1}(\rho)$, defined via Rodrigues' formula $L_q^p(\rho) = \frac{e^\rho \rho^{-p}}{q!}\frac{d^q}{d\rho^q}\left(e^{-\rho}\rho^{q + p}\right)$. Imposing $\int_0^\infty |R_{nl}(r)|^2 r^2\,dr = 1$ yields the normalized radial functions (with $\rho = \frac{2Zr}{na_0}$):

$$\boxed{R_{nl}(r) = \sqrt{\left(\frac{2Z}{na_0}\right)^3\frac{(n - l - 1)!}{2n\,(n + l)!}}\,e^{-\rho/2}\,\rho^l\,L_{n - l - 1}^{2l + 1}(\rho)}$$

Explicit ground and $n = 2$ Hydrogen ($Z = 1$) radial wave functions (verify every normalization!):

$$R_{10}(r) = \frac{2}{a_0^{3/2}}\,e^{-r/a_0}, \qquad \psi_{100}(r) = R_{10}Y_0^0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}}\,e^{-r/a_0}$$

$$R_{20}(r) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,a_0^{3/2}}\left(1 - \frac{r}{2a_0}\right)e^{-r/(2a_0)}, \qquad R_{21}(r) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}\,a_0^{3/2}}\left(\frac{r}{a_0}\right)e^{-r/(2a_0)}$$

### 3.3 Radial Probability Density $P_{nl}(r)$, Nodes and Shapes of $s, p, d$ Orbitals

#### Radial Probability Density $P_{nl}(r) = r^2|R_{nl}(r)|^2$

Integrating $|\psi_{nlm}(r,\theta,\phi)|^2\,d^3r = |R_{nl}(r)|^2 |Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)|^2 r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$ over all solid angles (using $\int |Y_l^m|^2\,d\Omega = 1$) gives the probability of finding the electron inside the spherical shell between radius $r$ and $r + dr$:

$$\boxed{P_{nl}(r)\,dr = r^2|R_{nl}(r)|^2\,dr = |u_{nl}(r)|^2\,dr}$$

**Critical Conceptual Contrast:** Although $|\psi_{100}(r)|^2 = \frac{1}{\pi a_0^3}e^{-2r/a_0}$ is *maximum at the nucleus* ($r = 0$), the spherical-shell volume $4\pi r^2 dr$ vanishes at $r = 0$, driving $P_{10}(0) = 0$. The competing $r^2$ growth and $e^{-2r/a_0}$ decay peak sharply at the **most probable radius** $r_{\text{mp}} = a_0$.

- **Circular Orbitals ($l = n - 1$, zero radial nodes: $1s, 2p, 3d$):** Since $P_{n,n-1}(r) \propto r^{2n}e^{-2Zr/(na_0)}$, setting $\frac{dP}{dr} = 0$ immediately yields:
  $$\boxed{r_{\text{mp}} = \frac{n^2 a_0}{Z}} \qquad (1s \to a_0,\; 2p \to 4a_0,\; 3d \to 9a_0)$$
  recovering the exact quantized radii of the Bohr model!
- **$2s$ Orbital ($n = 2, l = 0$):** $P_{20}(r) = \frac{r^2}{2a_0^3}\left(1 - \frac{r}{2a_0}\right)^2 e^{-r/a_0}$ has $1$ radial node at $r = 2a_0$, a small inner peak at $(3 - \sqrt{5})a_0 = 0.764\,a_0$, and a tall outer peak at $(3 + \sqrt{5})a_0 = 5.236\,a_0$.
- **Exact Nodal Surface Count:** Every eigenstate $\psi_{nlm}$ has **$n_r = n - l - 1$ radial spherical nodes** (excluding $r = 0, \infty$), **$l$ angular nodal surfaces** ($|m|$ azimuthal planes + $l - |m|$ polar cones), and **$n - 1$ total nodes**.

#### Shapes of $s, p, d$ Orbitals (Real Chemical Combinations)

- **$s$-orbitals ($l = 0$):** $Y_0^0 = (4\pi)^{-1/2}$ is strictly **spherically symmetric** with $0$ angular nodes and non-zero electron density at the nucleus ($r = 0$).
- **$p$-orbitals ($l = 1$, $1$ nodal plane through origin):** Forming real combinations of $Y_1^m$ aligns three orthogonal **dumbbell** clouds along the Cartesian axes:
  $$p_z = Y_1^0 \propto \frac{z}{r}, \qquad p_x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(Y_1^{-1} - Y_1^1\right) \propto \frac{x}{r}, \qquad p_y = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left(Y_1^{-1} + Y_1^1\right) \propto \frac{y}{r}$$
- **$d$-orbitals ($l = 2$, $2$ angular nodal surfaces):** Four orbitals—$d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$ (four-lobe **cloverleafs** bisecting coordinate planes) and $d_{x^2 - y^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(Y_2^2 + Y_2^{-2}) \propto \frac{x^2 - y^2}{r^2}$ (lobes along $\pm x, \pm y$)—possess two perpendicular nodal planes. The fifth, $d_{z^2} = Y_2^0 \propto (3\cos^2\theta - 1)$, forms a **$z$-axis dumbbell encircled by an equatorial torus (doughnut)** separated by two conical nodal surfaces at $\theta = \arccos(\pm 1/\sqrt{3}) = 54.7^\circ$ and $125.3^\circ$.

### 3.4 Radial Expectation Values via Virial, Hellmann–Feynman and Kramers' Relation

Rather than integrating high-order Laguerre polynomials by brute force, exact Hydrogen expectation values follow cleanly from three operator theorems:

#### 1. $\langle r^{-1}\rangle$ via the Quantum Virial Theorem

For any homogeneous potential $V(r) \propto r^k$, the Virial Theorem states $2\langle T\rangle = k\langle V\rangle$. For Coulomb ($k = -1$), $2\langle T\rangle = -\langle V\rangle$, so $E_n = \langle T\rangle + \langle V\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\langle V\rangle \implies \langle V\rangle = 2E_n$:

$$-\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left\langle\frac{1}{r}\right\rangle = 2\left(-\frac{Z^2 e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 n^2 a_0}\right) \implies \boxed{\left\langle\frac{1}{r}\right\rangle_{nl} = \frac{Z}{n^2 a_0}}$$

#### 2. $\langle r^{-2}\rangle$ via the Hellmann–Feynman Theorem

The **Hellmann–Feynman Theorem** states that if $\hat{H}(\alpha)|\psi(\alpha)\rangle = E(\alpha)|\psi(\alpha)\rangle$ depends smoothly on a parameter $\alpha$, then $\frac{\partial E}{\partial\alpha} = \left\langle\frac{\partial\hat{H}}{\partial\alpha}\right\rangle$. Treating $l$ as a continuous parameter in the radial Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_r(l) = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2}{dr^2} - \frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} + \frac{l(l + 1)\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2}$ while holding radial node number $n_r$ fixed (so $n = n_r + l + 1 \implies \frac{\partial n}{\partial l} = 1$):

$$\left\langle\frac{\partial\hat{H}_r}{\partial l}\right\rangle = \frac{(2l + 1)\hbar^2}{2\mu}\left\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\right\rangle = \frac{\partial E_n}{\partial n}\frac{\partial n}{\partial l} = \frac{\partial}{\partial n}\left(-\frac{\hbar^2 Z^2}{2\mu a_0^2 n^2}\right) = +\frac{\hbar^2 Z^2}{\mu a_0^2 n^3}$$

Dividing by $\frac{(2l + 1)\hbar^2}{2\mu}$ immediately yields:

$$\boxed{\left\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\right\rangle_{nl} = \frac{Z^2}{n^3\left(l + \frac{1}{2}\right)a_0^2}}$$

#### 3. Kramers' Relation and Exact $\langle r\rangle$, $\langle r^2\rangle$, $\langle r^{-3}\rangle$

Multiplying the reduced radial equation by $\left(r^{s+1}u' - \frac{s+1}{2}r^s u\right)$ and integrating by parts over $[0, \infty)$ yields **Kramers' recurrence relation** (valid for every integer $s \ge -2l - 1$):

$$\boxed{\frac{s + 1}{n^2}\langle r^s\rangle - (2s + 1)\frac{a_0}{Z}\langle r^{s-1}\rangle + \frac{s}{4}\left[(2l + 1)^2 - s^2\right]\left(\frac{a_0}{Z}\right)^2\langle r^{s-2}\rangle = 0}$$

Evaluating at $s = 0, 1, 2, -1$ extracts every canonical Hydrogen expectation value effortlessly:
- **$s = 0$:** $\frac{1}{n^2}(1) - \frac{a_0}{Z}\langle r^{-1}\rangle = 0 \implies \langle r^{-1}\rangle = \frac{Z}{n^2 a_0}$ (checks Virial!).
- **$s = 1$** (using $(2l + 1)^2 - 1 = 4l(l + 1)$ and $\langle r^{-1}\rangle = Z/(n^2 a_0)$):
  $$\frac{2}{n^2}\langle r\rangle - 3\frac{a_0}{Z} + \frac{l(l + 1)}{n^2}\frac{a_0}{Z} = 0 \implies \boxed{\langle r\rangle_{nl} = \frac{a_0}{2Z}\left[3n^2 - l(l + 1)\right]}$$
  *(Checks: $1s \to \frac{3}{2}a_0$; $2s \to 6a_0$; $2p \to 5a_0$—notice $\langle r\rangle_{2s} > \langle r\rangle_{2p}$ even though $2s$ penetrates closer to the nucleus!)*
- **$s = 2$** (substituting $\langle r\rangle$ and $(2l + 1)^2 - 4 = 4l(l + 1) - 3$):
  $$\boxed{\langle r^2\rangle_{nl} = \frac{n^2 a_0^2}{2Z^2}\left[5n^2 + 1 - 3l(l + 1)\right]}$$
- **$s = -1$** (essential for atomic spin-orbit fine structure, $l \ge 1$):
  $$0 + \frac{a_0}{Z}\langle r^{-2}\rangle - l(l + 1)\left(\frac{a_0}{Z}\right)^2\langle r^{-3}\rangle = 0 \implies \boxed{\left\langle\frac{1}{r^3}\right\rangle_{nl} = \frac{Z^3}{n^3 l\left(l + \frac{1}{2}\right)(l + 1)a_0^3}}$$

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Hydrogen $1s$ Probability Beyond $a_0$ and $2p$ Most Probable Distance & Density
> **Problem:** (a) The ground-state wave function of the Hydrogen atom is $\psi_{100}(r) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}}e^{-r/a_0}$. Compute the exact probability of finding the electron outside the Bohr radius ($r > a_0$).
> (b) For the Hydrogen $2p$ state, evaluate the most probable distance $r_{\text{mp}}$ of the electron from the nucleus and compute the exact value of the radial probability density $P_{21}(r_{\text{mp}})$ (both in terms of $a_0$ and in $\text{m}^{-1}$).
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Probability of finding the $1s$ electron beyond $r = a_0$:**
> Radial probability density:
> $$P_{10}(r) = 4\pi r^2 |\psi_{100}(r)|^2 = \frac{4r^2}{a_0^3}e^{-2r/a_0}$$
> Integrating from $r = a_0$ to $\infty$ with substitution $u = \frac{2r}{a_0}$ ($r = \frac{a_0 u}{2}$, $dr = \frac{a_0}{2}du$; limits $r = a_0 \to u = 2$):
> $$P(r > a_0) = \int_{a_0}^{\infty}\frac{4r^2}{a_0^3}e^{-2r/a_0}\,dr = \frac{1}{2}\int_2^{\infty} u^2 e^{-u}\,du$$
> Using $\int u^2 e^{-u}\,du = -e^{-u}(u^2 + 2u + 2)$:
> $$P(r > a_0) = \frac{1}{2}\left[-e^{-u}(u^2 + 2u + 2)\right]_2^{\infty} = \frac{1}{2}e^{-2}(4 + 4 + 2) = 5e^{-2} = \frac{5}{7.3891} = \boxed{0.677 \;\;(67.7\%)}$$
> *(Over two-thirds of the $1s$ electron cloud lies outside the Bohr radius—consistent with $\langle r\rangle_{10} = 1.5\,a_0 > a_0$!)*
>
> **(b) Most probable distance $r_{\text{mp}}$ and radial probability density $P_{21}(r_{\text{mp}})$ for $2p$:**
> The normalized $2p$ radial wave function is $R_{21}(r) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}\,a_0^{3/2}}\left(\frac{r}{a_0}\right)e^{-r/(2a_0)}$, giving radial probability density:
> $$P_{21}(r) = r^2|R_{21}(r)|^2 = \frac{r^4}{24 a_0^5}e^{-r/a_0}$$
> *(Normalization check: $\frac{1}{24 a_0^5}\int_0^\infty r^4 e^{-r/a_0}\,dr = \frac{4!}{24} = 1$.)*
> Differentiate with respect to $r$ and set to zero:
> $$\frac{dP_{21}}{dr} = \frac{1}{24 a_0^5}\left(4r^3 - \frac{r^4}{a_0}\right)e^{-r/a_0} = \frac{r^3}{24 a_0^5}\left(4 - \frac{r}{a_0}\right)e^{-r/a_0} = 0$$
> Excluding the zero-density boundaries $r = 0$ and $r \to \infty$, the unique interior maximum occurs at:
> $$\boxed{r_{\text{mp}} = 4a_0 = 4(0.5292\text{ \AA}) = 2.117\text{ \AA}}$$
> *(Check second derivative: $\left.\frac{d}{dr}(4 - r/a_0)\right|_{4a_0} = -1/a_0 < 0 \implies$ strict maximum!)*
> Evaluating $P_{21}(r)$ at $r = 4a_0$ (using $e^4 = 54.598$):
> $$P_{21}(4a_0) = \frac{(4a_0)^4}{24 a_0^5}e^{-4} = \frac{256}{24 e^4 a_0} = \frac{32}{3 e^4 a_0} = \boxed{\frac{0.1954}{a_0} = 3.69 \times 10^{9}\text{ m}^{-1}} \quad (= 0.369\text{ \AA}^{-1})$$

## 4. High-Yield Traps and Exam-Hall Diagram Blueprints

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> - **Missing the $r^2$ Spherical Jacobian in Radial Probability $P_{nl}(r)$:** The single deadliest Hydrogen exam trap is maximizing $|R_{nl}(r)|^2$ or $|\psi_{nlm}|^2$ instead of $P_{nl}(r) = r^2|R_{nl}(r)|^2$! For $1s$, $|\psi_{100}|^2$ peaks at $r = 0$, whereas $P_{10}(r)$ peaks at $r_{\text{mp}} = a_0$; for $2p$, $|R_{21}|^2$ peaks at $2a_0$, whereas $P_{21}(r)$ peaks at $4a_0$! Always include the volume element $r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$.
> - **Confusing $\langle r\rangle$ vs. $r_{\text{mp}}$ vs. $1/\langle r^{-1}\rangle$:** In Hydrogen $1s$, $r_{\text{mp}} = a_0$, $1/\langle r^{-1}\rangle = a_0$, while $\langle r\rangle = 1.5\,a_0$! Never write $\langle r^{-1}\rangle = 1/\langle r\rangle$—by Jensen's inequality for strictly positive $r$, $\langle r^{-1}\rangle > 1/\langle r\rangle$ strictly!
> - **Forgetting the Spin Degeneracy Factor $g_s = 2$ (or Octant Factor $1/8$) in $k$-Space Density of States:** In 3D standing-wave $k$-space ($k_i = n_i\pi/L$), you MUST restrict to the positive octant ($\frac{1}{8}$) AND multiply by $g_s = 2$ for electrons—forgetting either changes $g(E)$ and $E_F$ by factors of $2^{2/3}$ or $4$! Verify against $N(k) = \frac{Vk^3}{3\pi^2}$ every time.
> - **Atomic Mass vs. Molar Mass Unit Error in Free-Electron Fermi Energy:** When computing $n = \frac{\rho N_A}{M}$, make sure $\rho$ and $M$ use matching mass units (either both in grams with $\text{cm}^{-3} \to 10^6\text{ m}^{-3}$, or strictly SI $\rho$ in $\text{kg/m}^3$ and $M$ in $\text{kg/mol}$)! Also verify the valency $Z_v$ (Na, Cu, Ag have $1$ free electron/atom; Mg, Zn have $2$; Al has $3$).
> - **Sign & Cyclic-Order Errors in Angular Momentum Commutators:** Keep the cyclic wheel $(x \to y \to z \to x)$ locked in: $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = +i\hbar\hat{L}_z$, hence $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_z] = -i\hbar\hat{L}_y$ (NOT $+i\hbar\hat{L}_y$!). Flipping one sign destroys $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$.
> - **Associated Laguerre Normalization Convention:** Standard Rodrigues definition $L_q^p(\rho) = \frac{e^\rho \rho^{-p}}{q!}\frac{d^q}{d\rho^q}(e^{-\rho}\rho^{q+p})$ puts a single $(n + l)!$ in the denominator of $R_{nl}$; older texts (Schiff) omit $1/q!$ and multiply by $(n + l)!$, putting $[(n + l)!]^3$ in the denominator. Always state your definition of $L_q^p$ and check $R_{10}(r) = \frac{2}{a_0^{3/2}}e^{-r/a_0}$!

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Omitting the $r^2$ Volume Factor in Hydrogen Most-Probable Radius Questions:** On questions like CSE 2023 ($2p$ most probable distance), candidates who maximize $|R_{21}(r)|^2 \propto r^2 e^{-r/a_0}$ get $r = 2a_0$ instead of $4a_0$ and lose $70\%$ of the 15 marks immediately; explicitly writing $P_{21}(r) = r^2|R_{21}(r)|^2$ and checking normalization $\int_0^\infty P_{21}(r)\,dr = 1$ locks down full step-marks.
- **Skipping the Single-Valuedness Proof Restricting Orbital $l$ to Integers:** In angular momentum ladder proofs, deriving $2j = N \implies j = 0, \frac{1}{2}, 1, \dots$ is only half the story; examiners explicitly look for the coordinate-space boundary condition $\Phi_m(\phi + 2\pi) = \Phi_m(\phi) \implies m \in \mathbb{Z}$ explaining why orbital $l$ rejects half-integers.
- **Dropping the $u(0) = 0$ Odd-Parity Filter in 3D Spherical Square Wells:** On CSE 2023 Q1c (minimum depth for two $l = 0$ bound states in a 3D spherical well), candidates who apply the 1D finite-well criterion ($z_0 > \pi/2$ for two states) forget that $u(0) = 0$ kills all even cosine solutions, requiring $z_0 > 3\pi/2$ (i.e., $V_0 > \frac{9\pi^2\hbar^2}{8ma^2}$) instead of $\pi/2$!
- **Forgetting Reduced Mass $\mu$ vs. Electron Mass $m_e$ and SI $4\pi\epsilon_0$:** Dropping $4\pi\epsilon_0$ (CGS/SI mixing) in the Coulomb potential $V(r) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ or skipping the $c_{j+1}/c_j \sim 1/j \implies e^{+\rho}$ divergence proof in the Hydrogen radial series costs crucial conceptual step-marks.
