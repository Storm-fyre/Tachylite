# Chapter 2: One-Dimensional Quantum Mechanics — Wells, Tunneling Barriers & the Linear Harmonic Oscillator

## 1. Stationary States and General Theorems of One-Dimensional Quantum Mechanics

Before solving individual potentials one by one, examine the common mathematical anatomy governing *every* one-dimensional quantum system with a static potential $V(x)$. Stripping away the complications of three-dimensional geometry exposes the exact mechanisms behind energy quantization, zero-point motion, parity classification, and tunneling directly through boundary conditions on a single second-order ordinary differential equation.

Mastering these universal theorems transforms one-dimensional quantum mechanics from a list of memorized formulas into a deductive toolkit: you can predict the number of nodes, the parity, the energy ordering, and the exact qualitative shape of $\psi(x)$ before solving a single algebraic equation.

### 1.1 Separation of Variables, Stationary States and Bohr-Frequency Beats

For a particle of mass $m$ moving in a time-independent 1D potential $V(x)$, the Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation (TDSE) is:

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi(x,t)}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\Psi(x,t)}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\Psi(x,t)$$

Seeking separable solutions of the form $\Psi(x,t) = \psi(x)T(t)$ and dividing through by $\psi(x)T(t)$ separates the variables cleanly into a real constant $E$ (the energy eigenvalue):

$$\frac{i\hbar}{T(t)}\frac{dT(t)}{dt} = \frac{1}{\psi(x)}\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi(x)}{dx^2} + V(x)\psi(x)\right] = E$$

Integrating the temporal equation yields $T(t) = e^{-iEt/\hbar}$, leaving the **1D Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation (TISE)**:

$$\boxed{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi(x)}{dx^2} + V(x)\psi(x) = E\psi(x) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \psi''(x) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\left[V(x) - E\right]\psi(x)}$$

A separable energy eigenstate $\Psi_n(x,t) = \psi_n(x)e^{-iE_n t/\hbar}$ is called a **stationary state** because its probability density $|\Psi_n(x,t)|^2 = |\psi_n(x)|^2$ and the expectation value of every time-independent observable $\langle\hat{A}\rangle = \langle\psi_n|\hat{A}|\psi_n\rangle$ remain strictly constant in time.

#### Superposition and Bohr-Frequency Beats

Dynamics returns the moment two or more stationary states are superposed. Consider a normalized two-level superposition of real energy eigenfunctions $\psi_1(x)$ and $\psi_2(x)$:

$$\Psi(x,t) = c_1\psi_1(x)e^{-iE_1 t/\hbar} + c_2\psi_2(x)e^{-iE_2 t/\hbar} \qquad (|c_1|^2 + |c_2|^2 = 1)$$

Computing the probability density $|\Psi(x,t)|^2 = \Psi^*\Psi$:

$$|\Psi(x,t)|^2 = |c_1|^2\psi_1^2(x) + |c_2|^2\psi_2^2(x) + 2\operatorname{Re}\!\left[c_1^* c_2\,e^{-i(E_2 - E_1)t/\hbar}\right]\psi_1(x)\psi_2(x)$$

Writing $c_1^* c_2 = |c_1||c_2|e^{i\delta}$ reveals a time-varying interference cross-term oscillating at the **Bohr transition frequency** $\omega_{21} = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{\hbar}$:

$$\boxed{|\Psi(x,t)|^2 = |c_1|^2\psi_1^2(x) + |c_2|^2\psi_2^2(x) + 2|c_1||c_2|\psi_1(x)\psi_2(x)\cos(\omega_{21}t - \delta)}$$

Consequently, the centroid $\langle x(t)\rangle$ sloshes harmonically back and forth at $\omega_{21}$, explaining why an atom radiates electromagnetic waves at frequencies proportional to energy *differences* rather than single orbital frequencies.

### 1.2 Boundary Continuity of $\psi(x)$ and $\psi'(x)$

To establish exact matching conditions across any boundary $x = x_0$, integrate the TISE across an infinitesimal interval $[x_0 - \epsilon,\, x_0 + \epsilon]$:

$$\psi'(x_0 + \epsilon) - \psi'(x_0 - \epsilon) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\int_{x_0 - \epsilon}^{x_0 + \epsilon} \left[V(x) - E\right]\psi(x)\,dx$$

Taking the limit $\epsilon \to 0^+$ distinguishes three exact boundary regimes:

1. **Finite potential everywhere (including finite step jumps):** The integrand $[V(x) - E]\psi(x)$ stays bounded across $x_0$, so the integral vanishes as $\epsilon \to 0$. Hence **both $\psi(x)$ and $\psi'(x)$ are strictly continuous at $x_0$**.
2. **Dirac delta potential $V(x) = \alpha\delta(x - x_0)$:** $\psi(x)$ remains continuous at $x_0$ (otherwise $\psi''$ would contain $\delta'(x - x_0)$, which cannot match $\delta(x - x_0)$), while the integral collapses to $\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}\psi(x_0)$, yielding a finite slope discontinuity:
   $$\boxed{\psi'(x_0^+) - \psi'(x_0^-) = \frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}\psi(x_0)}$$
3. **Infinite rigid wall ($V = \infty$ for $x > x_0$):** The particle cannot penetrate the wall ($\psi(x) \equiv 0$ for $x > x_0$). Continuity of $\psi$ forces $\psi(x_0) = 0$, while $\psi'(x_0)$ exhibits an arbitrary finite cusp because $V\psi$ forms an indeterminate $\infty \cdot 0$ product.

### 1.3 Curvature Rule, Energy Bound, Non-Degeneracy, Node Theorem and Parity

Writing the TISE in curvature form $\frac{\psi''(x)}{\psi(x)} = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}[V(x) - E]$ exposes the entire qualitative geometry of 1D wave functions:

- **Classically allowed region ($E > V(x)$):** $\psi''$ and $\psi$ have *opposite* signs, so $\psi(x)$ curves **toward** the $x$-axis and oscillates sinusoidally. Larger local kinetic energy $E - V(x)$ creates sharper curvature and shorter local wavelength.
- **Classically forbidden region ($E < V(x)$):** $\psi''$ and $\psi$ have the *same* sign, so $\psi(x)$ curves **away** from the $x$-axis (monotonic exponential decay or growth; normalizability selects pure decay toward zero).
- **Classical turning points ($E = V(x)$):** $\psi''(x) = 0$, marking exact inflection points where oscillatory curvature smoothly switches into exponential tails.

#### Theorem 1: Energy Lower Bound ($E > V_{\min}$)

For any normalizable eigenstate $\psi(x)$ of $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + V(\hat{x})$, integrating by parts on $(-\infty, \infty)$ with vanishing boundary terms gives:

$$E = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi^*\left(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + V\psi\right)dx = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi'(x)|^2\,dx + \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V(x)|\psi(x)|^2\,dx$$

Because a normalizable nonzero function cannot be constant everywhere, $\int |\psi'|^2 dx > 0$ strictly, while $V(x) \ge V_{\min}$ everywhere implies $\int V|\psi|^2 dx \ge V_{\min}\int |\psi|^2 dx = V_{\min}$. Hence $\boxed{E > V_{\min}}$ strictly—no bound state can ever sit motionless at the potential minimum!

#### Theorem 2: Non-Degeneracy of All 1D Bound States (Wronskian Proof)

**Statement:** Every bound-state energy level $E$ of a 1D potential is strictly non-degenerate.

*Proof:* Suppose $\psi_1(x)$ and $\psi_2(x)$ are two bound-state solutions sharing the same energy $E$:

$$\psi_1''(x) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}[V(x) - E]\psi_1(x), \qquad \psi_2''(x) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}[V(x) - E]\psi_2(x)$$

Multiply the first equation by $\psi_2$, the second by $\psi_1$, and subtract:

$$\psi_2\psi_1'' - \psi_1\psi_2'' = 0 \implies \frac{d}{dx}\left(\psi_2\psi_1' - \psi_1\psi_2'\right) = 0$$

Thus the **Wronskian** $W(x) \equiv \psi_2(x)\psi_1'(x) - \psi_1(x)\psi_2'(x)$ is strictly constant for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Because $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ are bound states, both vanish as $x \to \pm\infty$, forcing $W(\infty) = 0$ and therefore $W(x) \equiv 0$ everywhere:

$$\psi_2\psi_1' = \psi_1\psi_2' \implies \frac{\psi_1'}{\psi_1} = \frac{\psi_2'}{\psi_2} \implies \ln\psi_1(x) = \ln\psi_2(x) + \text{const} \implies \psi_1(x) = C\,\psi_2(x)$$

Hence $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ represent the same physical ray—no degeneracy is possible. *(Note: Continuous scattering states $E > V(\pm\infty)$ do NOT vanish at $\pm\infty$, so $W \neq 0$, allowing 2-fold degeneracy between left- and right-moving plane waves $e^{\pm ikx}$.)* $\blacksquare$

#### Theorem 3: Node Theorem and Definite Parity of Symmetric Potentials

1. **Sturm–Liouville Node Theorem:** Ordering bound-state energies $E_0 < E_1 < E_2 < \cdots$, the $n^{\text{th}}$ excited state $\psi_n(x)$ has **exactly $n$ interior zeros (nodes)** (for a 1D box indexed from $n = 1$, $\psi_n$ has $n - 1$ interior nodes). Higher kinetic energy produces faster spatial curvature and thus more nodes.
2. **Definite Parity Theorem:** If $V(-x) = V(x)$, then $[\hat{\Pi}, \hat{H}] = 0$. If $\psi(x)$ is a bound eigenstate with energy $E$, then $\psi(-x) = \hat{\Pi}\psi(x)$ is also an eigenstate with the *exact same* energy $E$. By Theorem 2 (non-degeneracy), $\psi(-x) = c\,\psi(x)$; applying $\hat{\Pi}$ twice gives $c^2 = 1 \implies c = \pm 1$. Thus **every bound state of a symmetric 1D potential is strictly even or strictly odd**, alternating even, odd, even, $\dots$ starting with a nodeless even ground state!

## 2. Particle in a 1D Box (Infinite Square Well)

The infinite square well is the purest laboratory for quantum confinement: a particle moves freely between two impenetrable rigid walls separated by distance $L$. Because the wave function is forced strictly to zero at both walls, only discrete standing de Broglie half-wavelengths fit inside the box—just like standing acoustic modes on a plucked guitar string.

```text
  V(x)
   ^
 ∞ |            V = ∞            |      V = 0      |            V = ∞
   |                             |                 |
   |                             |   -----------   |  E3 = 9 E1  (2 nodes)
   |                             |                 |
   |                             |                 |
   |                             |   -----------   |  E2 = 4 E1  (1 node)
   |                             |                 |
   |                             |   -----------   |  E1 = π²ħ²/(2mL²) (0 nodes)
 0 +-----------------------------+-----------------+-----------------------------> x
                                x=0               x=L
```

### 2.1 Exact Solution of the TISE - Boundary Conditions and Normalization

#### Physical Setup and Potential

$$V(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 < x < L \\ \infty, & x \le 0 \text{ or } x \ge L \end{cases}$$

Outside the box ($x \le 0$ and $x \ge L$), infinite potential forces $\psi(x) = 0$. Continuity of $\psi$ imposes the Dirichlet boundary conditions:

$$\psi(0) = 0, \qquad \psi(L) = 0$$

#### Unbroken Step-by-Step Derivation

Inside $0 < x < L$, the TISE reduces to:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E\psi \implies \frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + k^2\psi = 0, \qquad k \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$$

*(Why $E > 0$ strictly? If $E = 0$, $\psi(x) = Ax + B$, and $\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0$ forces $A = B = 0$. If $E < 0$, $\psi(x) = A e^{\kappa x} + B e^{-\kappa x}$, and $\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0$ gives $A + B = 0$ and $2A\sinh(\kappa L) = 0 \implies A = B = 0$. Both yield the unphysical null state!)*

For $E > 0$ ($k > 0$ real), the general solution inside $(0, L)$ is:

$$\psi(x) = A\sin(kx) + B\cos(kx)$$

1. **Apply $\psi(0) = 0$:** $A\sin(0) + B\cos(0) = B = 0 \implies \psi(x) = A\sin(kx)$.
2. **Apply $\psi(L) = 0$:** $A\sin(kL) = 0$. For a non-trivial solution ($A \neq 0$), we require:
   $$k_n L = n\pi \implies k_n = \frac{n\pi}{L} \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$
   *(Here $n = 0$ gives $\psi \equiv 0$, and negative integers $-n$ merely flip the unobservable overall sign of $\sin(n\pi x/L)$.)*
3. **Quantized energy spectrum:** Substituting $k_n = \frac{\sqrt{2mE_n}}{\hbar} = \frac{n\pi}{L}$:
   $$\boxed{E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} = \frac{n^2 h^2}{8mL^2} = n^2 E_1 \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)}$$
4. **Normalization:** Enforce $\int_0^L |\psi_n(x)|^2\,dx = 1$:
   $$1 = |A|^2\int_0^L \sin^2\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = |A|^2\int_0^L \frac{1 - \cos(2n\pi x/L)}{2}\,dx = |A|^2\left[\frac{x}{2} - \frac{L}{4n\pi}\sin\left(\frac{2n\pi x}{L}\right)\right]_0^L = |A|^2\frac{L}{2}$$
   Choosing real positive $A = \sqrt{2/L}$ gives the normalized eigenfunctions:
   $$\boxed{\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \qquad 0 \le x \le L}$$

#### Orthonormality and Symmetric-Box $[-L/2, +L/2]$ Representation

Using $\sin A\sin B = \frac{1}{2}[\cos(A - B) - \cos(A + B)]$, direct integration verifies $\int_0^L \psi_m(x)\psi_n(x)\,dx = \delta_{mn}$. If the box is instead centered symmetrically on $[-L/2, +L/2]$ (so $V(-x) = V(x)$), substituting $x \to x + L/2$ keeps the **identical energy spectrum** $E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$ while making parity manifest:

$$\psi_n(x) = \begin{cases} \sqrt{\dfrac{2}{L}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\right), & n = 1, 3, 5, \dots \;\;(\text{even parity}) \\[8pt] \sqrt{\dfrac{2}{L}}\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\right), & n = 2, 4, 6, \dots \;\;(\text{odd parity}) \end{cases}$$

### 2.2 Zero-Point Energy and Classical Correspondence ($n \to \infty$)

1. **Physical origin of Zero-Point Energy ($E_1 = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} > 0$):** Classically, a particle in a flat box can rest motionless with $E = 0$. Quantum mechanically, pinning $\psi = 0$ at $x = 0$ and $x = L$ forces the wave function to arch smoothly through at least one half-wavelength ($\lambda_{\max} = 2L$). That unavoidable spatial curvature generates minimum de Broglie momentum $p_1 = h/(2L) = \pi\hbar/L$ and minimum kinetic energy $E_1 = p_1^2/(2m)$—a direct consequence of Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. For macroscopic objects ($m \sim 0.1\text{ kg}, L \sim 1\text{ m}$), $E_1 \sim 10^{-66}\text{ J}$ is completely unmeasurable, whereas for an electron inside an atom ($L \sim 10^{-10}\text{ m}$), $E_1 \approx 37.6\text{ eV}$ dominates atomic chemistry.
2. **Dimensional & Limiting Checks:** $\left[\frac{\hbar^2}{mL^2}\right] = \frac{(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-1})^2}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-2} = \text{J}$. As $L \to \infty$, adjacent level spacing $\Delta E_n = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} \to 0$, recovering the continuous free-particle spectrum.
3. **Bohr's Correspondence Principle ($n \to \infty$):**
   - Fractional energy spacing vanishes: $\frac{E_{n+1} - E_n}{E_n} = \frac{2n + 1}{n^2} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0$.
   - Over any coarse-grained macroscopic interval $\Delta x \gg L/n$, rapid oscillations of $\sin^2(n\pi x/L)$ average to $\frac{1}{2}$, yielding uniform probability density $\overline{|\psi_n(x)|^2} = \frac{2}{L}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{L}$—identical to a classical particle bouncing back and forth at constant speed!

### 2.3 Exact Expectation Values ($\langle x\rangle, \langle x^2\rangle, \langle p\rangle, \langle p^2\rangle$) and Uncertainty Product

Working on $[0, L]$ in the $n^{\text{th}}$ stationary state $\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$:

1. **Expectation value $\langle x\rangle$:** Since $|\psi_n(x)|^2$ is symmetric about the midpoint $x = L/2$, shifting $u = x - L/2$ makes $u|\psi_n(u + L/2)|^2$ odd on $[-L/2, L/2]$, yielding:
   $$\boxed{\langle x\rangle = \frac{L}{2}}$$

2. **Expectation value $\langle x^2\rangle$:** Using $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{2}$ and defining $\alpha \equiv \frac{2n\pi}{L}$:
   $$\langle x^2\rangle = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L x^2\sin^2\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x^2\,dx - \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x^2\cos(\alpha x)\,dx$$
   Evaluate the second integral via integration by parts twice, noting $\sin(\alpha L) = \sin(2n\pi) = 0$ and $\cos(\alpha L) = \cos(2n\pi) = 1$:
   $$\int_0^L x^2\cos(\alpha x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^2\sin(\alpha x)}{\alpha} + \frac{2x\cos(\alpha x)}{\alpha^2} - \frac{2\sin(\alpha x)}{\alpha^3}\right]_0^L = \frac{2L}{\alpha^2} = \frac{2L}{(2n\pi/L)^2} = \frac{L^3}{2n^2\pi^2}$$
   Substituting back:
   $$\boxed{\langle x^2\rangle = L^2\left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}\right)} \implies (\Delta x)^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2 = L^2\left(\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}\right)$$
   *(Classical correspondence check: as $n \to \infty$, $(\Delta x)^2 \to \frac{L^2}{12}$, the exact variance of a uniform distribution on $[0, L]$!)*

3. **Expectation values $\langle p\rangle$ and $\langle p^2\rangle$:**
   $$\langle p\rangle = \frac{2}{L}(-i\hbar)\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = -i\hbar\frac{n\pi}{L^2}\left[\frac{L}{2n\pi}\sin^2\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\right]_0^L = \boxed{0}$$
   Inside $(0, L)$, $\hat{p}^2\psi_n = -\hbar^2\psi_n'' = \left(\frac{n\pi\hbar}{L}\right)^2\psi_n$, giving immediately:
   $$\boxed{\langle p^2\rangle = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{L^2} = 2mE_n} \implies \Delta p = \frac{n\pi\hbar}{L}$$

4. **Uncertainty Product:**
   $$\boxed{\Delta x\,\Delta p = n\pi\hbar\sqrt{\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}} = \hbar\sqrt{\frac{n^2\pi^2}{12} - \frac{1}{2}}}$$
   For the ground state $n = 1$: $\Delta x\,\Delta p = \hbar\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2}{12} - \frac{1}{2}} = 0.568\,\hbar > \frac{\hbar}{2}$, strictly satisfying Heisenberg's bound.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Superposition Beats, $\langle x(t)\rangle$, Ground Energy and Interval Probability in a 1D Box
> **Problem:** A particle of mass $m$ in a 1D infinite well $[0, L]$ is prepared at $t = 0$ in the superposition state $\Psi(x,0) = A\left[\psi_1(x) + 2\psi_2(x)\right]$.
> (a) Find the real positive normalization constant $A$, write $\Psi(x,t)$, and find $\langle\hat{H}\rangle$.
> (b) Derive the exact time-dependent centroid $\langle x(t)\rangle$ and identify its oscillation frequency.
> (c) For an electron in a box of width $L = 2.00\text{ \AA}$, calculate $E_1$ in $\text{eV}$ and find the probability of locating the ground-state electron within the central half $[L/4,\, 3L/4]$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Normalization, time evolution, and average energy:**
> By orthonormality $\langle\psi_m|\psi_n\rangle = \delta_{mn}$:
> $$1 = \langle\Psi(0)|\Psi(0)\rangle = A^2(1^2 + 2^2) = 5A^2 \implies \boxed{A = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}}$$
> Since $E_2 = 4E_1$, attaching stationary phase factors gives:
> $$\Psi(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left[\psi_1(x)e^{-iE_1 t/\hbar} + 2\psi_2(x)e^{-i4E_1 t/\hbar}\right]$$
> Energy measurement yields $E_1$ with probability $1/5$ or $4E_1$ with probability $4/5$, so:
> $$\langle\hat{H}\rangle = \frac{1}{5}E_1 + \frac{4}{5}(4E_1) = \boxed{\frac{17}{5}E_1 = 3.40\,E_1}$$
>
> **(b) Time-dependent centroid $\langle x(t)\rangle$:**
> $$\langle x(t)\rangle = \frac{1}{5}\langle 1|x|1\rangle + \frac{4}{5}\langle 2|x|2\rangle + \frac{4}{5}\langle 1|x|2\rangle\cos\!\left(\frac{(E_2 - E_1)t}{\hbar}\right)$$
> Since $\langle 1|x|1\rangle = \langle 2|x|2\rangle = L/2$, we compute the off-diagonal matrix element $\langle 1|x|2\rangle$ using $\sin A\sin B = \frac{1}{2}[\cos(A - B) - \cos(A + B)]$:
> $$\langle 1|x|2\rangle = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L x\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right) - \cos\!\left(\frac{3\pi x}{L}\right)\right]dx$$
> Using $\int_0^L x\cos(m\pi x/L)\,dx = \frac{L^2}{m^2\pi^2}\left[(-1)^m - 1\right] = -\frac{2L^2}{m^2\pi^2}$ for odd $m$:
> $$\langle 1|x|2\rangle = \frac{1}{L}\left(-\frac{2L^2}{\pi^2} + \frac{2L^2}{9\pi^2}\right) = -\frac{16L}{9\pi^2}$$
> Therefore:
> $$\boxed{\langle x(t)\rangle = \frac{L}{2} - \frac{64L}{45\pi^2}\cos\!\left(\frac{3E_1 t}{\hbar}\right)}$$
> The centroid sloshes between $0.356L$ and $0.644L$ at the Bohr frequency $\omega_{21} = \frac{3E_1}{\hbar} = \frac{3\pi^2\hbar}{2mL^2}$.
>
> **(c) Electron in $L = 2.00\text{ \AA} = 0.200\text{ nm}$ well and central-half probability:**
> Using $\hbar c = 197.33\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}$ and $m_e c^2 = 5.110 \times 10^5\text{ eV}$:
> $$E_1 = \frac{\pi^2(\hbar c)^2}{2(m_e c^2)L^2} = \frac{\pi^2(197.33\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm})^2}{2(5.110 \times 10^5\text{ eV})(0.200\text{ nm})^2} = \frac{3.843 \times 10^5}{4.088 \times 10^4} = \boxed{9.40\text{ eV}} \quad (= 1.506 \times 10^{-18}\text{ J})$$
> Probability in $[L/4,\, 3L/4]$ for ground state $\psi_1(x)$:
> $$P\!\left(\frac{L}{4} \le x \le \frac{3L}{4}\right) = \frac{2}{L}\int_{L/4}^{3L/4}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{1}{L}\left[x - \frac{L}{2\pi}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right)\right]_{L/4}^{3L/4} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\sin\frac{3\pi}{2} - \sin\frac{\pi}{2}\right] = \boxed{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi} \approx 0.818 \;(81.8\%)}$$

## 3. Particle in a Symmetric Finite Square Well

Real confining potentials—such as semiconductor quantum wells or short-range nuclear interactions—never have infinitely tall walls. Lowering the walls from $\infty$ to a finite height $V_0$ produces three dramatic physical changes: (i) only a *finite* number of discrete bound states exist for $0 < E < V_0$, (ii) the wave function leaks exponentially into the classically forbidden exterior $|x| > a$, and (iii) this spatial spreading lengthens the effective wavelength inside the well, pushing every bound-state energy strictly *below* the corresponding infinite-well level.

```text
  V(x)
   ^
V0 |===================+                     +===================  Continuum (E > V0)
   |   Region I        |      Region II      |     Region III
   |   (Forbidden)     |   ---- E3 (even) ---|   (Forbidden)
   |   e^(+κx) tail    |                     |   e^(-κx) tail
   |                   |   ---- E2 (odd)  ---|
   |                   |                     |
   |                   |   ---- E1 (even) ---|
 0 +-------------------+---------------------+-------------------> x
                      x=-a       x=0        x=+a
                         |<------ 2a ------>|
```

### 3.1 Matching Logarithmic Derivatives and Transcendental Equations

#### Physical Setup and Parity Exploitation

Consider a particle of mass $m$ in the symmetric finite square well of width $2a$ and wall height $V_0 > 0$:

$$V(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & |x| < a \quad (\text{Region II}) \\ V_0, & |x| > a \quad (\text{Regions I and III}) \end{cases}$$

*(Note: Shifting the energy zero so that $V = -V_0$ inside and $0$ outside simply shifts every eigenvalue by $-V_0$, leaving all wave functions and binding energies $V_0 - E$ completely unchanged.)*

For bound states ($0 < E < V_0$), define the two real positive wave numbers:

$$k \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}, \qquad \kappa \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$$

Notice the exact energy-independent constraint linking $k$ and $\kappa$:

$$k^2 + \kappa^2 = \frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}$$

The TISE in the three regions reads:
- **Regions I & III ($|x| > a$):** $\psi'' - \kappa^2\psi = 0 \implies$ general solution $C e^{-\kappa x} + D e^{+\kappa x}$. Normalizability ($\psi \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$) discards the exponentially diverging piece in each exterior region:
  $$\psi_{\text{I}}(x) = D e^{+\kappa x} \quad (x < -a), \qquad \psi_{\text{III}}(x) = C e^{-\kappa x} \quad (x > +a)$$
- **Region II ($|x| < a$):** $\psi'' + k^2\psi = 0 \implies \psi_{\text{II}}(x) = A\cos(kx) + B\sin(kx)$.

Because $V(-x) = V(x)$ is symmetric and all 1D bound states are non-degenerate, **every eigenstate has definite parity**—it is either purely even ($B = 0, D = +C$) or purely odd ($A = 0, D = -C$). Exploiting symmetry means matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = +a$ automatically enforces continuity at $x = -a$!

#### Case 1: Even-Parity Bound States ($\psi(-x) = +\psi(x)$)

Inside $|x| < a$, $\psi_{\text{II}}(x) = A\cos(kx)$; for $x > a$, $\psi_{\text{III}}(x) = C e^{-\kappa x}$. Requiring continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = a$:

$$A\cos(ka) = C e^{-\kappa a} \tag{1}$$

$$-kA\sin(ka) = -\kappa C e^{-\kappa a} \tag{2}$$

Dividing Equation $(2)$ by Equation $(1)$ (matching the **logarithmic derivative** $\psi'/\psi$ at $x = a$) eliminates $A$ and $C$ cleanly:

$$\boxed{k\tan(ka) = \kappa} \qquad (\text{Even-parity quantization condition})$$

#### Case 2: Odd-Parity Bound States ($\psi(-x) = -\psi(x)$)

Inside $|x| < a$, $\psi_{\text{II}}(x) = B\sin(kx)$; for $x > a$, $\psi_{\text{III}}(x) = C e^{-\kappa x}$. Continuity at $x = a$ gives:

$$B\sin(ka) = C e^{-\kappa a} \tag{3}$$

$$kB\cos(ka) = -\kappa C e^{-\kappa a} \tag{4}$$

Dividing Equation $(4)$ by Equation $(3)$ yields:

$$\boxed{-k\cot(ka) = \kappa} \qquad (\text{Odd-parity quantization condition})$$

### 3.2 Graphical Solution, Universal Bound-State Proof and Limiting Behavior

Introduce the dimensionless variables $\xi \equiv ka > 0$, $\eta \equiv \kappa a > 0$, and the dimensionless **well-strength parameter** $z_0$:

$$\xi \equiv ka, \qquad \eta \equiv \kappa a, \qquad \boxed{z_0 \equiv \frac{a\sqrt{2mV_0}}{\hbar}}$$

Then $k^2 + \kappa^2 = \frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}$ transforms into a **quarter-circle of radius $z_0$** in the first quadrant $(\xi > 0, \eta > 0)$:

$$\xi^2 + \eta^2 = z_0^2 \implies \eta = \sqrt{z_0^2 - \xi^2}$$

The allowed bound-state energies correspond to the exact intersection points of this quarter-circle with the transcendental branches:

$$\boxed{\eta = \xi\tan\xi \;\;(\text{even}), \qquad \eta = -\xi\cot\xi \;\;(\text{odd})}$$

Once an intersection $\xi_n$ is found, the quantized energy is immediately $E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 \xi_n^2}{2ma^2} = V_0\left(\frac{\xi_n}{z_0}\right)^2$.

#### Theorem: Every 1D Attractive Finite Well Has at Least One Bound State

*Proof:* On the interval $\xi \in \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$, the first even-parity curve $f(\xi) = \xi\tan\xi$ is strictly continuous, starts at $f(0) = 0$, and increases monotonically to $+\infty$ as $\xi \to (\pi/2)^-$. Meanwhile, the quarter-circle $g(\xi) = \sqrt{z_0^2 - \xi^2}$ starts at $g(0) = z_0 > 0$ on the vertical axis and drops continuously to $0$ at $\xi = z_0$. By the Intermediate Value Theorem, the difference $f(\xi) - g(\xi)$ changes sign from $-z_0 < 0$ to positive on $\left(0, \min(z_0, \pi/2)\right)$, guaranteeing a unique intersection $\xi_1$ for **any** $z_0 > 0$, no matter how shallow ($V_0 \to 0^+$) or narrow ($a \to 0^+$) the well! $\blacksquare$

*(Crucial 3D Contrast: For a 3D spherical square well with $l = 0$, the reduced radial function $u(r) = rR(r)$ must satisfy $u(0) = 0$, discarding all even cosine states and leaving ONLY the odd condition $-\xi\cot\xi = \eta$. Because the first odd branch begins at $\xi = \pi/2$, a 3D spherical well supports ZERO bound states unless $z_0 > \pi/2$, i.e., $V_0 a^2 > \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{8m}$, and requires $z_0 > 3\pi/2$ for two $l = 0$ bound states!)*

#### Counting Total Bound States and Limiting Checks

1. **Number of bound states $N$:** Successive branches (alternating even, odd, even, odd, $\dots$) leave the $\xi$-axis at $\xi = 0, \frac{\pi}{2}, \pi, \frac{3\pi}{2}, \dots = \frac{(n - 1)\pi}{2}$. A new bound state enters whenever $z_0$ crosses a multiple of $\pi/2$:
   $$\boxed{N = \left\lfloor\frac{2z_0}{\pi}\right\rfloor + 1} \qquad (\text{for } 2z_0/\pi \notin \mathbb{Z})$$
2. **Infinite-well limit ($V_0 \to \infty \implies z_0 \to \infty$):** The quarter-circle radius expands to infinity, pushing every intersection against the vertical asymptotes $\xi_n \to \frac{n\pi}{2}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$). Thus:
   $$E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 \xi_n^2}{2ma^2} \xrightarrow{V_0 \to \infty} \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m(2a)^2} = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$$
   recovering the exact infinite square well of width $L = 2a$!
3. **Shallow/narrow well limit ($z_0 \ll 1$):** Only one even bound state exists with $\xi_1 \approx z_0 \ll 1$, so $\tan\xi_1 \approx \xi_1$ and $\eta_1 = \xi_1\tan\xi_1 \approx z_0^2$. Hence $\kappa \approx \frac{2mV_0 a}{\hbar^2}$ and the binding energy below the top of the well is $E_B = V_0 - E_1 = \frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m} \approx \frac{2m(V_0 a)^2}{\hbar^2}$—matching the exact bound-state energy of an attractive Dirac delta well $V(x) = -(2V_0 a)\delta(x)$.
4. **Exponential penetration tails:** Outside $|x| > a$, the probability density decays as $|\psi(x)|^2 \propto e^{-2\kappa|x|}$ with characteristic amplitude penetration depth $\delta = \frac{1}{\kappa} = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}$. Higher excited states sit closer to $V_0$, yielding smaller $\kappa$ and much deeper tunneling tails into the forbidden exterior.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Bound-State Count, Ground Energy and Penetration Depth in a Finite Square Well

> (a) Evaluate the well-strength parameter $z_0$ and determine the exact number of bound states (specifying how many are even and how many are odd).
> (b) Solve the even transcendental equation numerically to 3 significant figures to obtain the ground-state energy $E_1$, compare it with the infinite-well energy $E_1^\infty$, and find the amplitude penetration depth $\delta = 1/\kappa$.
>

> **(a) Well-strength parameter $z_0$ and bound-state count:**

> $$z_0 = \frac{a\sqrt{2(m_e c^2)V_0}}{\hbar c} = \frac{0.200\sqrt{2(5.110 \times 10^5)(25.0)}}{197.33} = \frac{0.200 \times 5054.7}{197.33} = \boxed{5.123}$$
> Compare $z_0 = 5.123$ with integer multiples of $\frac{\pi}{2} = 1.571$:
> - $0 < z_0$: State 1 (even, $\xi_1 \in (0, \pi/2)$)
> - $\frac{\pi}{2} = 1.571 < z_0$: State 2 (odd, $\xi_2 \in (\pi/2, \pi)$)
> - $\pi = 3.142 < z_0$: State 3 (even, $\xi_3 \in (\pi, 3\pi/2)$)
> - $\frac{3\pi}{2} = 4.712 < z_0$: State 4 (odd, $\xi_4 \in (3\pi/2, 2\pi)$)
> - $2\pi = 6.283 > z_0$: Not reached.
>
> Hence there are exactly **$\boxed{4\text{ bound states}}$**: **2 even** ($n = 1, 3$) and **2 odd** ($n = 2, 4$).
>
> **(b) Ground-state energy $E_1$ and penetration depth $\delta$:**
> Solve $\xi_1\tan\xi_1 = \sqrt{5.123^2 - \xi_1^2} = \sqrt{26.245 - \xi_1^2}$ on $(0, 1.571)$:
> - At $\xi = 1.30\text{ rad}$: $\text{LHS} = 1.30\tan(1.30) = 4.683$; $\text{RHS} = \sqrt{26.245 - 1.690} = 4.955$ (too low).
> - At $\xi = 1.32\text{ rad}$: $\text{LHS} = 1.32\tan(1.32) = 5.152$; $\text{RHS} = \sqrt{26.245 - 1.742} = 4.950$ (too high).
> - Refining at $\xi_1 = 1.312\text{ rad}$: $\text{LHS} = 1.312\tan(1.312) = 4.956 \approx \text{RHS} = 4.952$ (converged to 3 digits!).
>
> Ground-state energy:
> $$E_1 = V_0\left(\frac{\xi_1}{z_0}\right)^2 = 25.0\left(\frac{1.312}{5.123}\right)^2 = 25.0(0.06559) = \boxed{1.64\text{ eV}}$$

> $$E_1^\infty = V_0\left(\frac{\pi/2}{z_0}\right)^2 = 25.0\left(\frac{1.5708}{5.123}\right)^2 = 2.35\text{ eV} \implies E_1 < E_1^\infty \;\checkmark$$
> Penetration depth into $|x| > a$ (with $\eta_1 = \kappa a = 4.952$):
> $$\delta = \frac{1}{\kappa} = \frac{a}{\eta_1} = \frac{2.00\text{ \AA}}{4.952} = \boxed{0.404\text{ \AA}}$$

## 4. 1D Scattering - Step Potential and Rectangular Barrier

When a particle's energy exceeds the asymptotic potential on at least one side ($E > 0$), it is no longer trapped in discrete quantized levels; instead, stationary **scattering states** exist for a continuous spectrum of energies. Here quantum wave mechanics breaks classical mechanics in both directions: waves partially reflect from a step they have more than enough energy to climb ($E > V_0$), and waves leak straight through a barrier they lack the energy to surmount ($E < V_0$).

```text
  (a) POTENTIAL STEP                              (b) RECTANGULAR TUNNELING BARRIER
  V(x)                                            V(x)
   ^                                               ^
   |                  V = V0                       |              V = V0
V0 |                  +================         V0 |          +------------+
   |   Region I       |  Region II                 |  Reg I   |   Reg II   |  Reg III
   |  (Incident +     |  E > V0: e^(ik2 x)         |          |  Evanescent|
   |   Reflected)     |  E < V0: e^(-κx)           |  ~~>  E  |   decay    |  ~~~>  T > 0
   |    V = 0         |                            |  V = 0   | (C e^κx +  |   V = 0
   |                  |                            |          |  D e^-κx)  |
 0 +------------------+---------------> x        0 +----------+------------+-----------> x
                     x=0                                     x=0          x=a
```

### 4.1 The Potential Step ($E > V_0$ and $E < V_0$)

#### Physical Setup

$$V(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0 \quad (\text{Region I}) \\ V_0 > 0, & x > 0 \quad (\text{Region II}) \end{cases}$$

A steady beam of particles of mass $m$ and energy $E > 0$ approaches the step from $x = -\infty$ moving right ($+x$). Because no source exists at $x = +\infty$, Region II contains *no* left-moving wave.

#### Case A: Over-the-Step Scattering ($E > V_0$)

Define the two real positive wave numbers $k_1 \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$ and $k_2 \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2m(E - V_0)}}{\hbar}$ (note $k_2 < k_1$):

$$\psi_{\text{I}}(x) = A e^{ik_1 x} + B e^{-ik_1 x} \quad (x < 0), \qquad \psi_{\text{II}}(x) = C e^{ik_2 x} \quad (x > 0)$$

Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = 0$:

$$A + B = C, \qquad ik_1(A - B) = ik_2 C \implies A - B = \frac{k_2}{k_1}C$$

Adding and subtracting yields the amplitude ratios:

$$\frac{C}{A} = \frac{2k_1}{k_1 + k_2}, \qquad \frac{B}{A} = \frac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$$

Evaluate probability currents using $J = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}(\psi^*\psi' - \psi\psi'^*)$:

$$J_{\text{inc}} = \frac{\hbar k_1}{m}|A|^2, \qquad J_{\text{ref}} = -\frac{\hbar k_1}{m}|B|^2, \qquad J_{\text{trans}} = \frac{\hbar k_2}{m}|C|^2$$

*(Critical step: The speed in Region II is $v_2 = \hbar k_2/m \neq v_1 = \hbar k_1/m$, so the factor $k_2/k_1$ MUST multiply $|C/A|^2$ in $T$!)*

$$\boxed{R = \frac{|J_{\text{ref}}|}{J_{\text{inc}}} = \left|\frac{B}{A}\right|^2 = \left(\frac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\sqrt{E} - \sqrt{E - V_0}}{\sqrt{E} + \sqrt{E - V_0}}\right)^2}$$

$$\boxed{T = \frac{J_{\text{trans}}}{J_{\text{inc}}} = \frac{k_2}{k_1}\left|\frac{C}{A}\right|^2 = \frac{4k_1 k_2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2} = \frac{4\sqrt{E(E - V_0)}}{\left(\sqrt{E} + \sqrt{E - V_0}\right)^2}}$$

- **Flux Conservation Check:** $R + T = \frac{(k_1 - k_2)^2 + 4k_1 k_2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2} = \frac{(k_1 + k_2)^2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2} = 1$.
- **Physical Interpretation & Limits:** Even when $E > V_0$, the sudden change in de Broglie wavelength ($\lambda_1 \to \lambda_2$) creates a wave-impedance mismatch—identical to Fresnel reflection of light at a glass interface ($R = \left(\frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2}\right)^2$). Remarkably, even for a **step-down cliff** ($V_0 < 0 \implies k_2 > k_1$), $R > 0$! As $E \to V_0^+$, $k_2 \to 0 \implies R \to 1, T \to 0$; as $E \gg V_0$, $k_2 \to k_1 \implies R \to 0, T \to 1$ (recovering classical transmission).

#### Case B: Sub-Threshold Reflection and Evanescent Penetration ($0 < E < V_0$)

Define real positive $\kappa \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$. In Region II, $\psi'' - \kappa^2\psi = 0$ has general solution $C e^{-\kappa x} + D e^{+\kappa x}$; finiteness as $x \to +\infty$ forces $D = 0$:

$$\psi_{\text{I}}(x) = A e^{ik_1 x} + B e^{-ik_1 x} \quad (x < 0), \qquad \psi_{\text{II}}(x) = C e^{-\kappa x} \quad (x > 0)$$

Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = 0$:

$$A + B = C, \qquad ik_1(A - B) = -\kappa C$$

Eliminating $C$ and solving for $B/A$ and $C/A$:

$$\frac{B}{A} = \frac{k_1 - i\kappa}{k_1 + i\kappa} = e^{-2i\delta} \quad \left(\tan\delta = \frac{\kappa}{k_1}\right), \qquad \frac{C}{A} = \frac{2k_1}{k_1 + i\kappa}$$

- **Reflection coefficient:** Since $k_1 - i\kappa$ and $k_1 + i\kappa$ are complex conjugates of identical modulus:
  $$\boxed{R = \left|\frac{B}{A}\right|^2 = \frac{k_1^2 + \kappa^2}{k_1^2 + \kappa^2} = 1}$$
- **Transmission coefficient:** In Region II, $\psi_{\text{II}}(x) = C e^{-\kappa x}$ yields $J_{\text{trans}} = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left[|C|^2 e^{-2\kappa x}(-\kappa) - |C|^2 e^{-2\kappa x}(-\kappa)\right] = 0$, giving $\boxed{T = 0}$.
- **Evanescent Penetration Depth:** Although $100\%$ of the incident flux ultimately reflects back with a phase shift $-2\delta$, the probability density inside the classically forbidden region is strictly **non-zero**:
  $$|\psi_{\text{II}}(x)|^2 = \frac{4k_1^2}{k_1^2 + \kappa^2}|A|^2 e^{-2\kappa x} = \frac{4E}{V_0}|A|^2 e^{-2\kappa x}$$
  decaying over the characteristic $1/e$ amplitude penetration depth $\delta = 1/\kappa$ (or probability decay length $1/(2\kappa)$). If the barrier ends after a finite thickness $a \sim \delta$ before this tail dies away, the particle escapes into the far side—producing **quantum tunneling**!

### 4.2 Rectangular Potential Barrier - Exact $4 \times 4$ Matching, Tunneling and Resonances

#### Physical Setup

Consider a rectangular barrier of height $V_0 > 0$ and width $a$:

$$V(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0 \quad (\text{Region I}) \\ V_0, & 0 \le x \le a \quad (\text{Region II}) \\ 0, & x > a \quad (\text{Region III}) \end{cases}$$

*(Note: If the problem places the barrier symmetrically on $[-a, +a]$, the barrier width is simply $2a$; every final expression below applies verbatim upon substituting $a \to 2a$!)*

#### Case 1: Quantum Tunneling ($0 < E < V_0$) — Unabridged $4 \times 4$ Boundary Elimination

Define real positive wave numbers $k \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$ and $\kappa \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$, satisfying $k^2 + \kappa^2 = \frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}$. For a particle beam incident from the left ($x \to -\infty$), the general solutions in the three regions are:

$$\psi_{\text{I}}(x) = A e^{ikx} + B e^{-ikx} \quad (x < 0)$$

$$\psi_{\text{II}}(x) = C e^{\kappa x} + D e^{-\kappa x} \quad (0 \le x \le a)$$

$$\psi_{\text{III}}(x) = F e^{ikx} \quad (x > a)$$

*(Crucial point: Unlike the semi-infinite step, Region II has finite width $a$, so $e^{+\kappa x}$ does NOT blow up and MUST be retained alongside $e^{-\kappa x}$!)*

Imposing continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at both edges ($x = 0$ and $x = a$) generates the exact $4 \times 4$ linear system for $(B, C, D, F)$ in terms of $A$:

$$\begin{aligned}
x = 0, \;\psi:\quad & A + B = C + D \tag{5} \\
x = 0, \;\psi':\quad & ik(A - B) = \kappa(C - D) \tag{6} \\
x = a, \;\psi:\quad & C e^{\kappa a} + D e^{-\kappa a} = F e^{ika} \tag{7} \\
x = a, \;\psi':\quad & \kappa\left(C e^{\kappa a} - D e^{-\kappa a}\right) = ik F e^{ika} \tag{8}
\end{aligned}$$

**Step 1: Solve Equations $(7)$ and $(8)$ for $C$ and $D$ in terms of $F$.**
Divide Equation $(8)$ by $\kappa$:

$$C e^{\kappa a} + D e^{-\kappa a} = F e^{ika}, \qquad C e^{\kappa a} - D e^{-\kappa a} = \frac{ik}{\kappa}F e^{ika}$$

Adding and subtracting isolates $C$ and $D$ cleanly:

$$C = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \frac{ik}{\kappa}\right)F e^{(ik - \kappa)a}, \qquad D = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \frac{ik}{\kappa}\right)F e^{(ik + \kappa)a} \tag{9}$$

**Step 2: Eliminate $B$ from Equations $(5)$ and $(6)$ to express $A$ in terms of $C$ and $D$.**
Divide Equation $(6)$ by $ik$ (using $\frac{1}{i} = -i$):

$$A + B = C + D, \qquad A - B = -\frac{i\kappa}{k}(C - D)$$

Adding these two equations eliminates $B$:

$$2A = \left(1 - \frac{i\kappa}{k}\right)C + \left(1 + \frac{i\kappa}{k}\right)D \tag{10}$$

**Step 3: Substitute Equation $(9)$ into Equation $(10)$ to obtain $A/F$.**

$$2A = \frac{1}{2}F e^{ika}\left[\left(1 - \frac{i\kappa}{k}\right)\left(1 + \frac{ik}{\kappa}\right)e^{-\kappa a} + \left(1 + \frac{i\kappa}{k}\right)\left(1 - \frac{ik}{\kappa}\right)e^{+\kappa a}\right]$$

Expand each complex product carefully (using $-i^2 = +1$):

$$\left(1 - \frac{i\kappa}{k}\right)\left(1 + \frac{ik}{\kappa}\right) = 1 + i\left(\frac{k}{\kappa} - \frac{\kappa}{k}\right) + 1 = 2 + i\left(\frac{k^2 - \kappa^2}{k\kappa}\right)$$

$$\left(1 + \frac{i\kappa}{k}\right)\left(1 - \frac{ik}{\kappa}\right) = 1 - i\left(\frac{k}{\kappa} - \frac{\kappa}{k}\right) + 1 = 2 - i\left(\frac{k^2 - \kappa^2}{k\kappa}\right)$$

Group the $e^{\kappa a} + e^{-\kappa a} = 2\cosh(\kappa a)$ and $e^{\kappa a} - e^{-\kappa a} = 2\sinh(\kappa a)$ terms and divide by $2$:

$$\frac{A}{F} = \frac{e^{ika}}{4}\left[2\left(e^{\kappa a} + e^{-\kappa a}\right) - i\left(\frac{k^2 - \kappa^2}{k\kappa}\right)\left(e^{\kappa a} - e^{-\kappa a}\right)\right]$$

$$\frac{A}{F} = e^{ika}\left[\cosh(\kappa a) - \frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{k^2 - \kappa^2}{k\kappa}\right)\sinh(\kappa a)\right]$$

**Step 4: Compute $|A/F|^2$ and the exact Transmission Coefficient $T$.**
Since $|e^{ika}|^2 = 1$, taking the squared modulus gives:

$$\left|\frac{A}{F}\right|^2 = \cosh^2(\kappa a) + \frac{(k^2 - \kappa^2)^2}{4k^2\kappa^2}\sinh^2(\kappa a)$$

Substitute the hyperbolic identity $\cosh^2(\kappa a) = 1 + \sinh^2(\kappa a)$ and combine the $\sinh^2(\kappa a)$ terms:

$$\left|\frac{A}{F}\right|^2 = 1 + \left[\frac{4k^2\kappa^2 + (k^4 - 2k^2\kappa^2 + \kappa^4)}{4k^2\kappa^2}\right]\sinh^2(\kappa a) = 1 + \frac{(k^2 + \kappa^2)^2}{4k^2\kappa^2}\sinh^2(\kappa a)$$

Insert $k^2 = \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}$, $\kappa^2 = \frac{2m(V_0 - E)}{\hbar^2}$, and $k^2 + \kappa^2 = \frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}$:

$$\frac{(k^2 + \kappa^2)^2}{4k^2\kappa^2} = \frac{V_0^2}{4E(V_0 - E)}$$

Because Regions I and III share the *same* wave number $k$, $J_{\text{inc}} = \frac{\hbar k}{m}|A|^2$ and $J_{\text{trans}} = \frac{\hbar k}{m}|F|^2$, so $T = \frac{J_{\text{trans}}}{J_{\text{inc}}} = \left|\frac{F}{A}\right|^2 = \left|\frac{A}{F}\right|^{-2}$:

$$\boxed{T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0 - E)}\right]^{-1}} \qquad (0 < E < V_0)$$

and by flux conservation $R = 1 - T$:

$$\boxed{R = \frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0 - E) + V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}}$$

#### Essential Tunneling Limits and the Infinite Thin Barrier (Dirac-Delta Limit)

1. **Thick/Opaque Barrier Limit ($\kappa a \gg 1$, Gamow Tunneling Law):**
   Using $\sinh(\kappa a) \approx \frac{1}{2}e^{\kappa a}$ and neglecting $1$ compared to the exponentially huge second term:
   $$\boxed{T \approx \frac{16E(V_0 - E)}{V_0^2}\,e^{-2\kappa a} = \frac{16E(V_0 - E)}{V_0^2}\exp\!\left[-\frac{2a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}\right]}$$
   This exponential sensitivity to width $a$, mass $\sqrt{m}$, and barrier deficit $\sqrt{V_0 - E}$ governs nuclear $\alpha$-decay half-lives, Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (STM), tunnel diodes, and stellar fusion.
2. **Exact Threshold Limit ($E \to V_0^-$):** As $\kappa \to 0$, $\sinh(\kappa a) \approx \kappa a$, so $\frac{\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{V_0 - E} \to \frac{2ma^2}{\hbar^2}$, yielding a completely smooth, non-zero value:
   $$\boxed{T(E = V_0) = \left(1 + \frac{mV_0 a^2}{2\hbar^2}\right)^{-1}}$$
3. **Infinite Thin Barrier Limit (Dirac-Delta Barrier $V(x) = \alpha\delta(x)$):**
   Take $a \to 0$ and $V_0 \to \infty$ keeping the barrier area (strength) $\alpha \equiv V_0 a$ fixed. Then $\kappa a \approx \frac{\sqrt{2mV_0}a}{\hbar} = \frac{\sqrt{2m\alpha a}}{\hbar} \to 0$, so $\sinh(\kappa a) \approx \kappa a$, and:
   $$\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0 - E)} \longrightarrow \frac{V_0^2\left(\frac{2mV_0 a^2}{\hbar^2}\right)}{4EV_0} = \frac{m(V_0 a)^2}{2\hbar^2 E} = \frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2 E}$$
   *(Direct verification from $V(x) = \alpha\delta(x)$: matching $\psi(0^+) = \psi(0^-) \implies A + B = F$ and $\psi'(0^+) - \psi'(0^-) = \frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}\psi(0) \implies ikF - ik(A - B) = \frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}F$ immediately eliminates $B = F - A$ to give $F = \frac{A}{1 + \frac{im\alpha}{\hbar^2 k}}$, yielding the identical exact result!)*
   $$\boxed{T_\delta = \frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2 E}}, \qquad R_\delta = \frac{\dfrac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2 E}}{1 + \dfrac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2 E}}}$$

#### Case 2: Over-the-Barrier Scattering and Transmission Resonances ($E > V_0$)

When $E > V_0$, the interior solution oscillates with real wave number $k_2 \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2m(E - V_0)}}{\hbar}$. Analytically continuing $\kappa \to -ik_2$ (which transforms $\sinh(\kappa a) \to -i\sin(k_2 a)$, hence $\sinh^2(\kappa a) \to -\sin^2(k_2 a)$, and $V_0 - E = -(E - V_0)$) turns the minus signs into a plus sign cleanly:

$$\boxed{T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2\sin^2(k_2 a)}{4E(E - V_0)}\right]^{-1}} \qquad (E > V_0)$$

- **Classical Limit ($E \gg V_0$):** $\frac{V_0^2}{4E(E - V_0)} \to 0 \implies T \to 1$.
- **Quantum Transmission Resonances ($T = 1$, Ramsauer–Townsend Effect):** Whenever $\sin(k_2 a) = 0$, i.e., $k_2 a = n\pi \iff a = n\frac{\lambda_2}{2}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$), the barrier becomes **100% transparent** ($T = 1, R = 0$) at discrete resonant energies:
  $$\boxed{E_n^{\text{res}} = V_0 + \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}}$$
  Physically, partial reflections from the front face ($x = 0$) and back face ($x = a$) interfere destructively—the matter-wave equivalent of a Fabry–Pérot interferometer or anti-reflection optical coating, observed experimentally as the **Ramsauer–Townsend transparency minimum** when low-energy electrons scatter off noble-gas atoms.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Potential Step Reflection Percentage and Rectangular Barrier Tunneling Probability
> **Problem:** (a) A beam of particles of energy $E = 9.0\text{ eV}$ is incident from the left on a potential step of height $V_0 = 8.0\text{ eV}$. What percentage of particles is reflected?
> (b) Electrons of energy $E = 5.0\text{ eV}$ impinge on a rectangular potential barrier of height $V_0 = 10.0\text{ eV}$ and width $a = 5.0\text{ \AA}$. Calculate the exact tunneling transmission probability $T$ and compare it with the thick-barrier Gamow approximation.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Potential Step ($E = 9.0\text{ eV} > V_0 = 8.0\text{ eV}$):**
> Since $k_1 \propto \sqrt{E} = \sqrt{9.0} = 3.0$ and $k_2 \propto \sqrt{E - V_0} = \sqrt{9.0 - 8.0} = 1.0$:
> $$R = \left(\frac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{3.0 - 1.0}{3.0 + 1.0}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4} = \boxed{25.0\%}$$
> *(And $T = 1 - R = 75.0\%$ are transmitted despite classical mechanics predicting $100\%$ transmission.)*
>
> **(b) Rectangular Barrier Tunneling ($E = 5.0\text{ eV} < V_0 = 10.0\text{ eV}$, $a = 0.50\text{ nm}$):**
> Step 1: Compute the evanescent decay constant $\kappa$ using $m_e c^2 = 5.110 \times 10^5\text{ eV}$ and $\hbar c = 197.33\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}$:
> $$\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2(m_e c^2)(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar c} = \frac{\sqrt{2(5.110 \times 10^5)(5.0)}}{197.33} = \frac{\sqrt{5.110 \times 10^6}}{197.33} = \frac{2260.5}{197.33} = 11.456\text{ nm}^{-1}$$
> $$\kappa a = (11.456\text{ nm}^{-1})(0.50\text{ nm}) = 5.728$$
> Step 2: Evaluate $\sinh(\kappa a)$ and the energy prefactor:
> $$e^{\kappa a} = e^{5.728} = 307.35, \qquad e^{-\kappa a} = 0.00325 \implies \sinh(\kappa a) = \frac{307.35 - 0.00325}{2} = 153.67$$
> $$\frac{V_0^2}{4E(V_0 - E)} = \frac{(10.0)^2}{4(5.0)(5.0)} = \frac{100}{100} = 1.00$$
> Step 3: Exact transmission coefficient:
> $$T = \left[1 + (1.00)(153.67)^2\right]^{-1} = \frac{1}{1 + 23615} = \boxed{4.234 \times 10^{-5}}$$
> Step 4: Thick-barrier Gamow approximation ($\kappa a = 5.73 \gg 1$):
> $$T_{\text{approx}} = \frac{16E(V_0 - E)}{V_0^2}\,e^{-2\kappa a} = 4\,e^{-11.456} = \frac{4}{(307.35)^2} = \frac{4}{94464} = \boxed{4.234 \times 10^{-5}}$$
> Because $\kappa a > 5$, the thick-barrier exponential formula matches the exact $4 \times 4$ solution to four significant figures.

## 5. The Linear Harmonic Oscillator - Analytic and Ladder-Operator Derivations

Every smooth potential $V(x)$ expanded in a Taylor series about a stable equilibrium minimum $x_0$ ($V'(x_0) = 0, V''(x_0) > 0$) behaves locally as a parabola: $V(x) \approx V(x_0) + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x - x_0)^2$. That universality makes the 1D Linear Harmonic Oscillator (LHO) the single most important solvable model in physics, describing vibrating diatomic molecules, quantized crystal lattice phonons, and electromagnetic field modes.

```text
          V(x) = (1/2) m ω² x²
       \                         /
        \    --- E3 = 7/2 ħω ---/       <-- Spacing ΔE = ħω (strictly uniform!)
         \        ^   |        /
          \       a†  a       /
           \  --- E2 = 5/2 ħω/
            \     ^   |     /
             \    a†  a    /
              \-- E1 = 3/2 ħω          (Odd parity, 1 node)
               \  ^   |  /
                \-E0 = 1/2 ħω          (Even Gaussian ground state, 0 nodes)
                 \     /
                  \___/                <-- Zero-point energy E0 = ħω/2 > 0
                    0                  x
```

Because UPSC examiners frequently mandate *either* the differential-equation Hermite solution *or* Dirac's algebraic ladder-operator method explicitly, both derivations are presented below in complete, self-contained detail.

---

### 5.1 Method I - Analytic Differential-Equation Solution (Hermite Polynomials)

#### Step 1: Dimensionless Reduction of the TISE

With potential $V(x) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$, the TISE is:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2\psi = E\psi$$

Multiply by $-\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}$:

$$\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + \left(\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2} - \frac{m^2\omega^2}{\hbar^2}x^2\right)\psi = 0$$

Introduce the dimensionless coordinate $\xi$ and dimensionless energy $\varepsilon$:

$$\xi \equiv \alpha x \quad \text{where } \alpha \equiv \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}, \qquad \varepsilon \equiv \frac{2E}{\hbar\omega}$$

Since $\frac{d^2}{dx^2} = \alpha^2\frac{d^2}{d\xi^2}$ and $\frac{m^2\omega^2}{\hbar^2}x^2 = \alpha^4 x^2 = \alpha^2\xi^2$, dividing through by $\alpha^2 = \frac{m\omega}{\hbar}$ produces the canonical dimensionless TISE:

$$\boxed{\frac{d^2\psi}{d\xi^2} + (\varepsilon - \xi^2)\psi = 0}$$

#### Step 2: Asymptotic Behavior ($|\xi| \to \infty$) and Hermite's Equation

As $|\xi| \to \infty$, the constant $\varepsilon$ becomes negligible compared to $\xi^2$, leaving $\psi'' \approx \xi^2\psi$. Testing $\psi_{\text{asym}}(\xi) = e^{\pm\xi^2/2}$ gives $\frac{d^2}{d\xi^2}(e^{\pm\xi^2/2}) = (\xi^2 \pm 1)e^{\pm\xi^2/2} \approx \xi^2 e^{\pm\xi^2/2}$. Square-integrability rejects $e^{+\xi^2/2}$ and selects the decaying Gaussian envelope $e^{-\xi^2/2}$.

Factor out this exact asymptotic behavior by setting $\psi(\xi) = H(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}$:

$$\psi' = \left(H' - \xi H\right)e^{-\xi^2/2}$$

$$\psi'' = \left[H'' - 2\xi H' + (\xi^2 - 1)H\right]e^{-\xi^2/2}$$

Substitute into $\psi'' + (\varepsilon - \xi^2)\psi = 0$ and cancel the never-vanishing factor $e^{-\xi^2/2}$—notice that $\xi^2 H$ cancels identically, yielding **Hermite's Differential Equation**:

$$\boxed{\frac{d^2 H}{d\xi^2} - 2\xi\frac{dH}{d\xi} + (\varepsilon - 1)H = 0}$$

#### Step 3: Power-Series Solution, Recurrence Relation and Quantization by Termination

Because $\xi = 0$ is an ordinary point, expand $H(\xi)$ in a Frobenius/Maclaurin power series:

$$H(\xi) = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} c_j \xi^j, \qquad H'(\xi) = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} j c_j \xi^{j-1}, \qquad H''(\xi) = \sum_{j=2}^{\infty} j(j-1)c_j \xi^{j-2} = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} (j+2)(j+1)c_{j+2}\xi^j$$

Substitute into Hermite's equation and group equal powers of $\xi^j$:

$$\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[(j+2)(j+1)c_{j+2} - 2j c_j + (\varepsilon - 1)c_j\right]\xi^j = 0$$

Since the powers $\xi^j$ are linearly independent, every bracket must vanish identically, giving the **two-term recurrence relation**:

$$\boxed{c_{j+2} = \frac{2j + 1 - \varepsilon}{(j+1)(j+2)}\,c_j \qquad (j = 0, 1, 2, \dots)}$$

Observe that even-power coefficients ($c_0, c_2, c_4, \dots$) and odd-power coefficients ($c_1, c_3, c_5, \dots$) decouple completely—expressing even and odd parity automatically.

**Normalizability Divergence Proof:** Suppose the series does *not* terminate. For very large $j \gg \varepsilon$, the ratio of successive coefficients approaches:

$$\frac{c_{j+2}}{c_j} \xrightarrow{j\to\infty} \frac{2j}{j^2} = \frac{2}{j}$$

Compare this with the Maclaurin expansion of $e^{+\xi^2} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\xi^{2k}}{k!} = \sum_{\text{even } j} a_j \xi^j$, where $a_j = \frac{1}{(j/2)!}$ satisfies the exact same asymptotic ratio:

$$\frac{a_{j+2}}{a_j} = \frac{(j/2)!}{(j/2 + 1)!} = \frac{1}{j/2 + 1} \xrightarrow{j\to\infty} \frac{2}{j}$$

Consequently, an infinite series diverges like $H(\xi) \sim e^{+\xi^2}$ as $|\xi| \to \infty$, forcing the total wave function $\psi(\xi) = H(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2} \sim e^{+\xi^2/2} \to \infty$—completely unnormalizable!

To preserve square-integrability, the series **must terminate into a finite polynomial of degree $n$**: set the opposite-parity seed ($c_1$ for even $n$, or $c_0$ for odd $n$) to zero, and force $c_{n+2} = 0$ for some non-negative integer $n \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$:

$$2n + 1 - \varepsilon = 0 \implies \varepsilon_n = 2n + 1 \implies \frac{2E_n}{\hbar\omega} = 2n + 1$$

$$\boxed{E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega \qquad (n = 0, 1, 2, \dots)}$$

#### Step 4: Hermite Polynomials $H_n(\xi)$ and Normalized Eigenfunctions

With standard physics normalization (leading term $2^n\xi^n$), the terminating polynomials are the **Hermite polynomials $H_n(\xi)$**, compactly generated by:
- **Generating function:** $g(\xi, s) \equiv e^{-s^2 + 2s\xi} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{H_n(\xi)}{n!}s^n$
- **Rodrigues' formula:** $H_n(\xi) = (-1)^n e^{\xi^2}\frac{d^n}{d\xi^n}\left(e^{-\xi^2}\right)$
- **First five Hermite polynomials:**
  $$H_0(\xi) = 1, \quad H_1(\xi) = 2\xi, \quad H_2(\xi) = 4\xi^2 - 2, \quad H_3(\xi) = 8\xi^3 - 12\xi, \quad H_4(\xi) = 16\xi^4 - 48\xi^2 + 12$$

**Orthogonality and Normalization Proof via Generating Function:** Multiply $g(\xi, s)g(\xi, t)e^{-\xi^2}$ and integrate over $(-\infty, \infty)$:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\xi^2} e^{-s^2 + 2s\xi} e^{-t^2 + 2t\xi}\,d\xi = e^{2st}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-(\xi - s - t)^2}\,d\xi = \sqrt{\pi}\,e^{2st} = \sqrt{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^n}{n!}s^n t^n$$

Comparing coefficients of $s^m t^n$ with $\sum_{m,n}\frac{s^m t^n}{m!n!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} H_m(\xi)H_n(\xi)e^{-\xi^2}\,d\xi$ immediately proves:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} H_m(\xi)H_n(\xi)e^{-\xi^2}\,d\xi = \sqrt{\pi}\,2^n n!\,\delta_{mn}$$

Reverting from $\xi = \alpha x$ ($dx = d\xi/\alpha$ with $\alpha = \sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$) gives the complete normalized LHO eigenfunctions:

$$\boxed{\psi_n(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!}}\,H_n\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}\,x\right)\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega x^2}{2\hbar}\right)}$$

*(Sanity checks: $\psi_n(-x) = (-1)^n\psi_n(x)$ has definite parity; $H_n$ is a degree-$n$ polynomial with $n$ real distinct roots, giving exactly $n$ nodes; and outside the classical turning points $|x| > x_t = \sqrt{(2n+1)\hbar/(m\omega)}$, $\psi_n$ penetrates exponentially into the classically forbidden region!)*

### 5.2 Method II - Dirac's Algebraic Ladder-Operator Derivation

Paul Dirac showed that the entire harmonic oscillator spectrum, all wave functions, and every expectation value can be extracted purely from $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$ without ever touching a differential equation or power series.

#### Step 1: Factoring the Hamiltonian via Creation and Annihilation Operators

Motivated by $u^2 + v^2 = (u - iv)(u + iv)$ for commuting numbers, define two dimensionless, mutually adjoint operators—the **lowering (annihilation) operator $\hat{a}$** and **raising (creation) operator $\hat{a}^\dagger$**:

$$\boxed{\hat{a} \equiv \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p}\right), \qquad \hat{a}^\dagger \equiv \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p}\right)}$$

*(Note: Because $\hat{x}^\dagger = \hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}^\dagger = \hat{p}$, $\hat{a}^\dagger$ is the exact Hermitian adjoint of $\hat{a}$, while neither $\hat{a}$ nor $\hat{a}^\dagger$ is individually Hermitian.)*

Adding and subtracting expresses $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}$ linearly in terms of $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger$:

$$\boxed{\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger\right), \qquad \hat{p} = i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}\left(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a}\right)}$$

#### Step 2: Computing $\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$, $\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger$, and the Fundamental Commutator

Multiply $\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ carefully without assuming $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}$ commute:

$$\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p}\right)\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p}\right) = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\left(\hat{x}\hat{p} - \hat{p}\hat{x}\right)\right]$$

Insert $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$ (so $\frac{i}{m\omega}(i\hbar) = -\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}$) and identify $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2$:

$$\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{1}{\hbar\omega}\left(\frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2\right) - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\hat{H}}{\hbar\omega} - \frac{1}{2}$$

Repeating the algebra in reverse order flips the commutator sign:

$$\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2} - \frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]\right] = \frac{\hat{H}}{\hbar\omega} + \frac{1}{2}$$

Subtracting yields the **canonical bosonic commutation relation**, and defining the Hermitian **number operator** $\hat{N} \equiv \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ casts the Hamiltonian into diagonal algebraic form:

$$\boxed{[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1}, \qquad \boxed{\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2}\right)}$$

#### Step 3: Ladder Commutators and Quantization Proof

Using the Leibniz product rule with $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$ (so $[\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}] = -1$):

$$[\hat{N}, \hat{a}] = [\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}] = [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}]\hat{a} = -\hat{a} \implies [\hat{H}, \hat{a}] = -\hbar\omega\hat{a}$$

$$[\hat{N}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = [\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \hat{a}^\dagger[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = +\hat{a}^\dagger \implies [\hat{H}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = +\hbar\omega\hat{a}^\dagger$$

Let $|n\rangle$ be a normalized eigenket of $\hat{N}$ with real eigenvalue $n$ ($\hat{N}|n\rangle = n|n\rangle$, $\langle n|n\rangle = 1$):

1. **Non-negativity of $n$:** Take the expectation value of $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$:
   $$n = \langle n|\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}|n\rangle = \left(\hat{a}|n\rangle\right)^\dagger\left(\hat{a}|n\rangle\right) = \|\hat{a}|n\rangle\|^2 \ge 0$$
   with equality $n = 0$ if and only if $\hat{a}|0\rangle = 0$.
2. **Ladder action of $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger$:**
   $$\hat{N}(\hat{a}|n\rangle) = (\hat{a}\hat{N} - \hat{a})|n\rangle = (n - 1)(\hat{a}|n\rangle)$$
   $$\hat{N}(\hat{a}^\dagger|n\rangle) = (\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{N} + \hat{a}^\dagger)|n\rangle = (n + 1)(\hat{a}^\dagger|n\rangle)$$
   Thus $\hat{a}$ lowers the energy by exactly one quantum $\hbar\omega$, while $\hat{a}^\dagger$ raises it by one quantum $\hbar\omega$.
3. **Proof that $n$ must be a non-negative integer:** Suppose $n$ were *not* a non-negative integer. Repeatedly applying $\hat{a}$ would generate non-null eigenkets with eigenvalues $n - 1, n - 2, \dots$ stepping past zero into negative eigenvalues $n - k < 0$, contradicting the positivity proof $n \ge 0$! The *only* escape is that the downward descent hits the exact zero state $|0\rangle$ obeying $\hat{a}|0\rangle = 0$, halting the ladder at $n = 0$. Climbing back up via $\hat{a}^\dagger$ generates every integer $n \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, proving:
   $$\boxed{E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega \qquad (n = 0, 1, 2, \dots)}$$
4. **Exact Normalization Factors and State Generation:**
   - $\|\hat{a}|n\rangle\|^2 = \langle n|\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}|n\rangle = n \implies \boxed{\hat{a}|n\rangle = \sqrt{n}\,|n - 1\rangle}$
   - $\|\hat{a}^\dagger|n\rangle\|^2 = \langle n|\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger|n\rangle = \langle n|(\hat{N} + 1)|n\rangle = n + 1 \implies \boxed{\hat{a}^\dagger|n\rangle = \sqrt{n + 1}\,|n + 1\rangle}$
   - By induction from the ground state $|0\rangle$:
     $$\boxed{|n\rangle = \frac{(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|0\rangle}$$
5. **Ground-State Wavefunction Directly from $\hat{a}|0\rangle = 0$:**
   In position space, $\hat{a}|0\rangle = 0$ becomes a trivial first-order separable ODE:
   $$\left(x + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\psi_0(x) = 0 \implies \frac{d\psi_0}{\psi_0} = -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\,dx \implies \psi_0(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega x^2}{2\hbar}\right)$$
   Applying $(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n/\sqrt{n!}$ to $\psi_0(x)$ reproduces Rodrigues' formula for every Hermite eigenstate $\psi_n(x)$!

#### Step 4: Matrix Elements, Selection Rules, Virial Theorem and Uncertainty Product

Using $\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)$ and $\hat{p} = i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})$ together with $\langle m|k\rangle = \delta_{mk}$ delivers all matrix elements in one line—zero integration required!

$$\boxed{\langle m|\hat{x}|n\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\sqrt{n}\,\delta_{m, n-1} + \sqrt{n+1}\,\delta_{m, n+1}\right)}$$

$$\boxed{\langle m|\hat{p}|n\rangle = i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}\left(\sqrt{n+1}\,\delta_{m, n+1} - \sqrt{n}\,\delta_{m, n-1}\right)}$$

*(Dipole Selection Rule: Because electric-dipole radiative transitions are proportional to $|\langle m|\hat{x}|n\rangle|^2$, a harmonic oscillator absorbs or emits radiation strictly for $\Delta n = \pm 1$!)*

Now compute the expectation values in state $|n\rangle$:
- **Means:** Setting $m = n$ immediately gives $\langle n|\hat{x}|n\rangle = 0$ and $\langle n|\hat{p}|n\rangle = 0$.
- **Second moments:** Expand $\hat{x}^2$ and $\hat{p}^2$ using $\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger = \hat{N} + 1$:
  $$\hat{x}^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(\hat{a}^2 + \hat{a}^{\dagger 2} + \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\right) = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(\hat{a}^2 + \hat{a}^{\dagger 2} + 2\hat{N} + 1\right)$$
  $$\hat{p}^2 = -\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger 2} + \hat{a}^2 - \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} - \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\right) = \frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}\left(2\hat{N} + 1 - \hat{a}^2 - \hat{a}^{\dagger 2}\right)$$
  Because $\hat{a}^2|n\rangle \propto |n - 2\rangle$ and $\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}|n\rangle \propto |n + 2\rangle$ are orthogonal to $\langle n|$, only the diagonal $2\hat{N} + 1$ term survives:
  $$\boxed{\langle n|\hat{x}^2|n\rangle = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}, \qquad \boxed{\langle n|\hat{p}^2|n\rangle = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)m\hbar\omega}$$
- **Quantum Virial Theorem:**
  $$\langle T\rangle_n = \frac{\langle\hat{p}^2\rangle_n}{2m} = \frac{1}{2}\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega = \frac{E_n}{2}, \qquad \langle V\rangle_n = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\langle\hat{x}^2\rangle_n = \frac{1}{2}\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega = \frac{E_n}{2} \implies \boxed{\langle T\rangle_n = \langle V\rangle_n}$$
- **Exact Heisenberg Uncertainty Product:**
  $$\boxed{(\Delta x)_n(\Delta p)_n = \sqrt{\langle\hat{x}^2\rangle_n}\sqrt{\langle\hat{p}^2\rangle_n} = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}}$$
  The ground state ($n = 0$) achieves the absolute minimum $\hbar/2$, while excited states broaden linearly with $n$.

### 5.3 Analytic vs. Ladder LHO Matrix and Box-vs-LHO Structural Comparison

#### Analytic vs. Ladder-Operator LHO Matrix

| Comparison Dimension | Analytic Differential-Equation Method (Schrödinger) | Algebraic Ladder-Operator Method (Dirac) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starting Engine** | Position-space 2nd-order ODE $\psi'' + (\varepsilon - \xi^2)\psi = 0$ | Canonical commutator $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar \implies [\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$ |
| **Origin of Energy Quantization** | Power series $H(\xi)$ must terminate to prevent $e^{+\xi^2/2}$ blow-up as $|\xi|\to\infty$ | Positivity $\|\hat{a}\|n\rangle\|^2 = n \ge 0$ forces downward ladder to halt at $\hat{a}\|0\rangle = 0$ |
| **Wavefunction Construction** | Directly yields explicit polynomials $\psi_n(x) \propto H_n(\alpha x)e^{-\alpha^2 x^2/2}$ | First-order ODE $\hat{a}\|0\rangle = 0$ gives $\psi_0$; excited states via $(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n\|0\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$ |
| **Expectation Values & Matrix Elements** | Tedious Gaussian–Hermite integrals or generating-function tricks | Instant 1-line algebra via $\hat{x} \propto (\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)$ and $\hat{p} \propto i(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})$ |
| **Generalization Power** | Specific to 1D coordinate-space ODEs | Directly generalizes to phonons, photons, coherent states, and QFT Fock spaces |
| **Optimal Mains Exam Use** | When asked explicitly for Hermite ODE, recursion, or explicit $\psi_2(x), \psi_3(x)$ plots | When asked for operator method, $\langle m\|\hat{x}\|n\rangle$, $\langle x^4\rangle$, virial theorem, or perturbation |

#### Qualitative Comparison: 1D Infinite Box vs. Linear Harmonic Oscillator

| Physical Property | Particle in a 1D Box ($[0, L]$) | Linear Harmonic Oscillator ($V = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Energy Eigenvalues** | $E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$) | $E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega$ ($n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) |
| **Level Spacing $\Delta E$** | Grows linearly with $n$: $E_{n+1} - E_n = (2n+1)E_1$ | Strictly uniform: $E_{n+1} - E_n = \hbar\omega$ (constant!) |
| **Zero-Point Energy** | $E_1 = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$ | $E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$ |
| **Spatial Penetration** | Strictly zero outside walls ($x \le 0, x \ge L$) | Exponentially non-zero outside classical turning points $|x| > x_t$ |
| **High-$n$ Probability Density** | Uniform classical average $1/L$ across entire box | Peaks sharply near classical turning points $\pm x_t$ where speed $v \to 0$ |
| **Minimum $\Delta x\,\Delta p$** | $0.568\,\hbar$ ($n = 1$, strictly exceeds $\hbar/2$) | $0.500\,\hbar$ ($n = 0$, exact Gaussian minimum uncertainty!) |

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Charged LHO in a Constant Electric Field, Half-Oscillator and $\langle n|\hat{x}^4|n\rangle$
> **Problem:** (a) A particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ moves in a 1D harmonic oscillator potential placed inside a uniform constant electric field $\mathcal{E}$ along $+x$, with Hamiltonian $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2 - q\mathcal{E}\hat{x}$. Derive the exact energy spectrum $E_n$ and eigenfunctions $\Psi_n(x)$.
> (b) If an impenetrable infinite wall is placed at $x = 0$ on a standard LHO ($V(x) = \infty$ for $x \le 0$, $V(x) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$ for $x > 0$), find the allowed energies and properly normalized eigenfunctions.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Charged LHO in a uniform electric field (exact completion of the square):**
> Complete the square in the total potential $V_{\text{eff}}(x) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 - q\mathcal{E}x$:
> $$V_{\text{eff}}(x) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\left(x^2 - \frac{2q\mathcal{E}}{m\omega^2}x + \frac{q^2\mathcal{E}^2}{m^2\omega^4}\right) - \frac{q^2\mathcal{E}^2}{2m\omega^2} = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x - x_0)^2 - \frac{q^2\mathcal{E}^2}{2m\omega^2}$$
> where the displaced classical equilibrium position is $x_0 \equiv \frac{q\mathcal{E}}{m\omega^2}$.
> Introduce the shifted coordinate $\hat{y} \equiv \hat{x} - x_0$. Because $x_0$ is a constant, $\hat{p}_y = -i\hbar\frac{d}{dy} = -i\hbar\frac{d}{dx} = \hat{p}_x$ and $[\hat{y}, \hat{p}_y] = i\hbar$. The Hamiltonian becomes a standard LHO centered at $x_0$ shifted downward by a constant energy offset:
> $$\hat{H} = \left(\frac{\hat{p}_y^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{y}^2\right) - \frac{q^2\mathcal{E}^2}{2m\omega^2}$$
> Therefore, the exact eigenvalues and normalized eigenfunctions ($n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) are:
> $$\boxed{E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega - \frac{q^2\mathcal{E}^2}{2m\omega^2}}, \qquad \boxed{\Psi_n(x) = \psi_n^{\text{LHO}}\!\left(x - \frac{q\mathcal{E}}{m\omega^2}\right)}$$
> *(Physical check: Every energy level drops by the exact quadratic Stark energy $-\frac{q^2\mathcal{E}^2}{2m\omega^2}$, leaving all transition frequencies $\Delta E = \hbar\omega$ strictly invariant—consistent with exact 2nd-order perturbation theory!)*
>
> **(b) Half-Harmonic Oscillator ($V(x) = \infty$ for $x \le 0$):**
> For $x > 0$, the TISE is identical to the full LHO, but the infinite wall forces $\phi(0) = 0$. Among the full LHO eigenstates $\psi_n^{\text{LHO}}(x)$:
> - Even-$n$ states ($n = 0, 2, 4, \dots$) satisfy $\psi_{2k}^{\text{LHO}}(0) \neq 0$ and are **completely eliminated**.
> - Odd-$n$ states ($n = 1, 3, 5, \dots = 2k + 1$ for $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) naturally satisfy $\psi_{2k+1}^{\text{LHO}}(0) = 0$ and survive!
>
> Since $|\psi_{2k+1}^{\text{LHO}}(x)|^2$ is even on $(-\infty, \infty)$, integrating over only the positive half-line gives $\int_0^\infty |\psi_{2k+1}^{\text{LHO}}(x)|^2\,dx = \frac{1}{2}$, requiring an extra renormalization factor of $\sqrt{2}$:
> $$\boxed{E_k^{\text{half}} = \left(2k + \frac{3}{2}\right)\hbar\omega}, \qquad \boxed{\phi_k(x) = \begin{cases} \sqrt{2}\,\psi_{2k+1}^{\text{LHO}}(x), & x > 0 \\ 0, & x \le 0 \end{cases}} \qquad (k = 0, 1, 2, \dots)$$
> Notice the zero-point energy triples from $\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$ to $\frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega$ and the level spacing doubles to $2\hbar\omega$.

## 6. High-Yield Traps and Exam-Hall Diagram Blueprints

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> - **Box Boundary $[0, L]$ vs. $[-L/2, +L/2]$ Parity Trap:** Never blindly write $\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$ if the exam specifies walls at $x = \pm L/2$ (or $\pm a$)! On $[-L/2, +L/2]$, odd $n$ takes $\cos(n\pi x/L)$ and even $n$ takes $\sin(n\pi x/L)$—and $\langle x\rangle = 0$ instead of $L/2$. Always check the box coordinates in Line 1 of your answer.
> - **Forgetting the $k_2/k_1$ Velocity Factor in Step-Potential Transmission:** For a potential step ($E > V_0$), writing $T = |C/A|^2$ gives $T = \frac{4k_1^2}{(k_1+k_2)^2} > 1$ when $k_1 > k_2$—violating $R + T = 1$! Transmission is ALWAYS the ratio of probability *currents* $T = J_{\text{trans}}/J_{\text{inc}} = \frac{k_2}{k_1}|C/A|^2$. (For a rectangular barrier with $V = 0$ on *both* sides, $k_3 = k_1$, so the ratio simplifies to $|F/A|^2$.)
> - **Dropping $e^{+\kappa x}$ Inside a Finite-Width Barrier:** Inside a semi-infinite step ($x \in [0, \infty)$), $e^{+\kappa x}$ diverges and is dropped; inside a *finite* rectangular barrier ($x \in [0, a]$), $e^{+\kappa x}$ is completely bounded and MUST be retained alongside $e^{-\kappa x}$—dropping it destroys the exact $\sinh^2(\kappa a)$ tunneling formula!
> - **Barrier Width $a$ vs. $2a$ Trap:** UPSC frequently specifies a symmetric barrier on $[-a, +a]$ (as in CSE 2025 Q2c), whose total thickness is **$2a$**, NOT $a$! Replace $a \to 2a$ inside $\sinh^2(2\kappa a)$ and $\sin^2(2k_2 a)$.
> - **Forgetting the $\sqrt{2}$ Renormalization in Half-Wells:** Whenever an infinite wall cuts a symmetric potential in half (e.g., half-harmonic oscillator), restricting odd eigenstates to $x > 0$ halves the normalization integral; you MUST multiply the full-space eigenfunction by $\sqrt{2}$.
> - **Sign Flip in $\hat{p}$ via Ladder Operators:** Remember $\hat{a} \propto (\hat{x} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p})$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger \propto (\hat{x} - \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p})$, so $\hat{p} = i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a}) = -i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}(\hat{a} - \hat{a}^\dagger)$. Verify quickly: $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\frac{\hbar}{2}[\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a}] = i\frac{\hbar}{2}([\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] - [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}]) = i\hbar$!
> - **Superposition Normalization Check:** Before computing probabilities or $\langle H\rangle$ for $\Psi = c_1\psi_1 + c_2\psi_2$, ALWAYS check whether $|c_1|^2 + |c_2|^2 = 1$; UPSC deliberately gives unnormalized combinations like $\psi_1 + 2\psi_2$ to test this step!

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Skipping the $4 \times 4$ Matching Steps in Barrier Tunneling:** Jumping straight from the boundary conditions at $x = \pm a$ to $T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(2\kappa a)}{4E(V_0 - E)}\right]^{-1}$ with phrases like "solving simultaneously" forfeits $50\%$ of the 15-mark allotment; examiners grade the explicit elimination of $C, D, B$ and the conversion from $\cosh^2$ to $1 + \sinh^2$.
- **Leaving Out the $k_2/k_1$ Flux Ratio in Step Potential Proofs:** Writing $T = |C/A|^2$ instead of $T = \frac{k_2}{k_1}|C/A|^2$ immediately breaks $R + T = 1$ and exposes a conceptual gap in probability current density.
- **Forgetting to Prove Why the Hermite Series Must Terminate:** Simply stating "set $2n + 1 - \varepsilon = 0$ to get a polynomial" misses the crucial asymptotic divergence comparison $c_{j+2}/c_j \sim 2/j \implies H(\xi) \sim e^{+\xi^2} \implies \psi \sim e^{+\xi^2/2} \to \infty$ that carries dedicated step-marks in analytic LHO derivations.
- **Unit & Molecular-Mass Conversion Errors in Zero-Point Calculations:** In problems like CSE 2023 Q1a involving an oxygen atom, candidates routinely confuse atomic mass ($16\text{ u} \approx 2.66 \times 10^{-26}\text{ kg}$) with molecular $\text{O}_2$ mass ($32\text{ u}$) or forget to divide molar mass by Avogadro's number $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$.

### 3. Unseen Conceptual & Analytical Twists

1. **Sudden Wall Expansion of a 1D Box (Quantum Quench):** A particle starts in the ground state $\psi_1(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)$ of a 1D infinite square well $[0, L]$.
