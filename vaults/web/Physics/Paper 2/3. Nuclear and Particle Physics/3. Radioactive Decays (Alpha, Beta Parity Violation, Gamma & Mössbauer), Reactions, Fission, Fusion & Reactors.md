# Chapter 3: Radioactive Decays (Alpha, Beta Parity Violation, Gamma, Mössbauer), Reactions, Fission, Fusion & Reactors

## 1. Alpha Decay — Energetics, Geiger–Nuttall Law & Gamow WKB Tunneling

### 1.1 Decay Energetics & Kinetic Energy Partition

Heavy nuclei ($A \gtrsim 150$) sit past the peak of the $B/A$ curve, where growing electrostatic repulsion makes the spontaneous emission of a tightly bound ${}^4_2\text{He}$ ($\alpha$) cluster ($B_\alpha = 28.3\text{ MeV}$) energetically favorable. Because emitting an individual proton or neutron breaks nuclear bonds without returning a large cluster binding bonus, heavy nuclei overwhelmingly prefer $\alpha$-emission:

$${}^A_Z X \longrightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2} Y + {}^4_2\text{He} + Q_\alpha$$

- **Atomic vs. Nuclear Mass $Q_\alpha$-Value:** Defining $Q_\alpha$ from bare nuclear masses and adding/subtracting $Z m_e = (Z - 2)m_e + 2m_e$ cancels all electron rest masses identically:
  $$\boxed{Q_\alpha = \left[M_N(X) - M_N(Y) - M_N(\alpha)\right]c^2 = \left[M_A(X) - M_A(Y) - M_A({}^4\text{He})\right]c^2 > 0}$$
- **Exact Kinetic Energy Sharing (Parent at Rest):** Linear momentum conservation forces the $\alpha$-particle and daughter $Y$ to recoil back-to-back with equal momentum magnitudes $p_\alpha = p_Y$. Non-relativistically ($T_i = p_i^2 / 2m_i$):
  $$T_Y = \frac{p_Y^2}{2 M_Y} = \frac{p_\alpha^2}{2 M_Y} = \frac{m_\alpha}{M_Y} T_\alpha$$
  Substituting into total energy release $Q_\alpha = T_\alpha + T_Y = T_\alpha\left(1 + \dfrac{m_\alpha}{M_Y}\right)$ and using $M_Y / m_\alpha \approx (A - 4)/4$:
  $$\boxed{T_\alpha = Q_\alpha \frac{M_Y}{M_Y + m_\alpha} \approx Q_\alpha\left(\frac{A - 4}{A}\right), \qquad T_Y \approx Q_\alpha\left(\frac{4}{A}\right)}$$
  *Sanity Check:* As $A \to \infty$, $T_\alpha \to Q_\alpha$ and $T_Y \to 0$. For $^{238}\text{U}$, the $\alpha$-particle takes $98.3\%$ of $Q_\alpha$, while the heavy daughter receives a $\sim 70\text{–}100\text{ keV}$ recoil kick. Because it is a strict **two-body decay**, $\alpha$-particles emerge in **discrete monoenergetic line spectra** (fine structure arises when decays populate excited states of $Y$).

### 1.2 The Coulomb-Barrier Paradox, Geiger–Nuttall Law & Gamow's WKB Derivation

Two stark experimental puzzles baffled classical nuclear physics until 1928:
1. **The Barrier Paradox:** At the touching radius $R = R_d + R_\alpha \approx 9.3\text{ fm}$ of a uranium nucleus ($Z_d = 90$), the electrostatic Coulomb barrier towers to $V_B = \frac{2 Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R} \approx \mathbf{28\text{ MeV}}$. Yet $^{238}\text{U}$ emits $\alpha$-particles with only $Q_\alpha = 4.27\text{ MeV} \ll V_B$! Classical mechanics strictly forbids escape.
2. **The Geiger–Nuttall Law (1911):** Varying $Q_\alpha$ by a mere factor of $2$ (from $4.27\text{ MeV}$ in $^{238}\text{U}$ to $8.95\text{ MeV}$ in $^{212}\text{Po}$) collapses the half-life across **24 orders of magnitude** ($4.5 \times 10^9\text{ yr}$ down to $3 \times 10^{-7}\text{ s}$!), obeying a tight linear correlation:
   $$\log_{10} \lambda = A_{\text{GN}} + B_{\text{GN}} \log_{10} R_{\text{air}} \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \log_{10} t_{1/2} = a + \frac{b}{\sqrt{Q_\alpha}}$$

George Gamow, Ronald Gurney, and Edward Condon (1928) solved both puzzles simultaneously in the very first triumph of quantum mechanics inside the nucleus: **quantum WKB tunneling through the Coulomb barrier**.

#### Unabridged WKB Derivation of the Gamow Factor & Geiger–Nuttall Law

- **(i) Physical Assumptions & Setup:** Assume a preformed $\alpha$-particle (reduced mass $\mu = \frac{m_\alpha M_Y}{m_\alpha + M_Y} \approx m_\alpha$) moves inside an attractive spherical nuclear well of radius $R$ with interior velocity $v_{\text{in}}$, striking the inner barrier wall at **assault frequency** $f \approx \dfrac{v_{\text{in}}}{2R}$ ($\sim 10^{21}\text{ s}^{-1}$). Outside $r > R$, it faces the repulsive Coulomb potential $V(r) = \dfrac{2 Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ ($z = 2$, daughter charge $Z_d = Z - 2$), emerging into the classically allowed region at the **outer turning point** $r_c$ where $V(r_c) = Q_\alpha$:
  $$r_c = \frac{2 Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 Q_\alpha}$$
- **(ii) Step-by-Step WKB Integration:**
  The decay constant is $\lambda = f\,T$, where the WKB barrier transmission probability is $T \approx e^{-2G}$ with the **Gamow integral**:
  $$G = \frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{R}^{r_c} \sqrt{2\mu\left[V(r) - Q_\alpha\right]}\,dr = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu Q_\alpha}}{\hbar}\int_{R}^{r_c} \sqrt{\frac{r_c}{r} - 1}\,dr$$
  Make the trigonometric substitution $r = r_c \cos^2\theta \implies dr = -2 r_c \cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta$. At $r = r_c$, $\theta = 0$; at $r = R$, $\theta = \theta_0 \equiv \arccos\sqrt{R/r_c}$. Since $\sqrt{\frac{r_c}{r} - 1} = \tan\theta$:
  $$\int_{R}^{r_c} \sqrt{\frac{r_c}{r} - 1}\,dr = 2 r_c \int_{0}^{\theta_0} \tan\theta\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta = 2 r_c \int_{0}^{\theta_0} \sin^2\theta\,d\theta = r_c\left(\theta_0 - \sin\theta_0\cos\theta_0\right)$$
  Substituting $\cos\theta_0 = \sqrt{R/r_c} = \sqrt{Q_\alpha / V_B}$ yields the **exact Gamow exponent**:
  $$G = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu Q_\alpha}}{\hbar}\,r_c\left[\arccos\sqrt{\frac{R}{r_c}} - \sqrt{\frac{R}{r_c}\left(1 - \frac{R}{r_c}\right)}\right]$$
  In the physical **thick-barrier regime** ($Q_\alpha \ll V_B \implies x \equiv \sqrt{R/r_c} \ll 1$), expand $\arccos x = \frac{\pi}{2} - x + \mathcal{O}(x^3)$ and $x\sqrt{1 - x^2} = x + \mathcal{O}(x^3)$, so the bracket simplifies cleanly to $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - 2\sqrt{\dfrac{R}{r_c}}$. Substituting $r_c = \dfrac{2 Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 Q_\alpha}$ into $2G$:
  $$\boxed{2G = \frac{2\pi Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{Q_\alpha}} - \frac{8}{\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{\mu Z_d e^2 R}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}} = \frac{4\pi Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar v_\alpha} - \frac{8}{\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{\mu Z_d e^2 R}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}}}$$
  Taking the natural logarithm of $\lambda = f\,e^{-2G}$ derives the **Geiger–Nuttall Law** directly from first principles:
  $$\boxed{\ln\lambda = \left[\ln\!\left(\frac{v_{\text{in}}}{2R}\right) + \frac{8}{\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{\mu Z_d e^2 R}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}}\right] - \frac{2\pi Z_d e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{Q_\alpha}} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \log_{10}\lambda = C_1(Z_d, R) - \frac{C_2 Z_d}{\sqrt{Q_\alpha}}}$$
- **(iii) Dimensional & Limiting-Case Sanity Checks:**
  - *Dimensions:* $\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar c} = \alpha_{\text{EM}} \approx \frac{1}{137}$ is dimensionless, and $c\sqrt{2\mu/Q_\alpha} = 2c/v_\alpha$ is dimensionless $\implies$ $2G$ is strictly dimensionless!
  - *Limiting Behavior:* As $Q_\alpha \to V_B$ ($R \to r_c$), $\theta_0 \to 0 \implies G \to 0 \implies T \to 1$ (free classical escape!). Because $Q_\alpha^{-1/2}$ sits inside a **huge negative exponential** ($2G \sim 60\text{–}90$), doubling $Q_\alpha$ slashes $2G$ by $\sim 55$, boosting $\lambda$ by $e^{55} \sim 10^{24}$!

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Alpha-Decay Kinematics & Gamow Tunneling Sensitivity of $^{226}_{88}\text{Ra}$
> **Problem:** $^{226}_{88}\text{Ra}$ decays to $^{222}_{86}\text{Rn}$ emitting $\alpha$-particles of kinetic energy $T_\alpha = 4.784\text{ MeV}$. (a) Find $Q_\alpha$ and the recoil kinetic energy of $^{222}\text{Rn}$. (b) Taking touching radius $R = 8.87\text{ fm}$ and reduced mass $\mu c^2 = 3660\text{ MeV}$, calculate the Gamow barrier factor $2G$ and transmission probability $T = e^{-2G}$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) $Q_\alpha$ & Daughter Recoil:**
> $$Q_\alpha = T_\alpha\frac{A}{A - 4} = 4.784 \times \frac{226}{222} = \mathbf{4.870\text{ MeV}}$$
> $$T_{\text{Rn}} = Q_\alpha - T_\alpha = 4.870 - 4.784 = \mathbf{0.086\text{ MeV}} = 86\text{ keV}$$
>
> **(b) Gamow Exponent $2G$ ($Z_d = 86$, $\alpha_{\text{EM}} = 1/137.036$, $\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 1.440\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}$, $\hbar c = 197.33\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}$):**
> - First term (Coulomb $Q_\alpha^{-1/2}$ term):
>   $$2\pi Z_d \alpha_{\text{EM}}\sqrt{\frac{2\mu c^2}{Q_\alpha}} = 2\pi(86)\left(\frac{1}{137.036}\right)\sqrt{\frac{7320}{4.870}} = 3.9432 \times 38.77 = \mathbf{152.88}$$
> - Second term (nuclear radius cutoff):
>   $$8\sqrt{\frac{\mu c^2 \cdot Z_d \cdot (1.440\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}) \cdot R}{(\hbar c)^2}} = 8\sqrt{\frac{3660 \times 86 \times 1.440 \times 8.87}{(197.33)^2}} = 8\sqrt{103.24} = \mathbf{81.29}$$
> - Net exponent & tunneling probability:
>   $$2G = 152.88 - 81.29 = 71.59 \implies T = e^{-71.59} = \mathbf{8.1 \times 10^{-32}}$$
> $$\boxed{Q_\alpha = 4.870\text{ MeV},\;\; T_{\text{Rn}} = 86\text{ keV} \quad\big|\quad 2G = 71.6,\;\; T \approx 8.1 \times 10^{-32}}$$

## 2. Beta Decay, Fermi Theory & Parity Violation in Weak Interactions

### 2.1 The Three Modes ($\beta^-, \beta^+$, EC) & Atomic-vs-Nuclear Mass Discipline

Beta decay is a weak-interaction isobaric transmutation ($A$ unchanged, $Z \to Z \pm 1$) that slides unstable nuclei down the isobaric mass parabola toward $Z_0$. Strict care is essential when converting nuclear masses $M_N(A, Z)$ into tabulated neutral **atomic masses** $M_A(A, Z) \approx M_N(A, Z) + Z m_e$:

1. **$\beta^-$ (Negatron) Decay:** ${}^A_Z X \to {}^A_{Z+1}Y + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$ (nucleon process: $n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$; occurs even for free neutrons with $t_{1/2} = 10.2\text{ min}$ since $m_n - m_p = 1.293\text{ MeV}/c^2 > m_e$).
   $$Q_{\beta^-} = \left[M_N(X) - M_N(Y) - m_e\right]c^2 = \left[(M_A(X) - Z m_e) - (M_A(Y) - (Z+1)m_e) - m_e\right]c^2$$
   $$\boxed{Q_{\beta^-} = \left[M_A(X) - M_A(Y)\right]c^2 > 0}$$
   *(All electron rest masses cancel identically!)*

2. **$\beta^+$ (Positron) Decay:** ${}^A_Z X \to {}^A_{Z-1}Y + e^+ + \nu_e$ (nucleon process: $p \to n + e^+ + \nu_e$; strictly forbidden for a free proton since $m_p < m_n$, allowed only inside proton-rich nuclei where daughter binding energy pays the deficit!).
   $$Q_{\beta^+} = \left[M_N(X) - M_N(Y) - m_e\right]c^2 = \left[(M_A(X) - Z m_e) - (M_A(Y) - (Z-1)m_e) - m_e\right]c^2$$
   $$\boxed{Q_{\beta^+} = \left[M_A(X) - M_A(Y) - 2 m_e\right]c^2 > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \left[M_A(X) - M_A(Y)\right]c^2 > 1.022\text{ MeV}}$$
   *(One $m_e$ is created as the emitted positron; the second $m_e$ is shed from the neutral parent's atomic shell when $Z$ drops by $1$—leaving an unavoidable **$-2m_e c^2 = -1.022\text{ MeV}$ threshold**!)*

3. **Orbital Electron Capture ($EC$):** ${}^A_Z X + e^-_K \to {}^A_{Z-1}Y + \nu_e$ (nucleon process: $p + e^- \to n + \nu_e$).
   $$Q_{EC} = \left[M_N(X) + m_e - M_N(Y)\right]c^2 - B_K = \left[(M_A(X) - Z m_e) + m_e - (M_A(Y) - (Z-1)m_e)\right]c^2 - B_K$$
   $$\boxed{Q_{EC} = \left[M_A(X) - M_A(Y)\right]c^2 - B_K > 0}$$
   where $B_K$ is the daughter K-shell binding energy (keV scale). Crucially, whenever $B_K < [M_A(X) - M_A(Y)]c^2 < 1.022\text{ MeV}$, **$\beta^+$ decay is strictly forbidden while $EC$ proceeds freely**, emitting a **monoenergetic neutrino** ($E_\nu = Q_{EC}$) plus characteristic daughter X-rays or Auger electrons!

### 2.2 Continuous $\beta$-Spectrum, Pauli's Neutrino & Fermi's Theory of Beta Decay

#### The Continuous Spectrum Paradox & Pauli's Neutrino Hypothesis (1930)
Unlike $\alpha$-rays, $\beta$-particles emerge with a **continuous kinetic energy distribution** spanning from $T_e = 0$ up to a sharp maximum **endpoint** $T_{\max} = Q_\beta$. A hypothetical two-body decay ($X \to Y + e^-$) violated three bedrock laws simultaneously:
1. **Energy conservation:** Electrons below $T_{\max}$ appeared to lose energy into thin air.
2. **Linear momentum conservation:** Cloud-chamber tracks of $Y$ and $e^-$ were **not collinear**!
3. **Angular momentum & Spin-statistics conservation:** In $n(\frac{1}{2}) \to p(\frac{1}{2}) + e^-(\frac{1}{2})$, a single fermion turned into two fermions (half-integer spin $\to$ integer spin)!

Wolfgang Pauli (1930) rescued all three conservation laws by postulating a third invisible particle—electrically neutral, spin-$\frac{1}{2}$, ultra-light, weakly interacting—named the **neutrino** ($\nu$) by Enrico Fermi (1934) and directly detected via inverse beta decay ($\bar{\nu}_e + p \to n + e^+$) near a nuclear reactor by Clyde Cowan and Frederick Reines (1956). Sharing $Q_\beta = T_e + E_\nu$ between electron and neutrino makes the continuous spectrum completely automatic!

#### Fermi's Theory (1934): Momentum Spectrum, Kurie Plot, $ft_{1/2}$ & Selection Rules

Enrico Fermi modeled $\beta$-decay as a weak four-fermion contact interaction of coupling strength $g_F$ ($\approx 1.4 \times 10^{-62}\text{ J}\cdot\text{m}^3$) and applied **Fermi's Golden Rule** to compute the transition probability into electron momentum range $(p_e, p_e + dp_e)$:

$$d\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\,g_F^2\,|M_{fi}|^2\,\frac{dn}{dE_0}$$

1. **Statistical Phase-Space Factor:** Inside normalization volume $V$, the number of states for electron momentum in $(p_e, p_e + dp_e)$ and neutrino momentum in $(p_\nu, p_\nu + dp_\nu)$ is $dn = \frac{4\pi V p_e^2 dp_e}{h^3}\cdot\frac{4\pi V p_\nu^2 dp_\nu}{h^3}$. For a massless neutrino ($E_\nu = p_\nu c = E_0 - E_e$, where $E_0 = T_{\max} + m_e c^2$ is total endpoint energy):
   $$p_\nu^2 \frac{dp_\nu}{dE_0} = \frac{(E_0 - E_e)^2}{c^3}$$
2. **Coulomb Distortion Factor $F(Z', E_e)$:** The emerging $\beta^-$ is pulled back by the daughter charge $+Z'e$ (decelerating it and **enhancing** low-energy electrons), whereas an emerging $\beta^+$ is accelerated outward (**suppressing** low-energy positrons). Multiplying by the **Fermi function** $F(Z', E_e) = |\psi_e(0)|^2 / |\psi_{\text{free}}(0)|^2$ yields **Fermi's Beta Momentum Spectrum**:
   $$\boxed{N(p_e)\,dp_e = \frac{g_F^2 |M_{fi}|^2}{2\pi^3 \hbar^7 c^3}\,F(Z', E_e)\,p_e^2\,(E_0 - E_e)^2\,dp_e}$$
3. **The Fermi–Kurie Plot & Direct Neutrino-Mass Test:**
   For allowed transitions ($|M_{fi}|$ independent of $p_e$), rearranging yields a strict linear function of $E_e$:
   $$\boxed{K(E_e) \equiv \sqrt{\frac{N(p_e)}{p_e^2\,F(Z', E_e)}} = \text{const}\cdot (E_0 - E_e)}$$
   Plotting $K(E_e)$ vs. $E_e$ (or $T_e$) produces a **straight line** intersecting the energy axis cleanly at $E_0$ if $m_\nu = 0$! If $m_\nu > 0$, the neutrino phase-space factor becomes $(E_0 - E_e)\sqrt{(E_0 - E_e)^2 - m_\nu^2 c^4}$, causing the **Kurie plot to bend vertically downward** and hit the axis prematurely at $E_0 - m_\nu c^2$ (the principle behind precision tritium $\beta$-endpoint measurements like KATRIN, bounding $m_\nu < 0.45\text{ eV}/c^2$!).
4. **Total Decay Rate, Sargent's Law & Comparative Half-Life ($ft_{1/2}$):**
   Integrating $N(p_e)\,dp_e$ from $p_e = 0$ to $p_{\max}$ defines the dimensionless **Fermi integral** $f(Z', E_0)$:
   $$\lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{t_{1/2}} = \frac{m_e^5 c^4 g_F^2 |M_{fi}|^2}{2\pi^3 \hbar^7}\,f(Z', E_0) \implies \boxed{f\,t_{1/2} = \frac{2\pi^3 \hbar^7 \ln 2}{m_e^5 c^4 g_F^2 |M_{fi}|^2} \propto \frac{1}{|M_{fi}|^2}}$$
   - For high-energy decays ($E_0 \gg m_e c^2$), $f(Z', E_0) \propto E_0^5$, immediately deriving **Sargent's Rule** ($\lambda \propto E_0^5$)!
   - Because $f$ removes all phase-space and Coulomb dependence, the **comparative half-life** ($\log_{10} ft$) isolates pure nuclear structure ($|M_{fi}|^2$), cleanly classifying beta decays by degree of forbiddenness:
     - **Superallowed** ($0^+ \to 0^+$ mirror/isobaric analog, e.g., $^{14}\text{O} \to {}^{14}\text{N}^*$): $\log_{10} ft \approx 3.0\text{–}3.7$.
     - **Allowed** ($l_\beta = 0$): $\log_{10} ft \approx 4\text{–}6$.
     - **First-forbidden** ($l_\beta = 1$): $\log_{10} ft \approx 6\text{–}9$; **Second-forbidden** ($l_\beta = 2$): $\log_{10} ft \approx 10\text{–}13$.

#### Beta-Decay Selection Rules (Allowed $l_\beta = 0 \implies \Delta\pi = \text{No}$)
- **Fermi (F) Transitions** (lepton spins antiparallel, $S_{e\nu} = 0$, vector current): **$\Delta I = 0,\quad \Delta\pi = \text{No}$** (permits **$0^+ \to 0^+$**!).
- **Gamow–Teller (GT) Transitions** (lepton spins parallel, $S_{e\nu} = 1$, axial-vector current): **$\Delta I = 0, \pm 1$ (NO $0 \to 0$!),\quad $\Delta\pi = \text{No}$** (e.g., $^{60}\text{Co}(5^+) \to {}^{60}\text{Ni}^*(4^+)$ is pure GT!).
- **Forbidden Transitions ($l_\beta = 1, 2, \dots$):** Each unit of orbital angular momentum carried by the lepton pair flips parity by $(-1)^{l_\beta}$ (so **first-forbidden** requires $\Delta\pi = \text{Yes}$ and $\Delta I = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2$) and suppresses the rate by $\sim (kR)^{2l_\beta} \sim 10^{-3}\text{–}10^{-4}$ per order!

### 2.3 Parity Violation in Weak Decays — $\theta\text{–}\tau$ Puzzle, C. S. Wu's $^{60}\text{Co}$ Experiment & Left-Handed Neutrinos

For thirty years after quantum mechanics was born, physicists treated mirror-reflection invariance (**Parity conservation**, $\mathbf{r} \to -\mathbf{r}$) as an unquestioned law of nature. Two discoveries shattered that assumption overnight:

1. **The $\theta\text{–}\tau$ Puzzle (1953–1956):** Cosmic-ray and accelerator data revealed two positively charged strange mesons—$\theta^+$ and $\tau^+$—with identical masses ($494\text{ MeV}/c^2$), identical spins ($0$), and identical weak lifetimes ($1.24 \times 10^{-8}\text{ s}$), yet opposite final-state parities:
   $$\theta^+ \longrightarrow \pi^+ + \pi^0 \;\; (\pi_{\text{final}} = (-1)(-1)(-1)^0 = +1) \qquad \text{vs.} \qquad \tau^+ \longrightarrow \pi^+ + \pi^+ + \pi^- \;\; (\pi_{\text{final}} = (-1)^3 = -1)$$
   If parity were strictly conserved, $\theta^+$ and $\tau^+$ had to be two miraculously mass-degenerate particles; if they were the **same particle ($K^+$)**, parity had to be violated!

2. **Lee–Yang Proposal (1956; Nobel Prize, 1957):** T. D. Lee and C. N. Yang audited the entire experimental literature and showed that while parity was rigorously proven in Strong and Electromagnetic interactions, **not a single experiment had ever tested parity in Weak interactions**! To detect parity violation, one must measure a **pseudoscalar observable**—specifically $\langle \mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{p}_e \rangle$ between the nuclear spin $\mathbf{J}$ (an **axial vector**: $\hat{P}\mathbf{J} = +\mathbf{J}$, like a current loop whose mirror image spins the same way!) and the emitted electron momentum $\mathbf{p}_e$ (a **polar vector**: $\hat{P}\mathbf{p}_e = -\mathbf{p}_e$):
   $$\hat{P}\left(\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{p}_e\right) = -\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{p}_e$$
   Under parity invariance, the angular intensity $I(\theta) = 1 + \alpha\dfrac{v}{c}\cos\theta$ ($\theta$ = angle between $\mathbf{J}$ and $\mathbf{p}_e$) MUST be strictly symmetric ($\alpha \equiv 0$, identical forward and backward electron counts)!

3. **C. S. Wu, Ambler, Hayward, Hoppes & Hudson's $^{60}\text{Co}$ Experiment (1957):**
   - **Nuclear Transition:** Pure Gamow–Teller decay of polarized Cobalt-60:
     $${}^{60}_{27}\text{Co}\,(5^+) \xrightarrow{\;\beta^-\;} {}^{60}_{28}\text{Ni}^*\,(4^+) + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e \xrightarrow{\;\gamma_1\;(1.17\text{ MeV})\;} 2^+ \xrightarrow{\;\gamma_2\;(1.33\text{ MeV})\;} 0^+$$
   - **Experimental Setup:** A thin crystalline surface layer of $^{60}\text{Co}$ was grown on a paramagnetic cerium magnesium nitrate (CMN) salt and cooled by **adiabatic demagnetization to $T \approx 0.01\text{ K}$** so thermal agitation ($k_B T$) could not scramble nuclear spin alignment. A small vertical solenoid magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ aligned the $^{60}\text{Co}$ spins along $\mathbf{B}$ via internal hyperfine fields. Two equatorial/polar NaI(Tl) counters monitored the $E2$ $\gamma$-ray anisotropy (confirming $>60\%$ spin polarization—since EM $\gamma$-decay conserves $P$, its radiation pattern depends only on $\cos^2\theta$!), while an anthracene crystal counted $\beta^-$ electrons emitted parallel ($\mathbf{B}$ up) vs. antiparallel ($\mathbf{B}$ down) to $\mathbf{J}$.
   - **Landmark Result:** As soon as the magnet turned on at $0.01\text{ K}$, a **massive forward–backward $\beta^-$ asymmetry** appeared ($\alpha \approx -1$ — **maximal parity violation**!): electrons were emitted **overwhelmingly OPPOSITE to the nuclear spin $\mathbf{J}$** ($\theta = 180^\circ$)! Reversing $\mathbf{B}$ flipped the asymmetry cleanly, and warming the crystal erased both $\gamma$-anisotropy and $\beta$-asymmetry simultaneously!

4. **Physical Meaning of "Neutrinos Are Left-Handed" (Helicity $h = -1$):**
   Define **helicity** $h$ as the projection of a particle's spin onto its direction of motion:
   $$\boxed{h = \frac{\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{p}}{|\mathbf{s}|\,|\mathbf{p}|} \in \{-1\;\text{(left-handed)},\; +1\;\text{(right-handed)}\}}$$
   In $^{60}\text{Co}(5^+, m = +5) \to {}^{60}\text{Ni}^*(4^+, m = +4) + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$, the nucleus loses $\Delta J_z = +1\hbar$ along $+\hat{z}$. By angular momentum conservation, **both** the electron spin ($\frac{1}{2}$) and the antineutrino spin ($\frac{1}{2}$) MUST point along $+\hat{z}$ (parallel to $\mathbf{J}$)! Because Wu's experiment proved that $e^-$ shoots preferentially along $-\hat{z}$ (while $\bar{\nu}_e$ recoils preferentially along $+\hat{z}$):
   - The emitted **electron ($e^-$)** has spin antiparallel to $\mathbf{p}$ (**left-handed**, helicity $\approx -v/c$).
   - The emitted **antineutrino ($\bar{\nu}_e$)** has spin **parallel** to $\mathbf{p}$ (**right-handed**, $h(\bar{\nu}_e) = +1$).
   - By $CP$ invariance—and directly verified in the brilliant **Goldhaber–Grodzins–Sunyar (1958)** $^{152}\text{Eu}$ electron-capture experiment—**all neutrinos ($\nu$) produced in weak interactions are strictly left-handed ($h = -1$)**, while **all antineutrinos ($\bar{\nu}$) are strictly right-handed ($h = +1$)**! Because spatial reflection $\hat{P}$ reverses $\mathbf{p}$ while leaving $\mathbf{s}$ fixed, $\hat{P}$ flips helicity ($h \to -h$), transforming a left-handed neutrino into a **right-handed neutrino** that does not couple to the weak $W^\pm$ boson—explaining from first principles why weak interactions violate $P$ (and $C$) maximally!

## 3. Gamma Decay, Internal Conversion & Mössbauer Spectroscopy

### 3.1 Gamma Multipole ($E_L, M_L$) Selection Rules & Nuclear Isomerism

Following $\alpha$- or $\beta$-decay (or a nuclear reaction), a daughter nucleus is frequently left in an excited state $E_i$ and de-excites to a lower state $E_f$ by emitting an electromagnetic photon of energy $E_\gamma = E_i - E_f - E_R \approx E_i - E_f$. Because electromagnetic fields conserve both angular momentum and parity strictly, the photon's multipole order $L$ and character (Electric $E_L$ vs. Magnetic $M_L$) obey exact selection rules:

1. **Angular Momentum Conservation (Triangle Rule):**
   $$\boxed{|I_i - I_f| \le L \le I_i + I_f \qquad (L = 1, 2, 3, \dots)}$$
   **Proof that $0 \to 0$ Single-Photon Emission Is Strictly Forbidden:** A real electromagnetic photon is a massless transverse spin-$1$ boson whose helicity is strictly $\pm 1$; it has **NO $L = 0$ (monopole) radiation field** (a spherically pulsating charge produces zero external radiation field by Gauss's law!). When $I_i = 0$ and $I_f = 0$, the triangle inequality forces $L = 0$, forbidding single-$\gamma$ emission absolutely! Thus $0^+ \to 0^+$ transitions (e.g., $^{16}\text{O}^*(6.05\text{ MeV}) \to 0^+$, $^{72}\text{Ge}^*(0.69\text{ MeV}) \to 0^+$) MUST proceed via **$E0$ Internal Conversion** or internal $e^+ e^-$ pair creation (if $\Delta E > 1.022\text{ MeV}$).
2. **Parity Conservation:**
   - **Electric Multipole ($E_L$):** $\pi(E_L) = (-1)^L \implies \pi_i \pi_f = (-1)^L$.
   - **Magnetic Multipole ($M_L$):** $\pi(M_L) = (-1)^{L+1} \implies \pi_i \pi_f = (-1)^{L+1}$.
3. **Weisskopf Rate Hierarchy & Nuclear Isomerism:**
   Single-particle Weisskopf transition rates plunge by a factor of $\sim (kR)^2 \sim 10^{-4}\text{–}10^{-6}$ for each unit increase in $L$, while $M_L$ is suppressed below $E_L$ by $\sim (\hbar / M_p c R)^2 \sim 10^{-2}$. Hence the **lowest allowed multipole dominates** (only $E2$ often competes with $M1$!). When an excited nuclear state lies at low energy ($\Delta E \sim 10\text{–}500\text{ keV}$) with a large spin mismatch ($\Delta I \ge 3$ or $4$, e.g., $M4$ or $E3$) to all lower levels, its $\gamma$-lifetime lengthens dramatically from $\sim 10^{-12}\text{ s}$ to milliseconds, hours, or even years—creating a metastable **nuclear isomer** (clustered in **islands of isomerism** just below magic $50, 82, 126$ where high-$j$ opposite-parity intruder shells $1g_{9/2}, 1h_{11/2}, 1i_{13/2}$ sit adjacent to $p_{1/2}, s_{1/2}$!).

#### Unified Alpha, Beta & Gamma Selection Rules Matrix

| Decay Mode | Interaction | Angular Momentum Rule | Parity Rule ($\Delta\pi = \pi_i\pi_f$) | Special Ban / Diagnostic Signature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alpha ($\alpha$)** | Strong + EM | $\|I_i - I_f\| \le l_\alpha \le I_i + I_f$ | $\Delta\pi = (-1)^{l_\alpha}$ | $0^+ \to 0^+$ allowed ($l_\alpha = 0$); $0^+ \to 0^-$ strictly forbidden! |
| **Beta — Fermi (F)** | Weak ($S_{e\nu}=0$) | $\Delta I = 0$ (allowed, $l_\beta = 0$) | $\Delta\pi = \text{No}$ ($+1$) | Allows superallowed $0^+ \to 0^+$ ($\log_{10} ft \approx 3.5$) |
| **Beta — Gamow–Teller (GT)** | Weak ($S_{e\nu}=1$) | $\Delta I = 0, \pm 1$ (allowed, $l_\beta = 0$) | $\Delta\pi = \text{No}$ ($+1$) | **$0 \to 0$ strictly forbidden** in pure GT! |

### 3.2 Internal Conversion (IC)

**Internal Conversion** is a direct, competing electromagnetic de-excitation process in which an excited nucleus transfers its transition energy $\Delta E$ directly—via the near-zone Coulomb/magnetic interaction—to one of its own orbital atomic electrons ($K, L, M, \dots$), ejecting that **conversion electron** with sharp discrete kinetic energy:

$$\boxed{T_e = \Delta E - B_i \qquad (i = K, L, M, \dots)}$$

where $B_i$ is the atomic binding energy of shell $i$. *(Critical Distinction: Internal conversion is **NOT** a two-step internal photoelectric effect in which a real $\gamma$-ray is emitted and later reabsorbed! That two-step picture is disproven decisively by **$0^+ \to 0^+$ transitions**, where single-photon $\gamma$-emission has zero probability yet $E0$ internal conversion proceeds cleanly via direct penetration of the atomic $s$-electron wavefunction $|\psi_s(0)|^2$ inside the nuclear volume!)*

- **Internal Conversion Coefficient ($\alpha$):** Defined as the ratio of the number of conversion electrons emitted ($N_e$) to the number of $\gamma$-photons emitted ($N_\gamma$) in the same nuclear transition:
  $$\boxed{\alpha = \frac{N_e}{N_\gamma} = \frac{\lambda_e}{\lambda_\gamma} = \alpha_K + \alpha_L + \alpha_M + \dots, \qquad \lambda_{\text{tot}} = \lambda_\gamma + \lambda_e = \lambda_\gamma(1 + \alpha)}$$
  Notice that internal conversion **shortens the observed half-life** of the nuclear level: $t_{1/2,\text{obs}} = \dfrac{t_{1/2,\gamma}}{1 + \alpha}$.
- **Exact Conditions Where Internal Conversion Dominates ($\alpha \gg 1$):**
  From non-relativistic perturbation theory for $K$-shell electric multipoles, $\alpha_K(E_L) \approx Z^3 \alpha_{\text{EM}}^4 \left(\dfrac{L}{L+1}\right)\left(\dfrac{2m_e c^2}{\Delta E}\right)^{L + 5/2}$, revealing four clear dominance criteria:
  1. **Heavy nuclei (Large $Z$):** $\alpha \propto Z^3$, because the inner $K$-shell Bohr radius shrinks as $1/Z$, concentrating electron density $|\psi_{1s}(0)|^2 \propto Z^3$ directly over the nucleus.
  2. **Low transition energy ($\Delta E$):** $\alpha \propto (\Delta E)^{-(L + 5/2)}$ rises explosively at low $\Delta E$ ($\lesssim 200\text{ keV}$).
  3. **High multipole order ($L$) / Nuclear Isomers:** Increasing $L$ suppresses far-field $\gamma$-radiation far more severely than near-field electron conversion, making $\alpha \gg 1$ for $M4, E3, E4$ isomeric transitions!
  4. **$0 \to 0$ transitions:** $\lambda_\gamma = 0 \implies \alpha = \infty$ (100% internal conversion when $\Delta E < 1.022\text{ MeV}$).
  5. **Shell Hierarchy:** Because $|\psi_{ns}(0)|^2 \propto 1/n^3$, $\alpha_K > \alpha_L > \alpha_M$ ($\sim 1 : \frac{1}{8} : \frac{1}{27}$) whenever $\Delta E > B_K$; measuring the $K/L$ ratio experimentally determines the exact multipolarity $L$ and parity of the nuclear transition!

### 3.3 The Mössbauer Effect & Spectroscopy (Recoilless Nuclear Resonance Absorption)

#### The Free-Nucleus Recoil Paradox
In atomic optics, resonance fluorescence occurs effortlessly because a $\sim 2\text{ eV}$ optical photon imparts negligible recoil to an atom ($E_R \sim 10^{-10}\text{ eV} \ll \Gamma_{\text{Doppler}} \sim 10^{-6}\text{ eV}$). For a nuclear $\gamma$-ray ($E_0 \sim 10\text{–}100\text{ keV}$), recoil ruins resonance completely!

When a free nucleus of mass $M$ at rest emits a $\gamma$-ray from excitation energy $E_0$, momentum conservation ($p_{\text{rec}} = p_\gamma \approx E_0 / c$) steals recoil kinetic energy:

$$\boxed{E_R = \frac{p_{\text{rec}}^2}{2M} = \frac{E_0^2}{2 M c^2}}$$

The emitted photon is therefore redshifted to $E_{\text{em}} = E_0 - E_R$, while an identical free absorbing nucleus demands a blueshifted photon $E_{\text{abs}} = E_0 + E_R$ to fund its own absorption recoil—creating a total gap of **$2 E_R$**! For the workhorse Mössbauer isotope **$^{57}\text{Fe}$** ($E_0 = 14.4\text{ keV}$, mean lifetime $\tau = 141\text{ ns}$):
- **Natural Linewidth:** $\Gamma = \dfrac{\hbar}{\tau} = \dfrac{6.582 \times 10^{-16}\text{ eV}\cdot\text{s}}{1.41 \times 10^{-7}\text{ s}} = \mathbf{4.67 \times 10^{-9}\text{ eV}}$ (an astonishing fractional sharpness $\Gamma / E_0 = \mathbf{3.2 \times 10^{-13}}$!).
- **Free-Nucleus Recoil:** $E_R = \dfrac{(14.4 \times 10^3\text{ eV})^2}{2(57 \times 931.5 \times 10^6\text{ eV})} = \mathbf{1.95 \times 10^{-3}\text{ eV}}$.
- **Mismatch Ratio:** $2 E_R / \Gamma \approx \mathbf{8.4 \times 10^5}$! The emission and absorption lines miss each other by nearly **a million linewidths**!

#### Mössbauer's Zero-Phonon Lattice Solution (1958; Nobel Prize, 1961)
Rudolf Mössbauer discovered that locking both emitting and absorbing nuclei tightly inside a **cold crystalline solid** restores resonance dramatically. In a crystal, vibrational energy is quantized into **phonons** of characteristic Debye energy $k_B \Theta_D \sim 0.03\text{–}0.04\text{ eV} \gg E_R \approx 0.002\text{ eV}$. Because the nucleus cannot excite a fraction of a phonon, a finite **recoilless (zero-phonon) fraction $f$** (governed by the **Debye–Waller factor**) emits and absorbs photons **without creating any lattice phonon**:

$$\boxed{f = \exp\!\left(-k_\gamma^2 \langle x^2 \rangle\right) = \exp\!\left\{-\frac{3 E_R}{2 k_B \Theta_D}\left[1 + \frac{2\pi^2}{3}\left(\frac{T}{\Theta_D}\right)^2\right]\right\} \quad (T \ll \Theta_D)}$$

In a zero-phonon transition, the recoil momentum is taken up coherently by the **entire macroscopic crystal** ($M_{\text{crystal}} \sim 10^{23} M \to \infty$), driving $E_R = \dfrac{E_0^2}{2 M_{\text{crystal}} c^2} \to 0 \ll \Gamma$! Both emission and absorption lines snap back onto $E_0$ with ultra-sharp Lorentzian natural width $\Gamma \sim 10^{-9}\text{ eV}$!
*(Optimal conditions for large $f$: low $\gamma$-energy $E_0 \lesssim 100\text{ keV}$, high Debye temperature $\Theta_D$ [stiff crystal lattice], and low sample temperature $T$.)*

#### Doppler Modulation & Landmark Applications
Because $\Gamma / E_0 \approx 3.2 \times 10^{-13}$ in $^{57}\text{Fe}$, moving the radioactive source ($^{57}\text{Co} \xrightarrow{EC} {}^{57}\text{Fe}^*$) relative to the absorber at a minuscule **Doppler velocity** $v$ shifts photon energy by $\Delta E = \dfrac{v}{c} E_0$: sweeping across one full linewidth $\Gamma$ takes only $v = c\dfrac{\Gamma}{E_0} \approx \mathbf{0.1\text{ mm/s}}$! Plotting transmitted $\gamma$-intensity versus Doppler velocity $v$ ($\text{mm/s}$) unlocks four extraordinary physical probes:

1. **Isomer (Chemical) Shift ($\delta$):** Because the nuclear charge radius differs slightly between excited ($R_e$) and ground ($R_g$) states, electrostatic overlap with atomic $s$-electron density $|\psi_s(0)|^2$ shifts the absorption dip by $\delta \propto \left(|\psi_s(0)|^2_{\text{abs}} - |\psi_s(0)|^2_{\text{src}}\right)(R_e^2 - R_g^2)$, directly distinguishing chemical oxidation states (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ vs. $\text{Fe}^{3+}$) and covalency.
2. **Electric Quadrupole Splitting ($\Delta E_Q$):** In $^{57}\text{Fe}$, the excited state ($I_e = \frac{3}{2}$, $Q \neq 0$) interacts with an asymmetric crystal **electric field gradient (EFG)** $V_{zz}$, splitting into $m_I = \pm\frac{3}{2}$ and $m_I = \pm\frac{1}{2}$—turning the single dip into a **symmetric doublet** probing local lattice symmetry.
3. **Magnetic Hyperfine (Zeeman) Splitting:** Inside ferromagnetic $\alpha$-iron ($B_{\text{int}} \approx 33\text{ T}$), nuclear Zeeman interaction splits $I_g = \frac{1}{2}$ into $2$ sublevels and $I_e = \frac{3}{2}$ into $4$ sublevels. Allowed magnetic-dipole ($M1$, $\Delta m = 0, \pm 1$) transitions produce the famous **6-line Mössbauer sextet**, measuring internal atomic magnetic fields and nuclear magnetic moments of excited states!
4. **Pound–Rebka Gravitational Redshift Test (1960):** Placed a $^{57}\text{Fe}$ source and absorber at the top and bottom of a **$H = 22.5\text{ m}$** tower at Harvard. General Relativity predicts a fractional frequency shift $\dfrac{\Delta \nu}{\nu} = \dfrac{g H}{c^2} = \dfrac{9.81 \times 22.5}{(3.0 \times 10^8)^2} = \mathbf{2.45 \times 10^{-15}}$—compensated by an ultra-slow Doppler speed $v = g H / c = 0.74\,\mu\text{m/s}$, confirming Einstein's equivalence principle to $\sim 1\%$ precision in a terrestrial laboratory!

## 4. Nuclear Reactions — Lab $Q$-Value Equation, Thresholds & Compound-Nucleus Resonance

### 4.1 Exact Non-Relativistic Lab $Q$-Value Equation & Endoergic Threshold Energy

Consider a general two-body nuclear reaction $a + X \to b + Y$ (short-hand $X(a, b)Y$), where a projectile $a$ of kinetic energy $T_a$ strikes a stationary target nucleus $X$ ($T_X = 0$) in the Lab frame, ejecting a light particle $b$ at Lab angle $\theta$ with kinetic energy $T_b$, while the heavy recoil nucleus $Y$ recoils at unmeasured angle $\phi$ with kinetic energy $T_Y$.

#### Unabridged Derivation of the $Q$-Value Equation
- **(i) Conservation Laws:** Define the reaction **$Q$-value** (rest-mass energy converted into kinetic energy; electron masses cancel automatically whenever neutral atomic masses are used on both sides):
  $$Q \equiv \left(m_a + m_X - m_b - m_Y\right)c^2 = T_b + T_Y - T_a \implies T_Y = Q + T_a - T_b$$
  - $Q > 0$: **Exoergic** (exothermic, releases kinetic energy; can occur even at $T_a \to 0$ for neutral projectiles).
  - $Q < 0$: **Endoergic** (endothermic, absorbs kinetic energy into rest mass; strictly requires $T_a \ge T_{\text{th}}$).
- **(ii) Eliminating the Unmeasured Recoil $Y$ ($T_Y, \phi$):**
  Linear momentum conservation along ($x$) and perpendicular to ($y$) the incident beam gives:
  $$p_Y \cos\phi = p_a - p_b\cos\theta, \qquad p_Y \sin\phi = p_b\sin\theta$$
  Squaring and adding eliminates $\phi$:
  $$p_Y^2 = p_a^2 + p_b^2 - 2 p_a p_b \cos\theta$$
  Substituting non-relativistic kinetic energies $p_i^2 = 2 m_i T_i$ ($T_Y = p_Y^2 / 2m_Y$):
  $$T_Y = \frac{m_a}{m_Y} T_a + \frac{m_b}{m_Y} T_b - \frac{2\sqrt{m_a m_b T_a T_b}}{m_Y}\cos\theta$$
- **(iii) Equating Both Expressions for $T_Y$:**
  $$Q + T_a - T_b = \frac{m_a}{m_Y} T_a + \frac{m_b}{m_Y} T_b - \frac{2\sqrt{m_a m_b T_a T_b}}{m_Y}\cos\theta$$
  Solving cleanly for $Q$ yields the **Exact Non-Relativistic Lab $Q$-Value Equation**:
  $$\boxed{Q = T_b\left(1 + \frac{m_b}{m_Y}\right) - T_a\left(1 - \frac{m_a}{m_Y}\right) - \frac{2\sqrt{m_a m_b T_a T_b}}{m_Y}\cos\theta}$$
- **(iv) Limiting Checks:** (1) At $\theta = 90^\circ$, $\cos\theta = 0$ kills the interference term; (2) For elastic scattering ($Q = 0, m_a = m_b, m_X = m_Y$) at $\theta = 180^\circ$, solving gives $T_b = T_a\left(\frac{m_X - m_a}{m_X + m_a}\right)^2$, the exact textbook elastic back-scattering rebound!

#### Endoergic Threshold Energy ($T_{\text{th}}$ for $Q < 0$)
A classic trap is assuming $T_{\text{th}} = |Q|$. In the Lab frame, **linear momentum conservation forbids the final products $(b, Y)$ from being created at rest**—the center of mass MUST keep moving with velocity $V_{\text{CM}} = \dfrac{m_a v_a}{m_a + m_X}$, carrying away unavailable kinetic energy $T_{\text{CM}} = \dfrac{m_a}{m_a + m_X} T_a$! Only the **CM relative kinetic energy** $T_{\text{rel}} = T_a - T_{\text{CM}} = \dfrac{m_X}{m_a + m_X} T_a$ is available to pay the rest-mass deficit $|Q| = -Q$. Setting $T_{\text{rel}} = -Q$ yields the **Lab Threshold Kinetic Energy**:

$$\frac{m_X}{m_a + m_X}\,T_{\text{th}} = -Q \implies \boxed{T_{\text{th}} = -Q\left(1 + \frac{m_a}{m_X}\right) = |Q|\left(\frac{m_a + m_X}{m_X}\right)}$$

*(Limiting Check: As target mass $m_X \to \infty$ [immovable wall], $T_{\text{CM}} \to 0$ and $T_{\text{th}} \to |Q|$. Note also that for charged projectiles $a$ with $Z_a, Z_X > 0$, overcoming electrostatic repulsion further requires $T_a$ near the Coulomb barrier $V_C \approx \frac{Z_a Z_X e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(R_a + R_X)}$ unless tunneling occurs.)*

### 4.2 Direct Reactions vs. Bohr's Compound Nucleus & Breit–Wigner $1/v$ Law

| Feature | Direct Reactions (Peripheral / Fast) | Compound Nucleus Reactions (Niels Bohr, 1936) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reaction Mechanism** | Single-step grazing transfer: $a + X \to b + Y$ (e.g., $(d, p)$ stripping, $(p, d)$ pickup) | Two-step thermalization: $a + X \to C^* \to b + Y$ |
| **Interaction Timescale** | Nuclear transit time $\tau \sim \frac{2R}{v} \sim \mathbf{10^{-22}\text{ s}}$ | Long metastable lifetime $\tau \sim \mathbf{10^{-16}\text{–}10^{-14}\text{ s}}$ ($10^6\times$ longer!) |
| **Energy Dependence** | Smooth, slowly varying cross-section (high $E_a \gtrsim 15\text{ MeV}$) | Sharp, closely spaced **resonance peaks** (low $E_a \lesssim 10\text{ MeV}$) |
| **CM Angular Distribution** | Strongly **forward-peaked** ($\theta \approx 0^\circ$, diffraction-like) | **Isotropic** or strictly symmetric about $\theta_{\text{CM}} = 90^\circ$ (memory erased!) |
| **Key Physical Proof** | Probes single-particle shell-model orbitals directly | **Bohr's Independence Hypothesis** (Ghoshal 1950: $^{64}\text{Zn}^*$ formed via $p + {}^{63}\text{Cu}$ or $\alpha + {}^{60}\text{Ni}$ decays identically!) |

#### Breit–Wigner Single-Level Resonance Formula & Proof of the Slow-Neutron $1/v$ Law
When an incident projectile $a$ matches a quasi-stationary discrete energy level $E_r$ of the compound nucleus $C^*$ (total width $\Gamma = \hbar/\tau = \sum_i \Gamma_i$, where $\Gamma_a, \Gamma_b$ are partial decay widths into entrance channel $a$ and exit channel $b$), the reaction cross-section exhibits a Lorentzian **Breit–Wigner resonance peak**:

$$\boxed{\sigma(a \to b) = \pi \lambdabar^2 \, g_J \, \frac{\Gamma_a \Gamma_b}{(E - E_r)^2 + (\Gamma/2)^2}}$$

where $\lambdabar = \hbar / p = \hbar / (\mu v)$ is the reduced de Broglie wavelength and $g_J = \dfrac{2J + 1}{(2s_a + 1)(2I_X + 1)}$ is the spin statistical weight.

- **Exact Proof of the Slow-Neutron $1/v$ Absorption Law:**
  Consider low-energy $s$-wave ($l = 0$) neutron capture $(n, \gamma)$ far below the lowest resonance ($E \ll E_r$, typical for thermal neutrons $E \approx 0.025\text{ eV}$):
  1. Denominator: $(E - E_r)^2 + (\Gamma/2)^2 \approx E_r^2 + (\Gamma/2)^2 \approx \text{const}$.
  2. Exit radiative width $\Gamma_\gamma$: depends on MeV-scale nuclear excitation energy ($E + S_n \approx S_n \approx 6\text{–}8\text{ MeV}$), hence $\Gamma_\gamma \approx \text{const}$.
  3. Entrance neutron width $\Gamma_n$: by detailed balance / phase-space velocity scaling for $l = 0$, $\Gamma_n \propto v$.
  4. Wavelength factor: $\lambdabar^2 = \left(\dfrac{\hbar}{m_n v}\right)^2 \propto \dfrac{1}{v^2}$.
  Multiplying all factors gives:
  $$\sigma_{n,\gamma}(v) \propto \frac{1}{v^2}\cdot v \cdot \text{const} = \boxed{\frac{\text{const}}{v}}$$
  *(Physical Consequence: Slowing $2\text{ MeV}$ fast fission neutrons down to thermal speeds $v_{\text{th}} \approx 2200\text{ m/s}$ multiplies the fission cross-section of $^{235}\text{U}$ by **~450-fold**, from $1.3\text{ b}$ to $\mathbf{585\text{ b}}$!)*

## 5. Nuclear Fission & Physics of Nuclear Reactors

### 5.1 Bohr–Wheeler Fission Dynamics, Thermal $^{235}\text{U}$ vs. Fast $^{238}\text{U}$ & Delayed Neutrons

When a heavy actinide absorbs a neutron, the compound nucleus oscillates like a charged liquid drop (Bohr & Wheeler, 1939). As derived in Chapter 2, surface tension resists elongation ($\Delta E_S \propto +\alpha_2^2 a_S A^{2/3}$) while Coulomb repulsion accelerates it ($\Delta E_C \propto -\alpha_2^2 a_C Z^2 A^{-1/3}$), defining the critical zero-barrier limit:

$$\boxed{\left(\frac{Z^2}{A}\right)_{\text{crit}} = \frac{2 a_S}{a_C} \approx 47.8, \qquad \text{Fissility parameter } x \equiv \frac{Z^2/A}{47.8}}$$

For actinides ($x \approx 0.74\text{–}0.78 < 1$), the nucleus must climb a **fission activation barrier** $E_f \approx 5.5\text{–}6.5\text{ MeV}$ to reach the saddle point before snapping asymmetrically into two neutron-rich fragments (typically $A_L \approx 95$, $A_H \approx 140$, governed by shell closures around $Z = 50, N = 82$) plus $\bar{\nu} \approx 2.43$ prompt fast neutrons ($\bar{E}_n \approx 2\text{ MeV}$) and $\approx \mathbf{200\text{ MeV}}$ total energy ($\sim 168\text{ MeV}$ fragment KE, $\sim 5\text{ MeV}$ prompt $n$, $\sim 7\text{ MeV}$ prompt $\gamma$, $\sim 13\text{ MeV}$ delayed $\beta/\gamma$, $\sim 12\text{ MeV}$ escaping $\bar{\nu}_e$).

#### Why Thermal Neutrons Fission $^{235}\text{U}$ (Fissile) but NOT $^{238}\text{U}$ (Fissionable / Fertile)
This stark contrast is a direct, dramatic triumph of the **SEMF pairing energy term ($\pm\delta$)**!
- **$^{235}_{92}\text{U} + n_{\text{th}} \to {}^{236}_{92}\text{U}^*$ (Even-Even!):** Capturing a zero-KE thermal neutron pairs the 144th neutron, adding $+2\delta$ extra binding energy. The delivered excitation energy $E^* = S_n({}^{236}\text{U}) = \mathbf{6.55\text{ MeV}}$ **exceeds** the fission barrier $E_f({}^{236}\text{U}) \approx \mathbf{5.7\text{ MeV}}$! Hence $^{235}\text{U}$ (and similarly odd-$N$ $^{239}_{94}\text{Pu}$ and $^{233}_{92}\text{U}$) fissions readily with zero-energy thermal neutrons ($\sigma_f \approx 585\text{ b}$)!
- **$^{238}_{92}\text{U} + n_{\text{th}} \to {}^{239}_{92}\text{U}^*$ (Even-Odd!):** Capturing a neutron creates an unpaired 147th neutron (zero pairing bonus!), delivering only $E^* = S_n({}^{239}\text{U}) = \mathbf{4.81\text{ MeV}}$, which falls **$1.4\text{ MeV}$ BELOW** its fission barrier $E_f({}^{239}\text{U}) \approx \mathbf{6.2\text{ MeV}}$! Thus $^{238}\text{U}$ can fission **only with fast neutrons ($T_n \gtrsim 1.4\text{ MeV}$)**; with slower neutrons it undergoes radiative capture to **breed fissile $^{239}\text{Pu}$**:
  $${}^{238}_{92}\text{U} + n \longrightarrow {}^{239}_{92}\text{U} \xrightarrow[23.5\text{ min}]{\;\beta^-\;} {}^{239}_{93}\text{Np} \xrightarrow[2.36\text{ d}]{\;\beta^-\;} {}^{239}_{94}\text{Pu} \;\; (t_{1/2} = 24,110\text{ yr})$$

#### Role of Delayed Neutrons ($\beta_{\text{del}} \approx 0.65\%$) in Reactor Control
Over $99.35\%$ of fission neutrons are **prompt** (emitted within $10^{-14}\text{ s}$), completing one thermal reactor generation in $l_p \sim 10^{-4}\text{–}10^{-3}\text{ s}$. If a reactor ran solely on prompt neutrons with a tiny supercriticality $\Delta k = +0.001$, neutron population would grow as $e^{(\Delta k / l_p) t} \sim e^{10 t}$—surging $22,000\times$ in $1\text{ second}$, far too fast for any mechanical safety rod! Fortunately, **$\beta_{\text{del}} \approx 0.65\%$** of neutrons are **delayed** (average delay $\bar{\tau}_d \approx 12.5\text{ s}$, spanning $0.2\text{ s}$ to $55.6\text{ s}$), emitted immediately after $\beta^-$-decay of neutron-rich fission precursors (e.g., $^{87}\text{Br} \xrightarrow[55.6\text{ s}]{\beta^-} {}^{87}\text{Kr}^* \to {}^{86}\text{Kr} + n$). Keeping the reactor strictly below **prompt criticality** ($1 < k_{\text{eff}} < 1 + \beta_{\text{del}} = 1.0065$) stretches the effective generation time to:

$$\bar{l}_{\text{eff}} = (1 - \beta_{\text{del}})\,l_p + \beta_{\text{del}}\,\bar{\tau}_d \approx 0.0005 + 0.0065(12.5) \approx \mathbf{0.08\text{ s}}$$

stretching the reactor power period from milliseconds to **minutes** and allowing $\text{Cd/B/Hf}$ control rods to regulate power smoothly and safely!

### 5.2 Physics of Nuclear Reactors — Four-Factor Formula, Buckling Criticality, Moderation & Breeders

#### 1. Infinite Multiplication Factor — The Four-Factor Formula ($k_\infty$)
Consider an infinite reactor core (zero surface leakage) and track $N_0$ thermal neutrons absorbed in the fuel through one full life cycle:

1. **Thermal Fission / Reproduction Factor ($\eta$):** Average number of fast fission neutrons born per thermal neutron absorbed in fuel (fission + parasitic fuel capture):
   $$\eta = \bar{\nu}\frac{\Sigma_f}{\Sigma_f + \Sigma_c^{\text{fuel}}} \qquad (\eta \approx 2.07\text{ for pure }{}^{235}\text{U};\;\; \eta \approx 1.33\text{ for natural U})$$
   After fuel absorption: $N_0 \eta$ fast neutrons.
2. **Fast Fission Factor ($\epsilon \approx 1.03$):** Ratio of total fast neutrons (including bonus fast fissions induced in $^{238}\text{U}$ above $1.4\text{ MeV}$) to thermal-fission fast neutrons $\implies N_0 \eta \epsilon$.
3. **Resonance Escape Probability ($p \approx 0.75\text{–}0.90$):** Fraction of fast neutrons that survive slowing down through the treacherous epithermal ($6\text{–}100\text{ eV}$) giant $(n,\gamma)$ capture resonances of $^{238}\text{U}$ $\implies N_0 \eta \epsilon p$ thermal neutrons. *(Crucial Engineering Insight: Lumping uranium into discrete fuel rods separated by moderator—a **heterogeneous lattice**—dramatically raises $p$ because neutrons slow down safely inside the moderator away from $^{238}\text{U}$ and only re-enter fuel once thermalized!)*
4. **Thermal Utilization Factor ($f \approx 0.80\text{–}0.90$):** Fraction of surviving thermal neutrons absorbed in fuel rather than lost to moderator/coolant/structural capture:
   $$f = \frac{\Sigma_a^{\text{fuel}}}{\Sigma_a^{\text{fuel}} + \Sigma_a^{\text{mod}} + \Sigma_a^{\text{struct}}}$$

After one generation, $N_1 = N_0 \eta \epsilon p f$ thermal neutrons are absorbed in fuel, giving the **Four-Factor Formula**:

$$\boxed{k_\infty \equiv \frac{N_1}{N_0} = \eta\,\epsilon\,p\,f}$$

#### 2. Finite Reactor Criticality — Six-Factor Formula, Diffusion Equation & Geometrical Buckling
In any real finite reactor, neutrons leak out through the outer boundary during both slowing down (fast non-leakage probability $P_F = e^{-B_g^2 \tau_F}$, where $\tau_F$ is Fermi age) and thermal diffusion (thermal non-leakage probability $P_T = \dfrac{1}{1 + L^2 B_g^2}$, where $L$ is thermal diffusion length). Defining the **effective multiplication factor** $k_{\text{eff}}$:

$$\boxed{k_{\text{eff}} = k_\infty\,P_F\,P_T = \frac{k_\infty\,e^{-B_g^2 \tau_F}}{1 + L^2 B_g^2} \approx \frac{k_\infty}{1 + M^2 B_g^2}, \qquad M^2 \equiv L^2 + \tau_F \;\text{ (migration area)}}$$

- **$k_{\text{eff}} < 1$:** Subcritical (chain reaction dies exponentially).
- **$k_{\text{eff}} = 1$:** **Critical** (steady-state self-sustained chain reaction!).
- **$k_{\text{eff}} > 1$:** Supercritical (power rises exponentially).

From steady-state one-group neutron diffusion theory ($D\nabla^2\phi - \Sigma_a\phi + k_\infty \Sigma_a\phi = 0$, with $L^2 = D/\Sigma_a$ generalized to $M^2$), the neutron flux satisfies the Helmholtz **Reactor Equation**:

$$\boxed{\nabla^2 \phi(\mathbf{r}) + B^2 \phi(\mathbf{r}) = 0, \qquad B_m^2 = \frac{k_\infty - 1}{M^2} \;\; (\text{Material Buckling})}$$

Criticality ($k_{\text{eff}} = 1$) occurs precisely when the **Geometrical Buckling $B_g^2$** (fixed by reactor shape and extrapolated dimensions where $\phi = 0$) equals the **Material Buckling $B_m^2$** (fixed by fuel/moderator composition): **$B_g^2 = B_m^2$**!

| Bare Reactor Geometry | Flux Profile $\phi(\mathbf{r})$ | Geometrical Buckling $B_g^2$ | Critical Dimension & Minimum Volume |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Sphere** (radius $R$) | $A\dfrac{\sin(\pi r/R)}{r}$ | $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{R}\right)^2$ | $R_c = \dfrac{\pi}{B_m}$; $\;\; V_c = \dfrac{130}{B_m^3}$ (**minimum leakage!**) |
| **Cube** (side $a$) | $A\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{a}\sin\dfrac{\pi y}{a}\sin\dfrac{\pi z}{a}$ | $3\left(\dfrac{\pi}{a}\right)^2$ | $a_c = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,\pi}{B_m}$; $\;\; V_c = \dfrac{161}{B_m^3}$ |
| **Cylinder** (radius $R$, height $H$) | $A\,J_0\!\left(\dfrac{2.405\,r}{R}\right)\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi z}{H}\right)$ | $\left(\dfrac{2.405}{R}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{\pi}{H}\right)^2$ | Optimal $H = 1.847\,R$; $\;\; V_c = \dfrac{148}{B_m^3}$ |

*(Adding a surrounding neutron **reflector**—such as graphite, $\text{Be}$, or $\text{D}_2\text{O}$—bounces escaping neutrons back into the core, reducing the required critical size and fuel mass significantly!)*

#### 3. Neutron Moderation Physics & Thermal vs. Fast Breeder Reactors
In elastic scattering of a neutron ($m_n = 1$) off a stationary moderator nucleus of mass number $A$, the minimum energy after a head-on collision is $E_{\min} = \alpha_{\text{col}} E_i$ where $\alpha_{\text{col}} = \left(\dfrac{A - 1}{A + 1}\right)^2$, giving the **average logarithmic energy decrement per collision**:

$$\xi = \left\langle \ln\frac{E_i}{E_f} \right\rangle = 1 + \frac{\alpha_{\text{col}}}{1 - \alpha_{\text{col}}}\ln\alpha_{\text{col}} \approx \frac{2}{A + 2/3}$$

Thermalizing a $2\text{ MeV}$ neutron down to $0.025\text{ eV}$ ($\ln(2\times 10^6 / 0.025) \approx 18.2$) requires $N_{\text{col}} = 18.2 / \xi$ collisions. A top-tier moderator maximizes **moderating ratio** $\dfrac{\xi \Sigma_s}{\Sigma_a}$ (low $A$ + near-zero absorption $\sigma_a$):
- **Light Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$):** Fewest collisions ($\sim 18$), compact, cheap, but H absorbs thermal neutrons ($\sigma_a = 0.33\text{ b} \implies$ requires $3\text{–}5\%$ enriched $^{235}\text{U}$, as in PWR/BWR).
- **Heavy Water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$):** Only $\sim 25$ collisions AND tiny absorption ($\sigma_a \approx 0.001\text{ b} \implies$ achieves $k_\infty > 1$ with **unenriched natural uranium**, as in India's PHWR fleet!).
- **Graphite ($\text{C}$):** $\sim 114$ collisions, low absorption, high-temperature stability (larger core volume).

**Thermal Reactors vs. Fast Breeder Reactors (FBR):**
- **Thermal Reactors:** Rely on a moderator to exploit $^{235}\text{U}$'s huge thermal $\sigma_f = 585\text{ b}$, burning mainly the rare $0.72\%$ $^{235}\text{U}$ isotope (Conversion Ratio $< 1$, typically $0.5\text{–}0.8$).
- **Fast Breeder Reactors (FBR):** Eliminate the moderator completely! Cool with **liquid sodium ($\text{Na}$)** ($A = 23$, minimal moderation, exceptional heat transfer) around a high-enrichment $^{239}\text{Pu}/^{235}\text{U}$ core wrapped in a fertile **$^{238}\text{U}$ (or $^{232}\text{Th}$)** blanket. At fast neutron energies ($\sim 0.1\text{–}1\text{ MeV}$), the reproduction factor of $^{239}\text{Pu}$ leaps to **$\eta \approx 2.7\text{–}2.9 \gg 2$**: 1 neutron sustains the chain reaction while $> 1$ excess neutron transmutes fertile $^{238}\text{U} \to {}^{239}\text{Pu}$ (or $^{232}\text{Th} \to {}^{233}\text{U}$), achieving **Breeding Ratio $> 1$**—producing MORE fissile fuel than it consumes and multiplying usable nuclear fuel reserves by $\sim 60\times$!

## 6. Nuclear Fusion, Gamow Window, Lawson Criterion & Stellar Energy Production

### 6.1 Coulomb–Gamow Tunneling Window & Terrestrial Lawson Criterion

Fusing two light nuclei (e.g., $p + p$ at $r \approx 2\text{ fm}$) faces a classical Coulomb repulsion barrier $V_C = \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r} \approx \mathbf{0.72\text{ MeV}}$, corresponding to a classical ignition temperature $T \sim V_C / k_B \sim 5 \times 10^9\text{ K}$. Yet the core of the Sun shines steadily at only $T_c \approx 1.5 \times 10^7\text{ K}$ ($k_B T_c \approx \mathbf{1.3\text{ keV}} \ll 720\text{ keV}$)!

Thermonuclear fusion succeeds exclusively through **quantum tunneling inside the Gamow Window**—the narrow overlap product of two steeply opposing exponentials:
1. **Maxwell–Boltzmann High-Energy Tail:** $f(E) \propto \exp\!\left(-\dfrac{E}{k_B T}\right)$ (plunges rapidly as $E$ rises).
2. **Gamow Barrier Penetration Factor:** $P(E) \propto \exp\!\left(-\sqrt{\dfrac{E_G}{E}}\right)$, where $E_G = 2\mu c^2(\pi\alpha_{\text{EM}} Z_1 Z_2)^2$ is the **Gamow energy** ($E_G = 493\text{ keV}$ for $p\text{-}p$).

Maximizing the reaction-rate integrand $\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{E}{k_B T} - \sqrt{\dfrac{E_G}{E}}\right)$ via $\dfrac{d}{dE}\left(\dfrac{E}{k_B T} + E_G^{1/2} E^{-1/2}\right) = 0$ locates the **Gamow Peak Energy $E_0$**:

$$\frac{1}{k_B T} - \frac{1}{2} E_G^{1/2} E_0^{-3/2} = 0 \implies \boxed{E_0 = \left[\frac{E_G (k_B T)^2}{4}\right]^{1/3}}$$

*Sanity Check for the Sun ($k_B T_c = 1.3\text{ keV}, E_G = 493\text{ keV}$):* $E_0 = \left[\frac{493 \times 1.69}{4}\right]^{1/3} = \mathbf{5.93\text{ keV}}$—roughly $4.6\,k_B T_c$ (far out in the Boltzmann tail, yet still $120\times$ below the classical barrier!).

#### Controlled Terrestrial Fusion & Lawson's Criterion ($D\text{-}T$)
Because $p\text{-}p$ fusion relies on an ultra-slow weak interaction, terrestrial fusion reactors exploit the strong-interaction **Deuterium–Tritium ($D\text{-}T$)** resonance reaction:

$${}^2_1\text{H} + {}^3_1\text{H} \longrightarrow {}^4_2\text{He}\;(3.5\text{ MeV}) + {}^1_0\text{n}\;(14.1\text{ MeV}) + \mathbf{17.6\text{ MeV}}$$

(with tritium bred in a lithium blanket via ${}^6_3\text{Li}(n, \alpha){}^3_1\text{H}$!). For a fusion plasma of ion density $n$, temperature $T$ ($k_B T \sim 10\text{–}20\text{ keV} \approx 1.5 \times 10^8\text{ K}$), and energy confinement time $\tau_E$ to generate net power exceeding Bremsstrahlung radiation and thermal transport losses, it must satisfy **Lawson's Criterion**:

$$\boxed{n\,\tau_E \ge 10^{20}\text{ m}^{-3}\cdot\text{s} \qquad \left(\text{Ignition Triple Product: } n\,T\,\tau_E \ge 3 \times 10^{21}\text{ keV}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}\right)}$$

Two complementary engineering paths target Lawson's bound: (1) **Magnetic Confinement (Tokamaks / ITER):** low $n \sim 10^{20}\text{ m}^{-3}$, long $\tau_E \sim \text{few seconds}$; and (2) **Inertial Confinement (Laser implosion / NIF):** extreme compressed $n \sim 10^{31}\text{ m}^{-3}$, fleeting $\tau_E \sim 10^{-11}\text{ s}$.

### 6.2 Energy Production in Stars — Proton–Proton ($p\text{-}p$) Chain vs. Bethe–Weizsäcker CNO Cycle

Main-sequence stars radiate luminosity for billions of years by fusing four protons into one helium-4 nucleus:

$$4\,{}^1_1\text{H} \longrightarrow {}^4_2\text{He} + 2 e^+ + 2\nu_e + Q_{\text{net}}$$

Using neutral atomic masses automatically includes the $2 \times 2m_e c^2 = 2.044\text{ MeV}$ released when the two positrons annihilate with ambient electrons ($2e^+ + 2e^- \to 4\gamma$):

$$\boxed{Q_{\text{net}} = \left[4 m({}^1\text{H}) - M_A({}^4\text{He})\right]c^2 = \left[4(1.007825) - 4.002603\right] \times 931.494 = \mathbf{26.73\text{ MeV}}}$$

(Subtracting $\sim 0.5\text{ MeV}$ carried out of the star by the two weakly interacting $\nu_e$ leaves $\approx \mathbf{26.2\text{ MeV}}$ of stellar thermal heat in $p\text{-}p$ I.) Hans Bethe (Nobel Prize, 1967) and Charles Critchfield showed that stars execute this net fusion via two temperature-separated pathways:

1. **The Proton–Proton ($p\text{-}p$ I) Chain (Dominates in Sun & Lower-Mass Stars, $M \lesssim 1.3\,M_\odot$, $T_c \lesssim 1.7 \times 10^7\text{ K}$):**
   $$\begin{aligned}
   \text{Step 1 } (\times 2):\quad & p + p \longrightarrow {}^2_1\text{H} + e^+ + \nu_e && (+0.42\text{ MeV} + 1.02\text{ MeV annih.}) \\
   \text{Step 2 } (\times 2):\quad & {}^2_1\text{H} + p \longrightarrow {}^3_2\text{He} + \gamma && (+5.49\text{ MeV}) \\
   \text{Step 3 } (\times 1):\quad & {}^3_2\text{He} + {}^3_2\text{He} \longrightarrow {}^4_2\text{He} + 2p && (+12.86\text{ MeV})
   \end{aligned}$$
   - *Energy Sum Check:* $2(1.44) + 2(5.49) + 12.86 = 2.88 + 10.98 + 12.86 = \mathbf{26.72\text{ MeV}}$!
   - *Why the Sun Does Not Explode:* Step 1 is the **weak-interaction bottleneck**—because the diproton $^2\text{He}$ is unbound, one proton must beta-decay into a neutron during the fleeting $10^{-21}\text{ s}$ collision! This gives each solar core proton a mean lifetime of $\sim 9 \times 10^9\text{ yr}$, throttling the Sun into a steady 10-billion-year reactor! *(Minor side branches $p\text{-}p$ II via $^7\text{Be}$ and $p\text{-}p$ III via $^8\text{B} \to {}^8\text{Be}^* + e^+ + \nu_e$ emit high-energy neutrinos up to $14\text{ MeV}$, the crucial diagnostic signals of the Solar Neutrino Problem!)*
   - *Temperature Scaling:* $\varepsilon_{pp} \propto T^4$ (gentle Coulomb barrier $Z_1 Z_2 = 1$).

2. **The Bethe–Weizsäcker CNO-I Cycle (Dominates in Massive Hotter Stars, $M \gtrsim 1.3\,M_\odot$, $T_c \gtrsim 1.8 \times 10^7\text{ K}$):**
   Uses trace $^{12}\text{C}$ as a **nuclear catalyst**—bypassing the slow $p\text{-}p$ weak fusion step completely:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   {}^{12}_6\text{C} + p &\longrightarrow {}^{13}_7\text{N} + \gamma && (+1.94\text{ MeV}) \\
   {}^{13}_7\text{N} &\longrightarrow {}^{13}_6\text{C} + e^+ + \nu_e && (+2.22\text{ MeV incl. annih.},\; t_{1/2} = 10\text{ min}) \\
   {}^{13}_6\text{C} + p &\longrightarrow {}^{14}_7\text{N} + \gamma && (+7.55\text{ MeV}) \\
   {}^{14}_7\text{N} + p &\longrightarrow {}^{15}_8\text{O} + \gamma && (+7.29\text{ MeV — slowest bottleneck!}) \\
   {}^{15}_8\text{O} &\longrightarrow {}^{15}_7\text{N} + e^+ + \nu_e && (+2.75\text{ MeV incl. annih.},\; t_{1/2} = 2\text{ min}) \\
   {}^{15}_7\text{N} + p &\longrightarrow {}^{12}_6\text{C} + {}^4_2\text{He} && (+4.97\text{ MeV — }{}^{12}\text{C regenerated!})
   \end{aligned}$$
   - *Energy Sum Check:* $1.94 + 2.22 + 7.55 + 7.29 + 2.75 + 4.97 = \mathbf{26.72\text{ MeV}}$!
   - *Temperature Scaling:* Because $p + \text{C/N}$ encounters a **$6\text{–}7\times$ taller Coulomb barrier** ($E_G \propto Z^2$), CNO energy generation scales explosively as $\varepsilon_{\text{CNO}} \propto T^{16\text{–}18}$! In our Sun ($T_c = 1.5 \times 10^7\text{ K}$), $p\text{-}p$ supplies $\sim 98.4\%$ of luminosity and CNO only $\sim 1.6\%$; above $1.8 \times 10^7\text{ K}$, CNO overtakes $p\text{-}p$ completely!

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Reactor $^{235}\text{U}$ Burnup, Critical Sphere Radius & Solar Hydrogen Fusion Rate
> **Problem:** (a) A nuclear power reactor operates steadily at $1000\text{ MW}$ thermal power using $^{235}\text{U}$ ($200\text{ MeV}$ recoverable energy per fission). Calculate the mass of $^{235}\text{U}$ consumed in one year ($3.156 \times 10^7\text{ s}$). (b) A bare spherical thermal reactor has $k_\infty = 1.08$ and migration area $M^2 = 350\text{ cm}^2$. Compute its critical radius $R_c$. (c) The Sun radiates luminosity $L_\odot = 3.83 \times 10^{26}\text{ W}$ via the $p\text{-}p$ chain ($26.2\text{ MeV}$ thermal heat per $4p \to {}^4\text{He}$). Find the mass of hydrogen fused per second.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) Annual $^{235}\text{U}$ Consumption ($1000\text{ MW}_{\text{th}}$):**
> - Energy per fission: $E_f = 200\text{ MeV} = 200 \times 1.6022 \times 10^{-13}\text{ J} = 3.204 \times 10^{-11}\text{ J}$.
> - Fission rate: $R_f = \dfrac{P}{E_f} = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^9\text{ J/s}}{3.204 \times 10^{-11}\text{ J}} = 3.121 \times 10^{19}\text{ fissions/s}$.
> - Total fissions in 1 year: $N_f = (3.121 \times 10^{19})(3.156 \times 10^7) = 9.850 \times 10^{26}\text{ nuclei}$.
> - Mass consumed ($N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$, molar mass $0.235\text{ kg/mol}$):
>   $$m = \frac{N_f}{N_A}\times 0.235\text{ kg} = \frac{9.850 \times 10^{26}}{6.022 \times 10^{23}}\times 0.235 = \mathbf{384.4\text{ kg/yr}} \;\; (\approx 1.05\text{ kg/day}!)$$
>
> **(b) Critical Radius of Bare Spherical Reactor ($k_{\text{eff}} = 1 \implies B_g^2 = B_m^2$):**
> $$B_m^2 = \frac{k_\infty - 1}{M^2} = \frac{1.08 - 1}{350\text{ cm}^2} = 2.286 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}^{-2} \implies B_m = 0.01512\text{ cm}^{-1}$$
> For a bare sphere, $B_g^2 = (\pi / R_c)^2$:
> $$R_c = \frac{\pi}{B_m} = \frac{3.1416}{0.01512\text{ cm}^{-1}} = \mathbf{207.8\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{2.08\text{ m}}$$
>
> **(c) Solar Hydrogen Consumption Rate:**
> - Thermal energy per $4p \to {}^4\text{He}$: $Q_{\text{th}} = 26.2 \times 1.6022 \times 10^{-13} = 4.198 \times 10^{-12}\text{ J}$.
> - Fusion cycles/s: $\dfrac{L_\odot}{Q_{\text{th}}} = \dfrac{3.83 \times 10^{26}}{4.198 \times 10^{-12}} = 9.123 \times 10^{37}\text{ s}^{-1}$.
> - Hydrogen mass fused per second ($4 m_p = 4 \times 1.673 \times 10^{-27} = 6.692 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$):
>   $$\frac{dM_{\text{H}}}{dt} = (9.123 \times 10^{37})(6.692 \times 10^{-27}) = \mathbf{6.11 \times 10^{11}\text{ kg/s}} \;\; (\sim 600\text{ million tonnes/s}!)$$
> $$\boxed{m({}^{235}\text{U}) = 384\text{ kg/yr} \quad\big|\quad R_c = 2.08\text{ m} \quad\big|\quad dM_{\text{H}}/dt = 6.11\times 10^{11}\text{ kg/s}}$$

## 7. Examiner Traps & Exam-Hall Diagram Blueprints

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **The Fatal $-2m_e c^2$ ($1.022\text{ MeV}$) Trap in $\beta^+$ Atomic-Mass $Q$-Values:** Always distinguish bare nuclear masses $M_N$ from neutral atomic masses $M_A$! In $Q_{\beta^-}$ and $Q_{EC}$, electron masses cancel identically; in **$Q_{\beta^+}$**, substituting $M_N = M_A - Z m_e$ leaves an exact **$-2m_e c^2 = -1.022\text{ MeV}$** deficit! Forgetting this factor misidentifies pure-$EC$ nuclei (like $^7\text{Be}$) as $\beta^+$ emitters and gets every positron endpoint wrong by $1.022\text{ MeV}$.
> 2. **Alpha Kinetic Energy vs. $Q_\alpha$ Trap:** Never equate measured $\alpha$-particle kinetic energy $T_\alpha$ with $Q_\alpha$! Always include daughter recoil via $Q_\alpha = T_\alpha \dfrac{A}{A - 4}$.
> 3. **Gamma $0 \to 0$ Ban & Electric vs. Magnetic Parity Inversion:** Never assign $E0$ as a single-photon gamma ray—single-photon $0 \to 0$ is **strictly forbidden** (proceeds via $E0$ internal conversion or pair creation). Also check parity carefully: **$E_L$ has parity $(-1)^L$**, whereas **$M_L$ has OPPOSITE parity $(-1)^{L+1}$** (so $E1$ flips parity while $M1$ preserves parity; $E2$ preserves parity while $M2$ flips parity!).
> 4. **Internal Conversion Misconception:** Never describe internal conversion as a "$\gamma$-ray emitted by the nucleus and photoelectrically absorbed by an atomic electron." State explicitly that IC is a **direct near-field electromagnetic interaction** between the nucleus and the penetrating orbital electron (proven by $0^+ \to 0^+$ $E0$ transitions!).
> 5. **Lab vs. CM Threshold Energy Trap ($T_{\text{th}} \neq |Q|$):** For endoergic reactions ($Q < 0$), always multiply $|Q|$ by the kinematic factor $\left(1 + \dfrac{m_a}{m_X}\right)$ to account for non-zero center-of-mass momentum!
> 6. **Mössbauer Free Recoil vs. Crystal Recoil:** Always distinguish $E_R = \dfrac{E_0^2}{2 M c^2}$ (single nucleus $\implies 2 E_R \gg \Gamma$, NO resonance) from $M_{\text{crystal}} \to \infty$ ($E_R \to 0 \ll \Gamma$, zero-phonon resonance!). Also remember the mismatch between emission and absorption is **$2 E_R$** (NOT $E_R$!).
> 7. **Thermal vs. Electrical Reactor Power Trap:** If a UPSC problem specifies **electrical power** ($P_e$, e.g., $300\text{ MW}_e$ at $30\%$ thermal efficiency $\eta_{\text{th}}$), ALWAYS divide by $\eta_{\text{th}}$ first to obtain thermal fission power $P_{\text{th}} = P_e / \eta_{\text{th}}$ before dividing by $200\text{ MeV/fission}$!
