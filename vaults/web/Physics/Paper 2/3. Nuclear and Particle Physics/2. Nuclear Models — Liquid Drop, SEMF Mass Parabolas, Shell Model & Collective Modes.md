# Chapter 2: Nuclear Models — Liquid Drop, SEMF Mass Parabolas, Shell Model & Collective Modes

## 1. The Nuclear Model Paradox & Pauli-Blocking Reconciliation

Unlike the hydrogen atom—where a single massive central Coulomb core dominates electron dynamics—a nucleus of $A$ nucleons is a fiercely interacting many-body system with no external center of force. Worse still, the nucleon–nucleon interaction combines a short-range repulsive core, spin–isospin exchange, and non-central tensor components, rendering an exact $A$-body Schrödinger solution analytically intractable beyond $A \approx 4$. Nuclear physicists therefore construct **complementary phenomenological models**, each isolating a distinct physical regime of nuclear matter.

At first glance, the two flagship models appear logically irreconcilable:
- **The Liquid Drop Model (Bohr, Kalckar, Weizsäcker):** Views the nucleus as a dense, strongly interacting, incompressible classical droplet whose nucleons collide violently with a mean free path much shorter than the nuclear radius ($\lambda \ll R$).
- **The Independent-Particle Shell Model (Goeppert-Mayer, Jensen):** Views each nucleon as gliding almost freely in a smooth mean-field potential with a mean free path exceeding the nuclear diameter ($\lambda > 2R$), filling discrete quantized orbits just like atomic electrons.

**Pauli Blocking Resolves the Paradox Completely.** Nucleons are spin-$1/2$ fermions packed into a degenerate quantum Fermi gas inside the nucleus (Fermi kinetic energy $\varepsilon_F \approx 38\text{ MeV}$). In the ground state and at low excitation energies, every momentum state below the Fermi level $p_F$ is **already completely occupied**. When two interior nucleons encounter one another via the strong force, energy–momentum conservation demands that they scatter into vacant final momentum states—but none exist below $p_F$! Collisions are therefore almost completely suppressed by the **Pauli Exclusion Principle**, lengthening the effective mean free path to $\lambda > 2R$ and giving birth to crisp **single-particle shell orbitals**. Conversely, when bulk energies are averaged over many nucleons (total binding energy $B(A, Z)$) or when high excitation energy opens abundant vacant states above $\varepsilon_F$ (compound reactions, nuclear fission), collective **liquid-drop dynamics** emerge seamlessly.

### Liquid Drop Model vs. Nuclear Shell Model Comparison Matrix

| Physical Feature | Liquid Drop Model (Collective / Macroscopic) | Nuclear Shell Model (Independent-Particle / Microscopic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Physical Picture** | Strongly coupled, incompressible charged fluid drop ($\lambda \ll R$) | Non-interacting nucleons in a smooth mean field $V(r) - f(r)\mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ ($\lambda > 2R$, Pauli-blocked) |
| **Primary Analog** | Classical spherical liquid droplet with surface tension & charge | Atomic periodic table (Hartree–Fock central field + inverted spin–orbit coupling) |
| **Signature Successes** | Smooth $B/A$ trend across all $A$; isobaric mass parabolas & $\beta$-stability valley ($Z_0$); nuclear fission energetics & barrier; compound-nucleus reactions | Magic numbers ($2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126$); ground-state spins & parities ($I^\pi$); magnetic moments (Schmidt limits); islands of nuclear isomerism |
| **Critical Failures** | Blind to magic-number binding spikes; predicts zero ground-state spin/parity systematics; fails to explain low-lying discrete spectra | Underpredicts large quadrupole moments ($Q \sim 5\text{–}10\text{ b}$) mid-shell by $10\text{–}30\times$; fails on enhanced $E2$ transitions, rotational/vibrational bands & fission |
| **Synthesis / Resolution** | Merged with Shell Model via Strutinsky shell corrections & **Bohr–Mottelson Collective Model** | Extended via **Nilsson deformed shell model** + **Bohr–Mottelson Collective Model** |

## 2. Liquid Drop Model & Weizsäcker's Semi-Empirical Mass Formula (SEMF)

### 2.1 Physical Analogy with a Charged Liquid Droplet

Four macroscopic nuclear observables mirror a classical droplet of incompressible liquid:
1. **Constant Density ($\rho_m \approx 2.3 \times 10^{17}\text{ kg/m}^3$):** Nuclear volume scales strictly as $V \propto A$ ($R = R_0 A^{1/3}$), just as a liquid drop's volume scales with molecule count.
2. **Short-Range Saturation ($B/A \approx 8\text{ MeV}$):** Total binding energy scales as $B \propto A$, analogous to the constant latent heat of vaporization per molecule in a liquid where intermolecular forces act only on nearest neighbors.
3. **Surface Tension Deficit:** Nucleons on the nuclear boundary lack neighbors on the outward side, lowering total binding energy in proportion to surface area $4\pi R^2 \propto A^{2/3}$.
4. **Deformation & Fission Oscillations:** Like an electrically charged raindrop (Rayleigh instability), competing surface tension and Coulomb repulsion govern quadrupole oscillations and spontaneous fission.

### 2.2 Exact Scaling & Physical Origin of All Five SEMF Terms

Building on this droplet picture and augmenting it with two purely quantum-mechanical corrections (Pauli asymmetry and nucleon pairing), Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker (1935) and Hans Bethe (1936) formulated the **Semi-Empirical Mass Formula (SEMF)** for the total binding energy $B(A, Z)$:

$$\boxed{B(A, Z) = a_V A - a_S A^{2/3} - a_C \frac{Z(Z - 1)}{A^{1/3}} - a_A \frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A} + \delta(A, Z)}$$

Every term has a transparent physical derivation and strict $A, Z$ scaling:

1. **Volume Term ($+a_V A$, $a_V \approx 15.75\text{ MeV}$):**
   Because the short-range strong nuclear force saturates, each interior nucleon binds only to a fixed number of nearest neighbors, contributing an equal positive increment $a_V$ to total binding energy:
   $$B_V = +a_V A$$
   *(Alone, this would predict a flat horizontal $B/A = a_V \approx 15.8\text{ MeV}$ for every nucleus.)*

2. **Surface Energy Term ($-a_S A^{2/3}$, $a_S \approx 17.80\text{ MeV}$):**
   The volume term overcounts binding for nucleons situated within the surface skin, which have roughly half as many interacting neighbors as interior nucleons. The number of surface nucleons is proportional to the nuclear surface area $4\pi R^2 = 4\pi R_0^2 A^{2/3}$:
   $$B_S = -a_S A^{2/3}$$
   *Impact on $B/A$:* Contributes $-a_S A^{-1/3}$, which is severe for light nuclei (explaining the steep drop on the left side of the $B/A$ curve) and becomes progressively smaller as $A$ grows.

3. **Coulomb Repulsion Term ($-a_C \dfrac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}$, $a_C \approx 0.711\text{ MeV}$):**
   Unlike the strong force, electrostatic repulsion between protons is **long-range** and does **not** saturate: every proton repels all $Z - 1$ other protons. For a uniformly charged sphere of charge $Ze$ and radius $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$, the electrostatic self-energy (excluding each proton's self-interaction by replacing $Z^2 \to Z(Z-1)$) is:
   $$E_C = \frac{3}{5}\frac{Z(Z-1)e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R} = \left(\frac{3}{5}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R_0}\right)\frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}} \equiv a_C \frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}$$
   *Direct First-Principles Check:* With $R_0 = 1.215\text{ fm}$ and $\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 1.440\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}$, $a_C = \frac{3}{5}\frac{1.440}{1.215} = \mathbf{0.711\text{ MeV}}$—matching the empirical fit to three significant figures! Because $E_C / A \propto Z^2 / A^{4/3} \propto A^{2/3}$, Coulomb repulsion drags $B/A$ steadily downward for heavy nuclei ($A > 62$).

4. **Pauli Asymmetry (Symmetry) Term ($-a_A \dfrac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A}$, $a_A \approx 23.70\text{ MeV}$):**
   In the absence of Coulomb repulsion, light nuclei strongly prefer equal proton and neutron counts ($N = Z = A/2$). This is a pure **quantum Fermi-gas consequence of the Pauli Exclusion Principle**.
   - *Quantitative Fermi-Gas Derivation:* Model protons and neutrons as two independent 3D spin-$1/2$ Fermi gases confined to the same nuclear volume $V \propto A$. Total kinetic energy scales as $E_K(N) = \frac{3}{5} N \varepsilon_F(N) \propto N(N/V)^{2/3} \propto N^{5/3} A^{-2/3}$. Writing $N = \frac{A}{2}(1 + \epsilon)$ and $Z = \frac{A}{2}(1 - \epsilon)$ with fractional neutron excess $\epsilon = \frac{N - Z}{A} = \frac{A - 2Z}{A}$:
     $$E_K(N) + E_K(Z) = \frac{3}{10} A \varepsilon_F \left[(1 + \epsilon)^{5/3} + (1 - \epsilon)^{5/3}\right]$$
     Taylor-expanding $(1 \pm \epsilon)^{5/3} = 1 \pm \frac{5}{3}\epsilon + \frac{5}{9}\epsilon^2 + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^3)$ cancels all odd powers identically:
     $$E_K(N) + E_K(Z) = \frac{3}{5} A \varepsilon_F + \frac{1}{3}\varepsilon_F\,A\,\epsilon^2 = E_K^{\text{sym}} + \frac{1}{3}\varepsilon_F \frac{(N - Z)^2}{A}$$
     With $\varepsilon_F \approx 38\text{ MeV}$, Pauli kinetic energy alone yields $\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon_F \approx 12.7\text{ MeV}$; adding the potential-energy preference for $n\text{-}p$ spin-triplet bonds over $nn/pp$ singlet bonds raises the empirical coefficient to $a_A \approx 23.7\text{ MeV}$:
     $$B_A = -a_A \frac{(N - Z)^2}{A} = -a_A \frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A}$$

5. **Pairing (Odd–Even) Energy Term ($+\delta(A, Z)$):**
   Empirically, nature strongly favors paired nucleons: among ~254 stable nuclides, **152 are even-even**, **55 are even-odd**, **43 are odd-even**, and only **4 light nuclei** ($^2_1\text{H}, {}^6_3\text{Li}, {}^{10}_5\text{B}, {}^{14}_7\text{N}$) are stable **odd-odd**! Like nucleons ($pp$ or $nn$) in identical spatial orbitals with opposite spins ($\uparrow\downarrow$) achieve maximum wavefunction overlap and gain extra attractive pairing energy:
   $$\delta(A, Z) = \begin{cases} +a_P A^{-1/2}, & \text{even-}Z,\;\text{even-}N \quad (\text{most bound}) \\ 0, & \text{odd-}A \quad (\text{even-odd or odd-even}) \\ -a_P A^{-1/2}, & \text{odd-}Z,\;\text{odd-}N \quad (\text{least bound}) \end{cases}$$
   with $a_P \approx 11.2\text{–}12.0\text{ MeV}$ (some texts parameterize $\delta = \pm a_P' A^{-3/4}$ with $a_P' \approx 33.5\text{–}34\text{ MeV}$; both yield $\sim 1.1\text{–}1.2\text{ MeV}$ at $A = 100$).

Substituting $B(A, Z)$ into the neutral atomic mass relation yields **Weizsäcker's Atomic Mass Formula**:

$$\boxed{M(A, Z)c^2 = Z m({}^1\text{H})c^2 + (A - Z)m_n c^2 - a_V A + a_S A^{2/3} + a_C \frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}} + a_A \frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A} - \delta(A, Z)}$$

## 3. Isobaric Mass Parabolas, Most Stable $Z_0$ & Spontaneous Fission Limit

### 3.1 Derivation of Isobaric Mass Parabolas & Most Stable Atomic Number ($Z_0$)

Isobars share the same mass number $A$ and transform into one another via isobaric beta decays ($\beta^-, \beta^+, EC$), sliding downhill in total atomic mass $M(A, Z)$ toward the bottom of the valley of $\beta$-stability. Because the competition between **Pauli asymmetry** (pulling toward $Z = A/2$) and **Coulomb repulsion** (pushing toward smaller $Z$) is quadratic in $Z$, $M(A, Z)$ traces an exact parabola for fixed $A$.

- **Setup:** Fix $A$ and rewrite $M(A, Z)c^2$ (using the continuum Coulomb term $a_C Z^2 A^{-1/3}$ for clean differentiation, and verifying the discrete $Z(Z-1)$ refinement below) in ascending powers of $Z$:
  $$M(A, Z)c^2 = A m_n c^2 - Z\left[m_n - m({}^1\text{H})\right]c^2 - a_V A + a_S A^{2/3} + a_C \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} + a_A\left(A - 4Z + \frac{4Z^2}{A}\right) - \delta(A, Z)$$
- **Grouping Powers of $Z$:**
  $$\boxed{M(A, Z)c^2 = \alpha A + \beta Z + \gamma Z^2 - \delta(A, Z)}$$
  where the three $A$-dependent coefficients are:
  $$\alpha = m_n c^2 - a_V + a_S A^{-1/3} + a_A, \qquad \beta = -\left[4a_A + (m_n - m({}^1\text{H}))c^2\right], \qquad \gamma = \frac{4a_A}{A} + \frac{a_C}{A^{1/3}} > 0$$
- **Finding the Minimum-Mass Isobar ($Z_0$):** Setting $\left(\dfrac{\partial M(A, Z)c^2}{\partial Z}\right)_A = 0$ (noting $\delta(A, Z)$ is constant along a given parity branch):
  $$\beta + 2\gamma Z_0 = 0 \implies Z_0 = -\frac{\beta}{2\gamma} = \frac{4a_A + (m_n - m({}^1\text{H}))c^2}{2\left(\dfrac{4a_A}{A} + \dfrac{a_C}{A^{1/3}}\right)}$$
  Dividing numerator and denominator by $4a_A / A$:
  $$Z_0 = \frac{A\left[1 + \dfrac{(m_n - m({}^1\text{H}))c^2}{4a_A}\right]}{2 + \dfrac{a_C}{2a_A}A^{2/3}}$$
  Since $(m_n - m({}^1\text{H}))c^2 = 0.782\text{ MeV} \ll 4a_A \approx 94.8\text{ MeV}$ (a tiny $0.8\%$ correction; using $Z(Z-1)$ adds another tiny $+a_C A^{-1/3}$ to the numerator), neglecting this $\sim 0.8\%$ offset gives the celebrated universal stability-valley formula:
  $$\boxed{Z_0 = \frac{A}{2 + \dfrac{a_C}{2a_A}A^{2/3}}}$$
  Using the fitted values $a_C = 0.711\text{ MeV}$ and $a_A = 23.702\text{ MeV}$, note that $\dfrac{a_C}{2a_A} = \dfrac{0.711}{47.404} = \mathbf{0.01500}$ exactly:
  $$Z_0 = \frac{A}{2 + 0.0150\,A^{2/3}}$$
- **Dimensional & Limiting-Case Checks:**
  - *Dimensions:* $a_C/a_A$ is dimensionless, so $Z_0$ is pure dimensionless proton count.
  - *Light-Nuclei Limit ($A \lesssim 40$, $0.015\,A^{2/3} \ll 2$):* $Z_0 \to A/2 \implies N = Z$ (e.g., $^4\text{He}, {}^{12}\text{C}, {}^{16}\text{O}, {}^{40}\text{Ca}$).
  - *Heavy-Nuclei Limit ($A \approx 208$):* $0.015 \times 208^{2/3} = 0.527 \implies Z_0 \approx \frac{208}{2.527} = 82.3 \implies N/Z \approx 1.53$! Heavy nuclei MUST accumulate excess neutrons ($N > Z$) because Coulomb repulsion grows as $Z^2 / A^{1/3}$ while asymmetry grows only as $(N-Z)^2/A$.

### 3.2 Odd-$A$ Single Parabola vs. Even-$A$ Double-Split Parabolas

Rewriting the quadratic around its minimum $Z_0$ gives $M(A, Z)c^2 = M_0 c^2 + \gamma(Z - Z_0)^2 - \delta(A, Z)$, which creates a stark topological contrast between odd-$A$ and even-$A$ isobaric chains:

1. **Odd-$A$ Isobars ($\delta = 0$ — Single Parabola):**
   All isobars lie on **one continuous parabola** with vertex at $Z_0$.
   - Isobars with $Z < Z_0$ (neutron-rich) step down the left branch via sequential **$\beta^-$ decays** ($Z \to Z + 1$).
   - Isobars with $Z > Z_0$ (proton-rich) step down the right branch via sequential **$\beta^+$ / Electron Capture ($EC$) decays** ($Z \to Z - 1$).
   - Consequently, for every odd $A$, there is **strictly ONE beta-stable isobar** (the integer closest to $Z_0$, e.g., $^{125}_{52}\text{Te}$ for $A = 125$). *(Only two ultra-rare near-degenerate odd-$A$ pairs exist in nature where $Z_0$ falls almost dead halfway between two integers with huge spin barriers: $^{113}\text{Cd}/^{113}\text{In}$ and $^{123}\text{Sb}/^{123}\text{Te}$.)*

2. **Even-$A$ Isobars ($\delta = \pm a_P A^{-1/2}$ — Two Parallel Parabolas Split by $2\delta$):**
   Alternating between even-$Z$ (even-even) and odd-$Z$ (odd-odd) flips the sign of $\delta(A, Z)$, producing **two vertically shifted parabolas** separated everywhere by $\Delta M c^2 = 2|\delta| = 2 a_P A^{-1/2}$:
   - **Lower parabola:** Even-even nuclei (more tightly bound, shifted downward by $-\delta$).
   - **Upper parabola:** Odd-odd nuclei (less tightly bound, shifted upward by $+\delta$).
   - **Three Profound Physical Consequences:**
     1. **Multiple Stable Even-Even Isobars (2, or occasionally 3):** Because ordinary $\beta$-decay changes $Z$ by $\pm 1$, an even-even nucleus on the lower parabola near $Z_0$ is flanked on both sides by heavier odd-odd neighbors on the upper parabola. It cannot decay by single $\beta^\pm$ emission! Thus even-$A$ chains routinely host **2 stable even-even isobars** differing by $\Delta Z = 2$ (e.g., $A = 64$: $^{64}_{28}\text{Ni}$ and $^{64}_{30}\text{Zn}$), and occasionally **3** when $2\delta$ is large (e.g., $A = 124$: $^{124}_{50}\text{Sn}, {}^{124}_{52}\text{Te}, {}^{124}_{54}\text{Xe}$).
     2. **Dual-Branch ($\beta^-$ AND $\beta^+/EC$) Decay of Odd-Odd Nuclei:** An odd-odd nucleus sitting between two lower even-even neighbors (such as $^{64}_{29}\text{Cu}$ between $^{64}_{28}\text{Ni}$ and $^{64}_{30}\text{Zn}$, or $^{40}_{19}\text{K}$ between $^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$ and $^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$) sits strictly higher in mass than **both** neighbors and decays simultaneously via **$\beta^-$** to $Z + 1$ AND **$\beta^+/EC$** to $Z - 1$! This also explains why **no heavy stable odd-odd nucleus exists** above $^{14}_7\text{N}$ (at light $A \le 14$, the parabola curvature $\gamma \approx 4a_A/A$ is so steep that even an odd-odd vertex lies below its even-even neighbors!).
     3. **Double-Beta Decay ($2\beta$):** When two stable even-even isobars $(A, Z)$ and $(A, Z+2)$ have unequal masses (e.g., $^{76}_{32}\text{Ge} \to {}^{76}_{34}\text{Se} + 2e^- + 2\bar{\nu}_e$), single $\beta^-$ decay to the intermediate odd-odd nucleus ($^{76}_{33}\text{As}$) is energetically forbidden, leaving second-order weak **double-beta decay** (half-lives $\sim 10^{20}\text{–}10^{21}\text{ yr}$) as the sole open decay channel!

### 3.3 Spontaneous Fission Stability Limit from the Liquid Drop Model

Consider splitting a heavy spherical nucleus $(A, Z)$ symmetrically into two identical spherical daughters $(A/2, Z/2)$. The volume and asymmetry terms cancel identically, leaving a direct duel between **surface tension** (opposing breakup) and **Coulomb repulsion** (driving breakup):
- **Surface Energy Cost:** $\Delta E_S = 2 a_S (A/2)^{2/3} - a_S A^{2/3} = (2^{1/3} - 1)a_S A^{2/3} = +0.260\,a_S A^{2/3}$.
- **Coulomb Energy Release:** $\Delta E_C = 2 a_C \dfrac{(Z/2)^2}{(A/2)^{1/3}} - a_C \dfrac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} = (2^{-2/3} - 1)a_C \dfrac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} = -0.370\,a_C \dfrac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}}$.
- **Exothermic Threshold ($Q_{\text{fiss}} = -(\Delta E_S + \Delta E_C) > 0$):**
  $$0.370\,a_C \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} > 0.260\,a_S A^{2/3} \implies \frac{Z^2}{A} > 0.703\frac{a_S}{a_C} \approx \mathbf{17.6}$$
  All nuclei heavier than silver ($A \gtrsim 100$) are energetically unstable to fission, yet they survive billions of years because of a **fission activation barrier**!
- **Bohr–Wheeler Zero-Barrier Spontaneous Fission Limit:** Under a small volume-conserving quadrupole elongation $R(\theta) = R_0[1 + \alpha_2 P_2(\cos\theta)]$, the surface energy increases by $\Delta E_S = +\frac{2}{5}\alpha_2^2 a_S A^{2/3}$ while Coulomb energy drops by $\Delta E_C = -\frac{1}{5}\alpha_2^2 a_C \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}}$. The spherical shape becomes **spontaneously unstable with zero barrier** ($\Delta E_S + \Delta E_C \le 0$) when:
  $$\frac{1}{5}a_C \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} \ge \frac{2}{5}a_S A^{2/3} \implies \boxed{\left(\frac{Z^2}{A}\right)_{\text{crit}} = \frac{2 a_S}{a_C} \approx 48\text{–}50}$$
  Defining the **fissility parameter** $x = \dfrac{Z^2/A}{(Z^2/A)_{\text{crit}}}$, nuclei with $x \ge 1$ ($Z \gtrsim 115\text{–}120$) fission instantaneously ($\sim 10^{-21}\text{ s}$) unless stabilized by superheavy shell closures!

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: SEMF Binding Energy of $^{235}_{92}\text{U}$ & Fission Energetics
> **Problem:** Using $a_V = 15.75\text{ MeV}$, $a_S = 17.80\text{ MeV}$, $a_C = 0.711\text{ MeV}$, $a_A = 23.70\text{ MeV}$, and $a_P = 11.18\text{ MeV}$ (with $A^{-1/2}$): (a) Compute the total binding energy and $B/A$ of $^{235}_{92}\text{U}$. (b) Estimate the energy released in symmetric fission of $^{235}_{92}\text{U}$ and verify why it requires neutron activation rather than disintegrating instantaneously.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(a) SEMF Breakdown for $^{235}_{92}\text{U}$ ($A = 235, Z = 92, N = 143$, odd-$A \implies \delta = 0$):**
> Compute powers: $A^{1/3} = 235^{1/3} = 6.171$, $A^{2/3} = 38.081$, $A - 2Z = 235 - 184 = 51$:
> - Volume: $+a_V A = +15.75 \times 235 = +3701.25\text{ MeV}$
> - Surface: $-a_S A^{2/3} = -17.80 \times 38.081 = -677.84\text{ MeV}$
> - Coulomb: $-a_C \dfrac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}} = -0.711 \times \dfrac{92 \times 91}{6.171} = -964.59\text{ MeV}$
> - Asymmetry: $-a_A \dfrac{(A-2Z)^2}{A} = -23.70 \times \dfrac{51^2}{235} = -262.31\text{ MeV}$
> - Pairing: $\delta = 0\text{ MeV}$
> $$B({}^{235}\text{U}) = 3701.25 - 677.84 - 964.59 - 262.31 = 1796.5\text{ MeV} \implies \frac{B}{A} = \frac{1796.5}{235} = \mathbf{7.645\text{ MeV/nucleon}}$$
> *(Experimental value: $7.591\text{ MeV/nucleon}$ — better than $0.7\%$ accuracy!)*
>
> **(b) Symmetric Fission Energy & Stability Parameter:**
> - Energy release: $Q_{\text{fiss}} \approx 0.370\,E_C - 0.260\,E_S = 0.370(964.59) - 0.260(677.84) = 356.9 - 176.2 = \mathbf{180.7\text{ MeV}}$.
> - Fission parameter: $\dfrac{Z^2}{A} = \dfrac{92^2}{235} = \mathbf{36.02}$, which exceeds the exothermic threshold ($17.6$) but lies well below the zero-barrier Bohr–Wheeler limit ($2a_S/a_C = 50.1$, so fissility $x = 0.72 < 1$). Thus a $\sim 5.8\text{ MeV}$ barrier prevents instantaneous spontaneous fission, which is overcome when a thermal neutron supplies pairing + binding energy ($\sim 6.5\text{ MeV}$) to form $^{236}\text{U}^*$.
> $$\boxed{B = 1796.5\text{ MeV},\;\; B/A = 7.645\text{ MeV},\;\; Q_{\text{fiss}} \approx 181\text{ MeV},\;\; Z^2/A = 36.0 < 50.1}$$

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Most Stable Isobar $Z_0$ (UPSC 2024 Coefficients) for Odd $A = 125$ & Even $A = 64$
> **Problem:** Using the SEMF coefficients $a_C = 0.711\text{ MeV}$ and $a_A = 23.702\text{ MeV}$, determine the most stable atomic number $Z_0$ and predict the beta-decay behavior for: (a) odd isobaric chain $A = 125$, and (b) even isobaric chain $A = 64$.
>
> **Solution:**
> First evaluate the universal ratio: $\dfrac{a_C}{2a_A} = \dfrac{0.711}{2 \times 23.702} = \dfrac{0.711}{47.404} = 0.01500$.
> Thus $Z_0 = \dfrac{A}{2 + 0.0150\,A^{2/3}}$.
>
> **(a) Odd-$A$ Chain ($A = 125$, $125^{2/3} = 25$):**
> $$Z_0 = \frac{125}{2 + 0.0150 \times 25} = \frac{125}{2.375} = \mathbf{52.63}$$
> Since $A$ is odd ($\delta = 0$), all isobars lie on a single parabola. The unique stable isobar is expected near $Z = 52\text{–}53$ (experimentally $^{125}_{52}\text{Te}$ is the sole stable isobar; $^{125}_{51}\text{Sb} \xrightarrow{\beta^-} {}^{125}_{52}\text{Te}$ and $^{125}_{53}\text{I} \xrightarrow{EC} {}^{125}_{52}\text{Te}$).
>
> **(b) Even-$A$ Chain ($A = 64$, $64^{2/3} = 16$):**
> $$Z_0 = \frac{64}{2 + 0.0150 \times 16} = \frac{64}{2.240} = \mathbf{28.57}$$
> Since $A$ is even, isobars split into an upper odd-odd parabola and a lower even-even parabola. The two even-$Z$ nuclei bracketing $Z_0 = 28.57$ are **$Z = 28$ ($^{64}_{28}\text{Ni}$)** and **$Z = 30$ ($^{64}_{30}\text{Zn}$)**—**both are experimentally stable**! Between them sits the odd-odd isobar **$Z = 29$ ($^{64}_{29}\text{Cu}$)** on the upper parabola, which decays in **both directions**: $\beta^-$ to $^{64}_{30}\text{Zn}$ ($38.5\%$) and $\beta^+/EC$ to $^{64}_{28}\text{Ni}$ ($61.5\%$).
> $$\boxed{A = 125:\; Z_0 = 52.63 \;({}^{125}_{52}\text{Te}) \quad\big|\quad A = 64:\; Z_0 = 28.57 \;(\text{stable } {}^{64}_{28}\text{Ni}, {}^{64}_{30}\text{Zn};\;\text{dual-decay } {}^{64}_{29}\text{Cu})}$$

## 4. The Nuclear Shell Model

### 4.1 Empirical Evidence for Magic Numbers ($2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126$)

Just as noble gases ($Z = 2, 10, 18, 36, 54, 86$) display extraordinary chemical inertness at closed electronic shells, nuclei with proton number $Z$ or neutron number $N$ equal to **$2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82,$ or $126$** (the **magic numbers**) exhibit abrupt, unmistakable discontinuities far beyond the smooth Liquid Drop Model:

1. **Discontinuities in Nucleon Separation Energies ($S_n, S_p$):** Removing the last nucleon from a magic shell costs abnormally high energy, whereas the very next nucleon ($N_{\text{magic}} + 1$) drops abruptly by $\sim 2\text{–}4\text{ MeV}$ (e.g., $S_n({}^{208}\text{Pb}, N=126) = 7.37\text{ MeV}$ plunges to $S_n({}^{209}\text{Pb}, N=127) = 3.94\text{ MeV}$).
2. **Binding Energy Residuals ($B_{\text{exp}} - B_{\text{SEMF}}$):** Sharp positive peaks of $+5$ to $+12\text{ MeV}$ occur precisely at $28, 50, 82, 126$, maximized in **doubly magic** nuclei: $^4_2\text{He}$, $^{16}_8\text{O}$, $^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$, $^{48}_{20}\text{Ca}$, and $^{208}_{82}\text{Pb}$.
3. **Abundance of Stable Isotopes & Isotones:** Tin ($Z = 50$) possesses **10 stable isotopes** (more than any other element) and Calcium ($Z = 20$) possesses **6**; similarly $N = 82$ has **7 stable isotones** and $N = 50$ has **6** (compared to 1–2 for typical non-magic numbers).
4. **Abnormally Low Thermal Neutron Capture Cross-Sections ($\sigma_{n,\gamma}$):** Nuclei with magic $N$ (such as $^{208}\text{Pb}$ or $^{88}\text{Sr}$) resist absorbing an extra neutron into the next high-energy shell, displaying $\sigma_{n,\gamma}$ values $10^2\text{–}10^4$ times lower than neighboring non-magic nuclei.
5. **High First Excited State $E(2^+_1)$ & Zero Quadrupole Moment ($Q \approx 0$):** Exciting a nucleon across a magic shell gap demands large energy (e.g., $E(2^+_1) = 4.09\text{ MeV}$ in $^{208}\text{Pb}$ vs. $0.045\text{ MeV}$ in $^{238}\text{U}$). Electric quadrupole moments vanish identically at closed shells and reverse sign from negative (just below magic) to positive (just above magic).
6. **Radioactive Decay Endpoints & Cosmic Abundances:** All three natural heavy radioactive series ($^{238}\text{U}, {}^{235}\text{U}, {}^{232}\text{Th}$) terminate at stable Lead ($Z = 82$) isotopes, and cosmic nucleosynthesis peaks sharply at magic nuclei.

### 4.2 Basic Assumptions, 3D Harmonic Oscillator Degeneracy & Proof that $\hbar\omega \approx 41\,A^{-1/3}\text{ MeV}$

#### Basic Assumptions of the Single-Particle Shell Model
1. Each nucleon moves independently inside an effective central **mean-field potential** $V(r)$ generated collectively by all other $A - 1$ nucleons (approximated analytically by a 3D harmonic oscillator or a realistic Woods–Saxon well $V_{\text{WS}}(r) = -V_0/[1 + e^{(r-R)/a}]$).
2. Protons and neutrons fill **separate, independent energy ladders** obeying the **Pauli Exclusion Principle** (maximum $2j + 1$ identical nucleons per $(n_r, l, j)$ subshell).
3. Paired like nucleons in any filled subshell (or any even number of like nucleons in a subshell) couple to **total angular momentum zero** ($J = 0$) and positive parity; thus the ground-state spin, parity, and magnetic moment of an odd-$A$ nucleus are governed solely by the **last unpaired nucleon** (Extreme Single-Particle Model).

#### 3D Spherical Harmonic Oscillator: Degeneracy & Failure Beyond 20

Consider a nucleon of mass $M$ in a 3D isotropic harmonic oscillator potential $V_{\text{HO}}(r) = -V_0 + \frac{1}{2}M\omega^2 r^2$. Solving the Schrödinger equation in Cartesian or spherical coordinates yields equally spaced major oscillator shells:

$$\boxed{E_N = \left(N + \frac{3}{2}\right)\hbar\omega - V_0, \qquad N = n_x + n_y + n_z = 2(n_r - 1) + l = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots}$$

where $n_r = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ is the radial quantum number (nuclear spectroscopy convention, where $1s, 1p, 1d, 1f$ ALL exist because $n_r$ simply counts the $1\text{st}, 2\text{nd}, \dots$ state of a given $l$!), and zero-point energy is $E_0 = \frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega$.

- **Large Accidental Degeneracy in $(n_r, l)$:** Every pair $(n_r, l)$ yielding the same oscillator quantum number $N = 2(n_r - 1) + l$ is strictly degenerate (e.g., for $N = 2$, $(n_r=1, l=2) \equiv 1d$ and $(n_r=2, l=0) \equiv 2s$ have identical energy!). All subshells inside major shell $N$ share the **same parity** $\pi = (-1)^l = (-1)^N$.
- **Exact Shell Capacity $g(N)$ and Cumulative Closures:** Counting non-negative integer triplets $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$ summing to $N$ gives $\frac{1}{2}(N+1)(N+2)$ spatial states; multiplying by $2$ for spin ($m_s = \pm \frac{1}{2}$) gives:
  $$g(N) = \sum_{l = N, N-2, \dots} 2(2l + 1) = (N + 1)(N + 2)$$
  Summing from $N = 0$ to $N_{\max}$ yields the cumulative shell closures predicted by a pure harmonic oscillator:
  $$\mathcal{N}_{\text{HO}}(N_{\max}) = \sum_{N=0}^{N_{\max}} (N+1)(N+2) = \frac{1}{3}(N_{\max}+1)(N_{\max}+2)(N_{\max}+3)$$

| Major Shell $N$ | Degenerate $(n_r l)$ Subshells | Parity $(-1)^N$ | Capacity $g(N) = (N+1)(N+2)$ | Cumulative $\mathcal{N}_{\text{HO}}$ | Experimental Magic? |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $0$ | $1s$ | $+$ | $2$ | **$2$** | Match ($2$) |
| $1$ | $1p$ | $-$ | $6$ | **$8$** | Match ($8$) |
| $2$ | $1d,\; 2s$ | $+$ | $12$ | **$20$** | Match ($20$) |
| $3$ | $1f,\; 2p$ | $-$ | $20$ | $40$ | **FAILS** (Real: **$28$**) |
| $4$ | $1g,\; 2d,\; 3s$ | $+$ | $30$ | $70$ | **FAILS** (Real: **$50$**) |
| $5$ | $1h,\; 2f,\; 3p$ | $-$ | $42$ | $112$ | **FAILS** (Real: **$82$**) |
| $6$ | $1i,\; 2g,\; 3d,\; 4s$ | $+$ | $56$ | $168$ | **FAILS** (Real: **$126$**) |

The pure 3D harmonic oscillator reproduces only the first three magic numbers ($2, 8, 20$) and **fails completely for $28, 50, 82, 126$**! (A pure infinite square well similarly gives $2, 8, 18, 20, 34, 40, 58, 92, 138$—also failing beyond $20$.)

#### Unabridged Proof: Major Oscillator Shell Spacing $\hbar\omega \approx 41\,A^{-1/3}\text{ MeV}$

- **Assumptions & Setup:** Consider a nucleus of mass number $A$ ($N \approx Z \approx A/2$) whose nucleons completely fill all harmonic-oscillator shells from $N = 0$ up to $N = N_0$, occupying a uniform nuclear sphere of radius $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$ ($R_0 = 1.2\text{ fm}$).
- **Step-by-Step Algebra:**
  1. **Total Nucleon Count $A$:** Including both protons and neutrons (factor of $2$):
     $$A = 2 \sum_{N=0}^{N_0} (N+1)(N+2) = \frac{2}{3}(N_0+1)(N_0+2)(N_0+3) \approx \frac{2}{3}(N_0 + 2)^3$$
     Solving for $N_0 + 2$:
     $$N_0 + 2 \approx \left(\frac{3A}{2}\right)^{1/3}$$
  2. **Total Oscillator Energy $E_{\text{tot}}$:** Summing $\left(N + \frac{3}{2}\right)\hbar\omega$ over all $A$ nucleons:
     $$E_{\text{tot}} = 2\hbar\omega \sum_{N=0}^{N_0} \left(N + \frac{3}{2}\right)(N+1)(N+2)$$
     Rewrite $N + \frac{3}{2} = (N + 3) - \frac{3}{2}$ and apply the telescoping rising-factorial identity $\sum_{N=0}^{N_0}(N+1)(N+2)(N+3) = \frac{1}{4}(N_0+1)(N_0+2)(N_0+3)(N_0+4)$:
     $$\sum_{N=0}^{N_0}\left(N+\frac{3}{2}\right)(N+1)(N+2) = \frac{1}{4}(N_0+1)(N_0+2)(N_0+3)\left[(N_0+4) - 2\right] = \frac{1}{4}(N_0+1)(N_0+2)^2(N_0+3)$$
     Substituting $A = \frac{2}{3}(N_0+1)(N_0+2)(N_0+3)$ gives the exact identity:
     $$E_{\text{tot}} = \frac{3}{4} A (N_0 + 2)\,\hbar\omega \approx \frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{1/3} A^{4/3}\,\hbar\omega$$
  3. **Virial Theorem Matching to Nuclear Size:** By the quantum virial theorem for a quadratic harmonic potential, total potential energy equals exactly half the total oscillator energy:
     $$\sum_{k=1}^{A}\left\langle \frac{1}{2}M\omega^2 r_k^2 \right\rangle = \frac{1}{2} E_{\text{tot}} \implies M\omega^2 \sum_{k=1}^{A}\langle r_k^2 \rangle = E_{\text{tot}}$$
     For a uniform sphere of radius $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$, the mean-square radial coordinate of a nucleon is $\langle r^2 \rangle = \frac{3}{5}R^2 = \frac{3}{5}R_0^2 A^{2/3}$, so $\sum_{k=1}^{A}\langle r_k^2 \rangle = \frac{3}{5}R_0^2 A^{5/3}$.
  4. **Solving for $\hbar\omega$:** Equating both expressions for $E_{\text{tot}}$:
     $$M\omega^2\left(\frac{3}{5}R_0^2 A^{5/3}\right) = \frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{1/3} A^{4/3}\,\hbar\omega \implies \hbar\omega = \frac{5}{4}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{1/3}\frac{\hbar^2}{M R_0^2}\,A^{-1/3}$$
  5. **Numerical Evaluation (SI / Nuclear Units):** Using $\hbar c = 197.33\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}$, $Mc^2 = 938.92\text{ MeV}$, $R_0 = 1.20\text{ fm}$, and $(3/2)^{1/3} = 1.1447$:
     $$\frac{5}{4}(1.1447)\frac{(197.33\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm})^2}{(938.92\text{ MeV})(1.20\text{ fm})^2} = 1.4309 \times 28.80\text{ MeV} = 41.2\text{ MeV}$$
     $$\boxed{\hbar\omega \approx 41\,A^{-1/3}\text{ MeV}}$$
- **Dimensional & Limiting Check:** $[\hbar^2/(MR_0^2)] = \text{J}$ (energy). As $A$ increases, the nuclear well widens ($R \propto A^{1/3}$), smoothly compressing major shell gaps from $\sim 16\text{ MeV}$ in $^{16}\text{O}$ down to $\sim 7\text{ MeV}$ in $^{208}\text{Pb}$.

### 4.3 Lifting Degeneracy: Centrifugal Flattening ($-D\mathbf{l}^2$) & Mayer–Jensen Spin–Orbit Coupling ($-f(r)\mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{s}$)

Two physical corrections to the idealized harmonic oscillator completely remove its degeneracy and unlock every single magic number:

1. **Step 1 — Woods–Saxon / Centrifugal Flattening Term ($-D\mathbf{l}^2$, $D > 0$):**
   The realistic nuclear potential has a flat interior and rounded edge (Woods–Saxon, intermediate between harmonic oscillator and square well), making it **deeper near the nuclear rim ($r \approx R$)** than a parabola. Nucleons with larger orbital angular momentum $l$ are pushed outward by the centrifugal barrier $\frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2Mr^2}$ into this deeper rim region, lowering their energy relative to small-$l$ orbitals in the same major shell $N$! Modeling this via $V(r) = V_{\text{HO}}(r) - D\mathbf{l}^2$ splits each $N$-shell cleanly by $l$ (e.g., $1d < 2s$; $1f < 2p$; $1g < 2d < 3s$), but **still leaves $28, 50, 82, 126$ unexplained**.

2. **Step 2 — Mayer–Jensen Inverted Spin–Orbit Coupling ($-f(r)\,\mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{s}$, $f(r) > 0$):**
   In 1949, Maria Goeppert-Mayer and J. Hans D. Jensen (Nobel Prize, 1963) independently added a strong, **attractive (inverted)** spin–orbit interaction to the single-particle Hamiltonian:
   $$\hat{H}_{SO} = -f(r)\,\hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{s}}, \qquad f(r) > 0 \quad \left(\text{peaked at the nuclear surface: } f(r) \propto \frac{1}{r}\frac{dV_{\text{WS}}}{dr}\right)$$
   Each orbital with $l \ge 1$ splits into a doublet labeled by total angular momentum $j = l + \frac{1}{2}$ (spin parallel to orbit) and $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$ (spin antiparallel to orbit). Using $\hat{\mathbf{j}} = \hat{\mathbf{l}} + \hat{\mathbf{s}} \implies \hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{s}} = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{\mathbf{j}}^2 - \hat{\mathbf{l}}^2 - \hat{\mathbf{s}}^2)$:
   $$\langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{s}} \rangle = \frac{\hbar^2}{2}\left[j(j+1) - l(l+1) - \frac{3}{4}\right] = \begin{cases} +\dfrac{l}{2}\hbar^2, & j = l + \dfrac{1}{2} \;\; \Longrightarrow \;\; \Delta E_{SO} < 0 \; (\mathbf{LOWER}) \\[10pt] -\dfrac{l+1}{2}\hbar^2, & j = l - \dfrac{1}{2} \;\; \Longrightarrow \;\; \Delta E_{SO} > 0 \; (\mathbf{HIGHER}) \end{cases}$$
   *(Sign Alert: This is strictly **OPPOSITE** to atomic fine structure, where $+\xi(r)\mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ puts $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$ lower!)*

   The total energy splitting between the spin–orbit partners scales **linearly with $(2l + 1)$**:
   $$\boxed{\Delta E_{SO} = E\!\left(l - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) - E\!\left(l + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \langle f(r) \rangle\,\frac{2l + 1}{2}\,\hbar^2 \propto (2l + 1)}$$
   For low-$l$ orbitals ($1p, 1d$), the splitting stays safely inside its parent harmonic-oscillator shell (preserving magic $2, 8, 20$). Starting at **$l = 3$ ($1f_{7/2}$)** and growing ever larger for **$l = 4$ ($1g_{9/2}$)**, **$l = 5$ ($1h_{11/2}$)**, and **$l = 6$ ($1i_{13/2}$)**, the splitting becomes comparable to the major oscillator gap $\hbar\omega$! Consequently, the highest-$j$ orbital **$j = l + \frac{1}{2}$ plunges down across the major shell gap** as an **intruder orbital** into the shell below—creating **every single experimental magic number ($28, 50, 82, 126$)** in one stroke!

### 4.4 Systematic Spin–Parity Predictions ($I^\pi$) & Nordheim's Rules

To predict the ground-state spin $I$ and parity $\pi$ of ANY nucleus rapidly in the exam hall:

1. **Even-$Z$, Even-$N$ (Even-Even) Nuclei:**
   All protons pair to $J_p = 0^+$ and all neutrons pair to $J_n = 0^+$ $\implies$ **ALWAYS $I^\pi = 0^+$** (zero exceptions across the entire periodic table!).
2. **Odd-$A$ Nuclei (Even-Odd or Odd-Even):**
   - Identify the **odd nucleon species** (whichever of $Z$ or $N = A - Z$ is odd).
   - Fill the spin–orbit ladder strictly in order:
     $$1s_{1/2}^2 \;\Big|\; 1p_{3/2}^4,\; 1p_{1/2}^2 \;\Big|\; 1d_{5/2}^6,\; 2s_{1/2}^2,\; 1d_{3/2}^4 \;\Big|\; 1f_{7/2}^8 \;\Big|\; 2p_{3/2}^4,\; 1f_{5/2}^6,\; 2p_{1/2}^2,\; 1g_{9/2}^{10} \;\Big|\; 1g_{7/2}^8,\; 2d_{5/2}^6,\; 2d_{3/2}^4,\; 3s_{1/2}^2,\; 1h_{11/2}^{12} \;\Big|\; 1h_{9/2}^{10},\; 2f_{7/2}^8,\; \dots$$
   - Read off the quantum numbers $(l, j)$ of the **single unpaired nucleon** (or single nucleon hole in a nearly full subshell):
     $$\boxed{I = j, \qquad \pi = (-1)^l}$$
     *(Recall spectroscopic orbital $l$-codes: $s \to 0,\; p \to 1,\; d \to 2,\; f \to 3,\; g \to 4,\; h \to 5,\; i \to 6$.)*
   - *Subtlety (High-$j$ Pairing Preference & Collective Exceptions):* Because pairing energy increases with $(2j+1)$, occasionally two nucleons jump up into a nearby high-$j$ orbital to pair, leaving the odd nucleon in the lower-$j$ orbital (e.g., $^{207}_{82}\text{Pb}$ has $N = 125$, leaving a hole in $3p_{1/2} \implies \frac{1}{2}^-$ even though $1i_{13/2}$ is nearby). Also, when 3 or 5 nucleons occupy a single high-$j$ shell in deformed nuclei, residual interactions sometimes couple them to $I = j - 1$ (notable classic exceptions: $^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$ has $(1d_{5/2})^3 \to \frac{3}{2}^+$ instead of $\frac{5}{2}^+$; $^{55}_{25}\text{Mn}$ has $(1f_{7/2})^5 \to \frac{5}{2}^-$ instead of $\frac{7}{2}^-$).
3. **Odd-$Z$, Odd-$N$ (Odd-Odd) Nuclei — Nordheim's Coupling Rules:**
   Both the unpaired proton $(l_p, j_p)$ and unpaired neutron $(l_n, j_n)$ contribute, giving exact parity $\pi = (-1)^{l_p + l_n}$ and spin $|j_p - j_n| \le I \le j_p + j_n$. Define the **Nordheim number** $\mathcal{N} = (j_p - l_p) + (j_n - l_n) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$:
   - **Nordheim's Strong Rule ($\mathcal{N} = 0$, opposite spin–orbit alignments, i.e., one $j = l + \frac{1}{2}$ and one $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$):**
     $$\boxed{I = |j_p - j_n|}$$
   - **Nordheim's Weak Rule ($\mathcal{N} = \pm 1$, same spin–orbit alignment, i.e., both $j = l + \frac{1}{2}$ or both $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$):**
     $$\boxed{I = j_p + j_n \quad (\text{or } |j_p - j_n|,\;\text{strongly favoring } j_p + j_n \text{ via } n\text{-}p \text{ triplet attraction})}$$

>   Nordheim number: $\mathcal{N} = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 0$ (**Strong Rule!**) $\implies I = |j_p - j_n| = |\frac{3}{2} - \frac{7}{2}| = 2$; parity $\pi = (-1)^{2+3} = -1 \implies \mathbf{2^-}$ (Exp: $2^-$!).
> $$\boxed{{}^{13}\text{N}:\tfrac{1}{2}^- \;\big|\; {}^{17}\text{O}:\tfrac{5}{2}^+ \;\big|\; {}^{35}\text{Cl}:\tfrac{3}{2}^+ \;\big|\; {}^{41}\text{Ca}:\tfrac{7}{2}^- \;\big|\; {}^{209}\text{Bi}:\tfrac{9}{2}^- \;\big|\; {}^{38}\text{Cl}:2^-}$$

### 4.5 Nuclear Magnetic Moments & Exact Derivation of the Schmidt Lines

In the extreme single-particle shell model, all paired core nucleons contribute identically zero to the magnetic dipole moment, so the entire nuclear magnetic moment of an odd-$A$ nucleus arises from the single valence nucleon in orbital $(l, j = l \pm \frac{1}{2})$ with $I = j$.

#### (i) Setup & Operator Algebra (Landé Projection Theorem)
Take the single-nucleon magnetic moment operator (in units of $\mu_N$, with $\hbar = 1$):

$$\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}} = g_l \hat{\mathbf{l}} + g_s \hat{\mathbf{s}}$$

where the free-nucleon $g$-factors are:
- **Odd Proton:** $g_l = 1, \quad g_s = +5.5857 \quad (\frac{1}{2}g_s = +2.7929)$.
- **Odd Neutron:** $g_l = 0, \quad g_s = -3.8261 \quad (\frac{1}{2}g_s = -1.9130)$.

The observable spectroscopic magnetic moment $\mu = \langle j, m_j = j | \hat{\mu}_z | j, m_j = j \rangle$ is evaluated via the **Wigner–Eckart / Landé Projection Theorem**:

$$\mu = \frac{\langle \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle}{j(j + 1)}\,j = \frac{g_l \langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle + g_s \langle \hat{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle}{j + 1}$$

Using $\hat{\mathbf{j}} = \hat{\mathbf{l}} + \hat{\mathbf{s}}$ and $s(s + 1) = \frac{3}{4}$:

$$\langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left[j(j+1) + l(l+1) - \frac{3}{4}\right], \qquad \langle \hat{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left[j(j+1) + \frac{3}{4} - l(l+1)\right]$$

#### (ii) Evaluating the Two Spin–Orbit Branches (Schmidt Limits)

1. **Parallel / Stretched Alignment ($j = l + \frac{1}{2}$, so $l = j - \frac{1}{2}$):**
   Since $l(l + 1) = (j - \frac{1}{2})(j + \frac{1}{2}) = j^2 - \frac{1}{4}$, we obtain $j(j + 1) - l(l + 1) = j + \frac{1}{4}$. Therefore:
   $$\langle \hat{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left(j + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{2}(j + 1), \qquad \langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle = j(j+1) - \frac{1}{2}(j+1) = (j + 1)\left(j - \frac{1}{2}\right)$$
   Dividing cleanly by $(j + 1)$ gives the upper/stretched **Schmidt Limit**:
   $$\boxed{\mu\left(j = l + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \left[\left(j - \frac{1}{2}\right)g_l + \frac{1}{2}g_s\right]\mu_N = \left(l g_l + \frac{1}{2}g_s\right)\mu_N}$$

2. **Antiparallel / Jack-Knifed Alignment ($j = l - \frac{1}{2}$, so $l = j + \frac{1}{2}$):**
   Since $l(l + 1) = (j + \frac{1}{2})(j + \frac{3}{2}) = j^2 + 2j + \frac{3}{4}$, we obtain $j(j + 1) - l(l + 1) = -j - \frac{3}{4}$. Therefore:
   $$\langle \hat{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left(-j - \frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{4}\right) = -\frac{j}{2}, \qquad \langle \hat{\mathbf{l}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{j}} \rangle = j(j+1) + \frac{j}{2} = j\left(j + \frac{3}{2}\right)$$
   Dividing cleanly by $(j + 1)$ gives the lower/antiparallel **Schmidt Limit**:
   $$\boxed{\mu\left(j = l - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{j}{j + 1}\left[\left(j + \frac{3}{2}\right)g_l - \frac{1}{2}g_s\right]\mu_N}$$

#### (iii) Explicit Numerical Schmidt Lines & Experimental Reality
- **Odd-Proton Nuclei ($g_l = 1, \frac{1}{2}g_s = +2.793$):**
  - $j = l + \frac{1}{2}$: $\quad \mu = (j + 2.293)\,\mu_N$
  - $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$: $\quad \mu = \dfrac{j}{j+1}(j - 1.293)\,\mu_N$
- **Odd-Neutron Nuclei ($g_l = 0, \frac{1}{2}g_s = -1.913$):**
  - $j = l + \frac{1}{2}$: $\quad \mu = -1.913\,\mu_N$ (flat horizontal line independent of $j$!)
  - $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$: $\quad \mu = +\dfrac{j}{j+1}(1.913)\,\mu_N$ (positive despite negative $\mu_n$ because $\mathbf{s}$ points opposite to $\mathbf{j}$!)
- **Sanity Check & Physical Verdict:** For $l = 0, j = \frac{1}{2}$ ($s$-state), the $j = l + \frac{1}{2}$ formula reproduces $\mu = \frac{1}{2}g_s\mu_N$ ($\mu_p$ or $\mu_n$) identically! Experimentally, doubly-magic $\pm 1$ nuclei (such as ${}_8^{17}\text{O}$ and ${}_7^{15}\text{N}$) sit right on the Schmidt lines, confirming the single-particle shell structure. However, almost all other odd-$A$ nuclei fall **inward between the two Schmidt limits** (quenched by $\sim 20\text{–}30\%$) due to **core spin-polarization**, **configuration mixing**, and **meson-exchange currents** inside nuclear matter.

### 4.6 Successes, Limitations of the Shell Model & the Bohr–Mottelson Collective Model

#### Successes vs. Limitations of the Spherical Shell Model
- **Major Successes:** (1) Exact reproduction of all 7 magic numbers ($2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126$); (2) Correct ground-state $I^\pi$ for virtually all even-even and near-magic odd-$A$ nuclei; (3) Magnetic moments cleanly bracketed by Schmidt limits; (4) Quantitative explanation of **islands of nuclear isomerism** just below $N, Z = 50, 82, 126$ via high-$j$ opposite-parity intruder orbitals ($1g_{9/2}, 1h_{11/2}, 1i_{13/2}$).
- **Glaring Limitations:**
  1. **Huge Electric Quadrupole Moments Far from Closed Shells:** The single-proton shell model predicts $Q_{\text{sp}} = -\dfrac{2j-1}{2(j+1)}\langle r^2 \rangle \sim 0.1\text{–}0.3\text{ b}$ (and $Q = 0$ for an odd neutron!). Yet mid-shell rare-earth ($150 < A < 190$) and actinide ($A > 220$) nuclei—including odd-neutron nuclei—exhibit massive positive quadrupole moments $Q \sim +5$ to $+10\text{ b}$ (**10–30 times larger** than $Q_{\text{sp}}$!).
  2. **Enhanced $E2$ Gamma Transitions:** Observed electric quadrupole decay rates in mid-shell even-even nuclei are $50\text{–}200$ times faster than single-particle Weisskopf estimates, proving coherent motion of dozens of protons simultaneously.
  3. **Low-Lying Regular Rotational & Vibrational Bands:** Even-even nuclei far from magic shells exhibit ultra-low excited states ($E(2^+) \sim 40\text{–}100\text{ keV}$) obeying exact rotor/vibrator energy ratios—inexplicable by breaking a MeV-scale nucleon pair!
  4. **Nuclear Fission & Bulk Binding Trends:** Independent single-particle orbits cannot describe large-amplitude shape fission.

#### Bohr–Mottelson Collective (Unified) Model (Nobel Prize, 1975)

James Rainwater (1950), Aage Bohr, and Ben Mottelson (1952–53) unified the Liquid Drop and Shell Models: valence nucleons orbiting outside closed shells exert centrifugal pressure on the core, **polarizing the core into collective surface vibrations or permanent ellipsoidal deformation**. Expanding the nuclear surface in spherical harmonics $R(\theta, \phi, t) = R_0\left[1 + \sum_{\lambda\mu} \alpha_{\lambda\mu}(t) Y_{\lambda\mu}(\theta, \phi)\right]$ reveals two clean collective excitation regimes in even-even nuclei:

1. **Collective Surface Vibrational Modes (Near Closed Shells — Spherical Equilibrium $\beta_0 = 0$):**
   - $\lambda = 0$ (monopole breathing) requires compressing incompressible nuclear matter (very high energy, $\sim 15\text{ MeV}$); $\lambda = 1$ (dipole) is merely rigid center-of-mass translation.
   - The lowest surface vibration is the **$\lambda = 2$ quadrupole oscillation**, quantized into bosonic **quadrupole phonons** each carrying spin-parity **$I^\pi = 2^+$** and energy $\hbar\omega_2 \sim 0.5\text{–}1.2\text{ MeV}$:
     - $n = 0$ phonons (ground state): **$0^+$** at $E = 0$.
     - $n = 1$ phonon (first excited): **$2^+$** at $E = \hbar\omega_2$.
     - $n = 2$ phonons (boson symmetry forbids odd $I = 1, 3$!): **degenerate triplet $0^+, 2^+, 4^+$** at $E \approx 2\hbar\omega_2$.
   - *Diagnostic Energy Ratio:* $\boxed{\dfrac{E(4^+_1)}{E(2^+_1)} \approx 2.0}$ (observed in spherical vibrators like $^{114}_{48}\text{Cd}$ and $^{106}_{46}\text{Pd}$).
   - ($\lambda = 3$ octupole pear-shaped vibration produces a negative-parity $3^-$ state.)

2. **Collective Rotational Modes (Mid-Shell Regions $150 < A < 190$ & $A > 220$ — Permanent Deformation $\beta_0 \neq 0$):**
   When many valence nucleons accumulate mid-shell, their polarizing force overcomes surface tension, locking the nucleus into a **permanently deformed prolate ellipsoid** (intrinsic quadrupole moment $Q_0 = \frac{3}{\sqrt{5\pi}} Z R_0^2 \beta_0(1 + 0.16\beta_0)$, effortlessly explaining $Q \sim 5\text{–}10\text{ b}$!).
   Because quantum mechanics forbids rotation around an axis of exact rotational symmetry (the wavefunction is unchanged, carrying zero angular momentum), the ellipsoid rotates exclusively about an axis **perpendicular** to its symmetry axis, yielding the quantized **rigid/superfluid rotor spectrum**:
   $$\boxed{E_I = \frac{\hbar^2}{2\mathcal{I}}\,I(I + 1)}$$
   In an even-even ground-state band ($K = 0$), invariance under $180^\circ$ rotation about the perpendicular axis restricts allowed spins strictly to **even positive-parity values: $I^\pi = 0^+, 2^+, 4^+, 6^+, 8^+, \dots$**!
   - *Diagnostic Energy Ratios:*
     $$E(2^+) = \frac{6\hbar^2}{2\mathcal{I}}, \quad E(4^+) = \frac{20\hbar^2}{2\mathcal{I}}, \quad E(6^+) = \frac{42\hbar^2}{2\mathcal{I}} \implies \boxed{\frac{E(4^+)}{E(2^+)} = \frac{10}{3} \approx 3.33, \qquad \frac{E(6^+)}{E(2^+)} = 7.00}$$
   - *Superfluid Moment of Inertia:* Empirically, $\mathcal{I}_{\text{irrot}} < \mathcal{I}_{\text{exp}} \approx (0.3\text{–}0.5)\,\mathcal{I}_{\text{rigid}}$—neither a rigid solid nor an irrotational classical fluid, proving **BCS-like nucleon pairing (nuclear superfluidity)** inside deformed nuclei!

> **(a) Schmidt Magnetic Moments (Odd-Neutron: $g_l = 0, \frac{1}{2}g_s = -1.913$):**

>   $$\mu({}^{15}_8\text{O}) = \frac{j}{j + 1}\left[-\frac{1}{2}g_s\right]\mu_N = \frac{1/2}{3/2}\left(+1.913\right)\mu_N = \mathbf{+0.638\,\mu_N}$$

> - **Prediction of $E(6^+)$:** $E(6^+) = \dfrac{6(7)}{2(3)} E(2^+) = 7 \times 44.9 = \mathbf{314.3\text{ keV}}$ (Exp: $307.2\text{ keV}$, slightly lowered by centrifugal stretching).

## 5. Examiner Traps & Exam-Hall Diagram Blueprints

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **Inverted Nuclear vs. Atomic Spin–Orbit Ordering (Fatal Sign Trap!):** In atomic physics, $+\xi(r)\mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ puts $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$ lower (e.g., $2P_{1/2}$ lies below $2P_{3/2}$). In nuclear physics, Mayer–Jensen coupling is **$-f(r)\mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ ($f > 0$)**, so **$j = l + \frac{1}{2}$ ALWAYS lies LOWER** ($1p_{3/2}$ below $1p_{1/2}$; $1d_{5/2}$ below $1d_{3/2}$; $1f_{7/2}$ below $1f_{5/2}$). Reversing this sign ruins every magic number and every spin prediction!
> 2. **Nuclear vs. Atomic Radial Quantum Number ($n_r$) Trap:** In the nuclear shell model, $1p, 1d, 1f, 1g, 1h, 1i$ are completely valid ground radial states ($n_r = 1$, zero interior radial nodes, with oscillator number $N = 2(n_r - 1) + l$). Never apply the atomic hydrogen restriction $l \le n - 1$!
> 3. **SEMF Asymmetry Factor-of-4 Convention Trap:** Some books write $-a_A \frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A}$ with $a_A \approx 23.7\text{ MeV}$ (giving $Z_0 = \frac{A}{2 + \frac{a_C}{2a_A}A^{2/3}}$), while others write $-a_A' \frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{4A}$ with $a_A' = 4a_A \approx 94.8\text{ MeV}$ (giving $Z_0 = \frac{A}{2 + \frac{2a_C}{a_A'}A^{2/3}}$). Always inspect the exact formula printed in the UPSC question before substituting coefficients!
> 4. **Parity Calculation Trap ($\pi = (-1)^l$, NOT $(-1)^j$!):** Nuclear parity depends strictly on the **orbital** angular momentum $l$ of the unpaired nucleon, NEVER on $j$ or $N$ blindly (e.g., $1f_{7/2}$ has $l = 3 \implies \pi = -1$; $1g_{9/2}$ has $l = 4 \implies \pi = +1$; $1h_{11/2}$ has $l = 5 \implies \pi = -1$).
> 5. **Even-$A$ Mass Parabola Vertical Offset Sign:** In $B(A, Z)$, even-even nuclei have **$+\delta$** (higher binding energy); in $M(A, Z)c^2 = \dots - B(A, Z)$, even-even nuclei sit on the **LOWER ($-\delta$)** mass parabola while odd-odd nuclei sit on the **UPPER ($+\delta$)** mass parabola! Never draw odd-odd below even-even!
> 6. **Schmidt $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$ Sign Trap for Odd Neutrons:** An odd neutron in $j = l - \frac{1}{2}$ (like $^{15}\text{O}$ in $1p_{1/2}$) has a **POSITIVE** magnetic moment ($+\frac{j}{j+1}(1.913)\,\mu_N$) even though the free neutron moment is negative, because its spin $\mathbf{s}$ points antiparallel to $\mathbf{j}$!
