# Chapter 1: Inertial & Non-Inertial Dynamics, Conservation Laws & Gravitational Potentials

Classical mechanics rests on three interlocking pillars: the **symmetry architecture** of space and time that dictates conservation laws, the **transformation geometry** that separates inertial frames from accelerating or rotating ones, and the **field formulation** of inverse-square gravitation that converts discrete particle sums into linear boundary-value problems. Treating Newton's laws merely as algebraic recipes misses why linear momentum survives variable mass exchange only inside a closed control volume, why rotating observers experience non-working Coriolis deflections alongside conservative centrifugal potentials, and why spherical shells shield their interiors from gravity while trapping negative field energy in empty space.

This chapter builds every derivation from first principles in strict SI units—moving directly through Galilean covariance, Noetherian symmetry intuition, conservative curl criteria, Tsiolkovsky rocket dynamics (under gravity, linear drag, and staging), rotating-frame operator calculus (including Earth's oblateness, eastward free-fall deviation, cyclonic vorticity, and Foucault's pendulum), and gravitational Gauss–Poisson boundary-value theory culminating in gravitational self-energy.

## 1. Galilean Relativity, Inertial Frames & Symmetry-Driven Conservation Laws

### 1.1 Inertial Frames & the Galilean Transformation Group

Newton's First Law is not a degenerate special case of $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$ with $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$; it is an **operational definition** of an **inertial reference frame**. An inertial frame is a spacetime coordinate system in which a genuinely isolated particle (infinitely distant from all other matter) moves with constant velocity. Once a single inertial frame $S$ exists, an entire continuous family of inertial frames $S'$ exists, connected to $S$ by the **10-parameter Galilean group**:

1. **Spatial translations** (3 parameters, $\mathbf{a}$): $\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{a}$
2. **Time translation** (1 parameter, $t_0$): $t' = t - t_0$
3. **Rigid spatial rotations** (3 parameters, $\mathbf{R} \in SO(3)$): $\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{r}$
4. **Uniform velocity boosts** (3 parameters, $\mathbf{V} = \text{const}$): $\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{V}t$, $t' = t$

Consider a pure Galilean boost between inertial frames $S$ and $S'$, where $S'$ translates at constant velocity $\mathbf{V}$ relative to $S$ with coinciding origins at $t = t' = 0$. Newtonian physics postulates **absolute time** ($t' = t$, hence $\frac{d}{dt'} = \frac{d}{dt}$) and **velocity-independent inertial mass** ($m' = m$). Differentiating the coordinate map twice yields the kinematic transformation rules:

$$\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{V}t, \qquad \mathbf{v}' = \frac{d\mathbf{r}'}{dt} = \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{V}, \qquad \mathbf{a}' = \frac{d\mathbf{v}'}{dt} = \mathbf{a}$$

Two-body Newtonian interactions depend solely on instantaneous relative separations $\mathbf{r}_{ij} = \mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j$ (and relative velocities $\mathbf{v}_{ij} = \mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j$ for velocity-dependent drag/damping). Under a boost:

$$\mathbf{r}'_i - \mathbf{r}'_j = (\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{V}t) - (\mathbf{r}_j - \mathbf{V}t) = \mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j, \qquad \mathbf{v}'_i - \mathbf{v}'_j = \mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j$$

Consequently, the force law $\mathbf{F}'_i = \mathbf{F}_i$ and acceleration $\mathbf{a}'_i = \mathbf{a}_i$ are individually invariant, making Newton's Second Law **form-invariant (covariant)** across all inertial frames:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{F}'_i = m_i \mathbf{a}'_i \iff \mathbf{F}_i = m_i \mathbf{a}_i}$$

A subtle distinction separates **invariant quantities** from **covariant laws**. Kinetic energy $T = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ and mechanical work $W = \int \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$ are *not* Galilean invariants:

$$T' = \frac{1}{2}m|\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{V}|^2 = T - m\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{V} + \frac{1}{2}mV^2$$

$$dW' = \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{F}\cdot(d\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{V}\,dt) = dW - (\mathbf{F}\,dt)\cdot\mathbf{V} = dW - d(m\mathbf{v})\cdot\mathbf{V}$$

Subtracting the two shifts cancels the boost-dependent cross-term identically: $dT' - dW' = dT - dW = 0$. Thus, while $T$ and $W$ are frame-dependent, the **Work–Energy Theorem** ($\Delta T' = W'$) remains strictly Galilean-covariant.

### 1.2 Noetherian Symmetry Spine of Conservation Laws

Conservation laws are not lucky algebraic integrals of Newton's equations; they are direct consequences of **continuous symmetries of empty spacetime** (Emmy Noether's theorem, 1918). For an isolated $N$-particle system described by the Lagrangian $L(\{\mathbf{r}_i\}, \{\dot{\mathbf{r}}_i\}, t) = T - V$, invariance of the action under a continuous transformation immediately generates a conserved quantity.

| Continuous Spacetime Symmetry | Infinitesimal Transformation | Constraint on Potential $V$ | Conserved Quantity | Newtonian Law Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Homogeneity of Time** | $t \to t + \delta t$ | $\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} = 0$ (no explicit time dependence) | **Total Mechanical Energy** $E = T + V$ | Work–Energy Theorem for conservative forces |
| **Homogeneity of Space** | $\mathbf{r}_i \to \mathbf{r}_i + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ (all $i$) | $V = V(\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j)$ only | **Total Linear Momentum** $\mathbf{P} = \sum_i m_i \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i$ | **Weak Newton's 3rd Law** ($\mathbf{F}_{ij} = -\mathbf{F}_{ji}$) |
| **Isotropy of Space** | $\mathbf{r}_i \to \mathbf{r}_i + \delta\boldsymbol{\theta}\times\mathbf{r}_i$ | $V = V(|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j|)$ (depends on distance only) | **Total Angular Momentum** $\mathbf{L} = \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{p}_i$ | **Strong Newton's 3rd Law** (central internal forces along $\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j$) |
| **Galilean Boost Symmetry** | $\mathbf{r}_i \to \mathbf{r}_i + \delta\mathbf{V}\,t$ | Velocity-independent central potential | **Center-of-Mass Motion** $\mathbf{G} = M\mathbf{R}_{\text{CM}} - \mathbf{P}t$ | Uniform velocity of CM ($\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{\text{CM}} = \text{const}$) |

To see the Newtonian bridge explicitly without needing formal variational calculus, impose spatial translational invariance on an isolated pair potential $V(\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2)$:

$$\delta V = \nabla_1 V \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon} + \nabla_2 V \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon} = -(\mathbf{F}_1 + \mathbf{F}_2)\cdot\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = 0 \quad \forall\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$$

Because $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ is arbitrary, $\mathbf{F}_{12} + \mathbf{F}_{21} = \mathbf{0}$, which forces $\frac{d\mathbf{P}}{dt} = \mathbf{0}$. Similarly, imposing rotational invariance $\delta\mathbf{r}_i = \delta\boldsymbol{\theta}\times\mathbf{r}_i$ yields:

$$\delta V = \sum_i \nabla_i V \cdot (\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}\times\mathbf{r}_i) = -\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} \cdot \sum_i (\mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{F}_i) = -\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{int}} = 0 \implies \frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \mathbf{0}$$

Whenever an external potential breaks a symmetry along a specific direction—say, Earth's uniform gravity $V = mgz$ breaks spatial homogeneity along $\hat{k}$ while preserving homogeneity in the $xy$-plane and rotational isotropy about $\hat{k}$—the corresponding component $P_z$ ceases to be conserved, whereas $P_x$, $P_y$, and $L_z$ remain exact constants of motion.

### 1.3 Conservative Force Fields & the Curl Criterion

A time-independent force field $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$ defined on a **simply-connected** region $\mathcal{D} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$ (a region where every closed loop can be continuously shrunk to a point without leaving $\mathcal{D}$) is **conservative** if and only if any one of the following four mathematically equivalent statements holds:

1. **Path Independence:** The work $W_{AB} = \int_A^B \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$ depends solely on endpoints $A$ and $B$, not on the contour connecting them.
2. **Vanishing Circulation:** $\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$ for every piecewise-smooth closed loop $C \subset \mathcal{D}$.
3. **Gradient of a Scalar Potential:** There exists a single-valued scalar potential energy function $V(\mathbf{r})$ such that $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = -\nabla V(\mathbf{r})$.
4. **Irrotationality (Zero Curl):** $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{0}$ everywhere in $\mathcal{D}$.

#### Formal Proof of Equivalence via Stokes' Theorem

**(i) Physical Setup & Sign Convention:** Let $S$ be any smooth orientable surface in simply-connected $\mathcal{D}$ bounded by closed contour $C = \partial S$, with right-hand unit normal $\hat{n}$. Adopt the physics potential energy convention $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla V$.

**(ii) Step-by-Step Proof:**
- **$(3) \implies (4)$:** Since mixed second partial derivatives commute for smooth $V \in C^2(\mathcal{D})$,
  $$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_i = -\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_j\partial_k V = 0 \implies \nabla \times (-\nabla V) \equiv \mathbf{0}$$
- **$(4) \implies (2)$:** Applying **Stokes' Theorem** to any closed loop $C = \partial S$ lying entirely inside simply-connected $\mathcal{D}$:
  $$\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_S \mathbf{0}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0$$
- **$(2) \implies (1)$:** Split $C$ into two arbitrary paths $C_1$ (from $A$ to $B$) and $-C_2$ (from $B$ back to $A$):
  $$\int_{C_1} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} - \int_{C_2} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0 \implies \int_{C_1} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_{C_2} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$$
- **$(1) \implies (3)$:** Fix a reference point $\mathbf{r}_0$ and define $V(\mathbf{r}) = -\int_{\mathbf{r}_0}^{\mathbf{r}} \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}')\cdot d\mathbf{r}'$. Because the integral is path-independent, $V(\mathbf{r})$ is single-valued. Varying the endpoint by $d\mathbf{r}$ yields $dV = -\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$, while the total differential is $dV = \nabla V \cdot d\mathbf{r}$; comparing coefficients of arbitrary $d\mathbf{r}$ gives:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = -\nabla V(\mathbf{r}) \iff \nabla \times \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{0} \quad (\text{on simply-connected }\mathcal{D})}$$

**(iii) Energy Conservation & Topological Sanity Check:** Taking the dot product of $m\ddot{\mathbf{r}} = -\nabla V(\mathbf{r})$ with $\dot{\mathbf{r}}$ gives $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}m\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2 + V(\mathbf{r})\right) = 0$. **Crucial topological check:** If $\mathcal{D}$ is *not* simply connected—for example, the vortex field $\mathbf{F} = \frac{-y\hat{i} + x\hat{j}}{x^2 + y^2}$ defined on $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{z\text{-axis}\}$—then $\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ everywhere in $\mathcal{D}$, yet $\oint_{x^2+y^2=a^2} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 2\pi \neq 0$ because any capping surface $S$ must pierce the singularity on the $z$-axis! Simply-connectedness is therefore physically indispensable.

If so, determine its potential energy function $V(x,y,z)$ (with $V(0,0,0) = 0$) and the work done moving a particle from $(1, -2, 1)\text{ m}$ to $(3, 1, 4)\text{ m}$.
> 2. Test whether $\mathbf{F}_2 = x^2 y\,\hat{i} + z y^2\,\hat{j} + x z^2\,\hat{k}\text{ N}$ is conservative.

> $$\nabla \times \mathbf{F}_1 = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ 2xy + z^3 & x^2 & 3xz^2 \end{vmatrix}$$
> - $x$-component: $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(3xz^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x^2) = 0 - 0 = 0$
> - $y$-component: $\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(2xy + z^3) - \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(3xz^2) = 3z^2 - 3z^2 = 0$
> - $z$-component: $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(2xy + z^3) = 2x - 2x = 0$
>

>
> To reconstruct $V(x,y,z)$ from $\mathbf{F}_1 = -\nabla V$:
> $$\frac{\partial V}{\partial x} = -(2xy + z^3), \qquad \frac{\partial V}{\partial y} = -x^2, \qquad \frac{\partial V}{\partial z} = -3xz^2$$
> Integrating the $x$-equation partially with respect to $x$:
> $$V(x,y,z) = -x^2 y - x z^3 + g(y,z)$$
> Differentiating with respect to $y$ and matching $\frac{\partial V}{\partial y} = -x^2$:
> $$-x^2 + \frac{\partial g}{\partial y} = -x^2 \implies \frac{\partial g}{\partial y} = 0 \implies g = h(z)$$
> Differentiating with respect to $z$ and matching $\frac{\partial V}{\partial z} = -3xz^2$:
> $$-3xz^2 + \frac{dh}{dz} = -3xz^2 \implies h(z) = C$$
> Imposing $V(0,0,0) = 0$ fixes $C = 0$:
> $$\boxed{V(x,y,z) = -(x^2 y + x z^3)\text{ J}}$$
> *(Note: If the examiner defines mathematical scalar potential $\psi$ via $\mathbf{F} = +\nabla\psi$, state both signs explicitly: $\psi = -V = x^2 y + x z^3$.)*

> $$V(B) = -\big[(3)^2(1) + (3)(4)^3\big] = -(9 + 192) = -201\text{ J}$$

>
> **Part 2:** Compute the curl of $\mathbf{F}_2 = x^2 y\,\hat{i} + z y^2\,\hat{j} + x z^2\,\hat{k}$:
> $$\nabla \times \mathbf{F}_2 = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ x^2 y & z y^2 & x z^2 \end{vmatrix} = (0 - y^2)\,\hat{i} + (0 - z^2)\,\hat{j} + (0 - x^2)\,\hat{k} = -(y^2\hat{i} + z^2\hat{j} + x^2\hat{k}) \neq \mathbf{0}$$
> Because $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}_2 \neq \mathbf{0}$ everywhere except the origin, **$\mathbf{F}_2$ is non-conservative**.

## 2. Many-Particle Systems, Center-of-Mass Decoupling & Variable-Mass Rocket Mechanics

### 2.1 Center-of-Mass Frame & König's Decomposition Theorems

For an $N$-particle system with total mass $M = \sum_{i=1}^N m_i$, define the **Center of Mass (CM)** position vector $\mathbf{R} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i m_i \mathbf{r}_i$ and express each particle's inertial coordinate as $\mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{R} + \mathbf{r}'_i$. By construction, the internal mass-moment and internal momentum vanish identically in the CM frame:

$$\sum_{i=1}^N m_i \mathbf{r}'_i = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^N m_i \dot{\mathbf{r}}'_i = \mathbf{0}$$

These two identities cleanly split every dynamical observable into an **orbital (CM) part** and an **intrinsic (relative-to-CM) part** (König's Theorems):

1. **Translational Equation of Motion:** Summing $m_i\ddot{\mathbf{r}}_i = \mathbf{F}_i^{\text{ext}} + \sum_{j\neq i}\mathbf{F}_{ij}$ over all $i$ cancels internal pairs by weak Newton's 3rd Law ($\mathbf{F}_{ij} + \mathbf{F}_{ji} = \mathbf{0}$):
   $$\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{dt} = M\ddot{\mathbf{R}}$$
2. **Kinetic Energy Decomposition:**
   $$T = \sum_i \frac{1}{2}m_i|\dot{\mathbf{R}} + \dot{\mathbf{r}}'_i|^2 = \frac{1}{2}M\dot{\mathbf{R}}^2 + \dot{\mathbf{R}}\cdot\underbrace{\sum_i m_i\dot{\mathbf{r}}'_i}_{\mathbf{0}} + \sum_i \frac{1}{2}m_i\dot{\mathbf{r}}_i'^2 = \frac{1}{2}M V_{\text{CM}}^2 + T_{\text{CM}}$$
3. **Angular Momentum Decomposition:**
   $$\mathbf{L} = \sum_i (\mathbf{R} + \mathbf{r}'_i) \times m_i(\dot{\mathbf{R}} + \dot{\mathbf{r}}'_i) = \mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{P} + \sum_i \mathbf{r}'_i \times m_i\dot{\mathbf{r}}'_i = \mathbf{L}_{\text{orb}} + \mathbf{L}_{\text{CM}}$$
4. **Torque About an Accelerating CM:** Differentiating $\mathbf{L}_{\text{CM}} = \sum_i \mathbf{r}'_i \times m_i\dot{\mathbf{r}}'_i$ directly gives:
   $$\frac{d\mathbf{L}_{\text{CM}}}{dt} = \sum_i \mathbf{r}'_i \times m_i(\ddot{\mathbf{r}}_i - \ddot{\mathbf{R}}) = \sum_i \mathbf{r}'_i \times \mathbf{F}_i^{\text{ext}} + \sum_{i<j}(\mathbf{r}'_i - \mathbf{r}'_j)\times\mathbf{F}_{ij} - \underbrace{\left(\sum_i m_i \mathbf{r}'_i\right)}_{\mathbf{0}} \times \ddot{\mathbf{R}}$$
   Under the strong 3rd Law ($\mathbf{F}_{ij} \parallel \mathbf{r}'_i - \mathbf{r}'_j$), the internal torque vanishes and the fictitious translational term $-\left(\sum m_i \mathbf{r}'_i\right)\times\ddot{\mathbf{R}}$ vanishes identically! Thus:
   $$\boxed{\frac{d\mathbf{L}_{\text{CM}}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{CM}}}$$
   remains **exact even when the Center of Mass accelerates arbitrarily**—a result that fails for any other accelerating reference point.

### 2.2 Variable-Mass Dynamics — The Momentum-Flux Control Volume Proof

A classic pitfall in mechanics is applying $\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d(m\mathbf{v})}{dt} = m\dot{\mathbf{v}} + \dot{m}\mathbf{v}$ blindly to a rocket or leaking cart. A quick Galilean boost $\mathbf{v}' = \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{V}$ exposes the fatal flaw: $m\dot{\mathbf{v}}' + \dot{m}\mathbf{v}' = m\dot{\mathbf{v}} + \dot{m}\mathbf{v} - \dot{m}\mathbf{V} \neq \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}}$. Because $\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{dt}$ applies **exclusively to a closed system of fixed total mass**, any variable-mass problem must be analyzed by enclosing both the rocket and its infinitesimal exhaust slug inside a fixed-mass control system over the interval $[t, t+dt]$.

#### Unabridged Derivation of the Master Variable-Mass Equation

**(i) Physical Setup & Sign Convention:** Consider a rocket moving in an inertial frame $S$ with mass $m(t)$ and velocity $\mathbf{v}(t)$ at time $t$, subjected to external force $\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}}$ (e.g., gravity and aerodynamic drag). During $dt > 0$, the rocket's mass changes by $dm$ ($dm < 0$ for mass ejection; $dm > 0$ for mass accretion). For ejection, an exhaust element of positive mass $\Delta m_{\text{ex}} = -dm > 0$ leaves the nozzle at velocity $\mathbf{v}_{\text{ex}}$ in $S$, so its velocity **relative to the rocket** is $\mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{ex}} - \mathbf{v}$.

**(ii) Closed-System Momentum Balance:**
- At time $t$, the closed system (rocket + fuel about to be expelled) has momentum:
  $$\mathbf{P}(t) = m\mathbf{v}$$
- At time $t + dt$, the same total mass consists of the remaining rocket (mass $m + dm$, velocity $\mathbf{v} + d\mathbf{v}$) plus the expelled exhaust slug (mass $-dm$, velocity $\mathbf{v}_{\text{ex}} = \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}$):
  $$\mathbf{P}(t + dt) = (m + dm)(\mathbf{v} + d\mathbf{v}) + (-dm)(\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}})$$
- Expand and neglect the second-order differential $dm\,d\mathbf{v}$:
  $$d\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{P}(t+dt) - \mathbf{P}(t) = m\,d\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{v}\,dm - \mathbf{v}\,dm - \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}\,dm = m\,d\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}\,dm$$
- Equating $\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{dt}$ yields the **Meshchersky / Master Variable-Mass Equation**:

$$\boxed{m\frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} + \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}\frac{dm}{dt}}$$

**(iii) Dimensional, Sign & Galilean Sanity Checks:**
- **Dimensions:** $[\mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}\,dm/dt] = (\text{m s}^{-1})(\text{kg s}^{-1}) = \text{kg m s}^{-2} = \text{N}$.
- **Direction of Thrust:** For a rocket moving along $+\hat{z}$, exhaust shoots backward ($\mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}} = -u\hat{z}$, $u > 0$) while mass decreases ($\frac{dm}{dt} = -\alpha < 0$), so the **thrust force** $\mathbf{F}_{\text{thrust}} = \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}\frac{dm}{dt} = (-u\hat{z})(-\alpha) = +\alpha u\,\hat{z}$ accelerates the rocket forward!
- **Galilean Invariance:** Under a boost $\mathbf{v} \to \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{V}$, $\mathbf{v}_{\text{ex}} \to \mathbf{v}_{\text{ex}} - \mathbf{V}$, so $\mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{ex}} - \mathbf{v}$ is strictly invariant, restoring exact Galilean covariance.
- **Accretion Limit ($dm > 0$):** If stationary dust ($\mathbf{v}_{\text{dust}} = \mathbf{0}$) sticks to a cart ($\mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}} = -\mathbf{v}$), the equation automatically gives $m\dot{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} - \mathbf{v}\dot{m} \implies \frac{d(m\mathbf{v})}{dt} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}}$—the *only* special case where naive $\frac{d(m\mathbf{v})}{dt}$ holds.

### 2.3 Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation — Free Space, Gravity, Drag & Multi-Staging

#### Case A: Field-Free Space ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \mathbf{0}$)

For 1D motion along $+\hat{z}$ with constant exhaust speed $u = |\mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}|$ opposite to motion:

$$m\frac{dv}{dt} = -u\frac{dm}{dt} \implies dv = -u\frac{dm}{m}$$

Integrating from initial state $(m_0, v_0)$ to instantaneous state $(m, v)$ gives the **Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation**:

$$\boxed{v(t) = v_0 + u\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m(t)}\right)}$$

Notice the striking engineering feature: **final velocity in free space is completely independent of the burn rate** $\alpha(t) = -dm/dt$; it depends solely on exhaust speed $u$ and the mass ratio $m_0/m_f$. Because the mass ratio grows **exponentially** with required $\Delta v$ ($m_0/m_f = e^{\Delta v / u}$), achieving $\Delta v = 3u$ demands $e^3 \approx 20.1$, meaning over $95\%$ of initial mass must be propellant.

#### Case B: Vertical Launch in Uniform Gravity ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = -mg\,\hat{z}$)

With constant mass-burn rate $\alpha = -\frac{dm}{dt} > 0$, the rocket mass evolves linearly as $m(t) = m_0 - \alpha t$ until fuel exhaustion at burnout time $t_b = \frac{m_0 - m_f}{\alpha}$.

1. **Lift-off Threshold:** At $t = 0$, $m_0 a(0) = \alpha u - m_0 g \ge 0 \implies \alpha u \ge m_0 g$. If thrust is lower, the rocket sits pinned on the launch pad burning propellant until $m(t)$ drops to $\alpha u / g$.
2. **Velocity Profile:** Dividing $m\frac{dv}{dt} = -u\frac{dm}{dt} - mg$ by $m$ and integrating over $[0, t]$ (for $t \le t_b$, starting from $v_0 = 0$):
   $$\int_0^v dv' = -u\int_{m_0}^{m(t)}\frac{dm'}{m'} - g\int_0^t dt' \implies \boxed{v(t) = u\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_0 - \alpha t}\right) - gt}$$
   Unlike free space, **gravity losses** ($-g t_b$) penalize slow burns! Maximizing burn rate $\alpha$ minimizes $t_b$ and preserves maximum burnout speed $v_b = u\ln(m_0/m_f) - g t_b$.
3. **Burnout Altitude $y_b$ & Maximum Coasting Apex $y_{\max}$:** Integrating $v(t)$ from $0$ to $t_b$ using the substitution $m = m_0 - \alpha t$ ($dt = -dm/\alpha$) and $\int \ln m\,dm = m\ln m - m$:
   $$y_b = \int_0^{t_b} u\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_0 - \alpha t}\right)dt - \frac{1}{2}g t_b^2 = -\frac{u}{\alpha}\int_{m_0}^{m_f}(\ln m_0 - \ln m)\,dm - \frac{1}{2}g t_b^2$$
   $$= \frac{u}{\alpha}\Big[(m_0 - m_f)\ln m_0 - (m_0\ln m_0 - m_0 - m_f\ln m_f + m_f)\Big] - \frac{1}{2}g t_b^2$$
   $$= \frac{u}{\alpha}\left[(m_0 - m_f) - m_f\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right)\right] - \frac{1}{2}g t_b^2$$
   Using $(m_0 - m_f)/\alpha = t_b$:
   $$\boxed{y_b = u t_b - \frac{u m_f}{\alpha}\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right) - \frac{1}{2}g t_b^2, \qquad y_{\max} = y_b + \frac{v_b^2}{2g}}$$
   *Sanity Check:* As $t_b \to 0$ with $\epsilon = \alpha t_b / m_0 \ll 1$, $\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\right) \approx \epsilon + \frac{\epsilon^2}{2}$, yielding $y_b \to \frac{u m_0}{\alpha}\left[\epsilon - (1-\epsilon)(\epsilon + \frac{\epsilon^2}{2})\right] - \frac{1}{2}gt_b^2 = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha u}{m_0} - g\right)t_b^2 = \frac{1}{2}a_0 t_b^2$.

#### Case C: Uniform Gravity Plus Linear Aerodynamic Drag ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = -mg\,\hat{z} - kv\,\hat{z}$)

With constant burn rate $\alpha$ ($m(t) = m_0 - \alpha t$) and linear drag constant $k > 0$ ($k \neq \alpha$), the equation of motion is a first-order linear ODE:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{k}{m_0 - \alpha t}v = \frac{\alpha u}{m_0 - \alpha t} - g$$

Multiply by the integrating factor $\mu(t) = \exp\left(\int \frac{k\,dt}{m_0 - \alpha t}\right) = (m_0 - \alpha t)^{-k/\alpha} = m^{-p}$ (setting $p \equiv k/\alpha$):

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(m^{-p} v\right) = \alpha u\, m^{-p-1} - g\, m^{-p}$$

Integrating using $dt = -dm/\alpha$ from $(m_0, 0)$ to $(m, v)$:

$$m^{-p} v = \frac{u}{p}\left(m^{-p} - m_0^{-p}\right) + \frac{g}{\alpha(1 - p)}\left(m^{1-p} - m_0^{1-p}\right)$$

Multiplying through by $m^p$ yields the exact closed-form velocity:

$$\boxed{v(t) = \frac{\alpha u}{k}\left[1 - \left(\frac{m}{m_0}\right)^{k/\alpha}\right] - \frac{g m_0}{k - \alpha}\left[\left(\frac{m}{m_0}\right) - \left(\frac{m}{m_0}\right)^{k/\alpha}\right]}$$

*Limiting Check ($k \to 0$):* Expanding $(m/m_0)^{k/\alpha} = 1 + \frac{k}{\alpha}\ln(m/m_0) + O(k^2)$ immediately recovers $v(t) \to u\ln(m_0/m) - gt$.

#### Case D: Multi-Stage Rockets — Breaking the Single-Stage Barrier

Every real rocket requires tanks, engines, and avionics (**structural mass** $m_s$) in addition to payload $m_p$ and fuel $m_{\text{fuel}}$. Define the structural fraction $\epsilon = \frac{m_s}{m_s + m_{\text{fuel}}}$. For a single-stage rocket, even in the absurd limit of zero payload ($m_p \to 0$), the final mass cannot fall below $m_s = \epsilon m_0$, imposing a hard velocity ceiling:

$$\Delta v_{\text{1-stage, max}} < u\ln\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)$$

With typical chemical propellants ($u \approx 3.0\text{ km/s}$) and $\epsilon \approx 0.12$, $\Delta v_{\text{1-stage, max}} \approx 6.36\text{ km/s}$—strictly below Low Earth Orbit velocity ($7.8\text{ km/s}$)! **Staging** solves this by discarding empty lower-stage tanks mid-flight so upper-stage fuel never wastes impulse accelerating dead metal.

For an $N$-stage rocket where stage $j$ starts at total vehicle mass $M_{0,j}$ and ends at burnout mass $M_{f,j} = M_{0,j} - m_{\text{fuel}, j}$ before jettisoning $m_{s,j}$ (leaving $M_{0,j+1} = M_{f,j} - m_{s,j}$):

$$\boxed{\Delta v_{\text{total}} = \sum_{j=1}^{N} u_j \ln\left(\frac{M_{0,j}}{M_{f,j}}\right) - g\sum_{j=1}^{N} t_{b,j}}$$

If all $N$ stages share identical exhaust speed $u$ and structural fraction $\epsilon$, Lagrange-multiplier optimization shows that maximum $\Delta v$ for fixed overall payload ratio $M_0 / m_p$ occurs when every stage shares the **same stage mass ratio** $\frac{M_{0,j}}{M_{0,j+1}} = \left(\frac{M_0}{m_p}\right)^{1/N}$, multiplying the effective mass ratio exponentially.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Vertical Rocket Burnout Speed, Burnout Altitude & Apex Height
> **Problem:** A single-stage rocket of total initial mass $m_0 = 2.0\times 10^4\text{ kg}$ carries $1.6\times 10^4\text{ kg}$ of propellant burned at a constant rate $\alpha = 200\text{ kg s}^{-1}$ with relative exhaust speed $u = 2500\text{ m s}^{-1}$. Launched vertically from rest in a uniform gravitational field $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$ (neglecting air drag), calculate: (i) initial thrust and lift-off acceleration, (ii) burnout velocity, (iii) altitude at burnout, and (iv) maximum height reached.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Givens:** $m_0 = 2.0\times 10^4\text{ kg}$, $m_f = m_0 - m_{\text{fuel}} = 4.0\times 10^3\text{ kg}$, $\frac{m_0}{m_f} = 5$, $\alpha = 200\text{ kg s}^{-1}$, $u = 2500\text{ m s}^{-1}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$.
>
> **(i) Thrust & Lift-off Acceleration:**
> $$F_{\text{thrust}} = \alpha u = (200)(2500) = 5.0\times 10^5\text{ N}$$
> Initial weight $W_0 = m_0 g = (2.0\times 10^4)(9.8) = 1.96\times 10^5\text{ N} < F_{\text{thrust}}$ (immediate lift-off).
> $$a_0 = \frac{F_{\text{thrust}} - m_0 g}{m_0} = \frac{(5.0 - 1.96)\times 10^5}{2.0\times 10^4} = \boxed{15.2\text{ m s}^{-2}}$$
>
> **(ii) Burnout Time & Velocity:**
> $$t_b = \frac{m_0 - m_f}{\alpha} = \frac{1.6\times 10^4}{200} = 80\text{ s}$$
> $$v_b = u\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right) - g t_b = 2500\ln(5) - (9.8)(80) = 2500(1.60944) - 784.0 = 4023.6 - 784.0 = \boxed{3239.6\text{ m s}^{-1}}$$
>
> **(iii) Burnout Altitude $y_b$:**
> $$y_b = u t_b - \frac{u m_f}{\alpha}\ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right) - \frac{1}{2}g t_b^2$$
> $$= (2500)(80) - \frac{(2500)(4000)}{200}(1.60944) - \frac{1}{2}(9.8)(80)^2$$
> $$= 200{,}000 - 80{,}471.9 - 31{,}360 = 88{,}168.1\text{ m} \approx \boxed{88.17\text{ km}}$$
>
> **(iv) Maximum Height $y_{\max}$:** After burnout, the rocket coasts ballistically under $-g$:
> $$\Delta y_{\text{coast}} = \frac{v_b^2}{2g} = \frac{(3239.6)^2}{2(9.8)} = 535{,}460\text{ m} \approx 535.46\text{ km}$$
> $$\boxed{y_{\max} = y_b + \Delta y_{\text{coast}} = 88.17 + 535.46 = 623.6\text{ km}}$$

> **Problem:** A spacecraft must accelerate a payload $m_p = 1000\text{ kg}$ in field-free space using engines with $u = 3000\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and total launch mass $M_0 = 64{,}000\text{ kg}$. Every stage has structural fraction $\epsilon = \frac{m_s}{m_s + m_{\text{fuel}}} = 0.15$. > **Solution:**

> - Final burnout mass: $M_f = m_p + m_s = 1000 + 9450 = 10{,}450\text{ kg}$
> $$\Delta v_{\text{1-stage}} = 3000\ln\left(\frac{64{,}000}{10{,}450}\right) = 3000\ln(6.1244) = \boxed{5.44\text{ km s}^{-1}}$$
>
> **(b) Optimal Two-Stage Design:**
> Equal stage mass ratios require $M_{0,2} = \sqrt{M_0 m_p} = \sqrt{(64{,}000)(1000)} = 8000\text{ kg}$.

## 3. Rotating Reference Frames & Non-Inertial Dynamics

### 3.1 The Rotating-Frame Derivative Operator & General Equation of Motion

An observer strapped to a rotating turntable sees an inertial straight-line trajectory bend into a spiral. Rather than abandoning Newton's laws, we express the inertial time derivative of any vector in terms of its rate of change measured against co-rotating basis axes, automatically uncovering every fictitious (inertial) force required to restore $\mathbf{F}_{\text{eff}} = m\mathbf{a}'$.

#### Unabridged Operator Derivation

**(i) Coordinate & Kinematic Setup:** Let $S$ be an inertial frame with fixed orthonormal basis $\{\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_2, \hat{e}_3\}$ and origin $O$, and let $S'$ be a non-inertial frame with origin $O'$ located at $\mathbf{R}_0(t)$ in $S$ and co-rotating orthonormal basis $\{\hat{e}'_1(t), \hat{e}'_2(t), \hat{e}'_3(t)\}$ spinning with instantaneous angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)$ relative to $S$.

**(ii) Poisson's Basis-Rotation Formula:** During an infinitesimal time $dt$, the basis frame undergoes an infinitesimal rotation $\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} = \boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\,dt$. Any vector rigidly locked to $S'$—in particular each unit basis vector $\hat{e}'_i(t)$—changes only in direction by $\delta\hat{e}'_i = \delta\boldsymbol{\theta} \times \hat{e}'_i$, giving:

$$\left(\frac{d\hat{e}'_i}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \hat{e}'_i \qquad (i = 1, 2, 3)$$

**(iii) Master Derivative Operator:** Expand an arbitrary vector $\mathbf{A}(t)$ in the rotating basis: $\mathbf{A}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^3 A'_i(t)\,\hat{e}'_i(t)$. Differentiating as viewed from the inertial frame $S$ via the product rule:

$$\left(\frac{d\mathbf{A}}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \frac{dA'_i}{dt}\,\hat{e}'_i + \sum_{i=1}^3 A'_i \left(\frac{d\hat{e}'_i}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{d\mathbf{A}}{dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} + \sum_{i=1}^3 A'_i (\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\hat{e}'_i)$$

By linearity of the cross product, $\sum_i A'_i(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\hat{e}'_i) = \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{A}$, establishing the universal operator identity:

$$\boxed{\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} \mathbf{A} = \left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} \mathbf{A} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{A}}$$

*(Note: Setting $\mathbf{A} = \boldsymbol{\omega}$ immediately proves $\left(\frac{d\boldsymbol{\omega}}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{d\boldsymbol{\omega}}{dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} \equiv \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}$, because $\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{\omega} \equiv \mathbf{0}$.)*

**(iv) Velocity & Acceleration Transformations:** A particle's inertial position is $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{R}_0 + \mathbf{r}'$, where $\mathbf{r}'$ is its position relative to $O'$. Applying the operator once:

$$\mathbf{v}_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{V}_0 + \left(\frac{d\mathbf{r}'}{dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{V}_0 + \mathbf{v}' + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}'$$

Applying $\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}}$ a second time to $\mathbf{v}_{\text{in}}$ (noting $\left(\frac{d\mathbf{V}_0}{dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{A}_0$):

$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{A}_0 + \left[\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\right]\left(\mathbf{v}' + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}'\right)$$

Expanding all four terms carefully without skipping a step:

$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{A}_0 + \left(\frac{d\mathbf{v}'}{dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} + \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\mathbf{r}' + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}' + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}' + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}')$$

$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{A}_0 + \mathbf{a}' + \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\mathbf{r}' + 2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}')$$

Substituting into Newton's Second Law in the inertial frame ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{real}} = m\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}}$) and solving for the acceleration $\mathbf{a}'$ measured in the rotating frame $S'$:

$$\boxed{m\mathbf{a}' = \mathbf{F}_{\text{real}} \underbrace{- m\mathbf{A}_0}_{\mathbf{F}_{\text{trans}}} \underbrace{- m(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\mathbf{r}')}_{\mathbf{F}_{\text{Euler}}} \underbrace{- 2m(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')}_{\mathbf{F}_{\text{Coriolis}}} \underbrace{- m\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}')}_{\mathbf{F}_{\text{centrifugal}}}}$$

#### Physical & Geometric Properties of Each Fictitious Force

1. **Translational Inertial Force** ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{trans}} = -m\mathbf{A}_0$): Uniform across $S'$; acts opposite to the linear acceleration of origin $O'$.
2. **Euler (Azimuthal) Force** ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{Euler}} = -m\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\mathbf{r}'$): Appears only during **non-uniform rotation** ($\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} \neq \mathbf{0}$). Non-conservative ($\nabla'\times\mathbf{F}_{\text{Euler}} = -2m\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} \neq \mathbf{0}$).
3. **Coriolis Force** ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{Cor}} = -2m(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')$, with locked Coriolis acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}} = -2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')$):
   - **Perpendicularity:** Since $\mathbf{A}\cdot(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}) = 0$ and $\mathbf{B}\cdot(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}) = 0$, we have $\mathbf{F}_{\text{Cor}}\cdot\mathbf{v}' = 0$ and $\mathbf{F}_{\text{Cor}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega} = 0$. The Coriolis force is strictly perpendicular to **both** the particle's velocity $\mathbf{v}'$ and the rotation axis $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.
   - **Zero Work Done:** The instantaneous power delivered in $S'$ is $P_{\text{Cor}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{Cor}}\cdot\mathbf{v}' = -2m(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')\cdot\mathbf{v}' \equiv 0$. Like the magnetic Lorentz force $q(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})$, Coriolis force **deflects trajectories without ever changing speed** in $S'$.
   - **Factor of 2 Origin:** One $\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}'$ arises because moving radially carries the particle to a region with different tangential frame speed; the second $\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}'$ arises because the rotating basis turns underneath a fixed velocity vector $\mathbf{v}'$.
4. **Centrifugal Force** ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{cf}} = -m\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}')$):
   - Applying the BAC–CAB vector triple product identity: $\mathbf{F}_{\text{cf}} = -m\big[(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\boldsymbol{\omega} - \omega^2\mathbf{r}'\big] = m\omega^2\boldsymbol{\rho}_\perp$, where $\boldsymbol{\rho}_\perp = \mathbf{r}' - (\mathbf{r}'\cdot\hat{\omega})\hat{\omega}$ is the **perpendicular radial vector directly outward from the rotation axis**.
   - **Conservativeness (for constant $\boldsymbol{\omega}$):** Since $\mathbf{F}_{\text{cf}} = m\omega^2\boldsymbol{\rho}_\perp = -\nabla'\left(-\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\rho_\perp^2\right)$, centrifugal force is strictly **conservative** with potential energy $V_{\text{cf}}(\mathbf{r}') = -\frac{1}{2}m|\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}'|^2$.
   - **Jacobi Integral:** For constant $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and time-independent conservative $\mathbf{F}_{\text{real}} = -\nabla' V$, because $\mathbf{F}_{\text{Cor}}$ does zero work, the rotating-frame energy $E' = \frac{1}{2}mv'^2 + V(\mathbf{r}') - \frac{1}{2}m|\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}'|^2$ is an exact constant of motion.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Time-Varying Rotating Frame — Coriolis, Euler & Centrifugal Accelerations
> **Problem:** Frame $S'$ shares a common origin with inertial frame $S$ and rotates with time-varying angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega}(t) = 2t\,\hat{i} - t^2\,\hat{j} + (2t + 4)\,\hat{k}\text{ rad s}^{-1}$. A particle of mass $m = 1\text{ kg}$ moves along the trajectory $\mathbf{r}'(t) = (t^2 + 1)\,\hat{i} - 6t\,\hat{j} + 4t^3\,\hat{k}\text{ m}$ as measured in $S'$. Calculate at $t = 1\text{ s}$: (i) the Coriolis acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}}$, (ii) the Euler acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{Euler}}$, and (iii) the Centrifugal acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{cf}}$ experienced in $S'$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Step 1 — Evaluate Kinematic Vectors at $t = 1\text{ s}$:**
> $$\boldsymbol{\omega}(1) = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 6\hat{k}\text{ rad s}^{-1}$$
> $$\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t) = 2\hat{i} - 2t\,\hat{j} + 2\hat{k} \implies \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(1) = 2\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\text{ rad s}^{-2}$$
> $$\mathbf{r}'(1) = 2\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}\text{ m}$$
> $$\mathbf{v}'(t) = \frac{d\mathbf{r}'}{dt} = 2t\,\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 12t^2\,\hat{k} \implies \mathbf{v}'(1) = 2\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 12\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-1}$$
>
> **Step 2 — Coriolis Acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}} = -2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')$:**
> $$\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}' = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 6 \\ 2 & -6 & 12 \end{vmatrix} = (-12 + 36)\hat{i} - (24 - 12)\hat{j} + (-12 + 2)\hat{k} = 24\hat{i} - 12\hat{j} - 10\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}$$
> *(Orthogonality check: $\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') = 48 + 12 - 60 = 0$, $\mathbf{v}'\cdot(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') = 48 + 72 - 120 = 0$.)*
> $$\boxed{\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}} = -2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') = -48\hat{i} + 24\hat{j} + 20\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}}$$
> *(State clearly in Mains: in the inertial acceleration expansion $\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{a}' + 2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') + \dots$, the Coriolis term is $+48\hat{i} - 24\hat{j} - 20\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}$, whereas the apparent Coriolis acceleration experienced in $S'$ is its negative, $-48\hat{i} + 24\hat{j} + 20\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}$.)*
>

> $$\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\mathbf{r}' = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -6 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = (-8 + 12)\hat{i} - (8 - 4)\hat{j} + (-12 + 4)\hat{k} = 4\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} - 8\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}$$
> $$\boxed{\mathbf{a}_{\text{Euler}} = -4\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 8\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}}$$
>
> **Step 4 — Centrifugal Acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{cf}} = -\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}') = \omega^2\mathbf{r}' - (\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\boldsymbol{\omega}$:**
> $$\omega^2 = 2^2 + (-1)^2 + 6^2 = 41\text{ s}^{-2}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\mathbf{r}' = 2(2) + (-1)(-6) + 6(4) = 34\text{ m s}^{-1}$$
> $$\mathbf{a}_{\text{cf}} = 41(2\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}) - 34(2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 6\hat{k}) = (82 - 68)\hat{i} + (-246 + 34)\hat{j} + (164 - 204)\hat{k}$$
> $$\boxed{\mathbf{a}_{\text{cf}} = 14\hat{i} - 212\hat{j} - 40\hat{k}\text{ m s}^{-2}}$$

### 3.2 Centrifugal Effects on Earth — Effective Gravity, Plumb-Line Deflection & Earth's Oblateness

Earth rotates about its polar axis once per **sidereal day** ($T_{\text{sid}} = 23\text{ h } 56\text{ min } 4\text{ s} = 86{,}164\text{ s}$), giving $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T_{\text{sid}}} = 7.2921\times 10^{-5}\text{ rad s}^{-1}$. At a surface point at geocentric latitude $\lambda$ (positive in the Northern Hemisphere, negative in the Southern Hemisphere), erect the standard right-handed local tangent frame:

- $\hat{i}$: **Due East** (tangent to parallel of latitude)
- $\hat{j}$: **Due North** (tangent to meridian)
- $\hat{k}$: **Local Radial Upward** (along $\mathbf{R}_E$ from Earth's center)

Because $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ points along the South–North polar axis and makes angle $(90^\circ - \lambda)$ with local radial $\hat{k}$ (angle $\lambda$ with local horizontal $\hat{j}$):

$$\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega\cos\lambda\,\hat{j} + \omega\sin\lambda\,\hat{k}, \qquad \mathbf{R}_E = R_E\hat{k}$$

#### Derivation of Effective Gravity $\mathbf{g}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)$ & Plumb-Line Deflection $\alpha$

For a stationary plumb bob ($\mathbf{v}' = \mathbf{0}$, $\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} = \mathbf{0}$), the effective acceleration due to gravity combines pure Newtonian gravitation $\mathbf{g}_0 = -g_0\hat{k}$ with centrifugal acceleration:

$$\mathbf{g}_{\text{eff}} = \mathbf{g}_0 - \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{R}_E)$$

Compute the centrifugal term directly in local $(\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k})$ coordinates:

$$\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{R}_E = (\omega\cos\lambda\,\hat{j} + \omega\sin\lambda\,\hat{k})\times(R_E\hat{k}) = \omega R_E\cos\lambda\,\hat{i}$$

$$-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{R}_E) = -(\omega\cos\lambda\,\hat{j} + \omega\sin\lambda\,\hat{k})\times(\omega R_E\cos\lambda\,\hat{i}) = -\omega^2 R_E\sin\lambda\cos\lambda\,\hat{j} + \omega^2 R_E\cos^2\lambda\,\hat{k}$$

Combining with $\mathbf{g}_0 = -g_0\hat{k}$ yields the exact vector effective gravity:

$$\mathbf{g}_{\text{eff}} = -\left(\omega^2 R_E\sin\lambda\cos\lambda\right)\hat{j} - \left(g_0 - \omega^2 R_E\cos^2\lambda\right)\hat{k}$$

Define the dimensionless rotational parameter $\eta \equiv \frac{\omega^2 R_E}{g_0} \approx \frac{(7.292\times 10^{-5})^2(6.371\times 10^6)}{9.81} \approx 3.45\times 10^{-3} \ll 1$. Expanding $|\mathbf{g}_{\text{eff}}|$ to first order in $\eta$:

$$g_{\text{eff}}(\lambda) = \sqrt{(g_0 - \omega^2 R_E\cos^2\lambda)^2 + (\omega^2 R_E\sin\lambda\cos\lambda)^2} = g_0\sqrt{1 - 2\eta\cos^2\lambda + \eta^2\cos^2\lambda}$$

$$\boxed{g_{\text{eff}}(\lambda) \approx g_0 - \omega^2 R_E\cos^2\lambda}$$

- **Poles ($\lambda = \pm 90^\circ$):** $g_{\text{eff}} = g_0$ (zero centrifugal reduction).
- **Equator ($\lambda = 0^\circ$):** $g_{\text{eff}} = g_0 - \omega^2 R_E \approx g_0 - 0.0339\text{ m s}^{-2}$ (a $0.345\%$ drop; combined with Earth's oblateness, the observed pole-to-equator difference reaches $9.832 - 9.780 = 0.052\text{ m s}^{-2}$).
- **Plumb-Line Deflection Angle $\alpha$:** The $-\hat{j}$ component tilts a hanging plumb bob slightly **southward** (toward the Equator in the Northern Hemisphere, northward in the Southern Hemisphere) away from the geocentric radial line by angle $\alpha$:

$$\boxed{\tan\alpha = \frac{|g_{\text{eff}, y}|}{|g_{\text{eff}, z}|} = \frac{\omega^2 R_E\sin\lambda\cos\lambda}{g_0 - \omega^2 R_E\cos^2\lambda} \approx \frac{\omega^2 R_E}{2g_0}\sin(2\lambda)}$$

Deflection vanishes at the Poles and Equator and peaks at $\lambda = 45^\circ$, where $\alpha_{\max} \approx \frac{1}{2}(3.45\times 10^{-3})\text{ rad} \approx 0.10^\circ \approx 6\text{ arcminutes}$.

#### Earth's Oblateness from Hydrostatic Equipotential Equilibrium

Over geological timescales, Earth's crust and oceans behave like a fluid whose free surface must lie perpendicular to $\mathbf{g}_{\text{eff}}$—meaning it must form a surface of **constant effective geopotential** $\Phi_{\text{eff}}(r,\lambda) = \phi_{\text{grav}} + \phi_{\text{cf}}$:

$$\Phi_{\text{eff}}(r,\lambda) = -\frac{GM_E}{r} - \frac{1}{2}\omega^2 r^2\cos^2\lambda = \text{const}$$

Equating $\Phi_{\text{eff}}$ at the Pole ($r = R_p$, $\lambda = 90^\circ$) and Equator ($r = R_e$, $\lambda = 0^\circ$):

$$-\frac{GM_E}{R_p} = -\frac{GM_E}{R_e} - \frac{1}{2}\omega^2 R_e^2 \implies GM_E\left(\frac{R_e - R_p}{R_e R_p}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\omega^2 R_e^2$$

Setting $R_e R_p \approx R_E^2$ and $GM_E/R_E^2 = g_0$ yields the first-order (spherical-core) equatorial bulge:

$$\boxed{\Delta R = R_e - R_p = \frac{\omega^2 R_E^2}{2g_0} \approx \frac{(7.292\times 10^{-5})^2 (6.371\times 10^6)^2}{2(9.81)} \approx 11.0\text{ km}}$$

*(Astrophysical Refinement: Because the equatorial bulge itself generates an attractive gravitational quadrupole potential pulling more mass toward the equator, self-consistent Clairaut hydrostatic equilibrium for a uniform fluid sphere raises the flattening to $f = \frac{5}{4}\frac{\omega^2 R_E}{g_0}$, doubling $\Delta R$ to $\sim 27.5\text{ km}$; Earth's dense iron core reduces the actual observed oblateness to $\Delta R_{\text{obs}} = 21.4\text{ km}$, $f \approx 1/298.25$.)*

### 3.3 Coriolis Dynamics on Earth — Free Fall, Horizontal Deflection & Cyclonic Vorticity

Absorbing the time-independent centrifugal term into the local plumb-line vertical ($\mathbf{g}_{\text{eff}} = -g\hat{k}$), the equation of motion of a freely moving projectile near Earth's surface at latitude $\lambda$ is:

$$\ddot{\mathbf{r}}' = -g\hat{k} - 2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\dot{\mathbf{r}}')$$

Expanding $\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\dot{\mathbf{r}}'$ with $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega\cos\lambda\,\hat{j} + \omega\sin\lambda\,\hat{k}$ and $\dot{\mathbf{r}}' = \dot{x}\hat{i} + \dot{y}\hat{j} + \dot{z}\hat{k}$:

$$\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\dot{\mathbf{r}}' = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 0 & \omega\cos\lambda & \omega\sin\lambda \\ \dot{x} & \dot{y} & \dot{z} \end{vmatrix} = \omega(\dot{z}\cos\lambda - \dot{y}\sin\lambda)\,\hat{i} + (\omega\dot{x}\sin\lambda)\,\hat{j} - (\omega\dot{x}\cos\lambda)\,\hat{k}$$

This yields the **Three Coupled Terrestrial Coriolis Equations**:

$$\begin{aligned}
\ddot{x} &= -2\omega(\dot{z}\cos\lambda - \dot{y}\sin\lambda) && \text{(East)} \\
\ddot{y} &= -2\omega\dot{x}\sin\lambda && \text{(North)} \\
\ddot{z} &= -g + 2\omega\dot{x}\cos\lambda && \text{(Up)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### Application 1: Eastward Deflection of a Freely Falling Body

**(i) Setup & Perturbative Scheme:** Release a particle from rest ($\dot{x}_0 = \dot{y}_0 = \dot{z}_0 = 0$) at height $h$ above ground ($x_0 = 0, y_0 = 0, z_0 = h$). Because $\omega T \sim 10^{-4} \ll 1$ for any terrestrial drop, solve iteratively in powers of $\omega$.

**(ii) Step-by-Step Solution:**
- **Zeroth Order ($O(\omega^0)$):** Setting $\omega = 0$:
  $$\ddot{z}^{(0)} = -g \implies \dot{z}^{(0)}(t) = -gt, \qquad z^{(0)}(t) = h - \frac{1}{2}gt^2, \qquad \dot{x}^{(0)} = \dot{y}^{(0)} = 0$$
  Ground impact occurs at $t = T = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}$.
- **First Order ($O(\omega^1)$):** Substitute zeroth-order velocities into the East, North, and Up equations:
  $$\ddot{x}^{(1)} = -2\omega\left[(-gt)\cos\lambda - 0\right] = +2\omega g t\cos\lambda$$
  $$\ddot{y}^{(1)} = 0, \qquad \ddot{z}^{(1)} = -g$$
  Integrate $\ddot{x}^{(1)}$ twice with $x(0) = 0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$:
  $$\dot{x}^{(1)}(t) = \omega g t^2\cos\lambda \implies x^{(1)}(t) = \frac{1}{3}\omega g t^3\cos\lambda$$
- Evaluate at impact time $T = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}$ (so $T^3 = \sqrt{\frac{8h^3}{g^3}}$):

$$\boxed{\Delta x_{\text{East}} = \frac{1}{3}\omega\cos\lambda\sqrt{\frac{8h^3}{g}}}$$

**(iii) Limiting, Sign & Physical Sanity Checks:**
- **Dimensions:** $[\omega]\sqrt{[h^3/g]} = \text{s}^{-1}\sqrt{\text{m}^3/(\text{m s}^{-2})} = \text{s}^{-1}\cdot\text{m s} = \text{m}$.
- **Hemisphere Sign Check:** $\cos\lambda > 0$ for **both** Northern ($\lambda > 0$) and Southern ($\lambda < 0$) Hemispheres! Therefore, a freely falling body deflects **Eastward in both hemispheres**, peaking at the Equator ($\lambda = 0^\circ$) and vanishing at the Poles ($\lambda = \pm 90^\circ$).
- **Second-Order Check ($O(\omega^2)$):** Feeding $\dot{x}^{(1)} = \omega g t^2\cos\lambda$ back into $\ddot{y} = -2\omega\dot{x}\sin\lambda$ gives $\ddot{y}^{(2)} = -2\omega^2 g t^2\sin\lambda\cos\lambda \implies \Delta y^{(2)} = -\frac{1}{6}\omega^2 g T^4 \sin\lambda\cos\lambda$ (a minuscule equatorward deflection $\sim 10^{-4}\Delta x$).
- **Inertial-Frame Subtlety (Why $\frac{1}{3}$ instead of $1$?):** Naively, the release point at radius $R_E + h$ moves eastward faster than the ground at $R_E$ by $\Delta v_E = \omega h\cos\lambda$, which would suggest $\Delta x = (\omega h\cos\lambda)T = \omega\cos\lambda\sqrt{\frac{2h^3}{g}}$—**three times too large**! Why? Viewed from an inertial frame, the body executes a narrow Keplerian ellipse whose radial gravity vector tilts slightly *westward* relative to the initial vertical as the body moves east, canceling $\frac{2}{3}$ of the naive eastward lead.

#### Application 2: Horizontal Deflection, Eötvös Effect & Cyclonic Vorticity

For purely horizontal motion ($\dot{z} = 0$, $\mathbf{v}_h = v_x\hat{i} + v_y\hat{j}$), the Coriolis acceleration reads:

$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}} = \underbrace{(2\omega v_y\sin\lambda)\,\hat{i} - (2\omega v_x\sin\lambda)\,\hat{j}}_{\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}, h}} + \underbrace{(2\omega v_x\cos\lambda)\,\hat{k}}_{\text{Eötvös vertical term}}$$

- **Direction of Horizontal Deflection:** Compute the cross product $\mathbf{v}_h \times \mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}, h} = -2\omega(v_x^2 + v_y^2)\sin\lambda\,\hat{k}$. By the right-hand rule, $\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}, h}$ points strictly **to the right** of $\mathbf{v}_h$ in the Northern Hemisphere ($\lambda > 0$) and **to the left** in the Southern Hemisphere ($\lambda < 0$), with magnitude $a_{\text{Cor}, h} = 2\omega v_h |\sin\lambda|$ independent of compass azimuth!
- **Baer's Law & Eötvös Effect:** Flowing rivers push persistently against their right bank in the NH (left bank in SH). Meanwhile, the vertical term $+2\omega v_x\cos\lambda\,\hat{k}$ slightly *reduces* apparent weight for eastward-moving aircraft/ships and *increases* it for westward motion (**Eötvös effect**).
- **Cyclonic & Anti-Cyclonic Circulation:** Air rushing radially inward toward a **Low-Pressure Center** deflects to the right in the NH until the Coriolis force balances the inward pressure-gradient force (**geostrophic balance**), locking the wind into a **counter-clockwise (CCW) vortex** in the NH and a **clockwise (CW) vortex** in the SH. Outflow from a **High-Pressure Center** (anti-cyclone) circulates oppositely (CW in NH, CCW in SH). Because $\sin\lambda = 0$ at the Equator, horizontal Coriolis torque vanishes there, explaining why tropical cyclones cannot organize within $\sim 5^\circ$ of the Equator.

### 3.4 Complete Vector Mechanics of Foucault's Pendulum

In 1851, Léon Foucault hung a $67\text{ m}$ iron pendulum inside the Panthéon in Paris and watched its swing plane rotate steadily across the marble floor—the first laboratory proof of Earth's rotation without astronomical reference. The physics is crisp: Earth turns beneath the pendulum's inertial oscillation plane, yet at mid-latitudes the local floor tilts continuously with respect to Earth's rotation axis, so the floor twists around the local vertical at rate $\omega_z = \omega\sin\lambda$ rather than the full $\omega$.

#### Unabridged Vector & Complex-Variable Derivation

**(i) Coordinate System & Small-Amplitude Geometry:** Erect the local Earth frame $(\text{East }\hat{i}, \text{North }\hat{j}, \text{Up }\hat{k})$ at latitude $\lambda$, with the pendulum bob of mass $m$ at equilibrium $(0,0,0)$ suspended from pivot $(0,0,l)$ by a massless wire of length $l$. For small oscillations ($x, y \ll l$):

$$z = l - \sqrt{l^2 - x^2 - y^2} \approx \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2l} \implies \dot{z} = \frac{x\dot{x} + y\dot{y}}{l} = O\!\left(\frac{a}{l}\right)\dot{x} \approx 0$$

The string tension vector $\mathbf{T}$ points from bob $(x,y,z)$ to pivot $(0,0,l)$:

$$\mathbf{T} = T\left(-\frac{x}{l}\hat{i} - \frac{y}{l}\hat{j} + \frac{l-z}{l}\hat{k}\right)$$

Since $\ddot{z} \approx 0$ and the small vertical Coriolis term $2m\omega\dot{x}\cos\lambda \ll mg$, vertical equilibrium gives $T \approx mg$ (where $g \equiv g_{\text{eff}}$ already absorbs Earth's centrifugal acceleration!). Hence:

$$T_x \approx -mg\frac{x}{l}, \qquad T_y \approx -mg\frac{y}{l}$$

**(ii) Coupled Horizontal Equations of Motion:** Substituting $\mathbf{F}_{\text{real}} = \mathbf{T} + m\mathbf{g}$ and $\dot{z} \approx 0$ into the horizontal Coriolis equations from Section 3.3, defining natural pendulum frequency $\omega_0 \equiv \sqrt{g/l}$ and vertical Earth spin component $\omega_z \equiv \omega\sin\lambda$:

$$\ddot{x} = -\omega_0^2 x + 2\omega_z\dot{y} \implies \ddot{x} - 2\omega_z\dot{y} + \omega_0^2 x = 0$$

$$\ddot{y} = -\omega_0^2 y - 2\omega_z\dot{x} \implies \ddot{y} + 2\omega_z\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 y = 0$$

**(iii) Complex-Plane Decoupling:** Introduce the complex horizontal coordinate $q(t) \equiv x(t) + i y(t)$. Multiply the $y$-equation by $i$ and add it to the $x$-equation:

$$(\ddot{x} + i\ddot{y}) + 2\omega_z(-\dot{y} + i\dot{x}) + \omega_0^2(x + iy) = 0$$

Factoring $i$ from $(-\dot{y} + i\dot{x}) = i(\dot{x} + i\dot{y}) = i\dot{q}$ decouples the system into a single second-order linear ODE:

$$\ddot{q} + 2i\omega_z\dot{q} + \omega_0^2 q = 0$$

Try exponential solution $q(t) = e^{rt}$:

$$r^2 + 2i\omega_z r + \omega_0^2 = 0 \implies r = \frac{-2i\omega_z \pm \sqrt{-4\omega_z^2 - 4\omega_0^2}}{2} = -i\omega_z \pm i\sqrt{\omega_0^2 + \omega_z^2}$$

Define $\Omega \equiv \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + \omega_z^2}$. Because $\omega_z \le 7.29\times 10^{-5}\text{ rad s}^{-1} \ll \omega_0 \sim 0.4\text{–}3\text{ rad s}^{-1}$, we have $\omega_z^2 / \omega_0^2 \sim 10^{-8}$, so $\Omega \approx \omega_0$ to eight significant digits. The general solution is:

$$\boxed{q(t) = e^{-i\omega_z t}\left(A e^{i\Omega t} + B e^{-i\Omega t}\right) \approx e^{-i(\omega\sin\lambda)t}\left(A e^{i\omega_0 t} + B e^{-i\omega_0 t}\right)}$$

**(iv) Physical Interpretation & Two Canonical Initial Conditions:**
In the complex $(x,y)$ plane, multiplying a trajectory by $e^{-i\omega_z t}$ rotates it **clockwise** (decreasing polar angle $\phi(t) = \phi_0 - \omega_z t$) when viewed from above in the Northern Hemisphere ($\omega_z = \omega\sin\lambda > 0$). Thus the plane of oscillation precesses at angular rate:

$$\boxed{\Omega_F = \omega\sin\lambda, \qquad T_F = \frac{2\pi}{\omega|\sin\lambda|} = \frac{T_{\text{sid}}}{|\sin\lambda|} \approx \frac{23.93\text{ h}}{|\sin\lambda|}}$$

- **Mode 1 — Released from Rest at $(x_0, 0)$:** Imposing $q(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{q}(0) = 0$ gives $A + B = x_0$ and $i(\Omega - \omega_z)A - i(\Omega + \omega_z)B = 0 \implies A = \frac{x_0}{2}\left(1 + \frac{\omega_z}{\Omega}\right)$, $B = \frac{x_0}{2}\left(1 - \frac{\omega_z}{\Omega}\right)$:
  $$q(t) = x_0 e^{-i\omega_z t}\left[\cos(\Omega t) + i\frac{\omega_z}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)\right]$$
  The bob traces a **cuspate hypocycloid rosette** that comes to momentary rest at outer cusps and *narrowly misses the origin* by minimum distance $d_{\min} = x_0\frac{\omega_z}{\Omega}$ (because releasing from rest on Earth gives it a tiny inertial tangential velocity!).
- **Mode 2 — Impulsively Launched Through Origin with Velocity $(v_0, 0)$:** Imposing $q(0) = 0$ and $\dot{q}(0) = v_0$ yields $A = -B = \frac{v_0}{2i\Omega}$:
  $$q(t) = \frac{v_0}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)\,e^{-i\omega_z t} \implies x(t) = \frac{v_0}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)\cos(\omega_z t), \quad y(t) = -\frac{v_0}{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)\sin(\omega_z t)$$
  This produces a clean **daisy rosette passing strictly through the origin** every half-period.

**(v) Limiting-Case Checks:**
- **North Pole ($\lambda = +90^\circ$):** $\Omega_F = +\omega$, $T_F = 23.93\text{ h}$ (CW)—pendulum stays locked to distant stars while Earth spins CCW beneath it.
- **Equator ($\lambda = 0^\circ$):** $\Omega_F = 0$, $T_F \to \infty$—no local twist of the floor around the vertical axis, hence zero precession.
- **Southern Hemisphere ($\lambda < 0$):** $\Omega_F < 0 \implies e^{-i\omega_z t} = e^{+i|\omega_z|t}$—oscillation plane rotates **counter-clockwise** viewed from above.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Eastward Drop Deflection & Foucault Pendulum Precession Rates

## 4. Gravitational Fields, Potentials, Gauss–Poisson BVP & Gravitational Self-Energy

### 4.1 Gravitational Field, Potential, Gauss's Law & Poisson's Equation

Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation is an action-at-a-distance pairwise inverse-square law. Recasting it into **local field equations**—gravitational field intensity $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r})$ (force per unit test mass, units $\text{N kg}^{-1} = \text{m s}^{-2}$) and gravitational potential $\phi(\mathbf{r})$ (potential energy per unit test mass, units $\text{J kg}^{-1} = \text{m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}$, anchored at $\phi(\infty) = 0$)—replaces tedious 3D vector integrals with Gauss's flux theorem and Poisson's partial differential equation.

For any continuous mass density $\rho(\mathbf{r}')$:

$$\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) = -G\iiint_V \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}')(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|^3}\,d^3r', \qquad \phi(\mathbf{r}) = -G\iiint_V \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|}\,d^3r'$$

Since $\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}\right) = -\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|^3}$, the field is strictly conservative and irrotational everywhere:

$$\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) = -\nabla\phi(\mathbf{r}), \qquad \nabla\times\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{0}$$

#### Unabridged Derivation of Gauss's Law & Poisson's Equation for Gravity

**(i) Flux from a Single Point Mass $m$:** Consider a closed orientable Gaussian surface $S$ with outward area element $d\mathbf{S} = \hat{n}\,dS$, and a point mass $m$ at origin $O$. Its field is $\mathbf{g} = -\frac{Gm}{r^2}\hat{r}$. The gravitational flux through $S$ is:

$$\Phi_g = \oiint_S \mathbf{g}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = -Gm\oiint_S \frac{\hat{r}\cdot d\mathbf{S}}{r^2} = -Gm\oiint_S d\Omega$$

where $d\Omega = \frac{\hat{r}\cdot d\mathbf{S}}{r^2}$ is the signed solid angle subtended by $d\mathbf{S}$ at $m$.
- If $m$ lies **inside** $S$, every radial ray exits $S$ one more time than it enters, so $\oiint_S d\Omega = +4\pi$, yielding $\Phi_g = -4\pi G m$.
- If $m$ lies **outside** $S$, every radial ray enters and exits $S$ an equal number of times with opposite signs of $\hat{r}\cdot\hat{n}$, so $\oiint_S d\Omega = 0$, yielding $\Phi_g = 0$.

**(ii) Superposition & Integral Gauss's Law:** Because Newtonian gravitation obeys linear superposition, summing over any discrete or continuous distribution inside $S$ (total enclosed mass $M_{\text{enc}} = \iiint_V \rho\,d^3r$) gives **Gauss's Law for Gravity (Integral Form)**:

$$\boxed{\oiint_S \mathbf{g}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = -4\pi G M_{\text{enc}} = -4\pi G \iiint_V \rho(\mathbf{r})\,d^3r}$$

**(iii) Differential Gauss's Law & Poisson's Equation:** Applying the **Gauss Divergence Theorem** $\oiint_S \mathbf{g}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_V (\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g})\,d^3r$ over an arbitrary volume $V$:

$$\iiint_V \big[\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g} + 4\pi G\rho(\mathbf{r})\big]\,d^3r = 0 \quad \forall\,V \implies \boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) = -4\pi G\rho(\mathbf{r})}$$

Substituting $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\phi$ yields **Poisson's Equation for Gravitation** (and **Laplace's Equation** in mass-free vacuum where $\rho = 0$):

$$\boxed{\nabla^2\phi(\mathbf{r}) = 4\pi G\rho(\mathbf{r}) \qquad (\text{vacuum: } \nabla^2\phi = 0)}$$

**(iv) Direct Green's Function & Sign Sanity Check:** Using the 3D Dirac delta identity $\nabla^2\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}\right) = -4\pi\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')$ directly on $\phi(\mathbf{r}) = -G\int\frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}\,d^3r'$ confirms $\nabla^2\phi(\mathbf{r}) = (-G)(-4\pi)\rho(\mathbf{r}) = +4\pi G\rho(\mathbf{r})$. Contrast the sign with electrostatics ($\nabla^2 V_e = -\rho_e/\epsilon_0$): gravity carries a **$+$ sign** on the RHS of Poisson's equation because like masses **attract** (flux lines sink *into* mass), whereas like electric charges repel (flux lines diverge *out* of positive charge).

### 4.2 Exact Gravitational Potentials & Fields of Spherical Shells and Solid Spheres

#### A. Thin Uniform Spherical Shell (Mass $M$, Radius $R$, Surface Density $\sigma = \frac{M}{4\pi R^2}$)

**(i) Direct Ring-Element Integration for $\phi(r)$:** Place the shell center at the origin and evaluate $\phi$ at field point $P$ on the $+z$-axis at distance $r$ from the center. Slice the shell into thin circular rings perpendicular to the $z$-axis at polar angle $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ and width $R\,d\theta$:

- Ring radius: $R\sin\theta$; ring area: $dA = (2\pi R\sin\theta)(R\,d\theta) = 2\pi R^2\sin\theta\,d\theta$.
- Ring mass: $dm = \sigma\,dA = \frac{M}{4\pi R^2}(2\pi R^2\sin\theta\,d\theta) = \frac{M}{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta$.
- Distance $s$ from every point on the ring to $P$ obeys the **Law of Cosines**:
  $$s^2 = r^2 + R^2 - 2rR\cos\theta$$
  Differentiating at fixed $r$ and $R$:
  $$2s\,ds = 2rR\sin\theta\,d\theta \implies \sin\theta\,d\theta = \frac{s\,ds}{rR}$$

Notice the **critical integration limits**: at $\theta = 0$, $s_{\min} = \sqrt{(r-R)^2} = |r - R|$ (never carelessly written as $r - R$!), while at $\theta = \pi$, $s_{\max} = r + R$. Integrating $d\phi = -\frac{G\,dm}{s}$:

$$\phi(r) = -\int_0^\pi \frac{G M}{2s}\sin\theta\,d\theta = -\frac{GM}{2rR}\int_{|r-R|}^{r+R} ds = -\frac{GM}{2rR}\Big[(r + R) - |r - R|\Big]$$

Evaluating both regimes cleanly:

- **Case 1 — Outside the Shell ($r > R$):** $|r - R| = r - R \implies (r + R) - (r - R) = 2R$:
  $$\boxed{\phi_{\text{out}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{r}, \qquad \mathbf{g}_{\text{out}}(r) = -\frac{d\phi}{dr}\hat{r} = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{r} \qquad (r > R)}$$
  *(Newton's Shell Theorem I: Outside a uniform spherical shell, gravity is identical to a point mass $M$ concentrated at the center.)*
- **Case 2 — Inside the Shell ($r < R$):** $|r - R| = R - r \implies (r + R) - (R - r) = 2r$:
  $$\boxed{\phi_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{R} = \text{const}, \qquad \mathbf{g}_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{d\phi}{dr}\hat{r} = \mathbf{0} \qquad (r < R)}$$
  *(Newton's Shell Theorem II: The gravitational field vanishes identically inside a hollow spherical shell—a property unique to exact $1/r^2$ forces!)*
- **Boundary Check at $r = R$:** Potential is **continuous** ($\phi(R^-) = \phi(R^+) = -\frac{GM}{R}$), whereas the radial field suffers a finite **step discontinuity** dictated by Gauss's pillbox law:
  $$g_r(R^+) - g_r(R^-) = -\frac{GM}{R^2} - 0 = -4\pi G\sigma$$

#### B. Uniform Solid Sphere (Mass $M$, Radius $R$, Uniform Density $\rho = \frac{3M}{4\pi R^3}$)

**(i) Field $\mathbf{g}(r)$ via Spherical Gauss's Law:** By spherical symmetry, $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) = g_r(r)\hat{r}$. Drawing a concentric Gaussian sphere of radius $r$ gives $4\pi r^2 g_r(r) = -4\pi G M_{\text{enc}}(r)$:

- **Outside ($r \ge R$):** $M_{\text{enc}} = M \implies \boxed{\mathbf{g}_{\text{out}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{r}}$.
- **Inside ($r \le R$):** $M_{\text{enc}}(r) = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho = M\frac{r^3}{R^3} \implies \boxed{\mathbf{g}_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{R^3}\mathbf{r} = -\frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho\,\mathbf{r}}$.

Unlike the thin shell, **$\mathbf{g}(r)$ is continuous across $r = R$** ($g_r(R^-) = g_r(R^+) = -GM/R^2$) because no surface mass sheet exists. Furthermore, a particle dropped into a straight diametral tunnel through a uniform planet executes exact Simple Harmonic Motion with $\omega_{\text{SHM}} = \sqrt{GM/R^3} = \sqrt{g/R}$ ($T \approx 84.5\text{ min}$ for Earth).

**(ii) Potential $\phi(r)$ via Line Integration & Shell Summation:**
- **Outside ($r \ge R$):**
  $$\phi_{\text{out}}(r) = -\int_\infty^r \left(-\frac{GM}{r'^2}\right)dr' = \boxed{-\frac{GM}{r}}$$
- **Inside ($r \le R$):** Integrate continuously inward from the surface $\phi(R) = -\frac{GM}{R}$:
  $$\phi_{\text{in}}(r) - \phi(R) = -\int_R^r g_r(r')\,dr' = -\int_R^r \left(-\frac{GM}{R^3}r'\right)dr' = \frac{GM}{2R^3}\left(r^2 - R^2\right)$$
  $$\phi_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{R} + \frac{GM}{2R^3}(r^2 - R^2) = \boxed{-\frac{GM}{2R^3}\left(3R^2 - r^2\right)}$$
  *(Alternative Check by Shell Summation: Decompose the solid sphere at radius $r$ into an inner solid sphere of mass $M_1 = M(r/R)^3$ giving $\phi_1 = -\frac{GM_1}{r} = -\frac{GMr^2}{R^3}$, plus concentric outer shells from $r$ to $R$ each contributing $d\phi_2 = -\frac{G(4\pi r'^2\rho\,dr')}{r'}$, giving $\phi_2 = -4\pi G\rho\frac{R^2-r^2}{2} = -\frac{3GM}{2R^3}(R^2-r^2)$. Summing $\phi_1 + \phi_2$ yields $-\frac{GM}{2R^3}(3R^2 - r^2)$ identically!)*

**(iii) Poisson & Center Sanity Checks:**
- At center $r = 0$: $\phi(0) = -\frac{3}{2}\frac{GM}{R} = \frac{3}{2}\phi(R)$ (deepest potential well; zero slope $\phi'(0) = 0$).
- Spherical Laplacian inside ($r < R$):
  $$\nabla^2\phi_{\text{in}} = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{d\phi_{\text{in}}}{dr}\right) = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\cdot\frac{GMr}{R^3}\right) = \frac{3GM}{R^3} = 4\pi G\rho \quad \checkmark$$

#### C. Thick Uniform Spherical Shell (Inner Radius $a$, Outer Radius $b$, Density $\rho$, Mass $M = \frac{4}{3}\pi\rho(b^3 - a^3)$)

Applying Gauss's law and continuous line integration across the three zones yields:

- **$r > b$:** $g_r = -\frac{GM}{r^2}$, $\quad \phi(r) = -\frac{GM}{r}$
- **$a \le r \le b$:** $M_{\text{enc}}(r) = \frac{4}{3}\pi\rho(r^3 - a^3) \implies g_r(r) = -\frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho\left(r - \frac{a^3}{r^2}\right)$, $\quad \phi(r) = -2\pi G\rho\left(b^2 - \frac{r^2}{3} - \frac{2a^3}{3r}\right)$

### 4.3 Gravitational Self-Energy ($U$) & Astrophysical Binding Applications

The **gravitational self-energy** $U$ of an extended body is the total work required to assemble it from infinitely dispersed dust ($r \to \infty$) against mutual gravity—equivalently, the **negative of the gravitational binding energy** ($E_{\text{bind}} = -U > 0$) needed to blow the body completely apart to infinity.

#### Method 1: Incremental Concentric Shell Assembly (Uniform Solid Sphere of Mass $M$, Radius $R$)

**(i) Setup:** Imagine building a uniform solid sphere of constant density $\rho = \frac{3M}{4\pi R^3}$ layer by layer. At an intermediate stage, a spherical core of radius $r$ and mass $m(r) = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\rho$ is already assembled.

**(ii) Step-by-Step Integration:** Bring an infinitesimal shell of mass $dm = 4\pi r^2\rho\,dr$ from infinity onto the surface of this core (where potential is $\phi_{\text{core}}(r) = -\frac{Gm(r)}{r}$). The incremental potential energy change is:

$$dU = \phi_{\text{core}}(r)\,dm = -\frac{G m(r)\,dm}{r} = -\frac{G\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\rho\right)(4\pi r^2\rho\,dr)}{r} = -\frac{16}{3}\pi^2 G\rho^2 r^4\,dr$$

Integrating from $r = 0$ to $r = R$:

$$U = -\frac{16}{3}\pi^2 G\rho^2 \int_0^R r^4\,dr = -\frac{16}{15}\pi^2 G\rho^2 R^5$$

Substitute $\rho = \frac{3M}{4\pi R^3} \implies \rho^2 = \frac{9M^2}{16\pi^2 R^6}$:

$$U = -\frac{16}{15}\pi^2 G \left(\frac{9M^2}{16\pi^2 R^6}\right) R^5 = \boxed{-\frac{3}{5}\frac{GM^2}{R}}$$

*(Thin Spherical Shell Check: Assembling a thin shell of fixed radius $R$ from fraction $q = 0$ to $q = M$ gives $dU = -\frac{G q\,dq}{R} \implies U_{\text{shell}} = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{GM^2}{R}$. Notice $|U_{\text{solid}}| = 0.6\frac{GM^2}{R} > |U_{\text{shell}}| = 0.5\frac{GM^2}{R}$ because interior mass elements sit closer together.)*

#### Method 2: Continuous Potential Integral & Negative Gravitational Field Energy Density

For any arbitrary mass distribution $\rho(\mathbf{r})$, generalizing the discrete pair sum $U = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j} m_i \phi_{ij}$ (with the factor $\frac{1}{2}$ preventing double-counting of every pair!) yields:

$$U = \frac{1}{2}\iiint_V \rho(\mathbf{r})\phi(\mathbf{r})\,d^3r$$

*(Quick Verification for Uniform Solid Sphere: $U = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^R \rho\left[-\frac{GM}{2R^3}(3R^2 - r^2)\right](4\pi r^2\,dr) = -\frac{\pi G M \rho}{R^3}\left(R^5 - \frac{R^5}{5}\right) = -\frac{4\pi G M \rho R^2}{5} = -\frac{3}{5}\frac{GM^2}{R}$!)*

Now substitute Gauss's Law $\rho = -\frac{1}{4\pi G}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g}$ and integrate over **all space** $\mathbb{R}^3$:

$$U = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^3} \phi(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g})\,d^3r$$

Apply the vector product identity $\nabla\cdot(\phi\mathbf{g}) = \phi(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g}) + \mathbf{g}\cdot(\nabla\phi) = \phi(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{g}) - |\mathbf{g}|^2$:

$$U = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left[\oiint_{S_\infty} \phi\mathbf{g}\cdot d\mathbf{S} + \iiint_{\mathbb{R}^3} |\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r})|^2\,d^3r\right]$$

For any localized mass distribution, as $r \to \infty$, $\phi \sim r^{-1}$, $|\mathbf{g}| \sim r^{-2}$, and $dS \sim r^2$, so the boundary integral decays as $O(r^{-1}) \to 0$, leaving the **Field Energy Representation**:

$$\boxed{U = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^3} |\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r})|^2\,d^3r \implies u_g(\mathbf{r}) = -\frac{|\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r})|^2}{8\pi G}}$$

**Physical Sanity Check on Field Energy Localization (Uniform Sphere):**
- Inside ($0 \le r \le R$, $|\mathbf{g}| = \frac{GMr}{R^3}$): $U_{\text{in}} = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int_0^R \frac{G^2 M^2 r^2}{R^6}(4\pi r^2\,dr) = -\frac{GM^2}{2R^6}\frac{R^5}{5} = -\frac{1}{10}\frac{GM^2}{R}$.
- Outside ($R \le r < \infty$, $|\mathbf{g}| = \frac{GM}{r^2}$): $U_{\text{out}} = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int_R^\infty \frac{G^2 M^2}{r^4}(4\pi r^2\,dr) = -\frac{GM^2}{2R}$.
- Total: $U_{\text{in}} + U_{\text{out}} = \left(-\frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{GM^2}{R} = -\frac{3}{5}\frac{GM^2}{R}$! Remarkable physical insight: **$83.3\%$ of a uniform sphere's gravitational self-energy resides in the vacuum field outside the sphere**, and the field energy density $u_g$ is strictly **negative** (opposite to electrostatic $u_e = +\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_0|\mathbf{E}|^2$).

#### Astrophysical Binding Application — The Kelvin–Helmholtz Contraction Paradox

In the 19th century, Lord Kelvin and Hermann von Helmholtz proposed that the Sun shines by slowly contracting under self-gravity. By the **Virial Theorem** for an inverse-square self-gravitating gas ($2\langle T_{\text{thermal}}\rangle + U = 0$), exactly half of the gravitational energy released during contraction heats the star's interior while the remaining half ($\Delta E_{\text{rad}} = \frac{1}{2}|U_\odot|$) radiates into space at luminosity $L_\odot$. Estimating the Sun's lifetime from a uniform-density model gives the **Kelvin–Helmholtz timescale**:

$$t_{\text{KH}} = \frac{|U_\odot|/2}{L_\odot} = \frac{3 G M_\odot^2}{10 R_\odot L_\odot} \approx 10^7\text{ years}$$

Geological and fossil records already proved Earth to be billions of years old ($4.54\text{ Gyr}$)—a factor of $450$ longer than $t_{\text{KH}}$. This stark quantitative mismatch from elementary gravitational self-energy proved that chemical and gravitational energy reservoirs are wholly inadequate to power stars, pointing physics directly toward thermonuclear fusion ($E = \Delta m c^2$).

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Radially Inhomogeneous Sphere $\rho(r) = \rho_0 (a/r)$ — Field, Potential & Self-Energy
> **Problem:** The mass density inside a solid sphere of radius $a$ varies radially as $\rho(r) = \rho_0 \frac{a}{r}$ for $0 < r \le a$ (where $\rho_0$ is the surface density) and $\rho = 0$ for $r > a$. Find: (i) the total mass $M$ of the sphere, (ii) the gravitational field $\mathbf{g}(r)$ inside ($r < a$) and at $r = 2a$, (iii) the potential $\phi(r)$ everywhere, and (iv) the gravitational self-energy $U$ in terms of $G, M, a$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **(i) Enclosed & Total Mass:**
> $$M(r) = \int_0^r \rho(r')\,4\pi r'^2\,dr' = \int_0^r \left(\rho_0\frac{a}{r'}\right)4\pi r'^2\,dr' = 4\pi\rho_0 a \int_0^r r'\,dr' = 2\pi\rho_0 a r^2 \quad (r \le a)$$
> Setting $r = a$:
> $$\boxed{M = M(a) = 2\pi\rho_0 a^3}$$
>
> **(ii) Gravitational Field via Gauss's Law ($g_r(r) = -GM(r)/r^2$):**
> - **Inside ($0 < r \le a$):**
>   $$g_r(r) = -\frac{G(2\pi\rho_0 a r^2)}{r^2} = \boxed{-2\pi G\rho_0 a = -\frac{GM}{a^2} = \text{const}}$$
>   *(Striking result: a $1/r$ density profile produces a uniform-magnitude radial gravitational field inside!)*
> - **Outside ($r \ge a$):**
>   $$g_r(r) = -\frac{GM}{r^2} = -\frac{2\pi G\rho_0 a^3}{r^2}$$
> - **At $r = 2a$:**
>   $$\boxed{\mathbf{g}(2a) = -\frac{2\pi G\rho_0 a^3}{(2a)^2}\hat{r} = -\frac{1}{2}\pi G\rho_0 a\,\hat{r} = -\frac{GM}{4a^2}\hat{r}}$$
>
> **(iii) Potential $\phi(r)$:**
> - **Outside ($r \ge a$):** $\phi(r) = -\frac{GM}{r} = -\frac{2\pi G\rho_0 a^3}{r}$.
> - **Inside ($r \le a$):** Integrate from surface $\phi(a) = -\frac{GM}{a}$:
>   $$\phi(r) = \phi(a) - \int_a^r \left(-\frac{GM}{a^2}\right)dr' = -\frac{GM}{a} + \frac{GM}{a^2}(r - a) = \boxed{-\frac{GM}{a}\left(2 - \frac{r}{a}\right)}$$
>   *(Check: $-\frac{d\phi}{dr} = -\frac{GM}{a^2} = g_r$, and $\nabla^2\phi = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{GMr^2}{a^2}\right) = \frac{2GM}{a^2 r} = 4\pi G\rho_0\frac{a}{r} = 4\pi G\rho(r)$.)*
>
> **(iv) Gravitational Self-Energy $U$:**
> Using shell assembly with $dm = 4\pi r^2\left(\rho_0\frac{a}{r}\right)dr = 4\pi\rho_0 a r\,dr$:
> $$U = -\int_0^a \frac{G M(r)\,dm}{r} = -\int_0^a \frac{G(2\pi\rho_0 a r^2)(4\pi\rho_0 a r\,dr)}{r} = -8\pi^2 G\rho_0^2 a^2 \int_0^a r^2\,dr = -\frac{8}{3}\pi^2 G\rho_0^2 a^5$$
> Since $M^2 = 4\pi^2\rho_0^2 a^6 \implies \pi^2\rho_0^2 a^5 = \frac{M^2}{4a}$:
> $$\boxed{U = -\frac{2}{3}\frac{GM^2}{a}}$$
> *(Sanity check: $\frac{2}{3} = 0.667 > \frac{3}{5} = 0.600$, confirming tighter gravitational binding because mass is concentrated toward the core.)*

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Gravitational Self-Energy of Earth & Kelvin–Helmholtz Age of the Sun
> **Problem:** Take $G = 6.674\times 10^{-11}\text{ N m}^2\text{ kg}^{-2}$.
> 1. Treating Earth as a uniform sphere of mass $M_e = 6.0\times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$ and radius $R_e = 6.4\times 10^6\text{ m}$, compute its gravitational self-energy $U_e$.
> 2. Treating the Sun as a uniform sphere ($M_\odot = 1.989\times 10^{30}\text{ kg}$, $R_\odot = 6.96\times 10^8\text{ m}$, luminosity $L_\odot = 3.828\times 10^{26}\text{ W}$), compute its gravitational self-energy and Virial Kelvin–Helmholtz contraction timescale in years ($1\text{ yr} = 3.156\times 10^7\text{ s}$).
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part 1 — Earth's Self-Energy:**
> $$U_e = -\frac{3}{5}\frac{G M_e^2}{R_e} = -0.6 \times \frac{(6.674\times 10^{-11})(6.0\times 10^{24})^2}{6.4\times 10^6}$$
> $$= -0.6 \times \frac{(6.674\times 10^{-11})(3.6\times 10^{49})}{6.4\times 10^6} = -\frac{1.4416\times 10^{39}}{6.4\times 10^6} = \boxed{-2.25\times 10^{32}\text{ J}}$$
> *(Astrophysical note: Earth's high-density iron-nickel core increases the real PREM-model binding energy slightly to $\approx 2.49\times 10^{32}\text{ J}$.)*
>
> **Part 2 — Sun's Self-Energy & Kelvin–Helmholtz Timescale:**
> $$U_\odot = -\frac{3}{5}\frac{G M_\odot^2}{R_\odot} = -0.6 \times \frac{(6.674\times 10^{-11})(1.989\times 10^{30})^2}{6.96\times 10^8}$$
> $$= -0.6 \times \frac{(6.674\times 10^{-11})(3.956\times 10^{60})}{6.96\times 10^8} = \boxed{-2.28\times 10^{41}\text{ J}}$$
> By the Virial Theorem, half of $|U_\odot|$ ($1.14\times 10^{41}\text{ J}$) is radiated away:
> $$t_{\text{KH}} = \frac{|U_\odot|/2}{L_\odot} = \frac{1.138\times 10^{41}\text{ J}}{3.828\times 10^{26}\text{ W}} = 2.973\times 10^{14}\text{ s}$$
> $$t_{\text{KH}} = \frac{2.973\times 10^{14}}{3.156\times 10^7}\text{ yr} = \boxed{9.42\times 10^6\text{ years (}\approx 9.4\text{ Myr)}}$$
> Even without the Virial factor of $1/2$ ($\sim 18.8\text{ Myr}$) or with realistic central density peaking ($\sim 30\text{ Myr}$), gravitational contraction falls short of Earth's $4.5\text{ Gyr}$ age by more than two orders of magnitude.

## 6. Exam-Hall Execution — Traps & Hand-Drawing Diagram Specs

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **Coriolis Sign Convention Split:** In the **inertial acceleration** expansion $\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{a}' + 2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}')$, the Coriolis term carries a **$+$ sign**; when moved to the RHS of $m\mathbf{a}' = \mathbf{F}_{\text{real}} - 2m(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}') - \dots$, the **fictitious Coriolis force and apparent acceleration in $S'$ carry a $-$ sign** ($\mathbf{a}_{\text{Cor}} = -2\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}'$). State your convention explicitly in one line so examiners never penalize a sign flip.
> 2. **Latitude $\lambda$ vs. Colatitude $\theta$ Trap:** Never confuse geographic **latitude** $\lambda$ (measured from the Equator, where $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega\cos\lambda\,\hat{j} + \omega\sin\lambda\,\hat{k}$, East deflection $\propto \cos\lambda$, Foucault precession $\propto \sin\lambda$) with spherical polar **colatitude** $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} - \lambda$ (measured from the North Pole, which swaps $\sin \leftrightarrow \cos$!). Declare your angle explicitly on a sketch.
> 3. **Non-Galilean Variable-Mass Trap:** Writing $F = m\dot{v} + v\dot{m}$ for a rocket is physically false unless exhaust leaves at zero velocity in the lab frame. Always derive $m\dot{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} + \mathbf{u}_{\text{rel}}\dot{m}$ via the closed-system momentum increment $\mathbf{P}(t+dt) - \mathbf{P}(t)$.
> 4. **Absolute-Value Lower Limit in the Spherical Shell Integral:** When transforming $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ to $s = \sqrt{r^2 + R^2 - 2rR\cos\theta}$, writing the lower limit as $(r - R)$ instead of $|r - R|$ gives the wrong sign inside the shell ($r < R$). Always keep $|r - R|$ and split the cases explicitly.
> 5. **Missing Factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ in Continuous Self-Energy:** Using $U = \int \rho\phi\,dV$ instead of $U = \frac{1}{2}\int \rho\phi\,dV$ double-counts every mass pair and yields $-\frac{6}{5}\frac{GM^2}{R}$ instead of $-\frac{3}{5}\frac{GM^2}{R}$.
> 6. **Solar Day vs. Sidereal Day for Earth's $\omega$:** Earth completes $2\pi$ radians in one **sidereal day** ($86{,}164\text{ s} \implies \omega = 7.292\times 10^{-5}\text{ rad s}^{-1}$), not $86{,}400\text{ s}$ ($7.272\times 10^{-5}\text{ rad s}^{-1}$). The $0.27\%$ difference shows up visibly in Foucault periods ($23.93\text{ h}$ at the pole, not $24.00\text{ h}$).
> 7. **Scalar Potential Sign Ambiguity:** In mechanics, potential energy satisfies $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla V$; in pure vector calculus, "scalar potential" is sometimes defined by $\mathbf{F} = +\nabla\psi$. State $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla V$ explicitly before integrating.

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Skipping Poisson's Basis-Rotation Proof:** Jumping straight from $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}'$ to $\mathbf{a}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{a}' + 2\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}' + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}')$ without proving $\frac{d\hat{e}'_i}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\hat{e}'_i$ forfeits 30–40% of derivation step-marks on 15- and 20-mark rotating-frame questions.
- **Unstated Frame & Sign Conventions on Rotating Numericals:** On CSE 2013/2022 archetypes, failing to distinguish between the inertial Coriolis term $+2(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')$ and the apparent rotating-frame Coriolis force $-2m(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}')$, or forgetting that $\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} = \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{F}_{\text{Euler}} = \mathbf{0}$ for uniform rotation, immediately costs half the marks.
- **Careless Limits & Missing Continuity Checks in Spherical Potentials:** Writing the lower ring-distance limit as $r - R$ instead of $|r - R|$ in the spherical shell potential derivation, or omitting the physical comment that $\phi$ is continuous while $\mathbf{g}$ jumps by $-4\pi G\sigma$ at $r = R$, signals rote memorization to university-professor evaluators.
- **Neglecting the Negative Sign & Physical Units in Self-Energy Numericals:** Reporting Earth's gravitational self-energy as $+2.25\times 10^{32}\text{ J}$ without explaining the negative sign (bound system vs. binding energy) or plugging $R_e$ in kilometres instead of metres is the most frequent numerical blunder in Paper I Section A.
