# Chapter 2: Magnetostatics, Vector Potential, Magnetic Shells & Magnetic Matter

## 1. Steady Currents, Biot-Savart Law, Ampere's Law and Magnetic Vector Potential

Whereas static charges produce radial, curl-free electric fields, steady electric currents generate circulating, divergence-free magnetic fields $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})$. Magnetic field lines never start or terminate at isolated point charges; they form closed loops wrapping around current streams.

Because $\mathbf{B}$ is solenoidal ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$) rather than irrotational, magnetostatics replaces the scalar potential $V$ of electrostatics with the **magnetic vector potential** $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})$ satisfying $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$. Exploiting gauge freedom collapses the entire vector problem into three decoupled Poisson equations, directly revealing the dipole hierarchy and magnetic interaction laws.

### 1.1 Lorentz Force, Steady Currents, and the Continuity Condition

A point charge $q$ moving with velocity $\mathbf{v}$ in electric field $\mathbf{E}$ and magnetic induction (magnetic field) $\mathbf{B}$ experiences the **Lorentz force**:

$$\mathbf{F} = q\left(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}\right)$$

Because the magnetic component $\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})$ is always orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$, magnetic forces perform **zero mechanical work** on free point charges:

$$dW_m = \mathbf{F}_m \cdot d\mathbf{r} = q(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\mathbf{v}\,dt = 0$$

For continuous charge flows, current sources are represented by three hierarchical densities:
- **Line current $I$** (SI unit: $\text{A}$): $dq\,\mathbf{v} \to I\,d\mathbf{l}'$, giving force $\mathbf{F} = \int I\,d\mathbf{l}' \times \mathbf{B}$.
- **Surface current density $\mathbf{K}$** (SI unit: $\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$): current per unit perpendicular width, $dq\,\mathbf{v} \to \mathbf{K}\,da'$.
- **Volume current density $\mathbf{J} = \rho\mathbf{v}$** (SI unit: $\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}$): current per unit perpendicular area, $dq\,\mathbf{v} \to \mathbf{J}\,d\tau'$.

Charge conservation obeys the universal **continuity equation** $\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$. Magnetostatics restricts attention to **steady currents** where charge neither piles up nor depletes anywhere ($\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} = 0$):

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0}$$

Physically, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ mandates that every steady current flows in closed continuous loops (or extends to infinity).

### 1.2 Biot-Savart Law and Vector-Calculus Derivation of Ampere's Law

The magnetic field $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})$ generated in free space (vacuum permeability $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}$) by a steady current distribution is given by the **Biot-Savart Law**:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int \frac{I\,d\mathbf{l}' \times \hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'} \frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') \times \hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\,d\tau'}$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}} \equiv \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'$ points from the source element $\mathbf{r}'$ to the field point $\mathbf{r}$, $\mathcal{R} = |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|$, and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}} = \boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}/\mathcal{R}$.

#### Two Benchmark Direct Evaluations
1. **Straight Wire Segment** (along the $z$-axis at perpendicular distance $s$ from field point $P$, subtending angles $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ relative to the perpendicular foot):
   $$d\mathbf{l}'\times\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}} = \frac{s\,dz'}{\sqrt{s^2 + z'^2}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}} \implies B_\phi = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\int_{z_1}^{z_2}\frac{s\,dz'}{(s^2+z'^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi s}\left(\sin\alpha_2 - \sin\alpha_1\right)$$
   For an infinitely long wire ($\alpha_2 = +\pi/2$, $\alpha_1 = -\pi/2$): $\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi s}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$.
2. **Axial Field of a Circular Loop** (radius $R$ in $xy$-plane, counterclockwise current $I$, evaluated at $(0,0,z)$):
   Horizontal components cancel by azimuthal symmetry, leaving only the $z$-projection $\frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2+z^2}}$:
   $$\mathbf{B}(0,0,z) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\frac{2\pi R\cdot R}{(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$

#### Unabridged Proof of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$ from Biot-Savart
Because $\nabla$ operates strictly on the field coordinate $\mathbf{r}$ while $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')$ depends only on $\mathbf{r}'$, we move $\nabla$ inside the volume integral:

1. **Divergence of $\mathbf{B}$ (No Magnetic Monopoles):**
   $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'} \nabla \cdot \left(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') \times \frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right) d\tau'$$
   Apply the identity $\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}) = \mathbf{Q}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{P}) - \mathbf{P}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{Q})$:
   - $\nabla\times\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') = \mathbf{0}$ (independent of $\mathbf{r}$).
   - $\nabla\times\left(\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right) = \nabla\times\left(-\nabla\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right) = \mathbf{0}$ (curl of any gradient vanishes identically).

   Hence:
   $$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0}$$

2. **Curl of $\mathbf{B}$ (Ampere's Differential & Integral Laws):**
   $$\nabla \times \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'} \nabla \times \left(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') \times \frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right) d\tau'$$
   Expand the curl of a cross product using $\nabla\times(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{Q}) = (\mathbf{Q}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{P} - (\mathbf{P}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{Q} + \mathbf{P}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{Q}) - \mathbf{Q}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P})$. Since $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')$ has zero derivatives with respect to $\mathbf{r}$, two terms vanish immediately, leaving:
   $$\nabla \times \left(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') \times \frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right) = \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\left[\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right)\right] - \left(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\cdot\nabla\right)\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}$$
   Evaluate both surviving terms carefully:
   - **First term:** Using the fundamental Dirac-delta identity $\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right) = 4\pi\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$:
     $$\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'} \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\,4\pi\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')\,d\tau' = \mu_0\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r})$$
   - **Second term:** Since $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}} = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'$, any derivative satisfies $\nabla f(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}) = -\nabla' f(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}})$. Thus for the $x$-component:
     $$-\left(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\cdot\nabla\right)\frac{\mathcal{R}_x}{\mathcal{R}^3} = +\left(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\cdot\nabla'\right)\frac{\mathcal{R}_x}{\mathcal{R}^3} = \nabla'\cdot\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}_x}{\mathcal{R}^3}\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\right) - \frac{\mathcal{R}_x}{\mathcal{R}^3}\left(\nabla'\cdot\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\right)$$
     For **steady currents**, $\nabla'\cdot\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') = 0$, killing the second piece identically. Integrating the first piece over a bounding volume $\mathcal{V}'$ enclosing all currents via the Divergence Theorem produces a boundary integral $\oint_{\mathcal{S}'} \frac{\mathcal{R}_x}{\mathcal{R}^3}\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{a}' = 0$ (since $\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{0}$ on $\mathcal{S}'$).

   Only the first term survives, yielding **Ampere's Circuital Law** in differential form, and (via Stokes' theorem over an open surface $\mathcal{S}$ bounded by closed loop $\mathcal{C}$) in integral form:

   $$\boxed{\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}, \qquad \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}}$$

**Symmetry Scope of Integral Ampere's Law:** Like Gauss's law, $\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$ always holds, yet yields $\mathbf{B}$ algebraically *only* for four canonical symmetries: (i) infinite straight wire/coaxial cylinder, (ii) infinite uniform current plane ($\mathbf{B} = \pm\frac{1}{2}\mu_0\mathbf{K}\times\hat{\mathbf{n}}$), (iii) infinite solenoid ($B_{\text{in}} = \mu_0 n I$, $B_{\text{out}} = 0$), and (iv) toroid ($B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi s}$ inside the core).

### 1.3 Magnetic Vector Potential $\mathbf{A}$ and the Coulomb Gauge

Since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ everywhere in space, Helmholtz's theorem guarantees the existence of a **magnetic vector potential** $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})$ (SI unit: $\text{T}\cdot\text{m} = \text{Wb}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$) such that:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})}$$

**Gauge Freedom & Choice of Coulomb Gauge:** Adding the gradient of any smooth scalar field $\lambda(\mathbf{r})$ leaves $\mathbf{B}$ strictly invariant because $\nabla\times(\nabla\lambda) \equiv \mathbf{0}$:

$$\mathbf{A}'(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) + \nabla\lambda(\mathbf{r}) \implies \nabla\times\mathbf{A}' = \nabla\times\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{B}$$

To fix $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}$, notice $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}' = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} + \nabla^2\lambda$. Choosing $\lambda$ to solve Poisson's equation $\nabla^2\lambda = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}$ always lets us enforce the **Coulomb (transverse) gauge**:

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0}$$

Now substitute $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ into Ampère's law $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$ and expand the double curl:

$$\nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) = \nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{A} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$$

Imposing the Coulomb gauge $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = 0$ eliminates the first term cleanly, giving **Poisson's vector equation** (three independent scalar Poisson equations for $A_x, A_y, A_z$ in Cartesian coordinates!):

$$\boxed{\nabla^2 \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = -\mu_0\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r})}$$

By exact mathematical analogy with electrostatic Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 V = -\rho/\epsilon_0$ (mapping $V \to A_i$ and $\rho/\epsilon_0 \to \mu_0 J_i$), the unique solution vanishing at infinity for any localized current distribution is:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'} \frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|}\,d\tau' \qquad \left(\text{line current: }\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\int \frac{d\mathbf{l}'}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|}\right)}$$

**Direct Verification of $\nabla\times\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{B}$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = 0$:**
1. Curl check: $\nabla\times\left(\frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')}{\mathcal{R}}\right) = \nabla\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right)\times\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') = \left(-\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\right)\times\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') = +\frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\times\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}$, recovering the Biot-Savart law identically!
2. Coulomb-gauge check: Using $\nabla(1/\mathcal{R}) = -\nabla'(1/\mathcal{R})$ and $\nabla'\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$:
   $$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'}\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\cdot\nabla'\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right)d\tau' = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}'}\nabla'\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')}{\mathcal{R}}\right)d\tau' = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{S}'}\frac{\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{a}'}{\mathcal{R}} = 0$$
3. **Physical Warning for Infinite Currents:** If $\mathbf{J}$ extends to infinity (such as an infinitely long straight wire), the integral $\int d\mathbf{l}'/\mathcal{R}$ diverges logarithmically; one must instead solve for a finite length $L$ and drop the infinite additive constant, or integrate $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ directly via $\oint\mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{a} = \Phi_B$.

### 1.4 Multipole Expansion of $\mathbf{A}$, Magnetic Dipole Moment, Torque, and Energy

Consider a localized closed current loop $\mathcal{C}$ carrying steady current $I$ inside a sphere of radius $a$, observed at a distant field point $r \gg a$. Expanding $\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|} = \frac{1}{r}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{r'}{r}\right)^l P_l(\cos\alpha)$ in the vector potential yields:

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{l+1}}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (r')^l P_l(\cos\alpha)\,d\mathbf{l}'$$

1. **Magnetic Monopole Term ($l = 0$):**
   $$\mathbf{A}_{\text{mono}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi r}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} d\mathbf{l}' = \mathbf{0}$$
   The closed line integral of displacement vanishes identically—confirming mathematically that **isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist**.

2. **Magnetic Dipole Term ($l = 1$):** Since $r' P_1(\cos\alpha) = \hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{r}'$:
   $$\mathbf{A}_{\text{dip}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi r^2}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\,d\mathbf{l}'$$
   To simplify the integral cleanly for any constant vector $\mathbf{c}$ (here $\mathbf{c} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}$), observe that the exact differential $d\left[(\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\mathbf{r}'\right] = (\mathbf{c}\cdot d\mathbf{r}')\mathbf{r}' + (\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{r}')d\mathbf{r}'$ integrates to zero around a closed loop $\mathcal{C}$ ($d\mathbf{r}' \equiv d\mathbf{l}'$). Hence:
   $$\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\,d\mathbf{l}' = -\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\mathbf{c}\cdot d\mathbf{l}')\,\mathbf{r}' = \frac{1}{2}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\left[(\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\,d\mathbf{l}' - (\mathbf{c}\cdot d\mathbf{l}')\,\mathbf{r}'\right]$$
   By the BAC-CAB identity, $(\mathbf{r}'\times d\mathbf{l}')\times\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\,d\mathbf{l}' - (\mathbf{c}\cdot d\mathbf{l}')\,\mathbf{r}'$. Factoring the constant vector $\mathbf{c} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}$ outside the integral gives:
   $$\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{r}')\,d\mathbf{l}' = \left(\frac{1}{2}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{r}'\times d\mathbf{l}'\right)\times\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \mathbf{a}\times\hat{\mathbf{r}}$$
   where $\mathbf{a} \equiv \frac{1}{2}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{r}'\times d\mathbf{l}'$ is the **vector area** of the loop (for a planar loop of area $a$ and right-hand normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, $\mathbf{a} = a\hat{\mathbf{n}}$). Defining the **magnetic dipole moment** $\mathbf{m}$ (SI unit: $\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^2 = \text{J}\cdot\text{T}^{-1}$):

   $$\boxed{\mathbf{m} \equiv I\mathbf{a} = \frac{1}{2}I\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{r}'\times d\mathbf{l}' = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}'} \mathbf{r}'\times\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')\,d\tau'}$$

   gives the exact dipole vector potential:

   $$\boxed{\mathbf{A}_{\text{dip}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{\mathbf{m}\times\hat{\mathbf{r}}}{r^2}}$$

3. **Magnetic Field of a Point Magnetic Dipole:** Align $\mathbf{m} = m\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ so $\mathbf{A}_{\text{dip}} = \frac{\mu_0 m\sin\theta}{4\pi r^2}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$. Evaluating $\mathbf{B}_{\text{dip}} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{\text{dip}}$ in spherical coordinates:
   $$B_r = \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(\sin\theta A_\phi) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{2m\cos\theta}{r^3}, \qquad B_\theta = -\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r A_\phi) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{m\sin\theta}{r^3}, \qquad B_\phi = 0$$
   In coordinate-free vector form (identical in spatial pattern to the electric dipole field outside the origin):
   $$\boxed{\mathbf{B}_{\text{dip}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}\left[3(\mathbf{m}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \mathbf{m}\right]}$$

#### Force, Torque, Energy, and Dipole-Dipole Interaction
Consider a rectangular loop of sides $a, b$ carrying steady current $I$ in a uniform magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$, tilted so its normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ makes angle $\theta$ with $\mathbf{B}$:
- Forces on opposite sides are equal and opposite, so net force $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$ in a uniform field.
- Forces on the two sides of length $b$ perpendicular to the tilt axis have magnitude $F = IbB$ separated by perpendicular lever arm $a\sin\theta$, producing a couple of magnitude $\tau = (IbB)(a\sin\theta) = (Iab)B\sin\theta = mB\sin\theta$ tending to align $\mathbf{m}$ with $\mathbf{B}$. Any arbitrary planar loop decomposes into a mesh of infinitesimal rectangles (interior currents canceling), so universally:

$$\boxed{\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m} \times \mathbf{B}, \qquad U = -\mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{B}, \qquad \mathbf{F} = \nabla(\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B})}$$

*(Warning: In an inhomogeneous external field where $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$ locally—i.e., zero external current at the dipole's site—$\nabla(\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B}) = (\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{B}$. However, inside an external current density $\mathbf{J}_{\text{ext}} \neq \mathbf{0}$, an Amperian current loop obeys $\mathbf{F} = \nabla(\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B})$, NOT $(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{B}$!)*

**Interaction Energy of Two Magnetic Dipoles ($\mathbf{m}_1, \mathbf{m}_2$ separated by $\mathbf{r}$):** Substituting $\mathbf{B}_1(\mathbf{r})$ into $U_{12} = -\mathbf{m}_2\cdot\mathbf{B}_1(\mathbf{r})$ yields:

$$\boxed{U_{12} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}\left[\mathbf{m}_1\cdot\mathbf{m}_2 - 3(\mathbf{m}_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})(\mathbf{m}_2\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\right]}$$

- **Global Stable Equilibrium (Free Rotation at Fixed $r$):** Let $\mathbf{m}_1, \mathbf{m}_2$ make angles $\theta_1, \theta_2$ with $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ (coplanar). Then $U_{12} \propto \cos(\theta_1 - \theta_2) - 3\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2 = \sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2 - 2\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2$. The absolute minimum ($-2$) occurs at $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0$ (or $\pi$): **both dipoles collinear and parallel (head-to-tail) along the line joining them**, giving $U_{\min} = -\frac{2\mu_0 m_1 m_2}{4\pi r^3}$! *(By contrast, the antiparallel broadside alignment $\theta_1 = \pi/2, \theta_2 = -\pi/2$ has $U = -\frac{\mu_0 m_1 m_2}{4\pi r^3}$, which is only half as deep and is a saddle point under simultaneous rotation!)*

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Vector Potential of a Finite Straight Wire & Two-Dipole Equilibrium
> **Problem:**
> *(a)* A straight wire of length $L = 2.0\text{ m}$ lies along the $z$-axis from $z = -L/2$ to $z = +L/2$ carrying steady current $I = 10\text{ A}$ in the $+\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ direction. Determine the magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}$ at a point $P$ on the perpendicular bisector at radial distance $x = 0.50\text{ m}$ from the wire. Verify $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ and evaluate both $|\mathbf{A}|$ and $|\mathbf{B}|$ numerically.
> *(b)* Two identical small circular coils ($N = 50$ turns, radius $R = 2.0\text{ cm}$, current $I = 2.0\text{ A}$) are held at fixed separation $r = 40\text{ cm}$ and allowed to rotate freely. Compute each magnetic dipole moment $m$ and the minimum interaction energy $U_{\min}$ in stable equilibrium.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a):**
> 1. **Exact Integration of $\mathbf{A}$:** Place $P$ at cylindrical coordinates $(\rho, \phi, 0)$ (so $\rho = x$). Source element is $d\mathbf{l}' = dz'\,\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ at $(0, 0, z')$, with separation $\mathcal{R} = \sqrt{\rho^2 + z'^2}$:
>    $$\mathbf{A}(\rho) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\hat{\mathbf{z}}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\frac{dz'}{\sqrt{\rho^2 + z'^2}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\hat{\mathbf{z}}\left[\ln\left(z' + \sqrt{\rho^2 + z'^2}\right)\right]_{-L/2}^{L/2}$$
>    $$\mathbf{A}(\rho) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\rho^2 + (L/2)^2} + L/2}{\sqrt{\rho^2 + (L/2)^2} - L/2}\right)\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$
>    Rationalizing the denominator (multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{\rho^2+(L/2)^2} + L/2$):
>    $$\mathbf{A}(\rho) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{L}{2\rho} + \sqrt{1 + \frac{L^2}{4\rho^2}}\right)\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\operatorname{arsinh}\left(\frac{L}{2\rho}\right)\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$
>    *(General segment from $z' = a$ to $z' = b$: $\mathbf{A}(\rho) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{b + \sqrt{\rho^2 + b^2}}{a + \sqrt{\rho^2 + a^2}}\right)\hat{\mathbf{z}}$.)*
> 2. **Verification via $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$:** In cylindrical coordinates for $\mathbf{A} = A_z(\rho)\hat{\mathbf{z}}$:
>    $$\mathbf{B} = -\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial \rho}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}} = -\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + L^2/(4\rho^2)}}\right)\left(-\frac{L}{2\rho^2}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}} = \frac{\mu_0 I L}{2\pi\rho\sqrt{4\rho^2 + L^2}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$$
>    In the infinite-wire limit ($L \gg \rho$), $B_\phi \to \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi\rho}$ and $A_z \approx \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\ln(L/\rho)$—exact Biot-Savart recovery!
> 3. **Numeric Values ($L = 2.0\text{ m}$, $\rho = x = 0.50\text{ m}$, $I = 10\text{ A}$):**
>    With $\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi} = \frac{(4\pi\times 10^{-7})(10)}{2\pi} = 2.00\times 10^{-6}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}$ and $\frac{L}{2x} = 2.0$:
>    $$A_z = (2.00\times 10^{-6})\ln\left(2.0 + \sqrt{5}\right) = (2.00\times 10^{-6})\ln(4.236) = \boxed{2.89\,\mu\text{T}\cdot\text{m}\;\hat{\mathbf{z}}}$$
>    $$B_\phi = \frac{2.00\times 10^{-6}}{0.50}\cdot\frac{2.0}{\sqrt{5}} = (4.00\times 10^{-6})(0.8944) = \boxed{3.58\,\mu\text{T}\;\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}$$
>
> **Part (b):**
> 1. Magnetic dipole moment of each coil:
>    $$m = N I \pi R^2 = (50)(2.0)\pi(0.020\text{ m})^2 = 0.04\pi = \boxed{0.126\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2}$$
> 2. Stable equilibrium occurs when both dipoles align collinearly and head-to-tail along the separation vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$:
>    $$U_{\min} = -\frac{2\mu_0 m^2}{4\pi r^3} = -2(10^{-7})\frac{(0.1257)^2}{(0.40)^3} = -2\times 10^{-7}\left(\frac{0.01579}{0.0640}\right) = \boxed{-49.3\text{ nJ}}$$

## 2. Magnetic Shell and Solid-Angle Scalar Magnetic Potential

Before vector potentials became standard, Ampère discovered an astonishing geometric equivalence: the magnetic field outside *any* thin current-carrying loop $\mathcal{C}$ is mathematically identical to the field produced by an ultra-thin magnetic wafer spanning that loop, magnetized uniformly perpendicular to its surface like a microscopic carpet of bar magnets.

This idealized object—called a **magnetic shell**—replaces messy vector Biot-Savart integrations with a single scalar geometric quantity: the **solid angle** $\Omega$ subtended by the loop at the observation point.

### 2.1 Definition and Strength of a Magnetic Shell

**Definition:** A **magnetic shell** is an infinitesimally thin sheet of ferromagnetic material (uniform thickness $t \to 0$) spanning an arbitrary smooth open surface $\mathcal{S}$ bounded by closed contour $\mathcal{C}$, magnetized everywhere normal to the sheet ($\mathbf{M} = M\hat{\mathbf{n}}$). One face acts as a uniform north-pole layer and the opposite face as a uniform south-pole layer.

An area element $da'$ of volume $d\tau' = t\,da'$ possesses an infinitesimal magnetic dipole moment:

$$d\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{M}\,d\tau' = (Mt)\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}'\,da' \equiv \Phi\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}'\,da'$$

The **strength of the magnetic shell** $\Phi$ is defined as the magnetic dipole moment per unit surface area (equivalently, magnetization times thickness):

$$\boxed{\Phi \equiv M t = \frac{dm}{da'}}$$

**Dimensional Insight:** In SI units, $[M] = \text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$ and $[t] = \text{m}$, giving $[\Phi] = \text{A}$ (**Amperes**) — foreshadowing its exact numerical identity with an equivalent boundary current $I$.

---

### 2.2 Solid-Angle Magnetic Scalar Potential $\phi_m$

Outside the shell (where no free current flows), $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$ locally, so we may write $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = -\nabla\phi_m(\mathbf{r})$ using a **magnetic scalar potential** $\phi_m$ (SI unit: $\text{T}\cdot\text{m}$; related to the $\mathbf{H}$-potential $\Phi_M$ by $\phi_m = \mu_0\Phi_M$).

For a single point magnetic dipole $d\mathbf{m}$ at $\mathbf{r}'$, verify that $\mathbf{B}_{\text{dip}} = -\nabla\phi_{m,\text{dip}}$ holds with:

$$d\phi_m(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{d\mathbf{m}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}} = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'$ points *from the dipole element $da'$ to the field point $\mathbf{r}$*. Substituting $d\mathbf{m} = \Phi\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}'\,da'$ and integrating over the entire shell surface $\mathcal{S}$:

$$\phi_m(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{S}} \frac{\hat{\mathbf{n}}'\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\,da'$$

Examine the geometric integrand $\frac{\hat{\mathbf{n}}'\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\,da' = \frac{da'\cos\theta}{\mathcal{R}^2}$: this is precisely the **signed differential solid angle $d\Omega$** subtended by area element $da'$ at the observation point $\mathbf{r}$!
- When $P(\mathbf{r})$ views the **positive (north) face** ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}'$ points toward $P$, $\cos\theta > 0$), $d\Omega > 0$.
- When $P(\mathbf{r})$ views the **negative (south) face** ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}'$ points away from $P$, $\cos\theta < 0$), $d\Omega < 0$.

Therefore, for a uniform magnetic shell ($\Phi = \text{const}$):

$$\boxed{\phi_m(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\Omega(\mathbf{r})}$$

**Crucial Geometric Feature:** Because $\Omega(\mathbf{r})$ depends **only** on the closed boundary rim $\mathcal{C}$ (and not on how the membrane $\mathcal{S}$ bulges inside $\mathcal{C}$), *every* magnetic shell spanning the same boundary $\mathcal{C}$ with strength $\Phi$ produces the exact same potential and magnetic field everywhere outside the sheet!

### 2.3 Proof of Equivalence Between a Magnetic Shell and a Current Loop ($\Phi = I$)

We establish **Ampère's Equivalence Theorem** by both a rigorous vector-calculus displacement proof and a physical microscopic current decomposition.

```
             P(r + dr)    <-- Field point displaced by dr
                ^             (Equivalent to shifting shell S by -dr)
                | dr
             P(r)
              /   \
             /     \   Solid angle Omega(r)
            /       \
       ====*=========*====  Original shell S  (normal +n_hat, rim C)
       |  d l' x dr      |  Side ribbon swept by C (outward area d l' x dr)
       ----*---------*----  Shifted shell S'  (normal -n_hat)
```

#### Rigorous Solid-Angle Displacement Proof
1. Displace the observation point from $\mathbf{r}$ to $\mathbf{r} + \delta\mathbf{r}$. Equivalently, hold $\mathbf{r}$ fixed and rigidly translate the entire magnetic shell $\mathcal{S}$ by $-\delta\mathbf{r}$ to a new surface $\mathcal{S}'$.
2. Form a **closed surface** $\mathcal{S}_{\text{closed}}$ consisting of three pieces: the original shell $\mathcal{S}$ (outward normal $+\hat{\mathbf{n}}$), the shifted shell $\mathcal{S}'$ (outward normal $-\hat{\mathbf{n}}$), and the thin side ribbon traced by rim $\mathcal{C}$ moving through $-\delta\mathbf{r}$.
3. Verify the outward area element on the side ribbon: for a contour element $d\mathbf{l}'$ oriented by the right-hand rule around $+\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, the vector $(-\delta\mathbf{r}) \times d\mathbf{l}' = d\mathbf{l}' \times \delta\mathbf{r}$ points strictly **outward** from the closed pillbox volume.
4. Because field point $\mathbf{r}$ lies *outside* the closed surface $\mathcal{S}_{\text{closed}}$, the total signed solid angle subtended by $\mathcal{S}_{\text{closed}}$ at $\mathbf{r}$ vanishes identically:
   $$\Omega_{\text{closed}} = \underbrace{\Omega(\mathbf{r})}_{\text{on }\mathcal{S}} - \underbrace{\Omega(\mathbf{r} + \delta\mathbf{r})}_{\text{on }\mathcal{S}'} + \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \frac{(d\mathbf{l}' \times \delta\mathbf{r})\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2} = 0$$
5. Rearranging for $\delta\Omega = \Omega(\mathbf{r} + \delta\mathbf{r}) - \Omega(\mathbf{r})$ and applying the cyclic scalar triple product identity $(d\mathbf{l}' \times \delta\mathbf{r})\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}} = -\delta\mathbf{r}\cdot(d\mathbf{l}' \times \hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}})$:
   $$\delta\Omega = -\delta\mathbf{r}\cdot\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \frac{d\mathbf{l}' \times \hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}$$
6. Since $\delta\phi_m = (\nabla\phi_m)\cdot\delta\mathbf{r} = -\mathbf{B}\cdot\delta\mathbf{r} = \frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\delta\Omega$ holds for **every** arbitrary displacement $\delta\mathbf{r}$:
   $$-\mathbf{B}\cdot\delta\mathbf{r} = -\frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\delta\mathbf{r}\cdot\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \frac{d\mathbf{l}'\times\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2} \implies \boxed{\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \frac{d\mathbf{l}' \times \hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}}$$

Comparing term-by-term with the Biot-Savart law proves that a magnetic shell of strength $\Phi$ spanning boundary $\mathcal{C}$ produces the **exact magnetic field** of a steady current $I = \Phi$ circulating around $\mathcal{C}$!

#### Physical Mesh Picture & Bound-Current Confirmation
Subdivide $\mathcal{S}$ into infinitesimal tiles of area $da'$. Replace each tiny dipole $dm = \Phi\,da'$ by a miniature current loop of current $I = \Phi$ running around the tile perimeter. Every internal shared boundary carries two equal and opposite currents that cancel completely, leaving only a single net current $I = \Phi$ circulating along the outer boundary $\mathcal{C}$ (equivalently, rim bound surface current $K_b = M$ integrated across thickness $t$ yields $I_{\text{rim}} = Mt = \Phi$).

### 2.4 Work Done Moving a Unit Magnetic Pole & Multi-Valuedness of $\phi_m$

Suppose a hypothetical unit north magnetic pole ($q_m = 1\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}$, experiencing force $\mathbf{F} = q_m\mathbf{B}$) starts at a point $P_+$ immediately adjacent to the **positive (north) face** of a planar circular shell spanning current loop $I$, travels around the exterior to the point $P_-$ immediately adjacent to the **negative (south) face**, and returns through the shell.

- At $P_+$ (facing north side), the infinite half-plane subtends $\Omega(P_+) = +2\pi$, so $\phi_m(P_+) = +\frac{\mu_0 I}{2}$.
- At $P_-$ (facing south side), the shell subtends $\Omega(P_-) = -2\pi$, so $\phi_m(P_-) = -\frac{\mu_0 I}{2}$.
- More generally, for *any* curved shell, crossing from the negative face to the positive face jumps the solid angle discontinuously by $\Delta\Omega = +4\pi$, so $\phi_m$ jumps by $+\mu_0 I$.

Moving the unit pole externally from $P_+$ around the wire to $P_-$, the work done by the magnetic field is:

$$W_{\text{field}} = \int_{P_+}^{P_-} \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\int_{P_+}^{P_-} \nabla\phi_m \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \phi_m(P_+) - \phi_m(P_-) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\left[2\pi - (-2\pi)\right] = \boxed{\mu_0 I}$$

Wait: in the actual current loop (unlike a solid magnet wafer), there is no physical barrier across $\mathcal{S}$, so the exterior path from $P_+$ to $P_-$ closes seamlessly into a loop $\Gamma$ linking current $I$ once! Thus:

$$\boxed{W = \oint_{\Gamma} \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 I}$$

This simultaneously proves **Ampère's Circuital Law** from the $4\pi$ solid-angle discontinuity and explains why the magnetic scalar potential $\phi_m$ of a current loop is inherently **multi-valued**: each complete loop around the wire shifts $\phi_m$ by $\pm\mu_0 I$.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Magnetic Shell Strength, Magnetization & Axial Solid-Angle Field
> **Problem:**
> *(a)* A uniform magnetic shell of thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$ and mass $100\text{ g}$ has density $\rho_m = 10.0\text{ g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3}$ and total magnetic dipole moment $m = 1.00\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$ (equivalent to $1000\text{ emu}$ in CGS). Calculate (i) its intensity of magnetization $M$, (ii) the strength of the magnetic shell $\Phi$, and (iii) its surface area $A$.
> *(b)* If this shell is shaped as a flat circular disk of radius $R = 4.0\text{ cm}$ with the same strength $\Phi = 200\text{ A}$, use the solid-angle scalar potential $\phi_m = \frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\Omega$ to compute $\Omega$, $\phi_m$, and the axial magnetic induction $B_z$ at distance $z = 3.0\text{ cm}$ above the center of its north face.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a):**
> 1. **Convert all inputs strictly to SI units:**
>    $t = 2.0\times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$, mass $= 0.100\text{ kg}$, $\rho_m = 10.0\text{ g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3} = 1.00\times 10^4\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$, $m = 1.00\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$.
> 2. **Volume of the shell:**
>    $$\mathcal{V} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\rho_m} = \frac{0.100\text{ kg}}{1.00\times 10^4\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}} = 1.00\times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3$$
> 3. **Intensity of Magnetization $M$:**
>    $$M = \frac{m}{\mathcal{V}} = \frac{1.00\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2}{1.00\times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3} = \boxed{1.00\times 10^5\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}}$$
> 4. **Strength of the Magnetic Shell $\Phi$ & Surface Area $A$:**
>    $$\Phi = M t = (1.00\times 10^5\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1})(2.0\times 10^{-3}\text{ m}) = \boxed{200\text{ A}}$$
>    $$A = \frac{\mathcal{V}}{t} = \frac{1.00\times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3}{2.0\times 10^{-3}\text{ m}} = \boxed{5.00\times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2 = 50.0\text{ cm}^2}$$
>    *(Consistency check: equivalent rim current $I = \Phi = 200\text{ A}$ gives dipole moment $m = I A = (200)(5.00\times 10^{-3}) = 1.00\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$!)*
>
> **Part (b):**
> 1. **Solid angle $\Omega(z)$ subtended by a disk of radius $R$ on its $+z$ axis:**
>    $$\Omega(z) = 2\pi\left(1 - \cos\theta_{\max}\right) = 2\pi\left(1 - \frac{z}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}\right)$$
>    With $R = 4.0\text{ cm}$, $z = 3.0\text{ cm}$, $\sqrt{R^2 + z^2} = 5.0\text{ cm}$:
>    $$\Omega(3.0\text{ cm}) = 2\pi\left(1 - \frac{3}{5}\right) = \frac{4\pi}{5} = \boxed{2.51\text{ sr}}$$
> 2. **Magnetic Scalar Potential $\phi_m$:**
>    $$\phi_m = \frac{\mu_0\Phi}{4\pi}\Omega = \left(10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}\right)(200\text{ A})\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}\right) = \boxed{5.03\times 10^{-5}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}}$$
> 3. **Axial Magnetic Induction $B_z = -\frac{\partial\phi_m}{\partial z}$:**
>    $$B_z = -\frac{\mu_0\Phi}{2}\frac{d}{dz}\left(1 - \frac{z}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}\right) = \frac{\mu_0\Phi R^2}{2(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}$$
>    Substituting $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times 10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}$, $\Phi = 200\text{ A}$, $R = 0.040\text{ m}$, $\sqrt{R^2+z^2} = 0.050\text{ m}$:
>    $$B_z = \frac{(4\pi\times 10^{-7})(200)(0.040)^2}{2(0.050)^3} = \frac{4\pi\times 10^{-5}(1.60\times 10^{-3})}{1.25\times 10^{-4}} = \boxed{1.61\text{ mT}}$$

## 3. Magnetization, Bound Currents, Auxiliary Field H and Uniformly Magnetised Sphere

Matter placed in a magnetic field responds via atomic orbital and spin magnetic dipoles. Smearing these microscopic magnetic moments into a continuum vector field defines the **Magnetization** $\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r})$ (magnetic dipole moment per unit volume, SI unit: $\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$):

$$\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}) \equiv \frac{d\mathbf{m}}{d\tau}$$

Just as spatially varying electric polarization $\mathbf{P}$ piles up bound electric charges ($\rho_b, \sigma_b$), spatially varying magnetization $\mathbf{M}$ creates real, physical macroscopic **bound electric currents** ($\mathbf{J}_b, \mathbf{K}_b$) that contribute directly to $\mathbf{B}$.

### 3.1 Bound Volume and Surface Currents ($\mathbf{J}_b$ and $\mathbf{K}_b$)

**Unabridged Vector-Calculus Derivation:** Consider a magnetized body of volume $\mathcal{V}$ bounded by closed surface $\mathcal{S}$. An infinitesimal volume element $d\tau'$ at $\mathbf{r}'$ carries magnetic dipole moment $d\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}')\,d\tau'$, contributing to the vector potential at field point $\mathbf{r}$ ($\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}} = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'$):

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}} \frac{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}') \times \hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}\,d\tau'$$

Using the source-gradient identity $\nabla'\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right) = +\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}}{\mathcal{R}^2}$:

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}} \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}') \times \nabla'\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right) d\tau'$$

Apply the curl product rule $\nabla'\times\left(\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathcal{R}}\right) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\left(\nabla'\times\mathbf{M}\right) + \nabla'\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right)\times\mathbf{M} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\left(\nabla'\times\mathbf{M}\right) - \mathbf{M}\times\nabla'\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right)$, which rearranges to:

$$\mathbf{M}\times\nabla'\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}\right) = \frac{\nabla'\times\mathbf{M}}{\mathcal{R}} - \nabla'\times\left(\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathcal{R}}\right)$$

Substitute into $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})$ and convert the second volume integral into a surface integral via the vector curl theorem $\int_{\mathcal{V}} (\nabla'\times\mathbf{W})\,d\tau' = -\oint_{\mathcal{S}} \mathbf{W}\times d\mathbf{a}'$:

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}} \frac{\nabla'\times\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}')}{\mathcal{R}}\,d\tau' + \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{S}} \frac{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}') \times \hat{\mathbf{n}}'}{\mathcal{R}}\,da'$$

where $\hat{\mathbf{n}}'$ is the outward unit normal to $\mathcal{S}$. Comparing directly with $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int \frac{\mathbf{J}}{\mathcal{R}}\,d\tau' + \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\oint \frac{\mathbf{K}}{\mathcal{R}}\,da'$ isolates the **bound volume current density** and **bound surface current density**:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{J}_b = \nabla \times \mathbf{M}, \qquad \mathbf{K}_b = \mathbf{M} \times \hat{\mathbf{n}}}$$

**Physical & Conservation Checks:**
- Inside a uniformly magnetized cylinder ($\mathbf{M} = M_0\hat{\mathbf{z}}$), $\mathbf{J}_b = \nabla\times\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$ because neighboring atomic current loops flow oppositely along shared interior edges and cancel; along the cylindrical wall ($\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}}$), uncanceled outer rims add coherently into a solenoid-like surface current $\mathbf{K}_b = M_0(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\times\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}}) = M_0\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$.
- Divergence check: $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}_b = \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{M}) \equiv 0$, confirming bound currents are strictly steady!

### 3.2 Auxiliary Field $\mathbf{H}$, Linear Media, and Interface Boundary Conditions

Decompose the total macroscopic current density into controllable **free current** $\mathbf{J}_f$ (conduction currents in wires) and material **bound current** $\mathbf{J}_b$:

$$\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{J}_f + \mathbf{J}_b = \mathbf{J}_f + \nabla\times\mathbf{M}$$

Substituting into Ampère's law $\frac{1}{\mu_0}\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{J}_f + \nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ groups material response cleanly on the left-hand side:

$$\nabla \times \left(\frac{\mathbf{B}}{\mu_0} - \mathbf{M}\right) = \mathbf{J}_f$$

Define the **magnetic field intensity (auxiliary field)** $\mathbf{H}$ (SI unit: $\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$):

$$\boxed{\mathbf{H} \equiv \frac{\mathbf{B}}{\mu_0} - \mathbf{M} \iff \mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})}$$

Its governing differential and integral equations are:

$$\boxed{\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_f, \qquad \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_{f,\text{enc}}, \qquad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{H} = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{M}}$$

**Critical Conceptual Trap:** Even though $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_f$ depends only on free currents, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{H} = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{M}$ does **not** vanish wherever $\mathbf{M}$ is non-uniform or terminates at a surface! Consequently, $\mathbf{H}$ is **not** determined solely by free currents unless geometry guarantees $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{M} = 0$ everywhere along the boundary (e.g., an infinite solenoid or closed toroid). In permanent magnets with zero free current, $\mathbf{H}$ is driven entirely by effective "magnetic surface poles" $\sigma_m = \mathbf{M}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$!

#### Linear, Isotropic, Homogeneous (LIH) Magnetic Media
In diamagnets and paramagnets (and non-saturated linear regimes), magnetization tracks $\mathbf{H}$ linearly:

$$\mathbf{M} = \chi_m \mathbf{H} \implies \boxed{\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(1 + \chi_m)\mathbf{H} = \mu_0\mu_r\mathbf{H} = \mu\mathbf{H}}$$

where $\chi_m$ is the dimensionless **magnetic susceptibility**, $\mu_r = 1 + \chi_m$ is the **relative permeability**, and $\mu = \mu_0\mu_r$ is the **absolute permeability**. Inside a homogeneous linear medium, $\mathbf{J}_b = \nabla\times(\chi_m\mathbf{H}) = \chi_m\mathbf{J}_f$; where $\mathbf{J}_f = \mathbf{0}$, bound current resides strictly on boundaries.

#### Magnetostatic Boundary Conditions (Medium 1 $\to$ Medium 2, Unit Normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ from 1 into 2)
1. **Normal Component of $\mathbf{B}$ (Gaussian Pillbox on $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$):**
   $$\boxed{B_{2\perp} - B_{1\perp} = 0} \qquad \implies \qquad H_{2\perp} - H_{1\perp} = -\left(M_{2\perp} - M_{1\perp}\right)$$
2. **Tangential Component of $\mathbf{H}$ (Amperian Loop on $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_f$, free surface current $\mathbf{K}_f$):**
   $$\boxed{\hat{\mathbf{n}} \times \left(\mathbf{H}_2 - \mathbf{H}_1\right) = \mathbf{K}_f \iff \mathbf{H}_{2\parallel} - \mathbf{H}_{1\parallel} = \mathbf{K}_f \times \hat{\mathbf{n}}}$$
   *(For $\mathbf{B}$: $\mathbf{B}_{2\parallel} - \mathbf{B}_{1\parallel} = \mu_0(\mathbf{K}_f + \mathbf{K}_b)\times\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.)*
3. **Law of Refraction of Magnetic Field Lines ($\mathbf{K}_f = \mathbf{0}$, linear media $\mu_1, \mu_2$):** Matching $B_1\cos\theta_1 = B_2\cos\theta_2$ and $(B_1/\mu_1)\sin\theta_1 = (B_2/\mu_2)\sin\theta_2$ gives:
   $$\boxed{\frac{\tan\theta_1}{\tan\theta_2} = \frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}}$$
   High-$\mu_r$ soft iron pulls magnetic flux lines sharply *into* the core—the physical basis of magnetic shielding and transformer cores!

### 3.3 Complete BVP Derivation - Uniformly Magnetised Sphere

**Physical Setup:** Consider a solid sphere of radius $R$ centered at the origin carrying permanent, strictly uniform magnetization $\mathbf{M} = M_0\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ inside ($r < R$) and $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$ outside ($r > R$), with **zero free currents** everywhere ($\mathbf{J}_f = \mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{K}_f = \mathbf{0}$).

Because $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_f = \mathbf{0}$ throughout all space, we can represent $\mathbf{H}$ globally as the negative gradient of a single-valued **magnetic scalar potential** $\Phi_M(r,\theta)$ (SI unit: $\text{A}$):

$$\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r}) = -\nabla\Phi_M(\mathbf{r})$$

#### Governing PDEs in Both Regions
- **Inside ($r < R$):** Since $\mathbf{M} = M_0\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ is spatially uniform, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{M} = 0$, so $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{M} = 0 \implies \nabla^2\Phi_{M,\text{in}} = 0$.
- **Outside ($r > R$):** $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0} \implies \nabla\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\text{out}} = 0 \implies \nabla^2\Phi_{M,\text{out}} = 0$.

#### Azimuthally Symmetric Legendre Expansions & Four Boundary Conditions
1. **Regularity at $r = 0$:** Rejecting $r^{-(l+1)}$ inside:
   $$\Phi_{M,\text{in}}(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} A_l r^l P_l(\cos\theta) \qquad (r \le R)$$
2. **Vanishing at $r \to \infty$:** Rejecting $r^l$ outside (zero external field):
   $$\Phi_{M,\text{out}}(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{B_l}{r^{l+1}} P_l(\cos\theta) \qquad (r \ge R)$$
3. **Continuity of Tangential $\mathbf{H}$ at $r = R$** ($\mathbf{K}_f = \mathbf{0} \iff \Phi_M$ continuous):
   $$\Phi_{M,\text{in}}(R,\theta) = \Phi_{M,\text{out}}(R,\theta) \qquad \forall\,\theta \in [0,\pi]$$
4. **Continuity of Normal $\mathbf{B}$ at $r = R$** ($B_{\text{in},r} = B_{\text{out},r}$):
   Using $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} + \mathbf{M}) = \mu_0(-\nabla\Phi_{M,\text{in}} + M_0\hat{\mathbf{z}})$, $\mathbf{B}_{\text{out}} = -\mu_0\nabla\Phi_{M,\text{out}}$, and $\hat{\mathbf{z}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \cos\theta = P_1(\cos\theta)$:
   $$-\left.\frac{\partial\Phi_{M,\text{in}}}{\partial r}\right|_{r=R} + M_0 P_1(\cos\theta) = -\left.\frac{\partial\Phi_{M,\text{out}}}{\partial r}\right|_{r=R} \qquad \forall\,\theta \in [0,\pi]$$

#### Unabridged Coefficient Determination via Legendre Orthogonality
Substitute the series into Conditions (3) and (4):

$$\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} A_l R^l P_l(\cos\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{B_l}{R^{l+1}} P_l(\cos\theta)$$

$$-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} l A_l R^{l-1} P_l(\cos\theta) + M_0 P_1(\cos\theta) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} (l+1)\frac{B_l}{R^{l+2}} P_l(\cos\theta)$$

Multiply both equations by $P_{l'}(\cos\theta)\sin\theta$ and integrate from $\theta = 0$ to $\pi$, using $\int_0^\pi P_l(\cos\theta)P_{l'}(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2}{2l'+1}\delta_{ll'}$ to isolate every harmonic $l$ uniquely:

1. From Condition (3):
   $$A_l R^l = \frac{B_l}{R^{l+1}} \implies B_l = A_l R^{2l+1} \qquad (\forall\,l \ge 0)$$
2. From Condition (4):
   $$-l A_l R^{l-1} + M_0 \delta_{l,1} = (l + 1)\frac{B_l}{R^{l+2}}$$
3. Substitute $B_l = A_l R^{2l+1}$ into Condition (4):
   $$-l A_l R^{l-1} + M_0\delta_{l,1} = (l + 1) A_l R^{l-1} \implies (2l + 1) A_l R^{l-1} = M_0\delta_{l,1}$$
4. Evaluate for every $l$:
   - **For $l \ge 2$:** $(2l + 1)R^{l-1} > 0 \implies A_l = 0$ and $B_l = 0$.
   - **For $l = 0$:** Condition (4) gives $0 = B_0/R^2 \implies B_0 = 0$, and Condition (3) gives $A_0 = 0$.
   - **For $l = 1$:** $3 A_1 = M_0$:
     $$\boxed{A_1 = \frac{M_0}{3}, \qquad B_1 = \frac{M_0 R^3}{3}}$$

#### Exact Potentials and Fields Inside and Outside

1. **Scalar Potentials:**
   $$\boxed{\Phi_{M,\text{in}}(r,\theta) = \frac{M_0}{3}r\cos\theta = \frac{M_0}{3}z \quad (r \le R), \qquad \Phi_{M,\text{out}}(r,\theta) = \frac{M_0 R^3}{3 r^2}\cos\theta \quad (r \ge R)}$$
   *(In $\phi_m = \mu_0\Phi_M$ units: $\phi_{m,\text{in}} = \frac{\mu_0 M_0}{3}r\cos\theta$ and $\phi_{m,\text{out}} = \frac{\mu_0 M_0 R^3}{3r^2}\cos\theta$.)*

2. **Inside the Sphere ($r < R$) — Uniform Opposing $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}}$ and Parallel $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}}$:**
   $$\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = -\nabla\left(\frac{M_0}{3}z\right) = \boxed{-\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{M}}$$
   $$\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = \mu_0\left(\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} + \mathbf{M}\right) = \mu_0\left(-\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{M} + \mathbf{M}\right) = \boxed{+\frac{2}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}}$$
   **Physical Significance:** Inside the uniformly magnetized sphere, both $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ are strictly uniform—yet they point in **opposite directions**! $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = -\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{M}$ is the **demagnetizing field** generated by surface north poles ($\sigma_m = +M_0\cos\theta$ on $+z$ cap) pointing back toward south poles ($-z$ cap), whereas $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = +\frac{2}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}$ flows upward along $\mathbf{M}$ to maintain unbroken divergence-free flux loops ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$).

```
         Magnetic Induction B               Auxiliary Field H
         (Closed loops, div B = 0)          (Starts on + cap, ends on - cap)
                 ^                                  ^
                 |                                  |
              .--+--.                            .--+--.
           .-'   ^   '-.                      .-' + + + '-.
          /  ^   |   ^  \                    /   |  |  |   \
         |   | B_in  |   |  B_in = +2/3 mu0 M|   v  v  v    | H_in = -1/3 M
          \  |  (up) |  /                    \   (down!)   /
           '-.   ^   .-'                      '-. - - - .-'
              '--+--'                            '--+--'
                 ^                                  ^
```

3. **Outside the Sphere ($r > R$) — Pure Magnetic Dipole Field:**
   Expressing $B_1 = \frac{M_0 R^3}{3} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 M_0\right) = \frac{m}{4\pi}$, where $\mathbf{m} = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\mathbf{M}$ is the total magnetic dipole moment of the sphere, gives $\Phi_{M,\text{out}} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\mathbf{m}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}}}{r^2}$. Taking $-\mu_0\nabla\Phi_{M,\text{out}}$ yields an **exact pure dipole field** for every $r > R$:
   $$\boxed{\mathbf{B}_{\text{out}}(\mathbf{r}) = \mu_0\mathbf{H}_{\text{out}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 M_0 R^3}{3 r^3}\left(2\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{r}} + \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}\left[3(\mathbf{m}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \mathbf{m}\right]}$$

4. **Independent Bound-Current Boundary Audit:**
   - Bound currents: $\mathbf{J}_b = \nabla\times\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$ inside; $\mathbf{K}_b = \mathbf{M}\times\hat{\mathbf{r}} = M_0\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ on $r = R$.
   - Normal $\mathbf{B}$ at $r = R$: $B_{\text{in},r} = \frac{2}{3}\mu_0 M_0\cos\theta = B_{\text{out},r}(R)$ (continuous!).
   - Tangential $\mathbf{H}$ at $r = R$: using $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, $H_{\text{in},\theta} = +\frac{1}{3}M_0\sin\theta = H_{\text{out},\theta}(R)$ (continuous!).
   - Tangential $\mathbf{B}$ jump at $r = R$: $B_{\text{out},\theta}(R) - B_{\text{in},\theta} = \left[\frac{1}{3} - \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\right]\mu_0 M_0\sin\theta = +\mu_0 M_0\sin\theta$, matching $\mu_0(\mathbf{K}_b\times\hat{\mathbf{r}})_\theta = +\mu_0 M_0\sin\theta$ identically!

*(Linear Magnetic Sphere Extension: Placing a linear sphere of permeability $\mu_r$ in a uniform external field $\mathbf{B}_0$ superposes $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{B}_0/\mu_0 - \mathbf{M}/3$ with $\mathbf{M} = (\mu_r - 1)\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}}$, yielding $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = \frac{3}{\mu_r+2}\frac{\mathbf{B}_0}{\mu_0}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = \frac{3\mu_r}{\mu_r+2}\mathbf{B}_0$—note the flux amplification up to $3\mathbf{B}_0$ as $\mu_r \to \infty$!)*

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Uniformly Magnetized Spherical Permanent Magnet

## 4. Magnetic Materials, Microscopic Models and Ferromagnetic Hysteresis

Macroscopic magnetic behavior sorts solids, liquids, and gases into three canonical families—**diamagnets**, **paramagnets**, and **ferromagnets**—depending on whether constituent atoms carry permanent magnetic moments and whether neighboring spins couple quantum-mechanically.

### 4.1 Diamagnetism - Larmor Precession and Langevin Susceptibility

Diamagnetism is a universal quantum-electrodynamic Lenz-law response present in **all** matter, though it is easily masked whenever atoms possess permanent magnetic dipoles. It dominates closed-shell atoms and molecules with zero net spin and orbital angular momentum ($\text{Bi}, \text{Cu}, \text{Ag}, \text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{N}_2$).

**Microscopic Derivation (Langevin-Larmor Model):** Consider an atomic electron of charge $-e$ and mass $m_e$ orbiting a nucleus in a circle of radius $\rho$ in a plane perpendicular to an external magnetic field $\mathbf{B} = B\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ ramped slowly from $0$ to $B$.
1. While $\mathbf{B}$ increases, Faraday's law induces a tangential electric field around the orbit:
   $$E_\phi(2\pi\rho) = -\pi\rho^2\frac{dB}{dt} \implies E_\phi = -\frac{\rho}{2}\frac{dB}{dt}$$
2. The tangential force $(-e)E_\phi = +\frac{e\rho}{2}\frac{dB}{dt}$ changes the electron's orbital speed by:
   $$\Delta v = \int_0^t \frac{e\rho}{2m_e}\frac{dB}{dt'}\,dt' = \frac{e\rho B}{2m_e} \implies \Delta\omega = \omega_L = \frac{eB}{2m_e} \quad (\text{Larmor angular frequency})$$
3. Because the electron carries negative charge, this Larmor precession generates an induced current $\Delta I = (-e)\frac{\omega_L}{2\pi} = -\frac{e^2 B}{4\pi m_e}$ and an induced magnetic dipole moment strictly **opposing** $\mathbf{B}$:
   $$\Delta m_z = (\Delta I)(\pi\rho^2) = -\frac{e^2\rho^2}{4m_e}B$$
4. For a spherically symmetric 3D atomic charge cloud of $Z$ electrons, $\rho^2 = x^2 + y^2$, and isotropy gives $\langle x^2\rangle = \langle y^2\rangle = \langle z^2\rangle = \frac{1}{3}\langle r^2\rangle$, so $\langle\rho^2\rangle = \frac{2}{3}\langle r^2\rangle$. Multiplying by $N$ atoms per unit volume and $Z$ electrons per atom yields $M = -\frac{N Z e^2\langle r^2\rangle}{6 m_e}B$. Since $B \approx \mu_0 H$ for $|\chi_m| \ll 1$, the **Langevin diamagnetic susceptibility** is:

$$\boxed{\chi_{\text{dia}} = -\frac{\mu_0 N Z e^2 \langle r^2 \rangle}{6 m_e}}$$

**Key Signatures:** Strictly **negative**, tiny ($\sim -10^{-5}$ to $-10^{-9}$), and **independent of temperature** (since $\langle r^2\rangle$ of inner atomic orbitals is insensitive to thermal energy $k_B T$).

---

### 4.2 Paramagnetism - Langevin Statistical Theory and Curie's Law

Paramagnetism occurs in materials whose atoms, ions, or molecules carry permanent magnetic dipole moments $\mathbf{m}$ due to unpaired electron spins or unquenched orbital angular momentum ($\text{O}_2$, $\text{Al}$, $\text{Pt}$, $\text{Mn}^{2+}$, $\text{Gd}^{3+}$). An external field $\mathbf{B}$ tries to align these dipoles ($U = -mB\cos\theta$), while thermal agitation $k_B T$ scrambles their orientations.

**Unabridged Langevin Derivation:** Consider $N$ non-interacting permanent magnetic dipoles per unit volume in thermal equilibrium at temperature $T$ in field $\mathbf{B} = B\hat{\mathbf{z}}$. By Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics, the probability of a dipole lying inside solid angle $d\Omega = 2\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta$ at angle $\theta$ is proportional to $e^{-U/(k_B T)} = e^{\alpha\cos\theta}$, where $\alpha \equiv \frac{mB}{k_B T}$ is the dimensionless magnetic-to-thermal energy ratio.

The mean alignment $\langle\cos\theta\rangle$ is:

$$\langle\cos\theta\rangle = \frac{\int_0^\pi \cos\theta\,e^{\alpha\cos\theta}\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\int_0^\pi e^{\alpha\cos\theta}\sin\theta\,d\theta}$$

Substitute $x = \cos\theta$ ($dx = -\sin\theta\,d\theta$, limits $x \in [-1, +1]$):
- Denominator: $I_0 = \int_{-1}^{1} e^{\alpha x}\,dx = \frac{e^\alpha - e^{-\alpha}}{\alpha} = \frac{2\sinh\alpha}{\alpha}$.
- Numerator: Recognize $\int_{-1}^{1} x e^{\alpha x}\,dx = \frac{dI_0}{d\alpha} = \frac{d}{d\alpha}\left(\frac{2\sinh\alpha}{\alpha}\right) = \frac{2\alpha\cosh\alpha - 2\sinh\alpha}{\alpha^2}$.

Dividing numerator by denominator defines the **Langevin function** $\mathcal{L}(\alpha)$:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{L}(\alpha) = \coth\alpha - \frac{1}{\alpha}, \qquad M = N m\,\mathcal{L}\!\left(\frac{mB}{k_B T}\right)}$$

**Limiting-Case Analysis:**
1. **Weak-Field / Room-Temperature Limit ($\alpha = \frac{mB}{k_B T} \ll 1$):** Expand $\coth\alpha = \frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{\alpha}{3} - \frac{\alpha^3}{45} + \dots$, giving $\mathcal{L}(\alpha) \approx \frac{\alpha}{3} = \frac{mB}{3k_B T}$. Since $B \approx \mu_0 H$ for small positive $\chi_m$:
   $$M \approx \frac{N m^2}{3 k_B T}\mu_0 H \implies \boxed{\chi_{\text{para}} = \frac{C}{T}, \qquad C \equiv \frac{\mu_0 N m^2}{3 k_B}} \quad \textbf{(Curie's Law)}$$
2. **Strong-Field / Cryogenic Limit ($\alpha \gg 1$):** $\coth\alpha \to 1$ and $1/\alpha \to 0$, so $\mathcal{L}(\alpha) \to 1$ and $M \to M_{\text{sat}} = Nm$ (complete magnetic saturation).
3. **Quantum Refinement:** Quantizing magnetic orientation to $2J + 1$ discrete levels $m_J \in \{-J, \dots, +J\}$ replaces $\mathcal{L}(\alpha)$ by the **Brillouin function** $B_J(\alpha)$, yielding the quantum Curie constant $C = \frac{\mu_0 N g_J^2 \mu_B^2 J(J+1)}{3 k_B}$ (which smoothly recovers the classical formula in the limit $J \to \infty$ with $g_J \mu_B J = m$ fixed).

### 4.3 Ferromagnetism - Exchange Coupling, Curie-Weiss Law and Domain Theory

Below a critical **Curie temperature $T_C$** ($1043\text{ K}$ for Fe, $1388\text{ K}$ for Co, $627\text{ K}$ for Ni), ferromagnetic materials develop massive **spontaneous magnetization** even in zero applied field, accompanied by nonlinear susceptibility ($\mu_r \sim 10^2\text{–}10^5$) and path-dependent **hysteresis**.

#### Quantum Exchange Origin & Weiss Molecular Field
Classical magnetic dipole-dipole interaction between adjacent iron atoms ($\sim \frac{\mu_0 \mu_B^2}{4\pi a^3} \sim 10^{-23}\text{ J}$) corresponds to $T \sim 1\text{ K}$—three orders of magnitude too weak to survive room temperature! Heisenberg proved that ferromagnetism stems from the **quantum mechanical exchange interaction**:

$$\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}} = -2\mathcal{J}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\mathbf{S}_i\cdot\mathbf{S}_j$$

rooted in electrostatic Coulomb repulsion combined with the Pauli exclusion principle for overlapping $3d$ electrons. When the exchange integral $\mathcal{J} > 0$, parallel spin alignment lowers the system's electrostatic energy drastically.

Pierre Weiss modeled this cooperative exchange coupling phenomenologically via an internal **Weiss molecular field** proportional to the magnetization itself:

$$\mathbf{H}_{\text{eff}} = \mathbf{H} + \lambda_W \mathbf{M}$$

where $\lambda_W$ is the dimensionless Weiss molecular field constant ($\lambda_W \sim 10^3\text{–}10^4$).

#### Derivation of the Curie-Weiss Law ($T > T_C$)
Above $T_C$, thermal disorder destroys spontaneous order and the material enters a high-susceptibility paramagnetic phase responding linearly to $\mathbf{H}_{\text{eff}}$ via Curie's law:

$$M = \frac{C}{T} H_{\text{eff}} = \frac{C}{T}\left(H + \lambda_W M\right)$$

Collect terms in $M$ and solve for $\chi_m = M/H$:

$$M\left(1 - \frac{C\lambda_W}{T}\right) = \frac{C}{T}H \implies \boxed{\chi_m = \frac{C}{T - T_C}, \qquad T_C \equiv C\lambda_W} \quad \textbf{(Curie-Weiss Law)}$$

- **Physical Check:** As $T \to T_C^+$, $\chi_m \to \infty$, signaling the second-order ferromagnetic phase transition below which $M$ acquires a nonzero spontaneous value $M_0(T)$ even at $H = 0$ (determined self-consistently by intersecting $M/M_s = \mathcal{L}(\alpha)$ with $M/M_s = \frac{T}{3T_C}\alpha$).

#### Weiss Domain Theory, Bloch Walls, and Pinning Mechanism
If every region below $T_C$ is spontaneously magnetized to saturation $M_s$, why does an ordinary iron nail appear unmagnetized?
1. **Energy Minimization via Domains:** A single uniformly magnetized iron crystal generates a huge external stray magnetic field costing immense magnetostatic energy $\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\int B_{\text{stray}}^2\,d\tau$. To slash this energy, the crystal breaks into microscopic **Weiss domains** ($\sim 10\text{–}100\,\mu\text{m}$ across), each internally saturated along an easy crystal axis, arranged in flux-closing orientations so net macroscopic $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$.
2. **Bloch Domain Walls:** Adjacent domains are separated by finite-thickness transition layers ($\sim 10\text{–}100\text{ nm}$) called **Bloch walls**, across which spins rotate gradually from one domain direction to the next. Wall thickness balances exchange energy (which favors wide, gentle spin twists) against magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy (which favors narrow walls minimizing spins pointing along hard crystal axes).
3. **Three-Stage Magnetization & Domain-Wall Pinning (Physical Root of Hysteresis):**
   - **Stage I (Small $H$, Reversible Wall Motion):** Domain walls bow elastically like membranes so domains favorably aligned with $\mathbf{H}$ expand smoothly. Removing $H$ restores the walls reversibly to their original sites.
   - **Stage II (Intermediate $H$, Irreversible Pinning & Barkhausen Jumps):** Real crystals contain dislocations, grain boundaries, internal strain fields, and non-magnetic impurities. Domain walls lower their energy by sitting on these defects—getting **pinned**. As $H$ increases, magnetic pressure builds until a wall abruptly breaks free and snaps to the next pinning site (**Barkhausen jump**). Each discontinuous jump induces microscopic eddy currents and lattice vibrations (phonons), irreversibly dissipating magnetic energy as heat!
   - **Stage III (High $H$, Coherent Domain Rotation):** Once unfavorably oriented domains are wiped out, remaining magnetization vectors rotate smoothly against crystal anisotropy until all spins align parallel to $\mathbf{H}$ (**magnetic saturation $B_s$**).

When $H$ is subsequently lowered back to zero, pinned domain walls **cannot** climb back over defect energy barriers on their own—leaving net **remanent magnetization** ($B_r$) and forcing a reverse field (**coercivity $H_c$**) to unpin and reverse the domains.

### 4.4 Hysteresis $B\text{-}H$ Loop and Proof of Energy Loss per Cycle

Cycling the magnetizing field $H$ from $+H_{\max} \to 0 \to -H_{\max} \to 0 \to +H_{\max}$ traces a closed, symmetric **hysteresis loop** in the $B\text{–}H$ plane, where $B$ perpetually lags behind $H$.

```
                     ^ B
                     |          A (+H_max, +B_s)
                  B_r*---------* (Saturation)
                 .-' |       .'
               .'    |     .'  <-- Virgin curve (O -> A)
      C (-H_c)       |   .'        dashed inside loop
   <----------*------+--*------------> H
            .'       |O  F (+H_c)
          .'         |  /
        .'           | /
       *-------------* E (-B_r)
    D (-H_max, -B_s) |
```

- **Saturation Induction ($B_s$, points $A, D$):** All atomic moments aligned with $\mathbf{H}$; further increase in $H$ raises $B$ only at the vacuum slope $\mu_0$.
- **Retentivity / Remanence ($B_r$, segment $OB$):** Residual magnetic induction retained at $H = 0$, measuring the specimen's ability to remain magnetized.
- **Coercivity ($H_c$, segment $OC$):** Reverse magnetizing field required to wipe out remanence and bring $B$ back to zero, measuring resistance to demagnetization.
- **Soft vs. Hard Ferromagnets:**
  - **Soft ferromagnets** (silicon steel, soft iron, permalloy, soft ferrites): low defect pinning → narrow hysteresis loop, small $H_c$, high initial $\mu_r$, minimal hysteresis loss per cycle → ideal for **AC transformer cores, inductors, and electromagnet yokes**.
  - **Hard ferromagnets** (Alnico, NdFeB, SmCo, hardened carbon steel): strong engineered defect pinning & high anisotropy → wide, square loop, large $B_r$, huge $H_c$, high maximum energy product $(BH)_{\max}$ → ideal for **permanent magnets and magnetic storage**.

#### Unabridged Proof of Hysteresis Energy Loss per Cycle ($w_{\text{cycle}} = \oint H\,dB$)

**Physical Setup:** Take a closed ferromagnetic ring (toroid) of mean circumference $l$ and uniform cross-sectional area $A$ (core volume $\mathcal{V} = Al$), wound tightly with $N$ turns of wire carrying a slowly varying current $I(t)$ driven by an external voltage source.

1. **Ampere's Law for $H$:** By toroidal symmetry, the magnetizing field inside the core is uniform and related to the free current $I$ by:
   $$\oint \mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = H l = N I \implies I = \frac{H l}{N}$$

2. **Faraday's Law for Back-EMF:** The total magnetic flux linking all $N$ turns is $\Phi_{\text{tot}} = N B A$. When the source adjusts the current over infinitesimal time $dt$ so $B$ changes by $dB$, Faraday's law induces a back-EMF opposing the change:
   $$\mathcal{E}_{\text{back}} = -\frac{d\Phi_{\text{tot}}}{dt} = -N A\frac{dB}{dt}$$

3. **Infinitesimal Work Done by the External Source:** Neglecting Ohmic ($I^2 R$) wire resistance, the external source must deliver instantaneous power $P_{\text{ext}} = (-\mathcal{E}_{\text{back}})I$ to push current $I$ against the back-EMF. In time $dt$, the work done on the magnetic field of the core is:
   $$dW_{\text{ext}} = (-\mathcal{E}_{\text{back}})I\,dt = \left(N A\frac{dB}{dt}\right)\left(\frac{H l}{N}\right)dt = (A l)\,H\,dB = \mathcal{V}\,H\,dB$$

4. **Work per Unit Volume & Closed-Cycle Energy Loss:** Dividing by core volume $\mathcal{V} = Al$ yields the infinitesimal magnetic work density $dw = H\,dB$. Integrating around **one complete closed hysteresis cycle** ($A \to B \to C \to D \to E \to F \to A$):
   - Along segments where $H$ and $dB$ have the *same* sign, the source supplies energy to the core; along segments where they have *opposite* sign, the core returns partial energy back to the source.
   - Because the magnetic state returns identically to point $A$, zero net reversible field energy accumulates over a full cycle; the positive excess integral is converted **100% into heat** inside the core (via irreversible Barkhausen domain-wall unpinning):

   $$\boxed{w_{\text{cycle}} = \oint_{\text{cycle}} H\,dB = \text{Area enclosed by the } B\text{–}H\text{ loop} \qquad (\text{SI unit: }\text{J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}\cdot\text{cycle}^{-1})}$$

5. **Equivalent $M\text{–}H$ Form & AC Power Dissipation:**
   - Substituting $B = \mu_0(H + M)$ gives $\oint H\,dB = \mu_0\oint H\,dH + \mu_0\oint H\,dM = 0 + \mu_0\oint H\,dM$ (because $\oint H\,dH = \oint d(\frac{1}{2}H^2) = 0$ over any closed path!).
   - At AC supply frequency $f$ (cycles per second), the hysteresis power dissipated as heat in a core of volume $\mathcal{V}$ (mass $m_{\text{core}}$, density $\rho_m$) is:
     $$\boxed{P_{\text{hyst}} = f\,\mathcal{V}\oint H\,dB = f\,\frac{m_{\text{core}}}{\rho_m}\oint H\,dB \qquad (\text{Watts})}$$

**Dimensional Check:** $[H\,dB] = (\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1})(\text{T}) = (\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1})(\text{N}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}) = \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^{-2} = \text{J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Transformer Core Hysteresis Power Loss & Curie-Weiss Determination
> **Problem:**
> *(a)* A transformer core of mass $m_{\text{core}} = 15.0\text{ kg}$ and density $\rho_m = 7.50 \times 10^3\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$ operates on a $f = 50\text{ Hz}$ AC supply. Its $B\text{–}H$ hysteresis loop plotted on graph paper encloses an area of $24.0\text{ cm}^2$, with axis scales $1\text{ cm} = 200\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$ along $H$ and $1\text{ cm} = 0.25\text{ T}$ along $B$. Calculate (i) hysteresis energy loss per unit volume per cycle, (ii) hysteresis power loss in the core, and (iii) total thermal energy wasted in $1\text{ hour}$.
> *(b)* Above its Curie temperature, a ferromagnetic material has magnetic susceptibility $\chi_1 = 1.50 \times 10^{-2}$ at $T_1 = 450\text{ K}$ and $\chi_2 = 7.50 \times 10^{-3}$ at $T_2 = 550\text{ K}$. Find its Curie temperature $T_C$ and Curie constant $C$.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a):**
> 1. **Energy Density Calibration per $\text{cm}^2$ of Graph Area:**
>    $$1\text{ cm}^2 \equiv (200\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1})(0.25\text{ T}) = 50.0\text{ J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$$
> 2. **Hysteresis Loss per Unit Volume per Cycle:**
>    $$w_{\text{cycle}} = \oint H\,dB = (24.0\text{ cm}^2)(50.0\text{ J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}\cdot\text{cm}^{-2}) = \boxed{1.20 \times 10^3\text{ J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}\cdot\text{cycle}^{-1}}$$
> 3. **Core Volume & Hysteresis Power Loss:**
>    $$\mathcal{V} = \frac{m_{\text{core}}}{\rho_m} = \frac{15.0\text{ kg}}{7.50 \times 10^3\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}} = 2.00 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3$$
>    $$P_{\text{hyst}} = f\,\mathcal{V}\,w_{\text{cycle}} = (50\text{ s}^{-1})(2.00 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3)(1.20 \times 10^3\text{ J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}) = \boxed{120\text{ W}}$$
> 4. **Energy Wasted as Heat in $1\text{ hour}$ ($3600\text{ s}$):**
>    $$E_{\text{heat}} = P_{\text{hyst}}\,t = (120\text{ W})(3600\text{ s}) = \boxed{4.32 \times 10^5\text{ J} = 432\text{ kJ}\;(0.120\text{ kWh})}$$
>
> **Part (b):**
> From the Curie-Weiss law $\chi_m(T) = \frac{C}{T - T_C}$, take the ratio at $T_1 = 450\text{ K}$ and $T_2 = 550\text{ K}$:
> $$\frac{\chi_1}{\chi_2} = \frac{T_2 - T_C}{T_1 - T_C} \implies \frac{1.50 \times 10^{-2}}{7.50 \times 10^{-3}} = 2.00 = \frac{550 - T_C}{450 - T_C}$$
> Solving linearly:
> $$2(450 - T_C) = 550 - T_C \implies 900 - 2T_C = 550 - T_C \implies \boxed{T_C = 350\text{ K}}$$
> Substituting back into $\chi_1 = C/(T_1 - T_C)$:
> $$C = \chi_1(T_1 - T_C) = (1.50 \times 10^{-2})(450 - 350) = \boxed{1.50\text{ K}}$$
> *(Cross-check at $T_2 = 550\text{ K}$: $\chi_2 = 1.50/(550 - 350) = 7.50 \times 10^{-3}$ — exact!)*

## 5. Master Synthesis Matrices - Magnetic Matter and B-H Boundary Duality

### 5.1 Comparative Matrix - Diamagnetism vs. Paramagnetism vs. Ferromagnetism

| Property | Diamagnetism | Paramagnetism | Ferromagnetism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Microscopic Physical Root** | Induced Larmor orbital precession (Lenz's law); zero permanent atomic moments | Alignment of permanent unpaired spin/orbital dipoles opposing thermal agitation $k_B T$ | Quantum Heisenberg exchange ($\mathcal{J} > 0$) + Weiss molecular field + magnetic domains |
| **Susceptibility $\chi_m$** | Small & **negative**: $-10^{-5}$ to $-10^{-9}$ | Small & **positive**: $+10^{-5}$ to $+10^{-3}$ | Very large, **positive & nonlinear**: $10^2$ to $10^5$ ($T < T_C$) |
| **Relative Permeability $\mu_r$** | Slightly $< 1$ ($0 \le \mu_r < 1$; superconductors: $\chi_m = -1, \mu_r = 0$) | Slightly $> 1$ | $\mu_r \gg 1$ (depends on $H$ and history) |
| **Temperature Dependence** | Independent of $T$ ($\chi_{\text{dia}} = -\frac{\mu_0 NZe^2\langle r^2\rangle}{6m_e}$) | **Curie's Law:** $\chi = C/T$ | Spontaneous $M_0(T)$ for $T < T_C$; **Curie-Weiss Law** $\chi = \frac{C}{T - T_C}$ for $T > T_C$ |
| **Hysteresis & Domains** | None (strictly linear & reversible) | None (reversible saturation via $\mathcal{L}(\alpha)$ / $B_J(\alpha)$) | Prominent **Weiss domains**, Barkhausen jumps, $B\text{–}H$ hysteresis loop ($B_r, H_c$) |
| **Behavior in Inhomogeneous $\mathbf{B}$** | Weakly repelled toward **weaker** field region | Weakly attracted toward **stronger** field region | Strongly pulled toward **stronger** field region |
| **Canonical Examples** | $\text{Bi}, \text{Cu}, \text{Ag}, \text{Au}, \text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{Si}$ | $\text{O}_2, \text{Al}, \text{Na}, \text{Pt}, \text{CuCl}_2$ | $\text{Fe}, \text{Co}, \text{Ni}, \text{Gd}$, Alnico, Silicon steel |

### 5.2 Electrostatics vs. Magnetostatics Field & Boundary-Condition Duality Matrix

| Concept | Electrostatics ($\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{D}, \mathbf{P}$) | Magnetostatics ($\mathbf{B}, \mathbf{H}, \mathbf{M}$) | Critical Asymmetry Watchpoint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fundamental Force Field** | $\mathbf{E}$ ($\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \rho/\epsilon_0$) | $\mathbf{B}$ ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$, $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$) | $\mathbf{E}$ is conservative; $\mathbf{B}$ is solenoidal |
| **Auxiliary Field Definition** | $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_0\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{P}$ | $\mathbf{H} = \frac{\mathbf{B}}{\mu_0} - \mathbf{M} \iff \mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})$ | **$\mu_0$ multiplies $\mathbf{M}$**, whereas $\epsilon_0$ does NOT multiply $\mathbf{P}$! |
| **Bound Sources** | $\rho_b = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$, $\sigma_b = \mathbf{P}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ | $\mathbf{J}_b = \nabla\times\mathbf{M}$, $\mathbf{K}_b = \mathbf{M}\times\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ | Bound charges are scalars; bound currents are vectors |
| **Auxiliary Field Equations** | $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D} = \rho_f$, $\nabla\times\mathbf{D} = \nabla\times\mathbf{P}$ | $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_f$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{H} = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{M}$ | Neither $\mathbf{D}$ nor $\mathbf{H}$ is universally fixed by free sources alone! |
| **Linear Constitutive Law** | $\mathbf{P} = \epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_0\epsilon_r\mathbf{E}$ | $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$, $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mu_r\mathbf{H}$ | $\mathbf{P} \propto \mathbf{E}$, but $\mathbf{M} \propto \mathbf{H}$ (NOT $\mathbf{B}$!) |
| **Normal Boundary Condition** | $D_{2\perp} - D_{1\perp} = \sigma_f$ | $B_{2\perp} - B_{1\perp} = 0$ | $D_\perp$ jumps across $\sigma_f$; $B_\perp$ is **always** continuous |
| **Tangential Boundary Condition** | $\mathbf{E}_{2\parallel} - \mathbf{E}_{1\parallel} = \mathbf{0}$ | $\mathbf{H}_{2\parallel} - \mathbf{H}_{1\parallel} = \mathbf{K}_f \times \hat{\mathbf{n}}$ | $\mathbf{E}_\parallel$ is always continuous; $\mathbf{H}_\parallel$ jumps across $\mathbf{K}_f$ |
| **Uniformly Polarized / Magnetized Sphere** | $\mathbf{E}_{\text{in}} = -\frac{\mathbf{P}}{3\epsilon_0}$, $\mathbf{D}_{\text{in}} = +\frac{2}{3}\mathbf{P}$ | $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = -\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{M}$, $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = +\frac{2}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}$ | $\mathbf{E}_{\text{in}}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}}$ antiparallel to source; $\mathbf{D}_{\text{in}}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}}$ parallel! |

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **Opposing Signs of $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}}$ and $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}}$ Inside a Permanent Magnet:** Students instinctively assume $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ always point in the same direction. Inside a uniformly magnetized sphere (with no free current), $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} = -\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{M}$ points **opposite** to $\mathbf{M}$, while $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = +\frac{2}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}$ points **parallel** to $\mathbf{M}$. Reversing the sign in $B_{\text{in},r} = B_{\text{out},r}$ destroys the factor of $2/3$!
> 2. **Cross-Product Order in Tangential $\mathbf{H}$ Boundary Condition:** $\mathbf{H}_{2\parallel} - \mathbf{H}_{1\parallel} = \mathbf{K}_f \times \hat{\mathbf{n}}$ (equivalently $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\times(\mathbf{H}_2 - \mathbf{H}_1) = \mathbf{K}_f$), where $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ points **from Medium 1 into Medium 2**. Writing $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\times\mathbf{K}_f$ flips the sign! Always check with a current sheet along $+\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ in the $xy$-plane ($\mathbf{H}$ points along $-\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ above $z > 0$).
> 3. **Two SI Conventions for Magnetic Scalar Potential ($\phi_m$ vs. $\Phi_M$):** Indian university papers frequently write $\mathbf{B} = -\nabla\phi_m$ (so $\phi_m = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\Omega$, units $\text{T}\cdot\text{m}$), whereas Griffiths/Jackson write $\mathbf{H} = -\nabla\Phi_M$ (so $\Phi_M = \frac{I}{4\pi}\Omega$, units $\text{A}$). They differ by an explicit factor of $\mu_0$: $\phi_m = \mu_0\Phi_M$. Always declare your defining equation on Line 1 of any proof!
> 4. **CGS-emu Leakage in Magnetic Shell & Hysteresis Problems:** Older PYQs occasionally quote magnetic moments in "units" (CGS emu) or density in $\text{g/cm}^3$ and thickness in $\text{mm}$. Remember exact conversions: $1\text{ emu} = 10^{-3}\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$, $1\text{ emu}\cdot\text{cm}^{-3} = 10^3\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$, $1\text{ Oe} = \frac{10^3}{4\pi}\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$, $1\text{ G} = 10^{-4}\text{ T}$. Convert cleanly to SI before computing $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times 10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}$!
> 5. **Forgetting $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ in Coulomb Gauge / Ampère Proof:** When deriving $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$ from Biot-Savart or verifying $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = 0$, explicitly invoke steady-current continuity $\nabla'\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$—otherwise the integration-by-parts remainder does *not* vanish (which is precisely where Maxwell's displacement current enters in Chapter 4!).
> 6. **Misusing $\mathbf{F} = (\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{B}$ Inside Free Currents:** The true force on an Amperian current-loop magnetic dipole is $\mathbf{F} = \nabla(\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B})$, which equals $(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{m}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})$. They match only in current-free space ($\mathbf{J}_{\text{ext}} = \mathbf{0}$).

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Confusing $\mathbf{H}$ with $\mathbf{B}$ Inside the Uniformly Magnetized Sphere (CSE 2018 Trap):** Candidates given $\Phi_{M,\text{in}} = \frac{M_0}{3}r\cos\theta$ take $-\nabla\Phi_M$ to get $-\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{M}$ and stop there—forgetting to add $+\mathbf{M}$ via $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}} + \mathbf{M}) = +\frac{2}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}$! Always display *both* $\mathbf{H}_{\text{in}}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}}$ explicitly and point out their antiparallel orientation.
- **Unfinished Vector-Potential Integrals (CSE 2008 & 2025 Trap):** When computing $\mathbf{A}$ for a finite straight wire, never leave the integral as an unevaluated $\operatorname{arsinh}$ without stating the exact logarithmic form $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\ln\!\left(\frac{z_2 + \sqrt{x^2+z_2^2}}{z_1 + \sqrt{x^2+z_1^2}}\right)\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ AND verifying $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ as a closing sanity check—this secures full 20/20 step-marks.
- **Qualitative Hand-Waving on Hysteresis Loss (CSE 2026 Trap):** Writing only "hysteresis happens because domains lag behind $H$" earns barely half credit. Examiners look explicitly for (i) Bloch wall **pinning** at dislocations/grain boundaries/inclusions, (ii) discontinuous **Barkhausen jumps** generating microscopic eddy currents and lattice heat, and (iii) the quantitative toroidal proof $w_{\text{cycle}} = \oint H\,dB$.
- **Incomplete Equilibrium Proof for Two Magnetic Dipoles (CSE 2008 Trap):** Merely guessing "parallel" without distinguishing collinear head-to-tail ($\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0$, $U = -2\frac{\mu_0 m_1 m_2}{4\pi r^3}$) from antiparallel side-by-side ($\theta_1 = \pi/2, \theta_2 = -\pi/2$, $U = -\frac{\mu_0 m_1 m_2}{4\pi r^3}$) loses equilibrium justification marks.

### 3. Unseen Conceptual & Analytical Twists

1. **Spherical Ferromagnetic Shell as a Magnetic Shield:** A hollow spherical shell of inner radius $a$, outer radius $b$, and high constant relative permeability $\mu_r \gg 1$ is placed in a uniform external magnetic field $\mathbf{B}_0 = B_0\hat{\mathbf{z}}$. Show that for $\mu_r \gg 1$ and $b > a$, the shielding scales inversely with $\mu_r$.
2. **Vector Potential Method for the Uniformly Magnetised Sphere:** Starting purely from the bound surface current $\mathbf{K}_b = M_0\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ on $r = R$ (with $\mathbf{J}_b = \mathbf{0}$ inside) and solving the azimuthal Poisson/Laplace boundary-value problem for $\mathbf{A}(r,\theta) = A_\phi(r,\theta)\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ directly (matching continuity of $A_\phi$ and the radial derivative jump $\left.\frac{\partial A_{\text{out}}}{\partial r}\right|_R - \left.\frac{\partial A_{\text{in}}}{\partial r}\right|_R = -\mu_0 K_b$), derive $\mathbf{A}_{\text{in}} = \frac{1}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}\times\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{\mathbf{m}\times\hat{\mathbf{r}}}{r^2}$, and confirm $\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ reproduces $\mathbf{B}_{\text{in}} = \frac{2}{3}\mu_0\mathbf{M}$ and the exterior dipole field identically.
