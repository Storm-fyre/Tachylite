# Chapter 3: Electromagnetic Induction, DC Transients, AC Networks & Transformers

## 1. Network Laws, Electromagnetic Induction, Inductance and Magnetic Energy

Once currents vary with time, electric and magnetic fields lose their static independence: a changing magnetic flux curls an electric field around itself, pushing charge through conductors without any battery in sight. This single phenomenon—Faraday's law of induction—bridges field theory directly to lumped electrical engineering.

In circuits whose physical dimensions are tiny compared to the electromagnetic wavelength ($\ell \ll \lambda = c/f$), distributed field energy collapses cleanly into discrete circuit parameters ($R, L, C, M$), turning Maxwell's partial differential equations into Kirchhoff's algebraic and ordinary differential equations.

### 1.1 Kirchhoff's Laws - Field-Theoretic Derivation and Network Analysis

Kirchhoff's two circuit laws are low-frequency lumped-element reductions of two bedrock conservation principles of physics:

1. **Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) — Conservation of Electric Charge:**
   Start from the local continuity equation $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} = 0$. Integrate over an infinitesimal closed volume $\mathcal{V}$ enclosing a circuit junction (node) where $n$ wires meet, and apply the Divergence Theorem:
   $$\oint_{\mathcal{S}} \mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{a} = -\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal{V}} \rho\,d\tau = -\frac{dQ_{\text{node}}}{dt}$$
   Because an ideal conducting junction cannot accumulate net charge ($\frac{dQ_{\text{node}}}{dt} = 0$), the net outward current flux vanishes identically:
   $$\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{n} I_k = 0 \qquad (\text{algebraic sum of currents meeting at any node is zero})}$$

2. **Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) — Conservation of Energy:**
   In a static conservative field, $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$, meaning the net work done per unit charge traversing any closed circuit loop is zero. When time-varying magnetic flux is localized strictly inside discrete inductors, Faraday's law gives $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$. Moving the inductive back-EMF term to the left-hand side defines the lumped inductor terminal voltage drop $v_L = +L\frac{dI}{dt}$, restoring the closed-loop algebraic sum:
   $$\boxed{\sum_{\text{closed loop}} V_k = 0 \qquad \left(\sum \mathcal{E}_{\text{sources}} = \sum I R + \sum L\frac{dI}{dt} + \sum \frac{q}{C}\right)}$$

**Systematic Circuit-Solving Strategies (Mains Speed Protocol):**
- **Mesh (Loop) Analysis:** Assign an independent circulating current $I_m$ to each elementary window of a planar circuit and apply KVL around every mesh. Best when the number of meshes is smaller than the number of non-reference nodes.
- **Nodal Analysis:** Choose one ground node ($V = 0$), assign unknown potentials $V_1, V_2, \dots$ to the remaining nodes, and write KCL at each node in terms of conductances $1/R$. Fastest for parallel multi-source networks and bridge circuits.
- **Thévenin Equivalent Shortcut:** To find the current through a single target branch (such as a galvanometer across an unbalanced Wheatstone bridge), remove that branch, compute the open-circuit voltage $V_{\text{Th}}$ and dead-network resistance $R_{\text{Th}}$ seen across its terminals, and compute $I_{\text{branch}} = \frac{V_{\text{Th}}}{R_{\text{Th}} + R_{\text{branch}}}$ in three lines.

### 1.2 Faraday's Law, Lenz's Law, and Unity of Motional vs. Transformer EMF

Michael Faraday established that whenever the magnetic flux $\Phi_B = \int_{\mathcal{S}} \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{a}$ threading a closed circuit $\mathcal{C}$ changes with time, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced around $\mathcal{C}$:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal{S}} \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{a}}$$

**Lenz's Law (Physical Origin of the Minus Sign):** The induced EMF drives current in a direction whose own magnetic field **opposes the change** in magnetic flux that produced it. Lenz's law is directly enforced by **conservation of energy**: if the induced current aided the flux change, an infinitesimal motion of a magnet toward a coil would accelerate the magnet indefinitely, creating infinite kinetic and Joule energy from nothing.

#### Two Distinct Mechanisms — Motional EMF vs. Transformer EMF
1. **Motional EMF (Moving Circuit in a Static Magnetic Field $\mathbf{B}$):**
   Suppose a loop $\mathcal{C}$ moves with local velocity $\mathbf{v}$ through a time-independent magnetic field $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})$. Free charges inside the wire feel the magnetic Lorentz force per unit charge $\mathbf{f}_m = \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$, generating the motional EMF:
   $$\mathcal{E}_{\text{mot}} = \oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{l}$$
   *Proof of equivalence to $-\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$:* In infinitesimal time $dt$, contour $\mathcal{C}$ sweeps out a side ribbon with outward area element $d\mathbf{a}_{\text{ribbon}} = d\mathbf{l}\times(\mathbf{v}\,dt)$. Applying Gauss's law for magnetism $\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{a} = 0$ over the closed pillbox formed by $\mathcal{S}(t)$ (inward $-\hat{\mathbf{n}}$), $\mathcal{S}(t+dt)$ (outward $+\hat{\mathbf{n}}$), and the side ribbon yields:
   $$\Phi_B(t+dt) - \Phi_B(t) + \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{B}\cdot\left(d\mathbf{l}\times\mathbf{v}\,dt\right) = 0$$
   Using the cyclic scalar triple product $\mathbf{B}\cdot(d\mathbf{l}\times\mathbf{v}) = (\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ gives:
   $$\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\mathcal{E}_{\text{mot}} \implies \mathcal{E}_{\text{mot}} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$
   *(Note on energy: the magnetic force still does zero net work—an external mechanical agent pushing the loop against the opposing magnetic drag force supplies all the electrical energy!)*

2. **Transformer EMF (Stationary Circuit in a Time-Varying Field $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}, t)$):**
   Hold the loop strictly fixed ($\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$) and vary $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}, t)$. Here magnetic forces on stationary charges vanish identically; yet current still flows! Faraday concluded that a changing magnetic field generates a **non-conservative induced electric field** $\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r}, t)$:
   $$\mathcal{E}_{\text{trans}} = \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal{S}} \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{a} = -\int_{\mathcal{S}} \frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}\cdot d\mathbf{a}$$
   Applying Stokes' theorem $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_{\mathcal{S}} (\nabla\times\mathbf{E})\cdot d\mathbf{a}$ for any arbitrary surface $\mathcal{S}$ gives **Faraday's Law in differential form**:
   $$\boxed{\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}}$$

3. **Relativistic Unity:** In pre-relativistic physics, it looked like a miraculous coincidence that moving a wire past a stationary magnet (Lorentz force $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$) and moving a magnet past a stationary wire (induced field $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$) obey the exact same flux rule $-\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$. Special relativity unifies both: $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are frame-dependent projections of a single electromagnetic field tensor $F^{\mu\nu}$.

### 1.3 Self-Inductance, Mutual Inductance, Neumann's Formula, and Magnetic Energy

#### Mutual Inductance $M$ and Neumann's Reciprocity Theorem ($M_{12} = M_{21}$)
Consider two rigid stationary closed circuits $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$ in vacuum carrying currents $I_1$ and $I_2$. By Biot-Savart, $\mathbf{B}_1(\mathbf{r})$ is strictly linear in $I_1$, so the magnetic flux $\Phi_{21}$ through $\mathcal{C}_2$ due to $I_1$ is proportional to $I_1$:

$$\Phi_{21} = M_{21} I_1 \implies \mathcal{E}_2 = -\frac{d\Phi_{21}}{dt} = -M_{21}\frac{dI_1}{dt}$$

**Derivation of Neumann's Formula:** Write $\mathbf{B}_1 = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}_1$ and apply Stokes' theorem to convert the surface integral over $\mathcal{S}_2$ into a line integral around $\mathcal{C}_2$:

$$\Phi_{21} = \int_{\mathcal{S}_2} (\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_1)\cdot d\mathbf{a}_2 = \oint_{\mathcal{C}_2} \mathbf{A}_1(\mathbf{r}_2)\cdot d\mathbf{l}_2$$

Substitute the Coulomb-gauge vector potential $\mathbf{A}_1(\mathbf{r}_2) = \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_1}\frac{d\mathbf{l}_1}{|\mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1|}$ and divide by $I_1$:

$$\boxed{M_{21} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_1}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_2} \frac{d\mathbf{l}_1 \cdot d\mathbf{l}_2}{|\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2|} = M_{12} \equiv M}$$

**Two Deep Consequences of Neumann's Formula:**
1. $M$ is a **purely geometric factor** (depending only on loop shapes, dimensions, separation, and relative orientation, scaled by $\mu_0$ or core permeability $\mu$).
2. Swapping indices $1 \leftrightarrow 2$ leaves $d\mathbf{l}_1\cdot d\mathbf{l}_2$ and $|\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2|$ completely unchanged, proving **reciprocity $M_{12} = M_{21}$** unconditionally—even for two wildly asymmetric, oddly tilted coils!

#### Self-Inductance $L$ and Canonical Inductance Formulas
A single loop carrying current $I$ links its own magnetic flux $\Phi = LI$, defining **self-inductance** $L$ (SI unit: Henry, $1\text{ H} = 1\text{ Wb}\cdot\text{A}^{-1} = 1\text{ V}\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}$) and self-induced back-EMF $\mathcal{E}_{\text{back}} = -L\frac{dI}{dt}$.

1. **Long Solenoid** (length $l \gg R$, cross-sectional area $A = \pi R^2$, total turns $N$, turn density $n = N/l$, core permeability $\mu$):
   Internal field $B = \mu n I$, total flux linkage $\Phi_{\text{tot}} = N(BA) = \mu n^2 A l I$:
   $$\boxed{L_{\text{sol}} = \mu n^2 A l = \frac{\mu N^2 A}{l}}$$
2. **Two Coaxial Long Solenoids** (outer solenoid: $n_1 = N_1/l$, radius $R_1$; inner solenoid: total turns $N_2$, radius $R_2 < R_1$):
   Outer current $I_1$ produces uniform $B_1 = \mu n_1 I_1$ filling the inner coil's **smaller** cross-section $A_2 = \pi R_2^2$:
   $$\Phi_{21} = N_2 B_1 A_2 = \mu n_1 N_2 (\pi R_2^2) I_1 \implies \boxed{M_{\text{coax}} = \mu n_1 N_2 \pi R_2^2 = \frac{\mu N_1 N_2 \pi R_2^2}{l}}$$
3. **Coaxial Cable** (inner conductor radius $a$, outer return sheath radius $b$, length $l$, vacuum dielectric):
   Between $a < s < b$, Ampère's law gives $B_\phi = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi s}$. Flux through the radial strip $l \times (b - a)$ is $\Phi = \int_a^b \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi s}l\,ds = \frac{\mu_0 l I}{2\pi}\ln(b/a)$:
   $$\boxed{\frac{L}{l} = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\ln\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)}$$
4. **Coupled Coils & Coupling Coefficient $k$:**
   For two coupled coils ($L_1, L_2, M$), total stored energy $W = \frac{1}{2}L_1 I_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}L_2 I_2^2 \pm M I_1 I_2$ must remain non-negative for arbitrary $(I_1, I_2)$. Completing the square requires $M^2 \le L_1 L_2$:
   $$\boxed{M = k\sqrt{L_1 L_2} \quad (0 \le k \le 1), \qquad L_{\text{series}} = L_1 + L_2 \pm 2M}$$
   ($+$ for flux-aiding connection, $-$ for flux-opposing connection).

#### Magnetic Field Energy - Unabridged Derivation of $\frac{1}{2}LI^2 = \int\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}\,d\tau$
1. **Circuit Work Form:** To ramp current quasistatically from $0$ to $I$ against self-induced back-EMF $\mathcal{E}_{\text{back}} = -L\frac{dI'}{dt}$, an external source delivers power $P = (-\mathcal{E}_{\text{back}})I' = L I'\frac{dI'}{dt}$:
   $$W = \int_0^I L I'\,dI' = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}L I^2}$$
2. **Field-Energy Volume Integral:** Using $\Phi = L I = \int_{\mathcal{S}} (\nabla\times\mathbf{A})\cdot d\mathbf{a} = \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$, generalize $I\,d\mathbf{l} \to \mathbf{J}\,d\tau$ for a distributed steady current:
   $$W = \frac{1}{2}I\Phi = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}_R} (\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{A})\,d\tau$$
   Substitute Ampère's law $\mathbf{J} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})$ and apply the vector product rule $\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}) = B^2 - \mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})$:
   $$W = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\int_{\mathcal{V}_R} B^2\,d\tau - \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\oint_{\mathcal{S}_R} (\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{a}$$
   Extend the bounding sphere $\mathcal{S}_R$ to $R \to \infty$. For any localized current distribution (lowest surviving multipole is a magnetic dipole), $|\mathbf{A}| \sim R^{-2}$, $|\mathbf{B}| \sim R^{-3}$, and $da \sim R^2$, so the surface integral decays as $R^{-3} \to 0$:
   $$\boxed{W = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\int_{\text{all space}} B^2\,d\tau, \qquad u_m \equiv \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} \quad \left(\text{linear medium: } u_m = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{H} = \frac{B^2}{2\mu}\right)}$$

**Dimensional Check:** $[B^2/\mu_0] = \text{T}^2/(\text{T}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}) = (\text{N}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}\cdot\text{m}^{-1})(\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}) = \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^{-2} = \text{J}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$.
**Solenoid Cross-Check:** $u_m \cdot (Al) = \frac{(\mu_0 n I)^2}{2\mu_0}Al = \frac{1}{2}(\mu_0 n^2 A l)I^2 = \frac{1}{2}LI^2$ — exact agreement!

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Unbalanced Wheatstone Bridge & Coaxial Solenoid Mutual Induction
> **Problem:**
> *(a)* The four arms of an unbalanced Wheatstone bridge have resistances $R_{AB} = 100\,\Omega$, $R_{BC} = 10\,\Omega$, $R_{CD} = 5\,\Omega$, and $R_{DA} = 60\,\Omega$. A galvanometer of resistance $R_g = 15\,\Omega$ is connected across $BD$, and a $10.0\text{ V}$ battery is connected across $AC$. Calculate the current through the galvanometer.
> *(b)* A long solenoid has $n_1 = 220\text{ turns}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}$ and diameter $3.2\text{ cm}$. Inside at its center sits a coaxial $130$-turn close-packed coil of diameter $2.1\text{ cm}$. If the solenoid current increases steadily from $0$ to $1.5\text{ A}$ in $0.16\text{ s}$, find the mutual inductance $M$ and the magnitude of the induced EMF in the central coil.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a) (Thévenin Equivalent Method — Verified by Nodal KCL):**
> 1. Remove the galvanometer across $BD$ and set $V_A = 10.0\text{ V}$, $V_C = 0\text{ V}$.
>    - Open-circuit node potentials:
>      $$V_B = 10\left(\frac{10}{100 + 10}\right) = \frac{10}{11}\text{ V}, \qquad V_D = 10\left(\frac{5}{60 + 5}\right) = \frac{10}{13}\text{ V}$$
>    - Thévenin voltage:
>      $$V_{\text{Th}} = V_B - V_D = \frac{10}{11} - \frac{10}{13} = \frac{20}{143}\text{ V} = 0.1399\text{ V}$$
> 2. Short the ideal voltage source ($A$ tied to $C$) to find Thévenin resistance across $BD$:
>    $$R_{\text{Th}} = (R_{AB} \parallel R_{BC}) + (R_{DA} \parallel R_{CD}) = \frac{100 \times 10}{110} + \frac{60 \times 5}{65} = \frac{100}{11} + \frac{60}{13} = \frac{1960}{143}\,\Omega = 13.71\,\Omega$$
> 3. Reconnect $R_g = 15\,\Omega$ across $BD$:
>    $$I_g = \frac{V_{\text{Th}}}{R_{\text{Th}} + R_g} = \frac{20/143}{(1960 + 15\times 143)/143} = \frac{20}{4105} = \frac{4}{821}\text{ A} = \boxed{4.87\text{ mA}\;(\text{from }B\text{ to }D)}$$
>
> **Part (b) (Coaxial Solenoid Mutual Inductance):**
> 1. Convert strictly to SI: $n_1 = 220\text{ cm}^{-1} = 2.20 \times 10^4\text{ m}^{-1}$, $N_2 = 130$, inner coil radius $r_2 = \frac{2.1\text{ cm}}{2} = 1.05 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$.
>    *(Critical Trap: Use the **inner coil's** area $A_2 = \pi r_2^2$, NOT the solenoid's larger diameter $3.2\text{ cm}$!)*
>    $$A_2 = \pi(1.05 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m})^2 = 3.464 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$$
> 2. Mutual inductance $M = \mu_0 n_1 N_2 A_2$:
>    $$M = (4\pi \times 10^{-7})(2.20 \times 10^4)(130)(3.464 \times 10^{-4}) = \boxed{1.245\text{ mH}}$$
> 3. Magnitude of induced EMF in the central coil:
>    $$|\mathcal{E}_2| = M\frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t} = (1.2448 \times 10^{-3}\text{ H})\left(\frac{1.5\text{ A}}{0.16\text{ s}}\right) = \boxed{11.7\text{ mV}}$$

## 2. DC Transients - LR, CR, Series LCR Discharge and Ballistic Galvanometer

Capacitors resist instantaneous voltage jumps ($v_C = q/C$ requires finite time to accumulate charge through $R$), while inductors resist instantaneous current jumps ($v_L = L\frac{dI}{dt}$ would diverge for a step in $I$). Whenever a DC switch flips, circuits therefore transition smoothly along exponential or damped-oscillatory **transient** trajectories before settling into steady state.

### 2.1 Growth and Decay of Current in an $LR$ Circuit

**Growth (Battery $\mathcal{E}$ switched on at $t = 0$, $I(0) = 0$):** KVL gives $L\frac{dI}{dt} + RI = \mathcal{E}$. Setting steady-state current $I_0 \equiv \mathcal{E}/R$ and separating variables:

$$\int_0^I \frac{dI'}{I_0 - I'} = \frac{R}{L}\int_0^t dt' \implies -\ln\!\left(\frac{I_0 - I}{I_0}\right) = \frac{R}{L}t \implies \boxed{I(t) = I_0\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_L}\right), \qquad \tau_L \equiv \frac{L}{R}}$$

**Decay (Battery shorted out at $t = 0$ with initial current $I(0) = I_0$):** $L\frac{dI}{dt} + RI = 0 \implies \boxed{I(t) = I_0 e^{-t/\tau_L}}$.

- **Inductive Time Constant $\tau_L = L/R$:** Time required for growing current to reach $(1 - e^{-1}) \approx 63.2\%$ of $I_0$, or for decaying current to fall to $e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%$ of $I_0$.
- **Instantaneous Energy Rate Balance:** Multiplying KVL by $I$ gives $\mathcal{E}I = I^2 R + \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}LI^2\right)$, showing that battery power splits cleanly at every instant into Joule dissipation $I^2 R$ and magnetic energy storage rate $LI\frac{dI}{dt} = I(\mathcal{E} - IR)$.

---

### 2.2 Charging and Discharging of a $CR$ Circuit

**Charging (Uncharged capacitor $q(0) = 0$ connected to battery $\mathcal{E}$ through $R$ at $t = 0$):** KVL gives $R\frac{dq}{dt} + \frac{q}{C} = \mathcal{E}$. With final charge $Q_0 \equiv C\mathcal{E}$:

$$\int_0^q \frac{dq'}{Q_0 - q'} = \frac{1}{RC}\int_0^t dt' \implies \boxed{q(t) = Q_0\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_C}\right), \qquad I(t) = \frac{dq}{dt} = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}e^{-t/\tau_C}, \qquad \tau_C \equiv RC}$$

**Discharging ($q(0) = Q_0$ shorted through $R$ at $t = 0$):** $R\frac{dq}{dt} + \frac{q}{C} = 0 \implies \boxed{q(t) = Q_0 e^{-t/\tau_C}, \qquad I(t) = -\frac{Q_0}{RC}e^{-t/\tau_C}}$.

**Universal 50% Charging Dissipation Theorem:** Over $[0,\infty)$, the battery supplies total work $W_{\text{batt}} = Q_0\mathcal{E} = C\mathcal{E}^2$. Exactly half is stored electrostatically ($W_C = \frac{1}{2}C\mathcal{E}^2$), while the remaining half is irreversibly dissipated as Joule heat in $R$—*independent of the numerical value of $R$*:

$$W_R = \int_0^\infty I(t)^2 R\,dt = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}\int_0^\infty e^{-2t/(RC)}\,dt = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}\left(\frac{RC}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}C\mathcal{E}^2$$

### 2.3 Series $LCR$ Discharge - Overdamped, Critically Damped, and Underdamped Regimes

When both $L$ and $C$ are present, energy sloshes back and forth between the capacitor's electric field and the inductor's magnetic field while resistor $R$ bleeds energy away as heat.

**Governing ODE & Initial Conditions:** Discharge a capacitor charged initially to $q(0) = Q_0$ with zero initial current $I(0) = \dot{q}(0) = 0$ through series $L$ and $R$ at $t = 0$:

$$L\frac{d^2 q}{dt^2} + R\frac{dq}{dt} + \frac{q}{C} = 0 \implies \frac{d^2 q}{dt^2} + 2b\frac{dq}{dt} + \omega_0^2 q = 0$$

where $b \equiv \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{\gamma}{2}$ (damping coefficient, with $\gamma = R/L$) and $\omega_0 \equiv \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$ (undamped natural angular frequency). Substituting $q \propto e^{rt}$ yields the characteristic roots:

$$r_{\pm} = -b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - \omega_0^2}$$

The discriminant $b^2 - \omega_0^2$ divides the transient physics into three qualitatively distinct regimes:

```
    q(t)/Q_0
      1.0 *---.
          |    \   '.  Overdamped (R > 2 sqrt(L/C)): sluggish monotonic decay
          |     \    '-.
          |      \      '---.__   Critically damped (R = 2 sqrt(L/C)): fastest non-oscillatory
      0.0 +-------\------.-----'----------> t
          |        \    / \  _.---  Underdamped (R < 2 sqrt(L/C)): decaying oscillations
          |         '--'   ''       at omega_d = sqrt(omega_0^2 - b^2)
```

1. **Case I — Overdamped (Dead-Beat), $b > \omega_0 \iff R > 2\sqrt{L/C}$:**
   Let $\beta \equiv \sqrt{b^2 - \omega_0^2} > 0$. Both roots are real and negative: $q(t) = e^{-bt}\left(A e^{\beta t} + B e^{-\beta t}\right)$. Enforcing $q(0) = Q_0 \implies A + B = Q_0$ and $\dot{q}(0) = 0 \implies (-b + \beta)A + (-b - \beta)B = 0 \implies A - B = \frac{b}{\beta}Q_0$:
   $$A = \frac{Q_0}{2}\left(1 + \frac{b}{\beta}\right), \qquad B = \frac{Q_0}{2}\left(1 - \frac{b}{\beta}\right)$$
   Combining exponentials into hyperbolic functions and differentiating carefully (note $\beta^2 - b^2 = -\omega_0^2$):
   $$\boxed{q(t) = Q_0 e^{-bt}\left(\cosh\beta t + \frac{b}{\beta}\sinh\beta t\right), \qquad I(t) = \frac{dq}{dt} = -\frac{Q_0\omega_0^2}{\beta}e^{-bt}\sinh\beta t}$$
   Charge decays monotonically to zero without ever reversing sign.

2. **Case II — Critically Damped, $b = \omega_0 \iff R = 2\sqrt{L/C}$:**
   Repeated root $r = -b$ yields general solution $q(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-bt}$. Enforcing $q(0) = Q_0 \implies A = Q_0$ and $\dot{q}(0) = B - bA = 0 \implies B = bQ_0$:
   $$\boxed{q(t) = Q_0(1 + bt)e^{-bt} = Q_0\left(1 + \frac{\gamma}{2}t\right)e^{-\gamma t/2}, \qquad I(t) = -Q_0 b^2 t e^{-bt}}$$
   *(Note: If instead the circuit starts with initial current $I(0) = I_0$ and $\dot{I}(0) = 0$, the identical ODE for $I(t)$ yields $I(t) = I_0(1 + \frac{\gamma}{2}t)e^{-\gamma t/2}$!)* Critical damping achieves the **fastest possible decay** to zero without oscillation—crucial for dead-beat electrical meters and automotive suspension analogies.

3. **Case III — Underdamped (Oscillatory), $b < \omega_0 \iff R < 2\sqrt{L/C}$:**
   Let $\omega_d \equiv \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{4L^2}}$ (damped angular frequency). Setting $\beta = j\omega_d$ directly in Case I (using $\cosh(j\omega_d t) = \cos\omega_d t$ and $\frac{\sinh(j\omega_d t)}{j\omega_d} = \frac{\sin\omega_d t}{\omega_d}$) yields:
   $$\boxed{q(t) = Q_0 e^{-bt}\left(\cos\omega_d t + \frac{b}{\omega_d}\sin\omega_d t\right) = \frac{Q_0\omega_0}{\omega_d}e^{-bt}\cos(\omega_d t - \alpha), \qquad I(t) = -\frac{Q_0\omega_0^2}{\omega_d}e^{-bt}\sin\omega_d t}$$
   where $\tan\alpha = b/\omega_d$, oscillation frequency is $f_d = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{4L^2}}$, and the **logarithmic decrement** (ratio of successive full-period peaks) is $\Lambda = \ln\frac{q(t)}{q(t + T_d)} = b T_d = \frac{\pi R}{\omega_d L}$.

*(Charging $LCR$ Circuit: With a DC battery $\mathcal{E}$ in series and $q(0) = 0, I(0) = 0$, substituting $u(t) = q(t) - C\mathcal{E}$ satisfies the exact same homogeneous ODE with $u(0) = -Q_0$, giving $q_{\text{charge}}(t) = Q_0 - q_{\text{discharge}}(t)$ in all three regimes!)*

### 2.4 Moving-Coil Ballistic Galvanometer (Impulse Charge & Damping Correction)

A **ballistic galvanometer** measures the **total electric charge** $Q = \int_0^\tau i(t)\,dt$ passed in a brief transient pulse (e.g., capacitor discharge or search-coil flux flip). Unlike a dead-beat ammeter, its moving coil is built with a **large moment of inertia $I_m$** (yielding a long natural period $T \sim 10\text{–}15\text{ s}$) wound on a **non-conducting frame** (minimal eddy damping) so the entire charge pulse finishes ($\tau \ll T$) *before* the coil deflects appreciably from $\theta = 0$.

**Unabridged Theory & Damping Correction:**
1. **Angular Impulse During the Pulse ($0 \le t \le \tau$):** In a radial magnetic field $B$, an $N$-turn coil of area $A$ carrying $i(t)$ experiences deflecting torque $\tau_d = N B A i(t)$. Because $\theta \approx 0$ during $\tau$, the suspension restoring torque $c\theta$ is negligible, so Newton's rotational law integrates cleanly:
   $$I_m \frac{d\omega}{dt} = N B A i(t) \implies I_m \omega_{\text{init}} = N B A \int_0^\tau i(t)\,dt = N B A Q \implies \omega_{\text{init}} = \frac{N B A Q}{I_m}$$
2. **Undamped Maximum Throw ($\theta_0$):** After $t = \tau$, the coil swings freely against the suspension's torsional constant $c$. Conservation of mechanical energy converts initial rotational kinetic energy completely into torsional potential energy at the peak throw $\theta_0$:
   $$\frac{1}{2}I_m \omega_{\text{init}}^2 = \frac{1}{2}c\theta_0^2 \implies \frac{(N B A Q)^2}{I_m} = c\theta_0^2 \implies Q = \frac{\sqrt{c I_m}}{NBA}\theta_0$$
   Eliminating $I_m$ via the natural oscillation period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{I_m/c} \implies \sqrt{c I_m} = \frac{c T}{2\pi}$ yields:
   $$Q = \left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\left(\frac{c}{NBA}\right)\theta_0 = \frac{T}{2\pi}k\,\theta_0$$
   where $k \equiv \frac{c}{NBA}$ is the steady-current reduction factor ($I = k\theta$).
3. **Logarithmic Decrement Damping Correction:** In practice, air friction and residual electromagnetic damping decay the amplitude exponentially as $e^{-bt}$. The observed first throw $\theta_1$ occurs at one **quarter-period** ($t = T/4$), so $\theta_1 = \theta_0 e^{-bT/4}$. Define the standard half-cycle **logarithmic decrement** $\lambda \equiv \ln\left(\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_2}\right) = \frac{bT}{2}$ (equivalently, full-cycle decrement $\Lambda = \ln\left(\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_3}\right) = bT = 2\lambda$), where $\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3$ are successive peak swings on alternating sides of zero. Then:
   $$\theta_0 = \theta_1 e^{+bT/4} = \theta_1 e^{+\lambda/2} \approx \theta_1\left(1 + \frac{\lambda}{2}\right) \qquad (\text{for small }\lambda \ll 1)$$
   Substituting into $Q$ gives the exact corrected ballistic formula:
   $$\boxed{Q = \frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\frac{c}{NBA}\right)\theta_1\left(1 + \frac{\lambda}{2}\right), \qquad \lambda = \ln\!\left(\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_2}\right) = \frac{1}{n}\ln\!\left(\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_{n+1}}\right)}$$

## 3. AC Circuits, Resonance, Quality Factor and AC Bridges

Driving an $LCR$ network with a sinusoidal EMF $v(t) = V_0\cos\omega t$ produces a steady-state sinusoidal response at the exact same frequency $\omega$, shifted in amplitude and phase. Representing sinusoids as rotating complex vectors (**phasors**) converts integro-differential circuit equations into elementary complex algebra, making resonance, sharpness, power factor, and bridge balance completely transparent.

### 3.1 Mean Values, RMS Values, Complex Phasors, and AC Power

For a sinusoidal current $i(t) = I_0\sin\omega t$ of period $T = 2\pi/\omega$:
- **Full-cycle mean:** $\langle i \rangle_{\text{full}} = 0$.
- **Half-cycle mean:** $I_{\text{mean}} = \frac{1}{T/2}\int_0^{T/2} I_0\sin\omega t\,dt = \frac{I_0}{\pi}[-\cos\omega t]_0^{\pi/\omega} = \boxed{\frac{2I_0}{\pi} \approx 0.637 I_0}$.
- **Root-Mean-Square (RMS / Effective) Value:** Defined as the equivalent DC current producing the same average Joule heat per cycle:
  $$I_{\text{rms}}^2 R = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T I_0^2 R\sin^2\omega t\,dt = \frac{1}{2}I_0^2 R \implies \boxed{I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 I_0, \qquad V_{\text{rms}} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}}}$$
  *(Form factor $\equiv I_{\text{rms}}/I_{\text{mean}} = \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}} \approx 1.11$.)*

#### Complex Phasors ($j \equiv \sqrt{-1}$) and Complex Impedance $Z$
Write $v(t) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\tilde{V} e^{j\omega t}\right)$ and $i(t) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\tilde{I} e^{j\omega t}\right)$. Differentiation becomes multiplication by $j\omega$ and integration becomes division by $j\omega$, reducing element laws to generalized Ohm's law $\tilde{V} = Z\tilde{I}$:
- **Resistor:** $Z_R = R$ (voltage and current in phase, $\phi = 0$).
- **Inductor:** $Z_L = j\omega L = jX_L$ (voltage **leads** current by $+90^\circ$).
- **Capacitor:** $Z_C = \frac{1}{j\omega C} = -j\frac{1}{\omega C} = -jX_C$ (voltage **lags** current by $-90^\circ$).

#### Average AC Power and Power Factor
For $v(t) = V_0\cos\omega t$ and $i(t) = I_0\cos(\omega t - \phi)$ (where $\phi = \arg(Z)$ is the phase angle by which voltage leads current):

$$\begin{aligned}
P_{\text{avg}} &= \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T V_0 I_0\cos\omega t\cos(\omega t - \phi)\,dt = \frac{V_0 I_0}{2T}\int_0^T \left[\cos\phi + \cos(2\omega t - \phi)\right]dt \\
&= \frac{1}{2}V_0 I_0\cos\phi = \boxed{V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi = I_{\text{rms}}^2 R}
\end{aligned}$$

Here $\cos\phi = \frac{R}{|Z|}$ is the **power factor**. Decomposing $\tilde{I}$ into an in-phase (**active**) component $I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi$ and a quadrature (**wattless**) component $I_{\text{rms}}\sin\phi$ shows that pure $L$ and $C$ ($\phi = \pm 90^\circ, \cos\phi = 0$) bounce reactive energy back and forth every quarter-cycle with **zero net power dissipation**.

### 3.2 Series $LCR$ Resonance (Acceptor Circuit) - Bandwidth, Sharpness, and $Q$-Factor

A series combination of $R, L, C$ driven by AC voltage $\tilde{V}$ has complex impedance:

$$Z(\omega) = R + j\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right), \qquad |Z(\omega)| = \sqrt{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}, \qquad \tan\phi = \frac{\omega L - 1/(\omega C)}{R}$$

1. **Resonance Condition ($\operatorname{Im}(Z) = 0$):** Inductive reactance cancels capacitive reactance ($\omega_0 L = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C}$):
   $$\boxed{\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}, \qquad f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}}$$
   At $\omega = \omega_0$: impedance drops to its pure real minimum $|Z|_{\min} = R$, phase $\phi = 0$ (unity power factor $\cos\phi = 1$), and current peaks at $I_{\max} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{R}$. Because it preferentially accepts signals at $f_0$, the series $LCR$ circuit is called an **acceptor circuit**.

2. **Exact Half-Power Frequencies ($\omega_1, \omega_2$) and Bandwidth ($\Delta\omega$):**
   The half-power frequencies are defined by $P(\omega) = \frac{1}{2}P_{\max} \iff I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{I_{\max}}{\sqrt{2}} \iff |Z(\omega)| = \sqrt{2}R \iff \left|\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right| = R$:
   - Upper half-power point $\omega_2 > \omega_0$: $\omega_2 L - \frac{1}{\omega_2 C} = +R \implies \omega_2^2 - \frac{R}{L}\omega_2 - \omega_0^2 = 0$.
   - Lower half-power point $\omega_1 < \omega_0$: $\omega_1 L - \frac{1}{\omega_1 C} = -R \implies \omega_1^2 + \frac{R}{L}\omega_1 - \omega_0^2 = 0$.
   Solving both quadratics (retaining positive physical frequencies):
   $$\omega_2 = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + \left(\frac{R}{2L}\right)^2} + \frac{R}{2L}, \qquad \omega_1 = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + \left(\frac{R}{2L}\right)^2} - \frac{R}{2L}$$
   Subtracting and multiplying yields two **exact** identities valid for all $R, L, C$ without approximation:
   $$\boxed{\Delta\omega \equiv \omega_2 - \omega_1 = \frac{R}{L} \quad \left(\Delta f = \frac{R}{2\pi L}\right), \qquad \omega_1\omega_2 = \omega_0^2}$$

3. **Quality Factor $Q$ — Equivalence of Energy, Selectivity, and Voltage-Magnification Definitions:**
   - **Energy Storage vs. Loss:** $Q \equiv 2\pi\frac{\text{Max energy stored at }\omega_0}{\text{Energy dissipated per cycle}} = 2\pi\frac{\frac{1}{2}L I_0^2}{\left(\frac{1}{2}I_0^2 R\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{\omega_0}\right)} = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R}$.
   - **Sharpness / Frequency Selectivity:** $Q = \frac{\omega_0}{\Delta\omega} = \frac{\omega_0}{R/L} = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R}$ (larger $Q$ means a razor-sharp narrow resonance peak!).
   - **Voltage Magnification:** At resonance, voltages across $L$ and $C$ are $180^\circ$ out of phase (canceling each other in the loop) while each reaches magnitude $V_L = V_C = I_{\max}(\omega_0 L) = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{R}\omega_0 L = Q V_{\text{rms}}$.
   $$\boxed{Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C R} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} = \frac{\omega_0}{\Delta\omega}}$$

4. **Universal $Q$-Parameterized Impedance Form:** Factoring $R$ out of $Z(\omega)$ using $\frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = Q$ and $\frac{1}{\omega_0 C R} = Q$ produces the compact expression frequently probed by UPSC:
   $$\boxed{Z(\omega) = R\left[1 + jQ\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_0} - \frac{\omega_0}{\omega}\right)\right]}$$
   - At resonance ($\omega = \omega_0$): $Z = R$.
   - At half-power points ($\omega_{1,2}$): $Z = R(1 \pm j) \implies |Z| = \sqrt{2}R$, $\phi = \pm 45^\circ$.
   - Low-frequency limit ($\omega \ll \omega_0$): $Z \approx -j Q R \frac{\omega_0}{\omega} = \frac{1}{j\omega C}$ (capacitor dominates).
   - High-frequency limit ($\omega \gg \omega_0$): $Z \approx +j Q R \frac{\omega}{\omega_0} = j\omega L$ (inductor dominates).

### 3.3 Parallel $LCR$ Resonance (Practical Tank / Rejector Circuit)

In real RF oscillators and filter traps, a practical coil (inductance $L$ with winding resistance $R$) is wired in parallel with a low-loss capacitor $C$ across supply voltage $\tilde{V}$. Analyze via complex **admittance** $Y(\omega) \equiv 1/Z(\omega)$:

$$Y(\omega) = \frac{1}{R + j\omega L} + j\omega C = \frac{R - j\omega L}{R^2 + \omega^2 L^2} + j\omega C = \frac{R}{R^2 + \omega^2 L^2} + j\left(\omega C - \frac{\omega L}{R^2 + \omega^2 L^2}\right)$$

1. **Resonance Condition (Unity Power Factor, $\operatorname{Im}(Y) = 0$):**
   $$\omega_r C = \frac{\omega_r L}{R^2 + \omega_r^2 L^2} \implies R^2 + \omega_r^2 L^2 = \frac{L}{C} \implies \boxed{\omega_r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{L^2}} = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{Q_0^2}}}$$
   where $Q_0 \equiv \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$. Real parallel resonance exists **only if** $R < \sqrt{L/C}$ ($Q_0 > 1$). When $Q_0 \gg 1$, $\omega_r \approx \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.

2. **Dynamic Resistance ($Z_d$) and Current Rejection:** At $\omega = \omega_r$, $\operatorname{Im}(Y) = 0$ and $R^2 + \omega_r^2 L^2 = L/C$, so:
   $$Y(\omega_r) = \frac{R}{L/C} = \frac{CR}{L} \implies \boxed{Z_d = R_d \equiv \frac{1}{Y(\omega_r)} = \frac{L}{CR}}$$
   For small $R$, $Z_d$ becomes enormous and line current drops to a sharp **minimum** $I_{\min} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{Z_d} = \frac{CR}{L}V_{\text{rms}}$—hence **rejector circuit**!

3. **Current Magnification & Ideal Parallel $R_p \parallel L \parallel C$ Duality:**
   Inside the tank loop at resonance, the branch current through the capacitor is $I_C = \omega_r C V_{\text{rms}}$, giving circulating-to-line current magnification $\frac{I_C}{I_{\min}} = \frac{\omega_r C}{CR/L} = \frac{\omega_r L}{R} = Q$.
   *(Ideal 3-Branch Remark: For a pure resistor $R_p$ in parallel with lossless $L$ and $C$, $Y = \frac{1}{R_p} + j(\omega C - \frac{1}{\omega L})$, which is the exact dual of series $LCR$: $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$, $Z_{\max} = R_p$, $\Delta\omega = \frac{1}{R_p C}$, and $Q_p = \frac{R_p}{\omega_0 L} = \omega_0 R_p C = R_p\sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}$.)*

### 3.4 AC Bridges - General Balance Condition and Four Classic Bridges

An AC four-arm Wheatstone bridge with complex impedances $Z_1, Z_2, Z_3, Z_4$ (arms $AB, AD, BC, DC$ respectively, source across $AC$, null detector across $BD$) achieves zero detector current ($V_B = V_D$) when:

$$\boxed{Z_1 Z_4 = Z_2 Z_3 \iff \begin{cases} |Z_1||Z_4| = |Z_2||Z_3| & \text{(Magnitude balance)} \\ \theta_1 + \theta_4 = \theta_2 + \theta_3 & \text{(Phase balance)} \end{cases}}$$

Equating real and imaginary parts provides **two independent real equations**, determining both the resistive and reactive components of an unknown impedance simultaneously.

#### 1. Maxwell's Inductance-Capacitance Bridge (Medium-$Q$ Inductance, $1 < Q < 10$)
- **Topology:** Arm $AB$: unknown coil $Z_1 = R_x + j\omega L_x$; Arm $AD$: pure resistor $R_2$; Arm $BC$: pure resistor $R_3$; Arm $DC$: parallel combination $R_4 \parallel C_4$ with admittance $Y_4 = \frac{1}{R_4} + j\omega C_4$.
- **Balance ($Z_1 = Z_2 Z_3 Y_4$):**
  $$R_x + j\omega L_x = R_2 R_3\left(\frac{1}{R_4} + j\omega C_4\right) \implies \boxed{R_x = \frac{R_2 R_3}{R_4}, \qquad L_x = R_2 R_3 C_4}$$
- **Merits & Limits:** Completely independent of supply frequency $\omega$; uses a standard capacitor (superior shielding and accuracy compared to standard inductors). However, requires an expensive variable standard capacitor $C_4$ and suffers slow convergence for very low-$Q$ ($Q < 1$) or very high-$Q$ ($Q > 10$) coils.

#### 2. Anderson's Bridge (Precision Low-$Q$ Inductance with a *Fixed* Standard Capacitor)
Anderson's bridge modifies Maxwell's bridge by replacing the variable capacitor with a **fixed standard capacitor $C$** and an adjustable resistor $r$ wired into an internal node $E$:
- **Topology:** Source across $A, C$; Arm $AB$: unknown coil $Z_1 = R_x + j\omega L_x$; Arm $BC$: $R_3$; Arm $AD$: $R_2$; Arm $DC$: $R_4$; Resistor $r$ connects $D \to E$; Fixed standard capacitor $C$ connects $E \to C$; Null detector connects $B \to E$.
- **Unabridged Null-Balance Derivation ($I_{\text{det}} = 0 \implies V_B = V_E$):**
  Let $I_1$ flow along $A \to B \to C$, and let $I_2$ through $R_2$ split at $D$ into $I_4$ (through $R_4$ to $C$) and $I_C$ (through $r$ and $C$ to $C$), so $I_2 = I_4 + I_C$.
  1. Equating potential drop $E \to C$ to $B \to C$:
     $$I_C\left(\frac{1}{j\omega C}\right) = I_1 R_3 \implies I_C = j\omega C R_3 I_1$$
  2. Equating potential drop $D \to C$ (via $R_4$) to $D \to E \to C$ (via $r + \frac{1}{j\omega C}$):
     $$I_4 R_4 = I_C\left(r + \frac{1}{j\omega C}\right) = I_1 R_3(1 + j\omega C r) \implies I_4 = \frac{R_3}{R_4}(1 + j\omega C r)I_1$$
  3. Equating potential drop $A \to B$ to $A \to D \to E$ (since $V_B = V_E$):
     $$I_1(R_x + j\omega L_x) = I_2 R_2 + I_C r = I_4 R_2 + I_C(R_2 + r)$$
  4. Substituting $I_4$ and $I_C$ and canceling $I_1$:
     $$R_x + j\omega L_x = \frac{R_2 R_3}{R_4}(1 + j\omega C r) + j\omega C R_3(R_2 + r) = \frac{R_2 R_3}{R_4} + j\omega C\frac{R_3}{R_4}\left[r(R_2 + R_4) + R_2 R_4\right]$$
  5. Separating real and imaginary parts:
     $$\boxed{R_x = \frac{R_2 R_3}{R_4}, \qquad L_x = C\frac{R_3}{R_4}\left[r(R_2 + R_4) + R_2 R_4\right]}$$
- **Sanity Check & Merits:** Setting $r = 0$ immediately recovers Maxwell's bridge ($L_x = R_2 R_3 C$). Frequency-independent; allows rapid non-interacting balance (balance $R_x$ with DC first via $R_2, R_3, R_4$, then tune $r$ alone for AC null!).

#### 3. De Sauty's Bridge (Capacitance Comparison)
- **Lossless Capacitors:** Arm $AB$: $C_1$; Arm $AD$: $C_2$; Arm $BC$: $R_3$; Arm $DC$: $R_4$. Balance $\left(\frac{1}{j\omega C_1}\right)R_4 = \left(\frac{1}{j\omega C_2}\right)R_3$ gives:
  $$\boxed{\frac{C_1}{C_2} = \frac{R_4}{R_3}}$$
- **Modified (Grover) De Sauty for Lossy Dielectrics:** Real capacitors carry equivalent series loss resistances $r_1, r_2$. Adding external series trimmers $R_1, R_2$ gives $Z_1 = (R_1 + r_1) + \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$ and $Z_2 = (R_2 + r_2) + \frac{1}{j\omega C_2}$, yielding simultaneous balance:
  $$\boxed{\frac{C_1}{C_2} = \frac{R_4}{R_3} = \frac{R_2 + r_2}{R_1 + r_1}}$$

#### 4. Wien's Bridge (Audio-Frequency Measurement & Oscillator Feedback Network)
- **Topology:** Arm $AB$: series $R_1, C_1$ ($Z_1 = R_1 + \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}$); Arm $AD$: parallel $R_2 \parallel C_2$ ($Y_2 = \frac{1}{R_2} + j\omega C_2$); Arm $BC$: $R_3$; Arm $DC$: $R_4$.
- **Balance ($Z_1 Y_2 = R_3 / R_4$):**
  $$\left(R_1 + \frac{1}{j\omega C_1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_2} + j\omega C_2\right) = \left(\frac{R_1}{R_2} + \frac{C_2}{C_1}\right) + j\left(\omega R_1 C_2 - \frac{1}{\omega C_1 R_2}\right) = \frac{R_3}{R_4}$$
- Equating real and imaginary parts yields **frequency-selective balance**:
  $$\boxed{\frac{R_3}{R_4} = \frac{R_1}{R_2} + \frac{C_2}{C_1}, \qquad \omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{R_1 R_2 C_1 C_2}} \implies f = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{R_1 R_2 C_1 C_2}}}$$
- **Standard Symmetric Design ($R_1 = R_2 = R$, $C_1 = C_2 = C$):** Fixes the resistance ratio $R_3 / R_4 = 2$ and measures unknown frequency cleanly via $f = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}$!

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Series AC Power Factor & Parallel Tank Rejector Resonance

Determine (i) the power factor of the circuit, (ii) the total average power absorbed by the circuit, and (iii) the power dissipated inside the coil alone.

Calculate (i) the parallel resonant frequency $f_r$, (ii) the dynamic impedance $Z_d$, (iii) the minimum line current $I_{\min}$, and (iv) the quality factor $Q$ at resonance.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a):**
> 1. **Total Resistance, Reactance, and Impedance:**
>    $$R = R_1 + R_2 = 44 + 36 = 80\,\Omega, \qquad X = X_L - X_C = 90 - 30 = +60\,\Omega\;(\text{inductive})$$

>    $$\cos\phi = \frac{R}{|Z|} = \frac{80}{100} = \boxed{0.80\;\text{(lagging)}}$$

>    $$I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{|Z|} = \frac{200\text{ V}}{100\,\Omega} = 2.00\text{ A}$$
>    $$P_{\text{tot}} = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi = (200)(2.00)(0.80) = \boxed{320\text{ W}}$$
>    *(Check: $I_{\text{rms}}^2 R = (2.00)^2(80) = 320\text{ W}$.)*
> 4. **(iii) Power Dissipated in the Coil:** Pure reactance $X_L$ dissipates zero average power; only the coil's internal resistance $R_2 = 36\,\Omega$ generates heat:
>    $$P_{\text{coil}} = I_{\text{rms}}^2 R_2 = (2.00)^2(36) = \boxed{144\text{ W}}$$
>
> **Part (b):**
> 1. **Parallel Resonant Frequency $f_r$:**
>    $$\frac{1}{LC} = \frac{1}{(0.20)(5.0 \times 10^{-6})} = 1.00 \times 10^6\text{ rad}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-2}, \qquad \left(\frac{R}{L}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{20}{0.20}\right)^2 = 1.00 \times 10^4\text{ rad}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$$

>    $$Z_d = \frac{L}{CR} = \frac{0.20}{(5.0 \times 10^{-6})(20)} = \boxed{2.00\text{ k}\Omega}$$

>    $$Q = \frac{\omega_r L}{R} = \frac{(995.0)(0.20)}{20} = \boxed{9.95}$$

## 4. Principle of the Transformer - Ideal Ratios, Impedance Matching, Phasor Diagram and Loss Analysis

A **transformer** is a static electromagnetic machine that steps AC voltage up or down at constant frequency via **mutual induction** between two electrically isolated coils—the **primary** ($N_p$ turns) and **secondary** ($N_s$ turns)—tightly linked through a common laminated high-permeability ferromagnetic core.

### 4.1 Ideal Transformer Equations and EMF Formula

Assume an **ideal transformer**: (i) zero winding resistances ($R_p = R_s = 0$), (ii) zero flux leakage (every flux line links both windings identically), (iii) infinite core permeability ($\mu_r \to \infty$, negligible magnetizing current), and (iv) zero core iron losses.

Let a sinusoidal alternating flux $\Phi(t) = \Phi_m\sin\omega t$ (where $\omega = 2\pi f$) circulate inside the core. By Faraday's law, the induced EMFs in primary and secondary windings are:

$$e_p(t) = -N_p\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\omega N_p\Phi_m\cos\omega t, \qquad e_s(t) = -N_s\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\omega N_s\Phi_m\cos\omega t$$

Both induced EMFs lag the core flux $\Phi$ by $90^\circ$ and have **RMS values** (the universal **Transformer EMF Equation**):

$$\boxed{E_{\text{rms}} = \frac{2\pi f N \Phi_m}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}\pi f N \Phi_m \approx 4.44\,f N \Phi_m}$$

For an ideal lossless transformer ($V_p = E_p$, $V_s = E_s$), defining the **voltage transformation ratio** $k \equiv N_s / N_p$:
- **Voltage Turns Ratio:** $\boxed{\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{E_s}{E_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} = k}$ ($k > 1$: step-up; $k < 1$: step-down).
- **Current Ratio (Power Conservation & Constant-Flux MMF Balance):** Equating input and output power $V_p I_p = V_s I_s$ (equivalently demanding net magnetomotive force $N_p I_p - N_s I_s = 0$ to keep core flux constant):
  $$\boxed{\frac{I_p}{I_s} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} = k}$$

---

### 4.2 Impedance Transformation and Maximum Power Transfer Matching

Connect a load impedance $Z_L$ across the secondary terminals so $Z_L = \frac{V_s}{I_s}$. The effective input impedance $Z_{\text{in}}$ presented to the primary AC source is:

$$Z_{\text{in}} = \frac{V_p}{I_p} = \frac{V_s / k}{k I_s} = \frac{1}{k^2}\frac{V_s}{I_s} \implies \boxed{Z_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{N_p}{N_s}\right)^2 Z_L}$$

**Maximum Power Transfer (Impedance Matching):** By the maximum power transfer theorem, a source of internal resistance $R_g$ delivers peak power to a resistive load $R_L$ only when $Z_{\text{in}} = R_g$. Choosing the transformer turns ratio:

$$\boxed{\frac{N_p}{N_s} = \sqrt{\frac{R_g}{R_L}}}$$

matches a high-impedance amplifier or transmission line cleanly to a low-impedance load (such as an $8\,\Omega$ loudspeaker or antenna feed).

---

### 4.3 Practical Loaded Transformer - Constant-Flux Physics and Phasor Diagram

In a **real transformer**, winding resistances ($R_p, R_s$), leakage reactances ($X_p, X_s$), finite core permeability, and core iron losses modify the ideal picture:

1. **No-Load State ($I_s = 0$):** Even with the secondary open, the primary draws a small **no-load current** $\mathbf{I}_0 = \mathbf{I}_m + \mathbf{I}_c$:
   - **Magnetizing component $\mathbf{I}_m$** (in phase with core flux $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$, lagging $\mathbf{V}_p$ by $90^\circ$): wattless current establishing $\Phi_m$.
   - **Core-loss component $\mathbf{I}_c$** (in phase with $\mathbf{V}_p$, leading $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ by $90^\circ$): active current supplying hysteresis and eddy-current losses ($P_{\text{iron}} = V_p I_c = V_p I_0\cos\phi_0$).
2. **Loaded Secondary (Lagging Inductive Load $\phi_2$):** When load current $\mathbf{I}_s$ flows, its demagnetizing MMF $N_s\mathbf{I}_s$ momentarily tries to weaken $\Phi$. Because $V_p \approx E_p = 4.44 f N_p\Phi_m$ holds $\Phi_m$ essentially **constant from no-load to full-load**, the primary instantly draws a counter-balancing load current $\mathbf{I}'_p = -\frac{N_s}{N_p}\mathbf{I}_s$ canceling the secondary MMF exactly! Total primary current is the phasor sum $\mathbf{I}_p = \mathbf{I}_0 + \mathbf{I}'_p$.
3. **Complete Terminal Phasor Equations:**
   $$\boxed{\mathbf{V}_p = -\mathbf{E}_p + \mathbf{I}_p(R_p + jX_p), \qquad \mathbf{V}_s = \mathbf{E}_s - \mathbf{I}_s(R_s + jX_s)}$$

```
                     ^ V_p
                     |  \   I_p R_p + j I_p X_p
                  -E_p   *
                     |  / \
                 I_c ^ /   I_p (lags V_p by phi_1)
                     |/ I'_p = -(N_s/N_p) I_s
                     |  I_0
     ----------------+------------------> Phi (Reference Core Flux) & I_m
                     |\
                     | \ I_s (lags V_s by phi_2)
                     |  \
                    V_s  *
                     |  /  I_s R_s + j I_s X_s
                     v E_s (lags Phi by 90 deg)
```

### 4.4 Energy Losses, Core Engineering, and Condition for Maximum Efficiency

Practical power transformers achieve efficiencies of $95\%\text{–}99\%$, yet five distinct physical loss mechanisms must be engineered down systematically:

| Loss Mechanism | Physical Origin & Scaling Law | Load Dependence | Engineering Minimization |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Copper ($I^2 R$) Loss** | Joule heating in primary & secondary wire resistances: $P_{\text{Cu}} = I_p^2 R_p + I_s^2 R_s$ | **Variable** ($\propto I^2$) | Use thick, high-conductivity annealed copper windings (heavier gauge on low-voltage/high-current side) |
| **2. Hysteresis Loss** | Irreversible Barkhausen domain-wall pinning per cycle: $P_h = f\,\mathcal{V}\oint H\,dB \propto f B_m^{1.6}$ | **Constant** (fixed by $V_p, f$) | Use narrow-loop soft ferromagnetic **CRGO silicon steel** ($3\text{–}4\%$ Si) or soft iron core |
| **3. Eddy-Current Loss** | Alternating core flux induces circulating Joule currents inside iron: $P_e \propto \frac{f^2 B_m^2 t^2}{\rho_{\text{core}}}$ | **Constant** (fixed by $V_p, f$) | Stack thin **insulated laminations** ($t \approx 0.35\text{ mm}$) parallel to $\mathbf{B}$ + silicon doping to raise $\rho_{\text{core}}$ |
| **4. Flux Leakage** | Flux closing through air instead of linking both coils; produces leakage reactances $X_p, X_s$ (voltage drop) | Variable reactance drop | Use closed shell-type core with interleaved **concentric / sandwich windings** |
| **5. Magnetostriction (Hum)** | Periodic elastic strain of ferromagnetic domains at $2f$ radiating acoustic vibration | Constant (tiny) | Rigidly clamp core laminations and vacuum-impregnate with varnish |

#### Efficiency and Proof of Maximum Efficiency Condition
Grouping constant core losses as $P_{\text{iron}} = P_h + P_e$ and referring total copper resistance to the secondary as $R_{\text{eq},s} = R_s + k^2 R_p$ (so $P_{\text{Cu}} = I_s^2 R_{\text{eq},s}$):

$$\eta = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} = \frac{V_s I_s\cos\phi_2}{V_s I_s\cos\phi_2 + P_{\text{iron}} + I_s^2 R_{\text{eq},s}} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{V_s\cos\phi_2}\left(\frac{P_{\text{iron}}}{I_s} + I_s R_{\text{eq},s}\right)}$$

Holding $V_s$ and load power factor $\cos\phi_2$ constant, $\eta$ is maximized when the denominator term $g(I_s) = \frac{P_{\text{iron}}}{I_s} + I_s R_{\text{eq},s}$ is minimized:

$$\frac{dg}{dI_s} = -\frac{P_{\text{iron}}}{I_s^2} + R_{\text{eq},s} = 0 \implies \boxed{I_s^2 R_{\text{eq},s} = P_{\text{iron}} \iff P_{\text{Cu}} = P_{\text{iron}}}$$

*(Second-derivative check: $\frac{d^2 g}{dI_s^2} = +\frac{2P_{\text{iron}}}{I_s^3} > 0$, confirming a strict minimum of losses / maximum of $\eta$!)* If full-load copper loss is $P_{\text{Cu,FL}}$, maximum efficiency occurs at fractional load $x = \frac{I_s}{I_{\text{FL}}} = \sqrt{\frac{P_{\text{iron}}}{P_{\text{Cu,FL}}}}$.

> [!example]- Worked Archetype: Impedance Matching & Transformer Maximum-Efficiency Load
> **Problem:**
> *(a)* An audio amplifier with open-circuit output voltage $V_g = 36\text{ V}_{\text{rms}}$ and internal resistance $R_g = 1.80\text{ k}\Omega$ drives an $R_L = 8.0\,\Omega$ loudspeaker through an ideal matching transformer. Determine (i) the required turns ratio $N_p : N_s$ for maximum power transfer, (ii) the primary and secondary currents, and (iii) the maximum power delivered to the speaker (comparing with direct connection without a transformer).
> *(b)* A $50\text{ kVA}$, $2200\text{ V} / 220\text{ V}$, $50\text{ Hz}$ step-down transformer has iron loss $P_{\text{iron}} = 450\text{ W}$ and full-load copper loss $P_{\text{Cu,FL}} = 800\text{ W}$. Calculate (i) its full-load efficiency at $0.80$ lagging power factor, (ii) the kVA load at which efficiency is maximum, and (iii) the maximum efficiency at $0.80$ power factor.
>
> **Solution:**
> **Part (a):**
> 1. **Required Turns Ratio for Impedance Matching ($Z_{\text{in}} = R_g$):**
>    $$\frac{N_p}{N_s} = \sqrt{\frac{R_g}{R_L}} = \sqrt{\frac{1800\,\Omega}{8.0\,\Omega}} = \sqrt{225} = \boxed{15 : 1}$$
> 2. **Primary & Secondary Currents:** With matched $Z_{\text{in}} = 1800\,\Omega$, total primary loop resistance is $R_g + Z_{\text{in}} = 3600\,\Omega$:
>    $$I_p = \frac{36\text{ V}}{3600\,\Omega} = \boxed{10.0\text{ mA}}, \qquad V_p = I_p Z_{\text{in}} = 18.0\text{ V}$$
>    $$I_s = \left(\frac{N_p}{N_s}\right)I_p = 15(10.0\text{ mA}) = \boxed{150\text{ mA}}, \qquad V_s = \frac{V_p}{15} = 1.20\text{ V}$$
> 3. **Power Delivered to Speaker:**
>    $$P_L = V_s I_s = (1.20\text{ V})(0.150\text{ A}) = \boxed{0.180\text{ W}}$$
>    *(Comparison without transformer: $P_{\text{direct}} = \left(\frac{36}{1808}\right)^2(8.0) = 3.17\text{ mW}$ — impedance matching increases delivered audio power by a factor of $57\times$!)*
>
> **Part (b):**
> 1. **Full-Load Efficiency ($\cos\phi_2 = 0.80$):**
>    $$P_{\text{out,FL}} = (50 \times 10^3\text{ VA})(0.80) = 40\,000\text{ W}$$
>    $$\eta_{\text{FL}} = \frac{40\,000}{40\,000 + 450 + 800} = \frac{40\,000}{41\,250} = \frac{32}{33} = \boxed{96.97\%}$$
> 2. **Load for Maximum Efficiency ($x^2 P_{\text{Cu,FL}} = P_{\text{iron}}$):**
>    $$x = \sqrt{\frac{P_{\text{iron}}}{P_{\text{Cu,FL}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{450}{800}} = \sqrt{\frac{9}{16}} = 0.750 \implies S_{\text{opt}} = (0.750)(50\text{ kVA}) = \boxed{37.5\text{ kVA}}$$
> 3. **Maximum Efficiency at $\cos\phi_2 = 0.80$:** At $x = 0.750$, $P_{\text{out}} = (0.750)(40\,000) = 30\,000\text{ W}$ and $P_{\text{Cu}} = P_{\text{iron}} = 450\text{ W}$ (total loss $= 900\text{ W}$):
>    $$\eta_{\max} = \frac{30\,000}{30\,000 + 900} = \frac{100}{103} = \boxed{97.09\%}$$

> [!warning] Derivation, Sign & Unit-Conversion Traps
> 1. **The Factor-of-4 Frequency Trap ($\omega_d$ vs. $\omega_r$):** Never confuse the transient **underdamped $LCR$ oscillation frequency** $\omega_d = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{4L^2}}$ with the AC **practical parallel tank resonant frequency** $\omega_r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{R^2}{L^2}}$! The transient damping formula carries $4L^2$ in the denominator; the parallel tank formula carries $L^2$!
> 2. **Peak vs. RMS Factor-of-2 in AC Power:** Mains voltages (e.g., "$200\text{ V}$, $60\text{ Hz}$ line") are ALWAYS **RMS values** unless explicitly stated as peak/amplitude $V_0$. Use $P = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi$ for RMS inputs and $P = \frac{1}{2}V_0 I_0\cos\phi$ for peak inputs—inserting an extra $1/2$ with RMS values halves your numerical answer!
> 3. **Inner vs. Outer Cross-Section in Coaxial Solenoid $M$:** When a smaller coil (radius $r_2$) sits inside a larger solenoid (radius $r_1 > r_2$), the magnetic flux linking the inner coil uses the **smaller area** $\pi r_2^2$ (CSE 2011 explicitly gives both diameters $3.2\text{ cm}$ and $2.1\text{ cm}$ to bait careless candidates!).
> 4. **Angular vs. Linear Bandwidth ($\Delta\omega$ vs. $\Delta f$):** $\Delta\omega = \frac{R}{L}$ is in $\text{rad}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$; when a question asks for resonance width $\Delta f$ in $\text{Hz}$ (CSE 2002!), you MUST divide by $2\pi$: $\Delta f = \frac{R}{2\pi L} = \frac{f_0}{Q}$.
> 5. **Forgetting the Square in Transformer Reflected Impedance:** Secondary load $Z_L$ reflects into the primary as $Z_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{N_p}{N_s}\right)^2 Z_L$—NOT $\frac{N_p}{N_s} Z_L$! Notice both $V$ scales by $1/k$ AND $I$ scales by $k$, multiplying to $1/k^2$.
> 6. **Ballistic Galvanometer Half-Cycle vs. Full-Cycle Decrement:** Clearly state whether $\lambda = \ln(\theta_1/\theta_2) = bT/2$ (half-cycle, giving $1 + \lambda/2$) or $\Lambda = \ln(\theta_1/\theta_3) = bT$ (full-cycle, giving $1 + \Lambda/4$). Both come directly from the quarter-period factor $e^{+bT/4}$.

### 2. Examiner Step-Marking & Numerical Trap Signature

- **Confusing Circuit-Level KVL with Field-Level $\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$ (CSE 2025 Trap):** When deriving KVL from energy conservation, explicitly clarify how non-conservative inductive fields ($\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$) are localized inside inductors and absorbed into lumped terminal drops $L\frac{dI}{dt}$; omitting this loses conceptual completeness marks.
- **Skipping Initial-Condition Proofs in Critical Damping (CSE 2014 Trap):** Simply quoting $I = (A + Bt)e^{-bt}$ without proving why the repeated root forces a $t e^{-bt}$ second solution (e.g., via $\lim_{\omega_d \to 0}\frac{\sin\omega_d t}{\omega_d} = t$) and explicitly matching $I(0) = I_0, \dot{I}(0) = 0$ loses 4–5 derivation marks.
- **Isolating Power in a Lossy Real Inductor (CSE 2025 Trap):** In multi-component AC problems containing a real coil ($R_2, X_L$) plus an external resistor ($R_1$), candidates mistakenly compute $I^2|Z_{\text{coil}}|$ instead of $I^2 R_2$ for power dissipated in the coil—remembering that reactive $X_L$ dissipates **zero** average power is mandatory!
- **Forgetting $N$ vs. $n$ (Total Turns vs. Turns per Unit Length) in Solenoid Mutual Induction:** Converting $220\text{ turns/cm} \to 2.20 \times 10^4\text{ turns/m}$ and distinguishing per-turn flux $\Phi_1$ from total flux linkage $N_1\Phi_1$ (CSE 2011 & 2018) accounts for the single highest rate of order-of-magnitude numerical slips in Paper I.
