# The Master Cheat Sheet: 23 MSP Crops

*Here is your master cheat sheet for the 23 crops backed by the Indian government's Minimum Support Price (MSP) (22 mandated MSP crops + Sugarcane under Fair and Remunerative Price / FRP). It breaks down the 19 food crops and 4 commercial crops, complete with their local Telugu context and why they matter.*

## 1. Cereals (The Energy Providers — 7 Crops)

These are the edible grasses that provide the bulk of our daily carbohydrates.

### The Major Cereals
*(High water needs, easy to process, everyday dietary staples)*
* **Paddy / Rice (Vari / Biyyam — వరి / బియ్యం):** The undisputed, water-guzzling king of the monsoon (Kharif) season. Naturally gluten-free, it acts as the daily canvas for curries across southern and eastern India.
* **Wheat (Godhumalu — గోధుమలు):** The winter (Rabi) powerhouse of the North. Thanks to gluten, its dough is stretchy and perfect for rolling out soft chapatis.
* **Maize / Corn (Mokka Jonna — మొక్కజొన్న / మక్కలు):** Highly versatile and naturally gluten-free; eaten directly but largely grown for poultry feed and industrial starch.

### The Millets / Nutri-Cereals
*(Drought-resistant, zero gluten, high fiber/minerals)*
* **Jowar (Jonnalu — జొన్నలు):** A large-seeded millet packed with iron. Because it has no gluten, the dough is patted by hand to make dense, thick flatbreads (Jonna Rotte — జొన్న రొట్టె).
* **Bajra (Sajjalu — సజ్జలు):** The ultimate desert survivor crop. It generates warmth in the body, making it a classic winter staple eaten with ghee (నెయ్యి) and jaggery (బెల్లం).
* **Ragi (Ragulu — రాగులు / చోళ్ళు):** A tiny, reddish millet with exceptionally high calcium. It is boiled into dense balls (Ragi Sangati — రాగి సంగటి) or cooled into a summer porridge (Ambali — అంబలి).

### The Cold-Weather Cereal
* **Barley (Barli Ginjalu / Yavalu — బార్లీ గింజలు / యవలు):** A hardy, gluten-containing winter grain primarily used today for brewing malt (beer and health drinks) or boiled down into cooling barley water (బార్లీ నీళ్ళు).

## 2. Pulses (The Protein Builders — 5 Crops)

These are the dried seeds of legume plants. They are the primary source of vegetarian protein and the foundation of Indian comfort food.

* **Red Gram (Kandi Pappu — కందిపప్పు):** The everyday staple. If you are eating a standard bowl of sambar or yellow tomato dal (టమాటా పప్పు), you are eating red gram.
* **Black Gram (Minapa Pappu — మినపప్పు):** The fermentation engine. It traps air to make batters rise, serving as the core science behind fluffy idlis, crispy dosas, and rich, thick Minapa Rotti (Dibba Rotti — దిబ్బ రొట్టె).
* **Green Gram (Pesara Pappu — పెసరపప్పు):** A light, easily digestible pulse. Eaten sprouted (పెసలు), or ground into batters with ginger to make Pesarattu (పెసరట్టు).
* **Bengal Gram (Senagalu — శనగలు / శనగపప్పు):** The snack king. Whole, they make guggillu (గుగ్గిళ్ళు); ground into powder (besan / శనగపిండి), they form the batter for crispy bajjis and pakodas.
* **Lentil (Masoor Pappu — మసూర్ పప్పు / ఎర్ర కందిపప్పు):** Fast-cooking, tiny pink/orange lentils that break down easily into thick stews, predominantly used in North India.

---

## 3. Oilseeds (The Essential Fats — 7 Crops)

Grown primarily to be crushed into cooking oils, but deeply woven into recipes as flavor enhancers and gravy thickeners.

* **Groundnut / Peanut (Palleelu / Verusenaga — పల్లీలు / వేరుశనగ):** The breakfast champion. Besides peanut oil, it is the roasted, spicy backbone of South Indian chutneys and tamarind rice (పులిహోర) crunch.
* **Mustard (Avalu — ఆవాలు):** While North India crushes it for pungent cooking oil, South India uses the whole seeds exclusively for talimpu (తాలింపు / పోపు — tempering) to finish off curries and dals.
* **Sesame (Nuvvulu — నువ్వులు):** Yields rich gingelly oil (నువ్వుల నూనె / పప్పు నూనె) for pickles. The roasted seeds are ground to create thick, rich gravies for dishes like stuffed eggplant (Gutti Vankaya — గుత్తి వంకాయ).
* **Soybean (Soyabean — సోయాబీన్):** Crushed for common refined cooking oil, with the high-protein leftover pressed into "Meal Maker" (మీల్ మేకర్ — soya chunks) for biryanis.
* **Sunflower (Poddu Tirugudu — పొద్దుతిరుగుడు గింజలు):** Grown almost entirely on a massive scale to stock supermarket shelves with refined, neutral-tasting cooking oil.
* **Safflower (Kusumalu — కుసుమలు):** A hardy, drought-tolerant oilseed grown alongside sunflower on a commercial scale to stock shelves with refined, neutral-tasting cooking oil.
* **Niger Seed (Valisalu — వలిసెలు):** A resilient tribal crop often dry-roasted and pounded with spices into pungent powders (podis — కారప్పొడులు) to eat with rice.

## 4. Commercial / Cash Crops (The Economy Drivers — 4 Crops)

These complete the list of 23. They aren't meant to fill a plate; they are grown for factories to keep industries moving.

* **Cotton (Patti — పత్తి):** Spun into yarn (నూలు దారం) for the textile industry.
* **Raw Jute (Janapanara — జనపనార):** The golden fiber woven into heavy-duty gunny sacks (గోనె సంచులు — which the government ironically uses to store all the cereals and pulses).
* **Copra (Endu Kobbari — ఎండు కొబ్బరి):** The fully dried meat of the coconut, crushed exclusively to extract pure coconut oil (కొబ్బరి నూనె) for cooking and cosmetics.
* **Sugarcane (Cheruku — చెరుకు):** Crushed to produce sugar (పంచదార / చక్కెర) and jaggery (bellam — బెల్లం), driving one of the largest agricultural industries in the country *(governed via Fair and Remunerative Price / FRP)*.

> **Cheat Sheet Mythbuster**
> **Sabudana (Saggubiyyam — సగ్గుబియ్యం)** is completely absent from this list. It is not a grain, millet, or seed—it is simply extracted starch from the tapioca root (కర్రపెండలం దుంప), rolled into balls for quick energy.

## 5. Seasonal Split & How MSP Is Calculated (CACP Framework)

### Kharif vs. Rabi Breakdown & The A2 + FL Cost Formula
* **14 Kharif Crops (Monsoon — Sown Jun–Jul, Harvested Oct–Nov):** Paddy, Jowar, Bajra, Ragi, Maize, Red Gram (Tur), Green Gram (Moong), Black Gram (Urad), Groundnut, Sunflower, Soybean, Sesame, Niger Seed, and Cotton.
* **6 Rabi Crops (Winter — Sown Oct–Nov, Harvested Mar–Apr):** Wheat, Barley, Bengal Gram (Chana), Lentil (Masoor), Mustard & Rapeseed, and Safflower.
* **3 Annual / Multi-Seasonal Commercial Crops:** Sugarcane (FRP), Copra, and Raw Jute.
* **Implicit "+2" Derived MSP Crops:** The government also fixes MSP for **Toria** (derived from Rapeseed/Mustard) and **De-husked Coconut** (derived from Copra).

> **Who Calculates & Approves MSP?** The **CACP** *(Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices)* recommends MSPs at a minimum of **1.5 times the A2 + FL cost** (where **A2** = actual paid-out expenses on seeds, fertilizers, fuel, and hired labor, and **FL** = imputed value of unpaid Family Labor). The **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)** makes the final binding announcement.
