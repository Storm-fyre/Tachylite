### Part 1: The Fragmentation of the Mughal Imperium, 18th-Century Regional Polities, and European Mercantilist Penetration (1707–1765)
- **Decline of centralized post-Aurangzeb Mughal authority:** wars of succession, Irani/Turani/Afghani/Hindustani factionalism (the Sayyid Brothers), Nadir Shah’s 1739 invasion (Battle of Karnal), Ahmad Shah Abdali’s raids, and the 18th-century historiographical debate
- **Emergence of three tiers of regional states:** Successor States (Bengal under Murshid Quli Khan, Shuja-ud-din, and Alivardi Khan with the Jagat Seth banking house; Awadh under Saadat Khan and Safdar Jung; Hyderabad under Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah), Warrior/Rebel States (Maratha Confederacy, Sikhs under Banda Bahadur and the 12 Misls, Jats under Suraj Mal of Bharatpur, Rohilla and Bangash Pathans), and Modernizing Southern Polities (Mysore under Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan: state commercial monopolies, French-trained infantry, Mysorean iron-cased rocket artillery; Travancore under Marthanda Varma: defeating the Dutch VOC at the Battle of Colachel, 1741)
- **European mercantilist trading companies:** Portuguese Estado da Índia (Cartaz-Armada-Cafila system), Dutch VOC, Danish, French Compagnie des Indes Orientales (Dupleix), and the English East India Company (1600 Charter, Thomas Roe, 1717 Farrukhsiyar Farman and Dastak abuse)
- The Three Carnatic Wars (1746–1763: Battle of St. Thome, Siege of Arcot, Battle of Wandiwash 1760)
- The Battles of Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764: Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula, Shah Alam II), and Clive’s Treaty of Allahabad (1765) securing the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa
- **Embedded European enclave architecture:** Portuguese Iberian Baroque and Manueline architecture in Goa and Diu (Basilica of Bom Jesus, Se Cathedral, patio-houses, 1556 printing press), French Cartesian gridiron town planning in Pondicherry, Dutch/Danish coastal factories, and British Factory-Forts (Fort St. George in Madras, Fort William in Calcutta).

### Part 2: The Architecture of Company Statehood: Diwani Extraction, Statutory Charter Acts, Military-Fiscal Conquests, and Company Aesthetics (1765–1849)
- Clive’s Dual Government in Bengal (1765–1772), cessation of bullion imports ("Plassey Plunder"), the Great Bengal Famine of 1770, and Warren Hastings’ Izaredari revenue farming
- **British parliamentary intervention:** Regulating Act of 1773 (Governor-General of Bengal in Council, Calcutta Supreme Court 1774 under Elijah Impey), Pitt’s India Act of 1784 (Board of Control vs. Court of Directors), Charter Act of 1793, Charter Act of 1813 (ending trade monopoly except tea and China trade, Christian missionary entry, Rs 1 lakh education grant), Charter Act of 1833 (ending all commercial activities, Governor-General of India, centralized legislation, First Law Commission under Macaulay), and Charter Act of 1853 (open competition for Covenanted Civil Services)
- **Instruments of imperial territorial expansion (Ring-Fence Policy and Lord Wellesley’s Subsidiary Alliance system):** Four Anglo-Mysore Wars (1767–1799: Treaties of Madras, Mangalore, Seringapatam), Three Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818: Treaties of Salbai, Bassein, Poona/Mandsaur), Conquest of Sindh (1843), Two Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845–1849: Treaty of Amritsar 1809, dismantling Ranjit Singh’s empire and Fauj-i-Ain army), and Imperial Buffer Wars (Anglo-Nepalese War and Treaty of Sagauli 1816, First and Second Anglo-Burmese Wars and Treaty of Yandabo 1826, First Anglo-Afghan War 1839–42, and Masterly Inactivity vs. Forward Policy doctrines)
- Institutionalization of the Cornwallis Code (1793), Covenanted Civil Services (Fort William and Haileybury Colleges), Daroga police system, and paternalist administrators (Munro, Elphinstone, Metcalfe, Malcolm)
- **Embedded early colonial aesthetics:** Neo-Classical and Palladian architecture in Calcutta and Madras (Wellesley’s Government House, Calcutta Town Hall), European Picturesque landscape painting (Thomas and William Daniell, Hodges, Zoffany), and the Company School of Painting (Patna Kalam, Murshidabad, Thanjavur, Delhi).

### Part 3: Colonial Agrarian Settlements, Commercialization, De-industrialization, Monetary Manipulation, and the Drain of Wealth (1793–1914)
- R.P. Dutt’s Three Stages of Colonialism (Mercantile Monopoly Plunder, Industrial Free-Trade Capitalism, Finance Imperialism)
- **The three colonial land revenue extraction systems:** Permanent Settlement (Zamindari, 1793) under Cornwallis and John Shore in Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Northern Circars, and Banaras (Sunset Law, Patni sub-infeudation, absentee landlordism), Ryotwari Settlement (1820) under Alexander Read, Thomas Munro, and Mountstuart Elphinstone in Madras, Bombay, Berar, and Assam (Ricardian rent theory, direct peasant taxation, periodic upward revisions), and Mahalwari Settlement (1822–1833) under Holt Mackenzie, R.M. Bird, and James Thomason in North-Western Provinces, Central Provinces, and Punjab (village Mahal assessment under Lambardars)
- Rural indebtedness, forced commercialization of cash crops (indigo Nij/Ryoti/Tinkathia, Bihar/Banaras opium monopoly for China export, cotton, jute), and the Assam Tea Plantation and overseas Girmitiya indentured labor regime (Workmen’s Breach of Contract Act 1859, Inland Emigration Acts)
- **De-industrialization mechanics:** destruction of urban handloom textile centers through one-way tariff discrimination, ruin of indigenous iron/saltpetre crafts, ruralization of labor, and the Railway Old and New Guarantee Systems (1849 onwards: 5% guaranteed return on British private capital paid from Indian taxes)
- **Economic nationalism and the "Drain of Wealth":** Dadabhai Naoroji (Poverty and Un-British Rule in India), R.C. Dutt (Economic History of India), M.G. Ranade, and G.V. Joshi on the anatomy of Home Charges
- **Colonial currency and exchange rate manipulation:** Herschell (1893), Fowler (1898), Babington Smith (1919), and Hilton Young Commission (1926, informed by B.R. Ambedkar’s The Problem of the Rupee), the 1s 6d vs. 1s 4d Rupee-Sterling ratio controversy, and the Acworth Committee (1921).

### Part 5: Late-Company Annexations, the 1857 Watershed, Social Coalitions, and the Imperial Reorganization (1848–1858)
- **Dalhousie’s annexationist offensive (1848–1856):** the Doctrine of Lapse (Satara, Jaitpur, Sambalpur, Baghat, Udaipur, Jhansi, Nagpur), abolition of titles/pensions, the 1856 annexation of Awadh on grounds of "misgovernance", the 1856 Summary Settlement dispossessing Taluqdars, and the Bombay Inam Commission
- **Systemic and military triggers:** evangelical anxieties (Lex Loci Act 1850) and disaffection of the Bengal Army Sepoy ("peasant in uniform" from Awadh: loss of Bhatta, Canning’s General Service Enlistment Act 1856, racial discrimination, and the Pattern 1853 Enfield Rifle greased cartridges)
- **Spatial spread, military tactics, and leadership of the 1857 revolt:** Meerut outbreak, Delhi (Bahadur Shah Zafar and General Bakht Khan’s Court of Administration / Jalsa-i-Intizamiya), Kanpur (Nana Saheb, Tantia Tope, Azimullah Khan), Lucknow/Awadh (Begum Hazrat Mahal, Birjis Qadr, Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah of Faizabad), Jhansi and Gwalior (Rani Lakshmibai, Tantia Tope), Bihar/Arrah (Kunwar Singh, Amar Singh), Bareilly (Khan Bahadur Khan), Baghpat (Shah Mal’s Chaurasee Des Jat mobilization), and Singhbhum (Gonoo’s Kol mobilization)
- Social coalitions, the Azamgarh Proclamation (August 1857), historiographical debates (Savarkar, S.N. Sen, R.C. Majumdar, Eric Stokes, Rudrangshu Mukherjee), and structural causes of defeat (princely loyalty as "breakwaters in the storm", middle-class aloofness, British telegraph/rail/command superiority)
- **The 1858 imperial reorganization:** Government of India Act 1858 (liquidation of the EIC, Secretary of State for India and 15-member Council of India, Viceroy title), Queen Victoria’s Proclamation (Nov 1, 1858: halting annexations, conservative alliance with princes and Taluqdars), and the Peel Commission (1859: raising European troop ratios, European artillery monopoly, and restructuring the Indian Army along "Martial Races" ideology).

### Part 6: The Post-1857 Colonial Coercive Grid, Forest Expropriation, Imperial Urbanism, and the Political Economy of Victorian Famines (1858–1905)
- **Codification of colonial statutory control:** Indian Penal Code (1860), Codes of Civil and Criminal Procedure (1859, 1861/1872), Indian High Courts Act (1861), Police Act of 1861, Frazer Police Commission (1902), and the Criminal Tribes Act (1871)
- **Expropriation of the ecological commons:** Imperial Forest Department (1864) and the Indian Forest Acts of 1865 and 1878 (Reserved, Protected, and Village forests monopolizing timber for railways and criminalizing Adivasi Jhum and grazing)
- **Constitutional enactments and reactionary imperialism:** Indian Councils Acts of 1861 (portfolio system, ordinances) and 1892 (indirect election, budget discussion), Mayo’s Resolution (1870) and Ripon’s Resolution on Local Self-Government (1882), Lytton’s reactionary policies (Royal Titles Act 1876, 1877 Delhi Durbar, Vernacular Press Act 1878, Indian Arms Act 1878, ICS age reduction to 19, Second Anglo-Afghan War), and the Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883 Anglo-Indian "White Mutiny")
- Civil Service Commissions (Aitchison 1886, Islington 1912, Lee 1924) and the Indian Factory Acts of 1881 and 1891 alongside early labor activism (Sasipada Banerji’s Bharat Shramjeevi and N.M. Lokhande’s Deenbandhu and Bombay Millhands Association 1890)
- **Political economy of Victorian famines:** Orissa famine (1866), Great Famine of 1876–78, and famines of 1896–1900 (Chhappania Akal)
- **Entitlement failure mechanics:** Laissez-Faire orthodoxy (Lytton and Richard Temple’s Anti-Charitable Contributions Act 1877 and starvation Temple Wages), grain exports via railroads during peak starvation, and the Campbell (1866), Strachey (1880), Lyall (1898), and MacDonnell (1901) Famine Commissions and Famine Codes alongside the Scott-Moncrieff Irrigation Commission (1901)
- **Embedded post-1857 colonial urbanism and architecture:** racialized spatial morphology (Red Fort/Old Delhi demolition, segregated Cantonments and Civil Lines, Hill Stations like Shimla), Victorian Neo-Gothic architecture in Bombay (Victoria Terminus by F.W. Stevens, High Court, Rajabai Clock Tower), the Indo-Saracenic architectural synthesis (Chisholm, Mant, Irwin, Swinton Jacob, Wittet, Emerson: Madras High Court, Chepauk Palace, Lakshmi Vilas Palace Baroda, Mysore Palace, Mayo College, Gateway of India, Victoria Memorial Calcutta), urban Parsi Theatre, and the Dramatic Performances Act (1876).

- First Round Table Conference (1930–31), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 5, 1931), 1931 Karachi Congress Session (Sardar Patel: Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme), Second Round Table Conference (1931), Third Round Table Conference (1932), and 1934 withdrawal.

### Part 13: Agrarian Radicalism (Kisan Sabhas), Working-Class Trade Unions, the Communist/Socialist Left, Princely Praja Mandals, and Socialist Realism (1920s–1940s)
- **Agrarian radicalization and the Kisan Sabha movement:** Baba Ramchandra’s Oudh Kisan Sabha (1920), Madari Pasi’s Eka Movement (1921–22), N.G. Ranga’s Andhra Ryots Association, Sardar Patel’s Bardoli Satyagraha (1928), Swami Sahajanand Saraswati’s Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha (1929) and Bihar Bakasht Land Agitation (Karyanand Sharma, Yadunandan Sharma, Rahul Sankrityayan), formation of the All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS, Lucknow April 1936), Godavari Parulekar’s Warli Adivasi Revolt in Maharashtra (1945–47), Punnapra-Vayalar Uprising in Travancore (1946), Tebhaga Movement in Bengal (1946–47: Bargadars demanding two-thirds share), and the Telangana Peasant Armed Struggle in Hyderabad State (1946–1951)
- **Industrial working-class movement and the Left:** B.P. Wadia’s Madras Labour Union (1918), All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC, Oct 1920: Lala Lajpat Rai, Dewan Chaman Lal), Trade Unions Act (1926), Girni Kamgar Union (1928), Trade Disputes Act (1929), and Whitley Commission on Labour (1929)
- The Communist Party of India (CPI: M.N. Roy at Tashkent 1920, S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmad, Singaravelu Chettiar, P.C. Joshi), Workers and Peasants Parties (WPPs), and the Peshawar (1922–27), Kanpur Bolshevik (1924), and Meerut Conspiracy Cases (1929–33)
- The Congress Socialist Party (CSP, 1934: Jayaprakash Narayan, Acharya Narendra Deva, Minoo Masani, Achyut Patwardhan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Yusuf Meherally, Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay)
- **The Princely States Peoples’ Movement (Praja Mandals):** All India States Peoples’ Conference (AISPC, 1927: Balwantrai Mehta, Maniklal Kothari, G.R. Abhyankar), 1938 Haripura and 1939 Tripuri/Ludhiana integration under Nehru, and agitations in Rajkot, Mysore, Hyderabad, Travancore, Odisha Garjat states, and Sheikh Abdullah’s National Conference in Kashmir
- **Embedded Socialist Realism and subaltern cultural mobilization:** Amrita Sher-Gil’s rural canvases, Chittaprosad, Somnath Hore, and Zainul Abedin’s 1943 Bengal Famine and Tebhaga linocuts, the Progressive Writers’ Association (PWA, 1936: Premchand, Sajjad Zaheer, Mulk Raj Anand, Faiz, Chughtai, Manto), and the Indian People’s Theatre Association (IPTA, 1943: Bijon Bhattacharya’s Nabanna, Balraj Sahni, Salil Chowdhury).

### Part 14: Imperial Constitutional Counter-Strategies, Wartime Famine, Quit India, the INA, and the Transfer of Power (1935–1947)
- **The Government of India Act 1935:** proposed All-India Federation and Central Dyarchy, three legislative lists (residuary powers with Viceroy), Provincial Autonomy (April 1937), and Federal Court
- The 1937 provincial elections, 28-month Congress ministries (1937–1939) and the Pirpur Committee Report (1938), 1938 Haripura Session (National Planning Committee), and the 1939 Tripuri Crisis (Subhas Chandra Bose vs. Pattabhi Sitaramayya, Pant Resolution, and formation of the Forward Bloc)
- World War II outbreak, October 1939 resignation of Congress ministries, Muslim League’s Lahore Resolution (March 1940), Linlithgow’s August Offer (1940), Individual Satyagraha (1940–41: Vinoba Bhave, Nehru, Brahma Dutt), and the Cripps Mission (March 1942: "local option" provincial secession clause)
- **The Quit India Movement (August 1942):** Gowalia Tank resolution ("Do or Die"), underground networks (Aruna Asaf Ali, Sucheta Kripalani, Ram Manohar Lohia, Achyut Patwardhan, Jayaprakash Narayan’s Azad Dasta, Usha Mehta’s Congress Radio), and parallel governments (Prati Sarkar in Ballia under Chittu Pandey, Tamluk/Midnapore Jatiya Sarkar and Matangini Hazra, Satara under Nana Patil and Y.B. Chavan, and Talcher)
- The 1943 Great Bengal Famine (wartime Sterling Balance inflation, Boat/Rice Denial Policies, Woodhead Commission)
- **Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj, 1941–45):** Mohan Singh and Rash Behari Bose, Provisional Government of Free India at Singapore (1943), Andaman and Nicobar handover, Rani of Jhansi Regiment (Lakshmi Swaminathan), and the 1944 Imphal-Kohima campaign
- **Post-war negotiations and upsurges:** C. Rajagopalachari Formula (1944), Desai-Liaquat Pact (1945), Wavell Plan and Simla Conference (June 1945), INA Red Fort Trials (Nov 1945: Sahgal, Dhillon, Shah Nawaz Khan), the Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny (Feb 1946: HMIS Talwar strike), the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946: three-tier federation and Section A/B/C grouping), Direct Action Day (Aug 16, 1946), the Interim Government (Sept 1946), Attlee’s Feb 20, 1947 Statement, Plan Balkan / Dickie Bird Plan (May 1947), the Mountbatten Plan (June 3, 1947), and the Indian Independence Act (1947).

### Part 15: The Partition: Boundary Commissions, Demographic Rupture, Refugee Rehabilitation, and Sovereign Asset Bifurcation (1946–1950)
- **Mechanics of partition:** June 1947 Punjab and Bengal Legislative Assembly votes, Sylhet Referendum, and NWFP Referendum (boycotted by Khudai Khidmatgars)
- **The Radcliffe Boundary Commissions:** 5-week cartographic deadline and anomalies (Gurdaspur and Ferozepur corridors, Murshidabad-Khulna Hooghly exchange, Chittagong Hill Tracts Chakma anomaly, and Cooch Behar Chhitmahal enclaves)
- Communal mass violence, 14–18 million refugee migrations (rapid two-way military evacuation in Punjab vs. protracted multi-decade migration in Bengal and the 1950 Nehru-Liaquat Pact), gendered violence, and the Abducted Persons (Recovery and Restoration) Act (1949: Mridula Sarabhai, Rameshwari Nehru, Kamlaben Patel)
- **Rehabilitation of displaced populations:** Administration of Evacuee Property Act (1950), East Punjab standard-acre land allotment and consolidation (laying the infrastructural base for the Green Revolution), planned refugee townships (Faridabad, Nilokheri, Rajpura, Ulhasnagar, Gandhidham), West Bengal Jabardakhal squatter colonies, and the Dandakaranya Project
- **Bifurcation of sovereign assets under the Partition Council and Arbitral Tribunal (Sir Patrick Spens, H.M. Patel, Chaudhri Muhammad Ali):** 82.5% : 17.5% division of national debt, railway rolling stock, civil service personnel, physical archives, Rs 400 crore cash balances (Rs 55 crore tranche dispute), and regimental splitting of the Armed Forces under Auchinleck.

### Part 16: Post-Independence Territorial Consolidation: Princely Integration, Enclave Annexations, and Linguistic Reorganization of States (1947–1987)
- Integration of 560+ princely states under Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and V.P. Menon (Ministry of States, July 1947: Instruments of Accession on Defense, External Affairs, and Communications, and Standstill Agreements)
- Overcoming Travancore, Bhopal, and Jodhpur resistance
- **The hard accession crises:** Junagadh (Arzi Hukumat and Feb 1948 Plebiscite), Hyderabad (Qasim Razvi’s Razakars, Telangana insurgency, and Operation Polo Sept 1948), Jammu & Kashmir (Oct 1947 Pakistani tribal-military invasion, Oct 26, 1947 Instrument of Accession, 1947–48 War, UNSC referral, 1949 Ceasefire Line, Article 370, and 1952 Delhi Agreement), and Manipur (1947 Accession, June 1948 adult-franchise election, and Sept 1949 Merger Agreement)
- Administrative merger into Part B States Unions and Part C Provinces and Privy Purses (Article 291)
- **Enclave integrations and Sikkim:** French settlements (1949 Chandernagore plebiscite; 1954/1962 transfer of Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, and Yanam via 14th Amendment), Portuguese enclaves (1954 Dadra & Nagar Haveli; Operation Vijay Dec 1961 liberating Goa, Daman, and Diu via 12th Amendment and 1967 Goa Opinion Poll), and constitutional integration of Sikkim (35th and 36th Amendments, 1974–75)
- **Linguistic reorganization of states:** Dhar Commission (1948), JVP Committee (1948–49), Potti Sreeramulu’s hunger strike and creation of Andhra State (1953), States Reorganisation Commission (1953–55: Fazl Ali, Panikkar, Kunzru), States Reorganisation Act 1956 (7th Amendment creating 14 States and 6 UTs), Samyukta Maharashtra and Mahagujarat movements (1960), Nagaland (1963), Punjabi Suba and Shah Commission (1966: Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh), North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act (1971), 1987 statehoods (Mizoram, Arunachal, Goa), 2000 states (Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand), and Telangana (2014)
- **Official language politics:** Munshi-Ayyangar formula, Kher Commission (1955), 1965 Tamil Nadu anti-Hindi agitation, and the Official Languages Act (1963/1967).

### Part 17: The Nehruvian Developmental State: Planning Machinery, Heavy Industrialization, Agrarian Reforms, Modernist Urbanism, and Non-Alignment (1947–1964)
- **Democratic institutionalization:** First General Elections (1951–52 under Sukumar Sen) and Rajni Kothari’s "Congress System"
- **Political economy of planning:** 1938 NPC, 1944 Bombay Plan, Industrial Policy Resolutions of 1948 and 1956, Industries (Development and Regulation) Act (1951), Planning Commission (1950), National Development Council (1952), First Five-Year Plan (1951–56: Harrod-Domar model, Bhakra-Nangal, DVC, and Hirakud dams), and Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61: P.C. Mahalanobis heavy-industrialization ISI strategy, Bhilai, Rourkela, and Durgapur steel plants, and PL-480 food imports)
- **Agrarian restructuring:** abolition of intermediaries (Zamindari Abolition Acts, First Constitutional Amendment 1951 and Ninth Schedule), tenancy reforms, land ceiling legislation, Vinoba Bhave’s Bhoodan and Gramdan movements, Community Development Programme (1952), and Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957 3-tier Panchayati Raj)
- **Social legislation:** the Hindu Code Bills controversy (Nehru and Ambedkar vs. Rajendra Prasad: Hindu Marriage Act 1955, Hindu Succession Act 1956, Minority and Guardianship Act 1956, Adoptions and Maintenance Act 1956)
- **Science and higher education infrastructure:** Scientific Policy Resolution (1958), Homi Bhabha (AEC/DAE), Vikram Sarabhai (INCOSPAR), S.S. Bhatnagar (CSIR), UGC (1953/56), and IITs/IIMs/AIIMS
- **Foreign policy foundations:** Asian Relations Conference (1947), Panchsheel (1954), Bandung Conference (1955), Non-Aligned Movement (Belgrade 1961), 1959 Tibetan asylum, and the 1962 Sino-Indian Border War
- **Embedded post-colonial modernist architecture and art:** Le Corbusier’s Chandigarh Capitol Complex, B.V. Doshi, Charles Correa, Laurie Baker, the Progressive Artists’ Group (PAG 1947: Souza, Raza, Husain, Tyeb Mehta, Gaitonde), Cholamandal Artists’ Village, the three National Akademis (Sangeet Natak, Lalit Kala, Sahitya), NSD, and Parallel Cinema (Satyajit Ray, Ritwik Ghatak, Bimal Roy, Guru Dutt).

### Part 18: Democratic Strains, the Green Revolution, the 1971 War, the Emergency, and the Fracturing of the Hegemonic Party System (1964–1984)
- Lal Bahadur Shastri’s transition (1964–66), "Jai Jawan Jai Kisan", the 1965 Indo-Pak War, and the Tashkent Declaration (Jan 1966)
- The Green Revolution (C. Subramaniam, M.S. Swaminathan: HYV wheat/rice seeds, chemical fertilizers, MSP/CACP, FCI 1965, regional prosperity imbalances, and rise of intermediate "Bullock Capitalist" peasant castes under Charan Singh’s BKD/Lok Dal) and Operation Flood (1970 White Revolution under Verghese Kurien, NDDB, and AMUL)
- The 1967 Fourth General Elections (SVD state coalitions), the 1967 Naxalbari peasant uprising (Charu Mazumdar, Kanu Sanyal), the 1969 Congress Split (Indira Gandhi vs. the Syndicate, V.V. Giri’s election), Nationalization of 14 major commercial banks (July 1969), MRTP Act (1969), Abolition of Privy Purses (26th Amendment, 1971), and the 1971 "Garibi Hatao" mandate
- **The 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War:** 10 million refugees, Mukti Bahini, 1971 Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation, December 1971 military victory under Sam Manekshaw (93,000 Pakistani POWs), the Simla Agreement (July 1972: Line of Control), and Pokhran-I (May 1974)
- Economic stagnation, 1973 oil shock, 1974 Railway Strike (George Fernandes), Kesavananda Bharati (1973) and CJI supersession, Gujarat Navnirman Movement, Jayaprakash Narayan’s (JP) Bihar Sampoorna Kranti Movement, and post-independence environmental movements (Chipko Movement 1973 under Chandi Prasad Bhatt, Sunderlal Bahuguna, and Gaura Devi; Silent Valley Movement)
- **The 1975–1977 Internal Emergency:** 1975 Allahabad High Court verdict (Raj Narain), Article 352 proclamation, MISA detentions, press censorship, 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976), ADM Jabalpur Habeas Corpus case (Justice H.R. Khanna’s dissent), and Shah Commission
- The 1977 general election, Morarji Desai’s Janata Party government, 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978), Mandal Commission appointment (1979), Indira Gandhi’s 1980 return, and the Punjab crisis (Anandpur Sahib Resolution, Bhindranwale, Operation Blue Star June 1984, Indira Gandhi’s assassination, and 1984 anti-Sikh pogroms).

### Part 19: Post-1980s Structural Transitions: Regional Accords, Mandal-Mandir-Market Realignments, Coalition Politics, and the Rights-to-Digital State (1984–Present)
- **Rajiv Gandhi’s premiership (1984–1989):** regional peace accords (Punjab Rajiv-Longowal Accord 1985, Assam Accord 1985 and Section 6A, Mizo Peace Accord 1986 with Laldenga), Sam Pitroda’s National Technology Missions (C-DOT telecom), 1986 National Education Policy, 52nd Amendment (Anti-Defection Law 1985), 61st Amendment (voting age to 18, 1988), the Shah Bano case (1985–86) and 1986 Babri Masjid lock-opening, 1987 Indo-Sri Lanka Accord and IPKF, Bofors scandal, and the 1989 outbreak of the Kashmir insurgency and Kashmiri Pandit exodus
- **The 1990–1992 "Mandal, Mandir, and Market" structural realignment:** V.P. Singh’s implementation of the Mandal Commission report (August 1990: 27% OBC reservation, Indra Sawhney 1992 Creamy Layer verdict, and rise of SP, RJD, and Kanshi Ram and Mayawati’s BSP), the Ram Janmabhoomi Rath Yatra and December 6, 1992 Babri Masjid demolition, and the 1991 Balance of Payments crisis and New Economic Policy (P.V. Narasimha Rao and Dr. Manmohan Singh: Rupee devaluation, dismantling of License-Permit Raj via the July 1991 Industrial Policy, Chelliah tax reforms, Narasimham banking reforms)
- **Era of coalition politics and decentralization (1992–2004):** 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992), Narmada Bachao Andolan (Medha Patkar) and MKSS (Aruna Roy) RTI mobilization, United Front governments (Gujral Doctrine), and Atal Bihari Vajpayee’s NDA government (1998–2004: Pokhran-II May 1998, Lahore Bus and 1999 Kargil War, Golden Quadrilateral and PMGSY, FRBM Act 2003, and creation of Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, and Jharkhand in 2000)
- **The Rights-Based Welfare Paradigm vs. the Digital-Centralized State (2004–Present):** UPA statutory entitlements (RTI 2005, MGNREGA 2005, Forest Rights Act 2006, RTE 2009, NFSA 2013, LARR 2013, and 2008 Indo-US Civil Nuclear Deal) transitioning into post-2014 structural transformations (NITI Aayog 2015, Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, GST 101st Amendment 2017, JAM Trinity and Digital Public Infrastructure UPI/DBT, August 2019 Article 370 reorganization of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, 2019 Ayodhya verdict, and 103rd EWS and 106th Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam Constitutional Amendments).
