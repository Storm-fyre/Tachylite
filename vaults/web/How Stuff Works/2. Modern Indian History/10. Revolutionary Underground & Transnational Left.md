## I. The Structural Political Economy of Revolutionary Militancy and Phase I Domestic Insurgency (1897–1915)

### 1. The Material, Fiscal, and Ideological Genesis of Armed Underground Resistance

The emergence of organized revolutionary underground networks across the Bombay Presidency, Bengal, Punjab, and Madras between **1897** and **1915** was not an anomalous eruption of elite youth romanticism, nor was it an isolated deviation from constitutional politics. From a structural political-economy perspective, Phase I revolutionary militancy represented a direct response to the closure of institutional bargaining channels under the high-imperial state, combined with the acute material squeeze experienced by the Western-educated vernacular intelligentsia (*bhadralok* in Bengal, Chitpavan Brahmins and urban middle classes in Maharashtra, and peasant-proprietors in Punjab) and the violent coercion of the colonial police-fiscal apparatus. While the colonial administration extracted escalating agrarian surpluses, manipulated the Rupee-Sterling exchange rate, and suppressed indigenous manufacturing, it simultaneously restricted Indian representation in the Covenanted Civil Services and municipal governance. When the Moderate strategy of "prayer, petition, and protest" failed to prevent reactionary enactments—such as the invasive plague sanitation measures in Pune (**1897**), **Curzon’s** **Indian Universities Act (1904)**, the **Partition of Bengal (1905)**, and the **Punjab Colonisation Bill (1906)**—and when the colonial state met mass **Swadeshi** picketing with lathi charges, deportations under **Regulation III of 1818**, and the banning of public assemblies, open mass politics was structurally choked.

In the absence of an organizational machinery capable of sustaining prolonged, mass-based non-violent civil disobedience, radicalized segments of the petty-bourgeois intelligentsia turned to clandestine armed organization. Ideologically, Phase I revolutionaries synthesized three distinct currents: 
1. **Neo-Vedantic and Shakta-Tantric sacralization of the nation**, derived from **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay’s** *Anandamath* (**1882**), **Swami Vivekananda’s** karma-yoga, and **Bal Gangadhar Tilak’s** activist reinterpretation of the *Bhagavad Gita* (*Gita Rahasya*), which framed anti-colonial violence as a dharmic obligation (*nishkama karma*) to liberate the Motherland (*Bharat Mata*);
2. **European romantic nationalism and insurrectionary carbonari models**, specifically **Giuseppe Mazzini’s** *Young Italy* and **Garibaldi’s** guerrilla campaigns, disseminated through vernacular biographies written by **Lala Lajpat Rai**, **Surendranath Banerjea**, and **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar**; and
3. **Russian Nihilist and Populist (*Narodnik*) chemical warfare tactics**, which relied on the scientific equalizer of nitroglycerine and picric acid bombs (*"the Russian method"*) to shatter the psychological invincibility of a numerically tiny European bureaucracy governing three hundred million subjects.

#### The Coercive Counter-Insurgency Grid and the Cellular Jail Panopticon

Every phase of revolutionary escalation triggered a symmetrical expansion of the colonial state's statutory and carceral apparatus. Because ordinary colonial criminal jurisprudence under the **Indian Penal Code (1860)** and **Code of Criminal Procedure** required open witness testimony—which revolutionary cells systematically neutralized by assassinating police investigators, approvers, and Crown prosecutors—the Viceroy’s Executive Council enacted an interlocking matrix of emergency statutes:

* **Prevention of Seditious Meetings Act (1907 / 1911):** Empowered provincial governments to declare "proclaimed areas" and prohibit any public gathering of more than twenty persons without prior police permission, effectively criminalizing open **Swadeshi** mobilization.
* **Explosive Substances Act (1908):** Enacted immediately after the **Muzaffarpur Bomb Case**, criminalizing the manufacture, possession, or control of any explosive substance under suspicious circumstances, shifting the burden of proof onto the accused and prescribing transportation for life.
* **Newspaper (Incitement to Offences) Act (1908):** Authorized District Magistrates to confiscate the printing presses and property of any newspaper publishing matter judged to incite murder, offences under the Explosive Substances Act, or any act of violence—Directly utilized to shut down the radical vernacular dailies *Sandhya* (edited by **Brahmabandhab Upadhyay**), *Yugantar* (edited by **Bhupendranath Datta** and **Barindra Ghosh**), and *Bande Mataram* (edited by **Aurobindo Ghosh**).
* **Indian Criminal Law Amendment Act (1908):** Part I created a Special Tribunal of three High Court Judges to try revolutionary conspiracy cases without a jury and without committal proceedings, admitting the untested statements of dead or missing witnesses into evidence; Part II empowered the Governor-General in Council to declare any association unlawful (*Samitis*), leading to the immediate banning of the **Dacca Anushilan Samiti**, **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti**, **Brati Samiti**, **Suhrid Samiti**, and **Sadhana Samiti** in January **1909**.
* **Indian Press Act (1910):** Imposed mandatory financial security deposits (ranging from Rs 500 to Rs 5,000) at the registration of printing presses and newspapers, subject to summary executive forfeiture for publishing "seditious" material, crippling underground print capitalism.
* **Spatial Architecture of Carceral Terror—The Cellular Jail (1906):** To physically sever convicted revolutionaries from the mainland body politic and prevent horizontal ideological contamination among prisoners, the colonial state constructed the **Cellular Jail** at Port Blair in the Andaman Islands (completed in **1906**). Designed along **Jeremy Bentham’s** Panopticon surveillance principle, the structure consisted of **seven three-story wings** containing **698 solitary cells** radiating outward like the spokes of a wheel from a central watchtower. Each cell measured $13.5 \times 7$ feet with a high ventilation window and an iron grate facing the corridor; the front of one wing faced the blank back wall of the next, eliminating all visual and auditory communication between inmates. Inside this carceral machine, political prisoners from the **Alipore**, **Nasik**, **Dacca**, **Lahore**, and **Benares** conspiracy cases were subjected to oil-mill (*kolhu*) grinding, coir-pounding, fetters, and solitary confinement, turning the Andamans into both a laboratory of penal torture and an unintended trans-regional university where **Bengali**, **Marathi**, and **Punjabi** revolutionaries forged an all-India revolutionary consciousness.

### 2. The Maharashtrian Vanguard: Chapekar Brothers, Mitra Mela, and Abhinav Bharat (1897–1910)

The earliest organized manifestations of anti-colonial revolutionary violence crystallized in the Bombay Presidency, specifically along the Pune–Nasik axis. In Maharashtra, memory of the **1818** overthrow of the Peshwa was kept alive by the **Chitpavan Brahmin** intelligentsia, while **Vasudev Balwant Phadke’s** **1879** **Ramosi** peasant-dacoity uprising had already demonstrated the possibility of armed insurrection against British rule. During the **1890s**, **Bal Gangadhar Tilak’s** transformation of the public **Ganapati Festival (1893)** and **Shivaji Festival (1895)**, alongside his fiery journalism in the Marathi weekly *Kesari* and the English weekly *Mahratta*, established a militant cultural public sphere that legitimized active resistance to foreign domination.

#### The Chapekar Brothers and the 1897 Pune Plague Assassinations

The structural catalyst for the first political assassination of the modern nationalist era was the intersection of agrarian famine and colonial biopolitical coercion during the **1896–1897 Bubonic Plague epidemic** in Pune. Fearing international trade embargoes against Indian port exports under the Venice Sanitary Convention, the Bombay Presidency appointed **W.C. Rand** (an Indian Civil Service officer) as Chairman of the Special Plague Committee in Pune. Deploying British troops to enforce quarantine, **Rand’s** administration executed house-to-house inspections that stripped women in public streets, desecrated family shrines, burned personal property without compensation, and forcibly dragged suspected patients to segregated plague camps.

* **The Vyayam Mandala:** **Damodar Hari Chapekar**, **Balkrishna Hari Chapekar**, and **Vasudev Hari Chapekar**—three brothers from Chinchwad near Pune—had organized a Hindu physical-culture and martial society (*Chapekar Club* or *Vyayam Mandala*) dedicated to stripping away Western cultural contamination and training youth in swordplay, slingshots, and firearms.
* **The June 22, 1897 Assassination:** On the night of **June 22, 1897**, as the colonial administration celebrated the Diamond Jubilee of Queen Victoria’s coronation at Government House in Ganeshkhind (Pune), **Damodar** and **Balkrishna Chapekar** ambushed departing carriages, shooting **W.C. Rand** and his military escort **Lieutenant Charles Egerton Ayerst** (who died instantly, while **Rand** succumbed to his wounds on July 3).
* **Retribution against Informers and Statutory Backlash:** When the Dravid brothers (**Ganesh Shankar Dravid** and **Ramchandra Shankar Dravid**) betrayed the **Chapekars** for a Rs 20,000 police reward, the youngest brother, **Vasudev Chapekar**, along with **Mahadev Vinayak Ranade** and **Khando Vishnu Sathe**, assassinated both Dravid brothers on February 8, **1899**. All three **Chapekar Brothers** and **Mahadev Ranade** were executed at Yerwada Jail between April **1898** and May **1899**. Using the assassination as a pretext, the colonial state prosecuted **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** under the newly amended **Section 124A of the IPC** (redefining sedition as simple "disaffection" or "absence of affection") for publishing a poem (*Shivaji’s Utterances*) and a defense of Shivaji’s killing of Afzal Khan in *Kesari*, sentencing him to 18 months’ rigorous imprisonment.

#### V.D. Savarkar, Mitra Mela (1899), Abhinav Bharat (1904), and the Nasik Conspiracy Case (1909)

The execution of the **Chapekar Brothers** directly inspired the sixteen-year-old **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar** and his elder brother **Ganesh (Babarao) Damodar Savarkar** to institutionalize the Maharashtrian underground, shifting from localized orthodox vigilantism to a modern, secularized, internationalist revolutionary secret society.

* **Mitra Mela (1899) to Abhinav Bharat (1904):** In **1899**, **V.D. Savarkar** and **Ganesh Savarkar** founded the **Mitra Mela** (Friends’ Union) in Nasik as a clandestine society embedded within public Ganapati and Shivaji celebrations (*melas*). In **1904**, at a secret conclave of 200 delegates in Nasik, **V.D. Savarkar** renamed and reorganized the network into **Abhinav Bharat Society** (Young India Society), explicitly modeled on **Giuseppe Mazzini’s** *Giovine Italia* (Young Italy). **Abhinav Bharat** established branches across Poona (Fergusson and Deccan Colleges), Bombay, Satara, Gwalior, and Baroda, recruiting students, lawyers, and government clerks through secret oaths before the image of Goddess Bhavani and Chhatrapati Shivaji.
* **Print Capitalism and Revolutionary Historiography:** Before departing for London in **1906** on a **Shyamji Krishna Varma** scholarship, **V.D. Savarkar** authored a Marathi translation of **Mazzini’s** autobiography (*Joseph Mazzini: Atmacharitra va Rajkiya Tattvajnana*, **1907**), with a 25-page introductory manifesto advocating the synthesis of politics (*Rajकारण*) and armed revolution. Its 2,000 copies sold out immediately before being proscribed by the Bombay government. In London, **Savarkar** researched in the India Office Library to write *The Indian War of Independence of 1857* (**1909**)—the first nationalist historiographical treatise to reframe the **1857 Sepoy Mutiny** as a planned, cross-communal war of national liberation, which was banned throughout the British Empire even before publication and smuggled into India wrapped in dust-jackets of *Pickwick Papers* and *Don Quixote*. Simultaneously, the Savarkar circle found ideological reinforcement in the Marathi journal *Kal*, edited by **Shivram Mahadev Paranjape** in Pune.
* **The Nasik Conspiracy Case (1909–1910):** In **1909**, the colonial state sentenced **Ganesh Savarkar** to transportation for life in the Andaman Cellular Jail merely for publishing a booklet of eight nationalist poems (*Laghu Abhinav Bharat Mala*). In response, **V.D. Savarkar** smuggled **20 Browning automatic pistols** and bomb manuals from Paris and London into Bombay inside the false bottom of a trunk carried by **Chaturbhuj Amin**, a cook at **India House**. On **December 21, 1909**, at the Vijayanand Theatre in Nasik, **Anant Lakshman Kanhere**, a 17-year-old member of an **Abhinav Bharat** cell in Aurangabad, assassinated **A.M.T. Jackson** (the District Magistrate of Nasik who had committed **Ganesh Savarkar** to trial) using one of the smuggled Browning pistols. In the ensuing **Nasik Conspiracy Case**, **Anant Lakshman Kanhere**, **Krishnaji Gopal Karve**, and **Vinayak Narayan Deshpande** were hanged in April **1910**, while 35 others—including **V.D. Savarkar**, who was arrested in London, made a daring escape through a porthole at Marseilles harbour (July **1910**), recaptured in violation of international extradition law, and sentenced to two consecutive terms of transportation for life (50 years) in the **Cellular Jail**—were convicted, effectively dismantling the **Abhinav Bharat** network in Maharashtra.

### 3. The Bengal Akhara-Samiti Matrix: Anushilan, Jugantar, and the Indo-German Conspiracy (1902–1915)

In Bengal, the structural contradiction between a bloated, Western-educated *bhadralok* class (dependant on shrinking clerical posts and fragmented intermediate *Patni* land tenures) and an arrogant colonial bureaucracy reached a breaking point during the **1905 Anti-Partition Swadeshi Movement**. Furthermore, colonial racial profiling—codified by **Thomas Babington Macaulay** and post-1857 "Martial Races" military recruitment doctrine—systematically caricatured the Bengali male as "effeminate" and physically cowardly. To smash this psychological inferiority complex, early Bengali revolutionary organizers founded physical-culture clubs (*akharas*), practicing *lathi*-play, wrestling, boxing, and swordsmanship while conducting social service during famines and epidemics.

#### The Dual Architecture of the Anushilan Samiti (Calcutta and Dacca) and the Jugantar Group

The institutional bedrock of Bengal’s revolutionary movement was the **Anushilan Samiti** ("Body-Building / Self-Cultivation Society"), named after **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay’s** philosophical treatise *Anushilan-Tattva* (*Dharmatattva*).

* **Calcutta Anushilan Samiti (1902):** In **March 1902**, **Satish Chandra Basu**, under the patronage of Calcutta High Court barrister **Pramathanath Mitra** (P. Mitra), established the **Calcutta Anushilan Samiti**. Simultaneously, **Aurobindo Ghosh** (then Vice-Principal of Baroda College, where he had been initiated into a secret western Indian revolutionary society by **Thakur Saheb** of Udaipur) dispatched **Jatindranath Banerjee** (*Niralamba Swami*, who had trained as a bodyguard in the Baroda army) and his younger brother **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** to Calcutta in **1902**, where they merged with **P. Mitra’s** group. They were joined by **Sister Nivedita** (Margaret Noble, the Irish disciple of **Swami Vivekananda**), who donated her personal library of European revolutionary literature and designed the first **Vajra (Thunderbolt)** nationalist flag (**1904**), and **Sarala Devi Chaudhurani**, who organized the **Birastami** and **Pratapaditya** martial festivals.
* **Dacca Anushilan Samiti (1906):** During a November **1905** visit to Dacca by **P. Mitra** and **Bipin Chandra Pal**, **Pulin Behari Das** stepped forward among eighty volunteers to found the **Dacca Anushilan Samiti** (**1906**). Unlike the looser, decentralized intellectual circles of Calcutta, **Pulin Das** constructed a rigid, highly centralized, military-style organization expanding to over **500 rural and urban branches** across Eastern Bengal and Assam with nearly 20,000 members. Members took graded vows (*Adya* and *Antya Pratijna*) in Sanskrit before an image of Goddess Kali with the *Bhagavad Gita* and a sword on their heads, swearing absolute obedience to their leader and death for any traitor who leaked organizational secrets. To fund arms procurement, **Pulin Das** orchestrated the famous **Barrah Dacoity** (June **1908**), a daylight boat-borne raid on the residence of a wealthy moneylender-zamindar in Dacca district that netted Rs 25,000 after a pitched gun battle across rivers. He was subsequently prosecuted in the **Dacca Conspiracy Case (1910–11)** and transported to the **Cellular Jail**.
* **The Jugantar Inner Circle and Print Radicalism (1906):** Within the **Calcutta Anushilan Samiti**, a sharp strategic rift emerged after the brutal police assault on delegates at the **April 1906 Barisal Provincial Conference**. While **P. Mitra** favored long-term physical preparation before confronting the Raj, an impatient militant faction led by **Barindra Kumar Ghosh**, **Bhupendranath Datta** (younger brother of **Swami Vivekananda**), **Abhinash Bhattacharya**, and **Debabrata Bose**—guided ideologically by **Aurobindo Ghosh** and **Charu Chandra Dutta**—formed an inner action circle. In **March 1906**, they launched the Bengali weekly ***Yugantar*** (*New Era*, taking its title from **Shivnath Shastri’s** novel), which achieved a circulation exceeding 20,000 copies. When **Bhupendranath Datta** was sentenced to one year’s rigorous imprisonment in July **1907** for two articles (*"Freedom from Fear"* and *"The Big Stick"*), *Yugantar* declared openly: *"Force must be stopped by force... Without bloodshed the worship of the Goddess will not be accomplished."*

#### Hemchandra Kanungo, the Muzaffarpur Bomb Case (1908), and the Alipore Conspiracy Case (1908–09)

To transition from *lathis* and crude country-made firearms to modern high-explosive warfare, the **Jugantar** leadership sought European scientific expertise. Earlier bomb experiments by **Ullaskar Dutt** (a self-taught chemist and son of a professor at Sibpur Engineering College) had failed during repeated attempts to blow up the special trains of the Lieutenant-Governor of Eastern Bengal and Assam, **Sir Bampfylde Fuller**, and the Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal, **Sir Andrew Fraser** (at Narayangarh near Midnapore in December **1907**).

* **Hemchandra Kanungo’s Paris Mission:** **Hemchandra Kanungo** (Hemchandra Das) of Midnapore sold a portion of his ancestral property to travel to Paris in **1906**. Through **Shyamji Krishna Varma** and **Madam Bhikaji Cama**, **Kanungo** connected with **Nicolas Safranski**, an exiled Russian Socialist-Revolutionary military engineer living in Paris, who trained him in the chemical synthesis of picric acid explosives, detonators, and cast-iron bomb casings, alongside organizational manuals for underground cells. Returning to India in January **1908**, **Kanungo** joined **Barindra Ghosh** and **Ullaskar Dutt** at a secluded garden house owned by the Ghosh family at **32 Muraripukur Road, Manicktala** (suburban Calcutta), establishing a secret bomb factory and revolutionary seminary (*ashram*).
* **The Muzaffarpur Bomb Case (April 30, 1908):** The **Jugantar** group’s primary target was **Douglas Kingsford**, who as Chief Presidency Magistrate of Calcutta had ordered the public flogging of fifteen-year-old student **Sushil Sen** and sentenced nationalist editors (**Bhupendranath Datta** and **Brahmabandhab Upadhyay**, who died in Campbell Hospital during his sedition trial after refusing to recognize the alien court) to harsh prison terms. Fearing for his life after **Hemchandra Kanungo** mailed him a book-bomb hollowed out inside a volume of **Herbert Spencer’s** works (which **Kingsford** left unopened on his shelf), the government promoted and transferred **Kingsford** to the post of District Judge at **Muzaffarpur** in Bihar. **Barindra Ghosh** dispatched eighteen-year-old **Khudiram Bose** (of Midnapore) and nineteen-year-old **Prafulla Chaki** (of Bogra) to assassinate him. On the evening of **April 30, 1908**, outside the European Club in Muzaffarpur, **Khudiram** and **Prafulla** threw a picric-acid bomb into a closed green carriage they believed carried **Kingsford**; tragically, the carriage belonged to barrister Pringle Kennedy, and the blast killed his wife and daughter (**Mrs. and Miss Kennedy**). **Prafulla Chaki**, cornered by police at Mokama Ghat railway station on May 1, shot himself dead rather than submit to arrest. **Khudiram Bose** was captured at Waini station (Samastipur), tried, and executed at Muzaffarpur Jail on **August 11, 1908**, ascending the gallows with the *Bhagavad Gita* in hand to become an immortal folk martyr across Bengal.
* **The Alipore Bomb Conspiracy Case (1908–1909):** Forty-eight hours after the Muzaffarpur blast, on **May 2, 1908**, Calcutta police raided the **Manicktala Garden House** and seven other hideouts, seizing bombs, dynamite, cartridges, and organizational rosters, and arresting **34 revolutionaries**, including **Aurobindo Ghosh**, **Barindra Kumar Ghosh**, **Ullaskar Dutt**, **Hemchandra Kanungo**, **Upendranath Banerjee**, and **Narendra Goswami** (who broke under interrogation and turned Crown approver). In the **Alipore Conspiracy Trial** (*Emperor vs. Aurobindo Ghosh and others*), the revolutionary underground demonstrated its lethal reach inside the courtroom and prison walls:
  1. On **August 31, 1908**, inside the hospital ward of Alipore Central Jail, co-accused prisoners **Kanailal Dutta** and **Satyendranath Bose** assassinated the approver **Narendra Goswami** using two revolvers smuggled into the prison, preventing his testimony from being cross-examined; both were promptly hanged.
  2. In November **1908**, **Sub-Inspector Nandalal Banerjee** (who had arrested **Prafulla Chaki**) was shot dead in Serpentine Lane, Calcutta; in February **1909**, the Public Prosecutor **Ashutosh Biswas** was assassinated outside the Alipore Courthouse by **Charu Chandra Bose** (a youth born with a crippled right palm who tied the revolver to his wrist with a cord); and in January **1910**, **Deputy Superintendent of Police Shamsul Alam** was shot dead on the stairs of the Calcutta High Court by **Birendranath Datta Gupta**.
  3. **Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das)** served for ten months without fee as defense counsel for **Aurobindo Ghosh**, dismantling the prosecution's handwriting evidence and delivering his prophetic summation: *"Long after the controversy will be hushed in silence, long after this turmoil and agitation will have ceased, long after he is dead and gone, he will be looked upon as the poet of patriotism, as the prophet of nationalism and the lover of humanity."* On **May 6, 1909**, Judge C.P. Beachcroft acquitted **Aurobindo Ghosh** (who subsequently retired to the French enclave of **Pondicherry** in **1910** to pursue spiritual yoga), while sentencing **Barindra Ghosh** and **Ullaskar Dutt** to death (later commuted on appeal to life transportation in the **Cellular Jail**, along with **Hemchandra Kanungo** and **Upendranath Banerjee**).

#### Bagha Jatin and the Indo-German Zimmerman Plan (1914–1915)

Following the **Alipore** and **Dacca** trials, the shattered **Jugantar** cells across Western and Northern Bengal were consolidated under the charismatic leadership of **Jatindranath Mukherjee**, universally revered as **Bagha Jatin** (having single-handedly killed a royal Bengal tiger with a *khukri* dagger in **1906**). Operating as a clerk in the Bengal Secretariat and later as a railway construction contractor under the cover firm **Harry & Sons** in Calcutta, **Bagha Jatin** shifted the **Jugantar** strategy from isolated assassinations and small-scale *Swadeshi dacoities* to the preparation of a synchronized pan-Indian armed insurrection assisted by Indian regiments and foreign anti-British powers. Although temporarily arrested in the **Howrah-Sibpur Conspiracy Case (1910–11)**—where the prosecution attempted to charge 46 revolutionaries with subverting the loyalty of the **10th Jats Regiment** stationed at Fort William—**Bagha Jatin** was acquitted for lack of evidence and spent the **1913 Damodar River floods** linking district revolutionary commanders (**Atulkrishna Ghosh**, **Jadugopal Mukherjee**, **Narendranath Bhattacharya** [later **M.N. Roy**], **Manoranjan Gupta**, and **Chittapriya Ray Chaudhuri**) into a federated **Jugantar** network.

When **World War I** erupted in **August 1914**, **Bagha Jatin’s** group immediately seized **50 Mauser pistols** and **46,000 rounds of ammunition** in a daring daylight heist of a customs consignment belonging to the Calcutta arms dealer **Rodda & Co.** (August 26, **1914**, orchestrated by **Srish Chandra Mitra** and **Anukul Mukherjee**), distributing these weapons across virtually every revolutionary action in Bengal for the next decade. Simultaneously, linking up with **Rash Behari Bose** in Upper India and the **Berlin Committee** in Germany (under the **Indo-German Conspiracy** or **Zimmerman Plan**, sanctioned by German Foreign Secretary **Arthur Zimmermann**), **Bagha Jatin** formulated an audacious military operational plan:

* **The Maritime Arms Shipment (*Maverick* and *Annie Larsen*):** Financed by the German Consulate in San Francisco and the **Berlin Committee**, two vessels—the schooner *Annie Larsen* (carrying 10,000 rifles and four million rounds of ammunition) and the oil tanker *SS Maverick*—were dispatched from California via Socorro Island (Mexico) and Batavia (Dutch East Indies) to offload weapons at three strategic points along the Bay of Bengal coast: **Raimangal** in the Sundarbans, **Hatia Island**, and **Balasore** on the Orissa coast. **Narendranath Bhattacharya (M.N. Roy)** traveled twice to Batavia under the alias **Charles A. Martin** to transfer German gold and coordinate the landings through **Harry & Sons** and the **Universal Emporium** at Balasore.
* **Strategic Insurrection Blueprint:** Once the German rifles landed, **Bagha Jatin’s** forces were to blow up the three key bridges connecting Bengal to the rest of India (on the Madras, Bengal-Nagpur, and East Indian Railways), sever communication lines, storm Fort William in Calcutta in alliance with Indian troops, and proclaim a republic.
* **The Battle of Balasore (Chasakhand, September 9, 1915):** Due to American naval interdiction of the *Annie Larsen* at Hoquiam (Washington), coordination failures at Socorro Island, and leaks by Czech nationalist double-agents (**E.V. Voska** network) and British intelligence in Batavia, Calcutta Police raided **Harry & Sons** and tracked **Bagha Jatin** to his jungle hideout at **Kaptipada** in Mayurbhanj (Orissa). Retreating toward Balasore railway station, **Bagha Jatin** and four young comrades—**Chittapriya Ray Chaudhuri**, **Manoranjan Sengupta**, **Nirendranath Dasgupta**, and **Jyotish Chandra Pal**—took a final entrenched position behind the embankments of a dried-up tank inside a bamboo grove on the banks of the **Buribalam River** at **Chasakhand** (Balasore). On **September 9, 1915**, armed only with Mauser pistols against an encirclement of British police and military riflemen led by District Magistrate R.G. Kilby and Charles Tegart, they fought a heroic seventy-five-minute trench battle—the first open pitched gun-battle since **1857**. **Chittapriya** died in the trenches, **Bagha Jatin** succumbed to his bullet wounds the next day (**September 10, 1915**) at Balasore Government Hospital declaring *"We shall die to awaken the nation,"* **Manoranjan** and **Nirendranath** were hanged, and **Jyotish Pal** was transported to the **Cellular Jail**.

### 4. Regional Revolutionary Matrices: Madras, Punjab, and the 1912 Delhi Bomb Conspiracy

Far from being confined to Maharashtra and Bengal, Phase I revolutionary militancy developed distinct regional nodes in the Madras Presidency, the canal colonies of Punjab, and the imperial capital of Delhi, linking regional agrarian and linguistic grievances with transnational networks.

#### Madras Presidency: Vanchi Iyer, Nilakanta Brahmachari, and the Bharata Mata Association (1911)

In the Madras Presidency, the brutal suppression of **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai’s** **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** at Tuticorin and the **Tirunelveli riots of March 1908** (during which British Collector Wynch and Sub-Collector **Robert William d’Escourt Ashe** ordered police firing on unarmed crowds protesting the arrest of **V.O.C.** and **Subramania Siva**) catalyzed an underground militant response.

* **The Pondicherry Sanctuary:** Just as Chandernagore served as a French sanctuary for Bengal revolutionaries, the French enclave of **Pondicherry** became the logistics and propaganda hub for Tamil revolutionaries. **Subramania Bharati** (escaping arrest as editor of the Tamil weekly *India*), **Mandayam Srinivasachari**, and **M.P.T. Acharya** relocated to Pondicherry in **1908–09**, soon joined by **Aurobindo Ghosh** and **V.V.S. Aiyar** (who had served as **V.D. Savarkar’s** right-hand lieutenant at **India House** in London before escaping disguised as a Sikh gentleman via Paris to Pondicherry in **1910**).
* **Bharata Mata Association and the Assassination of Collector Ashe (June 17, 1911):** In **1910**, **Nilakanta Brahmachari** and **Shankara Krishna Aiyar** organized the secret **Bharata Mata Association** in Tirunelveli and Travancore, initiating members through blood-oaths signed on portraits of Kali to assassinate European officials ahead of King George V’s coronation. **Vanchi Iyer** (Vanchinathan, a 25-year-old forest department clerk in the Travancore state service at Punalur) traveled to Pondicherry, where **V.V.S. Aiyar** trained him in target practice with a Browning automatic pistol supplied by **Madam Bhikaji Cama** from Paris. On **June 17, 1911**, at **Maniyachi Railway Junction**, **Vanchi Iyer** stepped up to the first-class compartment carrying Tirunelveli District Collector **Robert Ashe** and his wife on their way to Kodaikanal, shot **Ashe** dead at point-blank range, and then ran into the station latrine and shot himself through the mouth to avoid capture. A Tamil letter found in **Vanchi’s** pocket declared that **Ashe** was executed for destroying **V.O.C.’s** indigenous shipping company and announced that 3,000 Madras youths had taken a vow to kill George V the moment he set foot in India. In the subsequent **Tirunelveli Conspiracy Case (1911–12)**, **Nilakanta Brahmachari** and 13 others were sentenced to rigorous imprisonment.

#### Punjab Agrarian Militancy: Ajit Singh, *Pagri Sambhal Jatta*, and the Anjuman-i-Mohisban-i-Watan (1907–1909)

In Punjab—the strategic recruiting ground of the post-1858 British Indian Army—militant mobilization took root not in urban colleges alone, but within the Jat Sikh and Muslim peasantry of the **Chenab and Bari Doab Canal Colonies** (Lyallpur and Rawalpindi).

* **Structural Agrarian Triggers (1906–07):** By late **1906**, the Punjab peasantry faced a triple fiscal-legal assault:
  1. The **Punjab Colonisation of Land Bill (1906)**, which stripped pioneer peasant settlers in the Chenab Canal Colony of inheritance rights by introducing strict primogeniture (reverting land to the Crown if a peasant died without male heirs), forbade the cutting of trees or transfer of property, and imposed retroactive fines engineered by bureaucracy;
  2. A **25% to 50% hike in the *abiana* (canal water rate)** on the **Bari Doab Canal**, directly hitting Jat Sikh families in Amritsar, Gurdaspur, and Lahore who supplied the bulk of the Indian cavalry and infantry; and
  3. A steep increase in land revenue assessment in Rawalpindi district, coinciding with a deadly plague epidemic that killed over 60,000 people per week across Punjab in early **1907**.
* **Ajit Singh and the *Pagri Sambhal Jatta* Movement (1907):** While **Lala Lajpat Rai** mobilized urban lawyers and Arya Samaj networks, the radical spearhead was **Sardar Ajit Singh** (paternal uncle of **Bhagat Singh**), supported by his brothers **Kishan Singh** and **Swaran Singh**, **Sufi Amba Prasad**, **Zia-ul-Haq**, and **Lal Chand Falak**. In **1906–07**, **Ajit Singh** founded the revolutionary organization **Anjuman-i-Mohisban-i-Watan** (*Society of Lovers of the Motherland*, also known as the **Bharat Mata Society**) in Lahore, with its Urdu journal ***Bharat Mata***. At a massive peasant rally in **Lyallpur on March 22, 1907**, the nationalist editor **Banke Dayal** (editor of *Jhang Syal*) recited his electrifying Punjabi poem ***Pagri Sambhal Jatta, Pagri Sambhal Oye*** (*"Guard your turban [honour], O Peasant!"*), which instantly became the eponymous battle cry of the movement. **Ajit Singh** toured rural districts urging peasants to stop paying canal water rates and revenue, calling upon the 100,000 Indian soldiers to turn their rifles on their 30,000 British masters.
* **Colonial Capitulation and Exile:** Terrified by the Commander-in-Chief **Lord Kitchener’s** warning that the **Colonisation Bill** was provoking mutiny inside Sikh regiments, the colonial state executed a dual maneuver in **May 1907**: it deported **Lala Lajpat Rai** and **Ajit Singh** to Mandalay (Burma) without trial under **Regulation III of 1818**, enacted the **Prevention of Seditious Meetings Act**, and simultaneously **vetoed the Punjab Colonisation Bill** and reduced canal rates. Released in November **1907**, **Ajit Singh** resumed underground publishing until **1909**, when, facing imminent arrest for sedition, he fled with **Sufi Amba Prasad** across the border to Iran (where **Amba Prasad** later died fighting British forces at Shiraz in **1917**), eventually operating across Paris, Geneva, Rio de Janeiro, and Rome until **1947**.

#### Rash Behari Bose, Sachin Sanyal, and the 1912 Delhi Bomb Attack on Viceroy Hardinge

By **1911–1912**, the center of underground gravity shifted toward an inter-regional network linking Bengal, the United Provinces, Punjab, and Delhi. The architect of this pan-northern consolidation was **Rash Behari Bose** (a head clerk at the Forest Research Institute in Dehra Dun with deep ties to the Chandernagore and **Jugantar** bomb networks) and **Sachindranath Sanyal** (who had founded a revolutionary **Anushilan Samiti** in Benares in **1908**, later renamed the **Young Men’s Association**). Simultaneously, in Punjab and Delhi, **Lala Hardayal** had left behind a radical cell of brilliant disciples before departing for Europe in **1908**—including **Master Amir Chand**, **Avadh Bihari**, **Balmokand**, and **Hanwant Sahai**—whom **Rash Behari Bose** welded together with the Bengal-Benares underground.

* **The December 23, 1912 Chandni Chowk Bombing:** When the colonial state announced the **1911 annulment of the Partition of Bengal** alongside the transfer of the imperial capital from Calcutta to **Delhi**, the **Viceroy Lord Hardinge** scheduled a grand ceremonial state entry into the old Mughal capital on **December 23, 1912**, designed to project British continuity with Mughal sovereignty. As **Viceroy Hardinge** and Lady Hardinge rode atop a caparisoned state elephant through **Chandni Chowk** in front of the Punjab National Bank building, a powerful picric-acid bomb (manufactured in Chandernagore by **Manindra Naik** and brought to Delhi) was hurled directly into the Viceregal howdah by **Basanta Kumar Biswas** (a young Bengali revolutionary disguised in female attire among the women spectators on the balcony), under the direct supervision of **Rash Behari Bose**. The explosion blew away the rear of the howdah, killed the umbrella-bearer instantly, and severely wounded **Lord Hardinge** with shrapnel across his back and shoulder.
* **The Delhi-Lahore Conspiracy Case (1914–15):** Immediately after the blast, **Rash Behari Bose** returned by night train to Dehra Dun, where he organized a loyalist citizens’ meeting the very next day to condemn the "dastardly attack" on the Viceroy, thoroughly deceiving British intelligence for over a year. However, following a follow-up bombing at Lawrence Gardens in Lahore (May **1913**, carried out by **Basanta Kumar Biswas**), police raids in Delhi and Lahore in February **1914** uncovered the *Liberty* revolutionary leaflets and bomb components. In the **Delhi Conspiracy Trial**, **Master Amir Chand**, **Avadh Bihari**, and **Balmokand** were hanged in Delhi on May 8, **1915**, and twenty-year-old **Basanta Kumar Biswas** was hanged at Ambala Central Jail on May 11, **1915**, while **Rash Behari Bose** evaded the dragnet to assume supreme command of the wartime **Ghadar** insurrection.

## II. Transnational Anti-Colonial Networks and Wartime Insurrections (1905–1918)

### 5. European Metropoles: India House in London, Madam Bhikaji Cama, and the Berlin Committee

When the post-**1907** statutory repression (**Explosive Substances Act**, **Press Acts**, and **Criminal Law Amendment Act**) strangled underground printing and bomb manufacturing inside British India, Indian revolutionaries exploited the geopolitical rivalries of European imperial powers and the bourgeois civil-liberty protections existing inside the metropolitan capitals of **London**, **Paris**, **Geneva**, and **Berlin**. These expatriate centers performed four strategic functions: ideological incubation free from Indian censorship, procurement and smuggling of modern automatic firearms, technical training in explosives, and diplomatic alliance-building with anti-British socialists, Irish Fenians, Egyptian nationalists, Russian revolutionaries, and eventually the **Imperial German General Staff**.

#### Shyamji Krishna Varma’s India House (1905) and Madan Lal Dhingra (1909)

The pioneer of the European revolutionary base was **Shyamji Krishna Varma** (**1857–1930**), a brilliant Kathiawari Sanskritist, Oxford graduate (Balliol College), barrister, former Diwan of Ratlam, Junagadh, and Udaipur, and disciple of both **Swami Dayananda Saraswati** and the British radical anti-imperialist philosopher **Herbert Spencer**. Disgusted by the arrest of **Tilak** in **1897**, **Varma** relocated permanently to London.

* **Institutional Matrix in London (1905):** In **January 1905**, **Shyamji Krishna Varma** launched the English monthly ***The Indian Sociologist***, carrying **Herbert Spencer’s** epigram on its masthead: *"Resistance to aggression is not simply justifiable but imperative. Non-resistance hurts both altruism and egoism."* In **February 1905**, he founded the **Indian Home Rule Society** (with **Varma** as President, **S.R. Rana** of Paris as Vice-President, and **M.R. Jayakar** on the committee) to secure complete *Swaraj* for India. In **July 1905**, **Varma** purchased a Victorian mansion at **65 Cromwell Avenue, Highgate, North London**, christening it **India House** (inaugurated by British Marxist leader **H.M. Hyndman**, Irish Home Ruler **Charlotte Despard**, and **Dadabhai Naoroji**). Endowing six **Rs 1,000 travelling fellowships** (named after **Rana Pratap**, **Shivaji**, **1857 martyrs**, and **Herbert Spencer**) on the sole condition that recipients would never accept any post or legal brief under the British Indian government, **Varma** brought **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar**, **Lala Hardayal**, **Madan Lal Dhingra**, **V.V.S. Aiyar**, **M.P.T. Acharya**, **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya**, **P.M. Bapat** (*Senapati Bapat*), and **G.D. Kumar** into **India House**.
* **Radicalization under Savarkar and the Assassination of Curzon Wyllie (July 1, 1909):** When British parliamentary questions and Scotland Yard surveillance forced **Shyamji Krishna Varma** to shift his headquarters to Paris in **1907**, **V.D. Savarkar** took direct operational command of **India House**, founding the **Free India Society**. Sunday meetings at **India House** became training sessions in revolutionary history (celebrating the **May 10, 1908** Golden Jubilee of the **1857 Revolt**), target shooting at a Tottenham Court Road range, and chemical bomb-making. On the night of **July 1, 1909**, at a social reception hosted by the National Indian Association at the **Imperial Institute in South Kensington**, **Madan Lal Dhingra**—an engineering student at University College London from an affluent loyalist family in Amritsar—stepped up to **Sir William Hutt Curzon Wyllie** (Political Aide-de-Camp to the Secretary of State for India, **Lord Morley**, who ran the intelligence network monitoring Indian students in Britain) and fired five shots into his face from a Colt automatic pistol, killing him along with a Parsi doctor, **Cawas Lalcaca**, who tried to intervene.
* **Dhingra’s Martyrdom Statement:** At his Old Bailey trial, **Madan Lal Dhingra** refused legal counsel, denied the legitimacy of the British court, and had a prepared political manifesto (*Challenge*) printed and circulated through **Savarkar** in the *Daily News* on the eve of his execution at **Pentonville Prison on August 17, 1909**: *"I believe that a nation held down by foreign bayonets is in a state of perpetual war. Since open battle is rendered impossible to a disarmed race, I attacked by surprise... Poor in wealth and intellect, a son like myself has nothing else to offer to the Mother but his own blood, and so I have sacrificed the same on her altar. The only lesson required in India at present is to learn how to die, and the only way to teach it is by dying ourselves."* The assassination triggered the immediate closure of **India House** and the shift of the revolutionary command center across the English Channel to **Paris**.

#### Madam Bhikaji Cama in Paris and the 1907 Stuttgart International Socialist Congress

In **Paris**, the expatriate revolutionary network was anchored by **Madam Bhikaji Rustom Cama** (**1861–1936**, born into a wealthy Bombay Parsi family, who had contracted plague while nursing patients in Bombay in **1897** and moved to Europe in **1902**), operating alongside the Kathiawari pearl merchant **Sardar Singhji Rewabhai Rana (S.R. Rana)** and **Shyamji Krishna Varma**. Known to French Socialists and British intelligence alike as the *"Mother of the Indian Revolution,"* **Madam Cama** financed young revolutionaries, smuggled Browning pistols and bomb literature through the French enclave of Pondicherry, and edited two influential revolutionary journals: ***Bande Mataram*** (launched in Geneva in **September 1909** to continue the legacy of **Aurobindo Ghosh’s** banned Calcutta daily) and ***Madan’s Talvar*** (published from Berlin/Paris in memory of **Madan Lal Dhingra**, edited by **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya**).

* **The Stuttgart Flag and Resolution (August 22, 1907):** In **August 1907**, **Madam Bhikaji Cama** and **S.R. Rana** attended the **Seventh International Socialist Congress at Stuttgart, Germany**, alongside a thousand delegates including **Vladimir Lenin**, **Rosa Luxemburg**, **Karl Liebknecht**, **Jean Jaurès**, and **H.M. Hyndman**. Overcoming opposition from British Labour Party delegate Ramsay MacDonald, **Madam Cama** took the podium on **August 22, 1907**, unfurling the **first version of the Indian National Flag** on foreign soil—a horizontal tricolor of **green, saffron (yellow), and red** (representing Islam, Hinduism/Buddhism, and liberation struggle), bearing eight blooming lotuses on the top band (representing the eight provinces of British India), the words ***Bande Mataram*** in Devanagari script across the middle band, and a sun and crescent moon on the bottom band (co-designed with **V.D. Savarkar** and **Shyamji Krishna Varma**, modeled on the **1906 Calcutta Boycott Flag**). She delivered a scathing indictment of British economic drain: *"Behold, the flag of independent India is born! It has been made sacred by the blood of young Indians who sacrificed their lives in its honour... Thirty-five million pounds sterling are taken annually from India to England without return, and in consequence people in India die of starvation by the half-million every year!"*

#### The Berlin Committee (Indian Independence Committee, 1914–1918)

With the outbreak of **World War I** in **August 1914**, the **Paris** and **Swiss** exile networks recognized that Britain’s existential struggle against **Imperial Germany** and the **Ottoman Empire** offered an unprecedented military-logistical window. Led by **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya** (*Chatto*, elder brother of **Sarojini Naidu**), **Bhupendranath Datta** (who had escaped to the United States after his release from the *Yugantar* sedition sentence), **Abhinash Bhattacharya**, **Chempakaraman Pillai** (who had already established the International Pro-India Committee in Zurich in **1912**), **Dhirendranath Sarkar**, and **M.P.T. Acharya**—later joined by **Lala Hardayal** and **Maulana Barkatullah**—Indian revolutionaries in Berlin negotiated a formal compact with Baron **Max von Oppenheim** (head of the *Nachrichtenstelle für den Orient* / Intelligence Bureau for the East) and **Arthur Zimmermann** of the German Foreign Office.

In **September 1914**, they constituted the **Berlin Committee** (formally renamed the **Indian Independence Committee [IIC]** in **1915**). Under their bilateral treaty with the German government, the **Berlin Committee** secured guaranteed financial credits (explicitly stipulated as a *national loan* to be repaid by a sovereign post-war Indian Republic, preserving ideological autonomy), weapons, ammunition, passports, and access to Indian prisoners-of-war held at the **Halfmoon Camp in Wünsdorf** near Zossen. From Berlin, the **IIC** coordinated a three-pronged global strategy:
1. Dispatching arms shipments (*Annie Larsen* and *Maverick*) and German funds via the United States and Batavia to **Bagha Jatin’s** **Jugantar** cells in Bengal and **Ghadar** cadres in Punjab;
2. Sending operatives (**Satyendranath Sen**, **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle**, **Kartar Singh Sarabha**) alongside **Ghadar** volunteers across the Pacific to ignite mutinies inside British Indian military cantonments from Punjab to Singapore and Rangoon; and
3. Launching an overland diplomatic-military expedition via Constantinople, Baghdad, and Tehran to **Kabul** to establish a **Provisional Government of Free India** on the northwest frontier of the Raj.

### 6. The Pacific Rim Crucible: The Ghadar Movement, Komagata Maru, and the 1915 Mutinies

While European revolutionary centers consisted primarily of middle-class students and intellectuals, the Pacific Coast of North America (**United States** and **Canada**) produced a mass-based, working-class and peasant-soldier revolutionary movement—the **Ghadar Party** (**1913**). Between **1904** and **1913**, severe agrarian debt, land fragmentation, and canal tax hikes in the **Jalandhar** and **Hoshiarpur** Doab districts of Punjab drove over **15,000 Punjabi peasants** (predominantly Jat Sikhs, many of them former soldiers of the British Indian Army who had served in Hong Kong, Shanghai, and Malaya) to migrate to **British Columbia (Vancouver and Victoria)** and the **Pacific Coast states of Washington, Oregon, and California**, working in lumber mills, railway construction, and fruit orchards.

#### Early Pacific Mobilization and the Founding of the Ghadar Party (1913)

In North America, Punjabi migrant workers encountered virulent white-supremacist labor riots (such as the **1907 Bellingham and Vancouver anti-Asian riots**), discriminatory immigration laws, and complete abandonment by British diplomatic consuls. Through this brutal experience, former loyalist Sikh veterans realized a fundamental structural truth: they were degraded abroad as *"coolies"* purely because they were colonial subjects of an enslaved nation at home.

* **Pre-Ghadar Pioneers (1907–1912):** Early activist nodes emerged in Vancouver and Seattle under **Taraknath Das** (who published the English monthly ***Free Hindustan*** from Vancouver/Seattle in **1908**), **G.D. Kumar** (who opened the **Swadesh Sevak Home** in Vancouver on the model of **India House** and published the Gurmukhi paper ***Swadesh Sevak***), **Ramnath Puri** (*Circular-i-Azadi*, **1907**), **Bhagwan Singh**, and the Khalsa Diwan Society in Vancouver.
* **The Astoria & San Francisco Foundation (May–November 1913):** In **1912–13**, Punjabi lumber-mill workers in **Oregon** led by **Baba Sohan Singh Bhakna**, **Kanshi Ram** (of Maroli), **Harnam Singh 'Tundilat'**, and **Udham Singh Kasel** invited **Lala Hardayal** (then lecturing at Stanford University after arriving in the United States in **1911**) to help organize a central political body. At historic delegates’ conferences at **Astoria, Oregon (May 1913)** and **Sacramento**, they founded the **Pacific Coast Hindi Association**, soon universally known after its newspaper as the **Ghadar Party**, with **Sohan Singh Bhakna** as **President**, **Lala Hardayal** as **General Secretary**, and **Pandit Kanshi Ram** as **Treasurer**.
* **The Yugantar Ashram and *Hindustan Ghadar* (November 1, 1913):** Establishing its central headquarters at **436 Hill Street, San Francisco**, named the **Yugantar Ashram**, the party launched its weekly newspaper ***The Ghadar*** (later *Hindustan Ghadar*) in Urdu on **November 1, 1913**, and in Gurmukhi on December 9, **1913** (later adding Gujarati and Hindi editions). The nineteen-year-old UC Berkeley chemistry student **Kartar Singh Sarabha** worked tirelessly operating the hand-cranked printing press, writing revolutionary poetry, and translating articles, alongside **Maulana Barkatullah** (formerly professor at Tokyo University where he published *Islamic Fraternity*) and **Ramchandra Peshawari** (who took over as leader after **Lala Hardayal** was arrested by US immigration authorities at British instigation in March **1914** and jumped bail to Switzerland and Berlin).
* **Secular Egalitarianism and the *Ghadar-di-Gunj* Aesthetic:** Recognizing that British rule survived by pitting Sikhs, Muslims, and Hindus against each other (highlighting how Sikh soldiers had been used to crush the **1857 Revolt**), the **Ghadar Party** enforced strict secularism at the **Yugantar Ashram**: religious debates were banned, caste taboos were abolished through a common mess, and its motto was *"Our religion is patriotism."* The opening issue of *The Ghadar* printed the famous recruitment advertisement on its front page:
  > ***"Wanted: Brave Soldiers to stir up Ghadar (Rebellion) in India. Pay: Death; Price: Martyrdom; Pension: Liberty; Field of Battle: India."***
  Every issue carried the ***"Angrezi Raj ka Kacha Chitha"*** (*A Balance Sheet of British Rule*), a 14-point structural indictment enumerating the annual drain of 50 crore rupees, famines killing 20 million Indians, expenditure of revenue on imperial frontier wars while neglecting education and sanitation, and the legal impunity given to British murderers of Indians. Simultaneously, its compiled poetry anthologies—***Ghadar-di-Gunj*** (*Echo of Mutiny*)—transformed Punjabi folk verse (*Baint* and *Korra Chhand*) from devotional mysticism into a fierce, secular call to anti-imperial insurrection, mailed free in thousands of copies to Indian army barracks in Hong Kong, Singapore, Shanghai, Penang, Rangoon, and Punjab.

#### The Komagata Maru Incident (1914)

Two months before the outbreak of World War I, the **Komagata Maru** episode catalyzed the transition of the **Ghadar Movement** from propaganda to immediate armed mobilization. To halt Indian immigration without explicitly naming British subjects by race, the **Canadian Government** had enacted **Order-in-Council PC 920 (the "Continuous Passage" Regulation of 1908)**, requiring all Asian immigrants to arrive via a single, uninterrupted ticket from their country of birth—a logistical impossibility since the British-Indian government barred shipping companies from operating direct steamer lines from Indian ports to Canada, while also requiring immigrants to possess $200 upon arrival.

* **Baba Gurdit Singh’s Voyage:** In **November 1913**, a Canadian judge briefly struck down the regulation on technical grounds in the *SS Panama Maru* case before Canada swiftly re-enacted tightened orders in January **1914**. Determined to challenge this imperial racial exclusion or expose British hypocrisy, **Baba Gurdit Singh** (a wealthy Sikh merchant and contractor from Sarhali, Amritsar, operating in Singapore and Malaya) chartered a Japanese steamship, the ***SS Komagata Maru*** (renamed *Guru Nanak Jahaz*), at **Hong Kong**, embarking on **April 4, 1914** via Shanghai, Moji, and Yokohama with **376 Punjabi passengers** (340 Sikhs, 24 Muslims, and 12 Hindus). During port stops in Japan, **Ghadar** leaders **Bhagwan Singh** and **Maulana Barkatullah** boarded the ship to distribute copies of *The Ghadar* and deliver fiery anti-colonial lectures.
* **The Two-Month Vancouver Standoff (May 23 – July 23, 1914):** Arriving in Burrard Inlet, **Vancouver Harbour, on May 23, 1914**, Canadian immigration authorities permitted only 24 returning residents to disembark, cordoning off the remaining **352 passengers** aboard the vessel and denying them food and fresh water. While a **Shore Committee** led by **Hussain Rahim**, **Sohan Lal Pathak**, and **Balwant Singh** raised $22,000 and fought a legal battle that failed in the British Columbia Court of Appeal, Canadian police attempted a midnight boarding assault on July 19, which the starving passengers repelled with a barrage of coal, bricks, and scrap metal. When the Canadian dominion government deployed the naval cruiser ***HMCS Rainbow*** with guns trained on the steamer, the **Komagata Maru** was forced to weigh anchor on **July 23, 1914**.
* **The Budge Budge Massacre (September 29, 1914):** While the ship was crossing the Pacific, **World War I** broke out, and the Government of India enacted the **Ingress into India Ordinance (September 5, 1914)**, empowering the state to detain, restrict to their villages, or imprison any person entering India. Refused entry at Hong Kong and Singapore, the **Komagata Maru** was diverted to **Budge Budge Harbour** (17 miles south of Calcutta), arriving on **September 27, 1914**. When British police ordered the passengers onto a special guarded train bound directly for detention in Punjab instead of allowing them to deposit the sacred *Guru Granth Sahib* at the Calcutta Gurdwara, the passengers marched on foot toward Calcutta. Intercepted at **Budge Budge railway station on September 29, 1914** by British troops (Royal Fusiliers) and armed police, a clash erupted in which **19 passengers were shot dead**, over 200 were arrested and interned, and **Baba Gurdit Singh** escaped into a seven-year underground existence until he surrendered to **Mahatma Gandhi** at Nankana Sahib in **1922**.

#### The 1915 Ghadar Mutiny Conspiracy and the Singapore Mutiny

Infuriated by the **Komagata Maru** standoff and seizing the outbreak of **World War I** (*"Britain’s difficulty is India’s opportunity"*), the **Ghadar Party** issued its formal **Ailan-e-Jang (Declaration of War)** against the British Empire in August **1914**.At mass rallies in Sacramento, Fresno, Portland, and Vancouver, over **8,000 Ghadarites** abandoned their farms, lumber jobs, and businesses in North America and East Asia to sail back to India aboard Japanese and American steamers (*Korea*, *Tosamaru*, *Mashimamaru*, *Namsang*) to organize an armed insurrection inside Punjab.

* **Operational Regrouping under Rash Behari Bose (January–February 1915):** Because the colonial state used the **Ingress into India Ordinance** to screen returning ships at Calcutta, Madras, and Colombo—immediately interning **400 top leaders** (including **Sohan Singh Bhakna** and **Jwala Singh**) in jail and restricting **2,500 others** to their villages—the surviving at-large cadres (**Kartar Singh Sarabha**, **Raghubir Dayal Gupta**, **Harnam Singh 'Tundilat'**, **Jagat Singh**) had to improvise rapidly. Recognizing the need for an experienced central commander, **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle** (a bold Maharashtrian engineering student from Seattle) traveled to Benares and brought **Rash Behari Bose** and **Sachin Sanyal** to Amritsar in **January 1915**. Establishing secret headquarters at Amritsar and later at Mochi Gate in **Lahore** with bomb factories at **Jhabewal** and **Lohatbaddi**, **Rash Behari Bose**, **Kartar Singh Sarabha**, and **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle** bypassed the apathetic urban middle classes and infiltrated British Indian Army cantonments stretching from **Bannu, Kohat, Peshawar, Rawalpindi, Jhelum, Ferozepur, Lahore (Mian Mir), Ambala, and Meerut** to **Agra, Benares, Allahabad, Faizabad, and Danapur**.
* **The February 21, 1915 Uprising Plan and Betrayal:** **Kartar Singh Sarabha** cycled incessantly between cantonments, securing promises of mutiny from the **23rd Cavalry at Mian Mir (Lahore)** and the **26th Punjabis at Ferozepur**. The synchronized rising was fixed for the night of **February 21, 1915** (beginning at Mian Mir and Ferozepur, seizing magazines, massacring British officers, cutting telegraph lines, and hoisting the newly stitched red-yellow-blue **Ghadar tricolor flag**). However, the Criminal Investigation Department (CID) under **Liaqat Hayat Khan** planted a spy, **Kirpal Singh**, directly inside the Lahore central committee through his cousin Balwant Singh. Discovering on February 15 that **Kirpal Singh** was leaking the plan, **Rash Behari Bose** desperately advanced the date of the uprising to **February 19, 1915**; **Kirpal Singh** discovered the revised date as well and signaled the police from the roof of the Mochi Gate headquarters on the morning of **February 19**. Within hours, British units surrounded the Lahore hideouts, disarmed the Indian regiments at Mian Mir and Ferozepur, and arrested the core leadership. **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle** was captured inside the lines of the **12th Cavalry at Meerut** on March 23 carrying a trunk containing ten high-explosive picric-acid bombs capable of destroying half a regiment, while **Rash Behari Bose** escaped to Japan in May **1915** under the literary alias **P.N. Tagore**.
* **Defence of India Act (1915) and the First Lahore Conspiracy Case:** To crush the **Ghadarites** without ordinary judicial delays or appeals, the Viceroy’s Legislative Council rushed through the **Defence of India Act (March 1915)** in a single sitting, establishing three-member **Special Tribunals** whose capital sentences were final and unappealable to any High Court. Across the **First Lahore Conspiracy Case** and supplementary trials (**1915–16**), **291 revolutionaries** were tried: **42 were sentenced to death and executed** (including the nineteen-year-old **Kartar Singh Sarabha**, **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle**, **Kanshi Ram**, **Jagat Singh**, and **Bakshish Singh**, who went to the gallows in Lahore Central Jail on **November 16, 1915**), **114 were sentenced to transportation for life** in the **Cellular Jail** (including **Sohan Singh Bhakna**, **Prithvi Singh Azad**, **Baba Gurmukh Singh**, and **Parmanand of Jhansi**), and scores more were executed by military courts-martial in the **Mandi**, **Benares** (where **Sachin Sanyal** received life transportation), and **Burma** conspiracy trials.
* **The Singapore Mutiny (February 15, 1915):** The **Ghadar** call to arms ignited the single largest full-scale military mutiny of the war at **Singapore**, the strategic naval chokepoint of the British Empire in Southeast Asia. The **5th Light Infantry**—a regiment composed entirely of Rajput and Pathan Muslims from Punjab and Haryana stationed at Alexandra Barracks—had been heavily exposed to *The Ghadar* newspaper, contact with **Komagata Maru** passengers, anti-colonial sermons by **Kassim Mansoor** and Pir **Nur Alam**, and anger over rumours that they were being shipped from Hong Kong to fight their Muslim brethren in the Ottoman Empire. At **3:00 PM on February 15, 1915** (Chinese New Year), as ammunition was being loaded onto lorries, over **800 sepoys** of the **5th Light Infantry**, joined by 100 men of the **Malay States Guides** Mule Battery under the leadership of **Jamadar Chisti Khan**, **Jamadar Abdul Gani**, and **Subedar Daud Khan**, mutinied. Seizing the regimental armoury, they divided into three columns: one marched to the **Tanglin Barracks** prisoner-of-war camp, shooting the British guards and throwing open the gates to invite the **300 interned German POWs** (including the crew of the cruiser *SMS Emden*) to lead them; the other two columns swept toward Singapore town, killing **32 British officers and 15 civilians** and holding the island in terror for nearly seven days. Because the German officers refused to assume command (fearing violation of their parole under the Hague Convention) and the mutineers lacked a unified political strategy to seize the telegraph and harbour fortifications, British Governor **Sir Arthur Young** radioed allied warships in the Straits. Troops landed from French (*Montcalm*), Russian (*Orel*), and Japanese (*Otowa* and *Tsushima*) cruisers alongside British volunteers to crush the mutiny. In the brutal aftermath, **47 mutineers were executed by public firing squad** outside Outram Road Prison before a crowd of 15,000 spectators, while **164 others** were sentenced to transportation or prison terms.

### 7. The Pan-Asian and Pan-Islamic Axis: The Kabul Provisional Government (1915) and the Silk Letter Conspiracy (1916)

Concurrently with the Pacific and Bengal fronts, the **Berlin Committee** and the **Ottoman Caliphate** opened a western overland revolutionary front across **Iran**, **Afghanistan**, and the **North-West Frontier Tribal Belt**, merging secular revolutionary nationalism with militant Pan-Islamic anti-imperialism.

#### The Provisional Government of India in Kabul (December 1, 1915)

In **April 1915**, the **Indo-German-Turkish Mission** (formally the **Niedermayer-Hentig Expedition**, led militarily by German officers **Captain Oskar von Niedermayer** and **Werner Otto von Hentig** and politically by **Raja Mahendra Pratap** of Hathras/Mursan, **Maulana Barkatullah**, and **Chempakaraman Pillai’s** associates) departed Berlin via Constantinople (where Sultan Mehmed V issued letters to Indian princes and the Amir of Afghanistan) and crossed the scorching deserts of Iran despite joint Anglo-Russian military cordons (the *East Persian Cordon*). Entering **Kabul** on **October 2, 1915**, they were received at the Bagh-e-Babur palace by **Amir Habibullah Khan** of Afghanistan and the anti-British court faction led by his brother **Nasrullah Khan** and son **Amanullah Khan** (later Amir).

In Kabul, the mission joined forces with **Maulana Ubaidullah Sindhi**—a revolutionary Sikh-convert scholar from the **Dar-ul-Uloom Deoband** who had been dispatched to Kabul in August **1915** by **Maulana Mahmud Hasan** (*Shaikh-ul-Hind*) to link the frontier Mujahideen tribes of **Yaghistan** (Asmas/Chamarkand) with the Afghan court. On **December 1, 1915** (celebrating **Raja Mahendra Pratap’s** birthday), they formally proclaimed the **Hukumat-i-Muwaqqata-i-Hind (Provisional Government of Free India in Exile)** at Kabul:

* **Cabinet Composition:** **Raja Mahendra Pratap** assumed office as **Life President** (serving until a democratic Congress elected a permanent head in liberated Delhi); **Maulana Barkatullah** (Ghadar Party and Berlin Committee) became **Prime Minister**; **Maulana Ubaidullah Sindhi** became **Minister for India (Home and Foreign Affairs)**; **Maulavi Muhammad Bashir** became **War Minister**; and **Champakaran Pillai** was named Foreign Minister abroad, with **Mathura Singh** and **Khuda Bakhsh** as plenipotentiaries.
* **Diplomatic Operations:** Operating as a sovereign state-in-exile, the **Kabul Provisional Government** negotiated a formal draft Indo-Afghan Treaty with **Amir Habibullah** promising Afghanistan territorial adjustments and subsidies in exchange for declaring war on British India. Furthermore, **Raja Mahendra Pratap** dispatched diplomatic missions to **Imperial Russia**—including afamous letter of greetings engraved on a solid **Gold Plate** sent to **Tsar Nicholas II** in **1916** via Tashkent (brought by **Mathura Singh** and **Mirza Muhammad Ali**, who were arrested by Tsarist authorities and handed over to the British, leading to **Mathura Singh’s** execution in Lahore). After the **1917 Bolshevik Revolution**, **Raja Mahendra Pratap** traveled personally to Petrograd and Moscow, meeting **Leon Trotsky** in **1918** and **Vladimir Lenin** in **1919** (where **Lenin** famously remarked that **Mahendra Pratap’s** comparative religion treatise *Religion of Love* was *"Tolstoyanism"*).

#### The Silk Letter Conspiracy (*Tehrek-e-Reshmi Rumal*, 1916)

Closely intertwined with the **Kabul Provisional Government** was the **Silk Letter Conspiracy (1916)**, engineered by the orthodox anti-colonial ulema of the **Dar-ul-Uloom Deoband** (which had retained its **1857** anti-British insurgency ethos).

* **Organizational Network:** **Maulana Mahmud Hasan** (*Shaikh-ul-Hind*, head mudarris at Deoband) established the **Jamiat-ul-Ansar** (**1909**) at Deoband and the **Nizarat-ul-Ma'arif** Quranic school at Delhi (**1913**) as ideological fronts to unite Deobandi traditionalists with Western-educated Aligarh youth. When World War I began, **Mahmud Hasan** sent his chief lieutenant **Maulana Ubaidullah Sindhi** to Kabul to organize the tribal frontier, while **Mahmud Hasan** himself departed India in **September 1915** for the **Hijaz (Mecca and Medina)** to secure a formal military alliance and proclamation of jihad (**Ghalibnama**) from the Ottoman Governor of Hijaz, **Ghalib Pasha**, and Ottoman War Minister **Enver Pasha**.
* **The Al-Junud-al-Rabbania (Army of God) and the Silk Letters:** In Kabul, **Ubaidullah Sindhi** and **Mahmud Hasan** designed a pan-Islamic military organization called **Al-Junud-al-Rabbania** (*The Army of God*), headquartered at Medina with **Mahmud Hasan** as General-in-Chief (*Al-Qaid*) and secondary command centers at Constantinople, Tehran, and Kabul (under **Ubaidullah Sindhi**), tasked with forging an alliance between Islamic kings and Indian revolutionaries to invade India across the Khyber Pass simultaneously with an internal uprising. On **July 9, 1916**, **Ubaidullah Sindhi** wrote three confidential letters neatly inscribed on **three lengths of yellow silk** (measuring $15 \times 10$ inches and $10 \times 8$ inches) addressed to **Maulana Mahmud Hasan** in Mecca, detailing the formation of the **Kabul Provisional Government**, the arrival of the German-Turkish mission, and the complete roster of **Army of God** generals and colonels inside India (including **Abul Kalam Azad**, **Hasrat Mohani**, and **Hakim Ajmal Khan**).
* **Betrayal at Multan and Malta Internment:** **Ubaidullah Sindhi** sewed the yellow silk letters inside the lining of the waist-coat of a trusted courier, **Abdul Haq** (a former Hindu boy from Multan converted to Islam), instructing him to deliver them to **Sheikh Abdur Rahim** in Hyderabad (Sindh) for onward transmission by Hajj pilgrim to Mecca. Passing through Multan in **August 1916**, **Abdul Haq** visited his former masters—the sons of **Khan Bahadur Rab Nawaz Khan**, who was personal assistant to the British Commissioner of Multan. Suspicious of his clothes, **Rab Nawaz Khan** ripped open the lining, extracted the **three yellow silk letters**, and handed them directly to the British Commissioner and Punjab Lieutenant-Governor **Sir Michael O’Dwyer**. The discovery triggered sweeping arrests across India, while in Mecca—where **Sharif Hussein** had just launched the British-backed Arab Revolt against the Ottomans in June **1916**—**Maulana Mahmud Hasan** and his associates (**Husain Ahmad Madani** and **Uzair Gul**) were arrested for refusing to sign a *fatwa* supporting the Sharif against the Ottoman Caliph, handed over to the British, and interned in a prisoner-of-war fortress on the island of **Malta** from **1917 to 1920**.

## III. Post-WWI Ideological Rupture, Socialist Realignment, and Phase II Militancy (1922–1935)

### 8. The HRA, *Bandi Jeevan*, and the Kakori Train Action (1924–1927)

Following the royal amnesty accompanying the **Government of India Act 1919**, surviving Phase I revolutionaries released from the **Cellular Jail** and provincial prisons—including **Sachindranath Sanyal**, **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee**, and **Barindra Ghosh**— initially suspended armed operations on the assurance of **Mahatma Gandhi** and **C.R. Das** that the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)** would achieve *Swaraj* within one year. Thousands of radical youth enthusiastically joined the **1920–22** mass upsurge. Consequently, when **Gandhi** unilaterally called off the movement at **Bardoli in February 1922** following the **Chauri Chaura** incident, a profound ideological crisis gripped the younger nationalist generation. Rejecting both the Gandhian fetishization of absolute non-violence (*Ahimsa*) and the parliamentary council-entry constitutionalism of the **Swarajists**, young cadres in the **United Provinces**, **Punjab**, and **Bengal** began a fundamental structural reassessment of revolutionary strategy.

Crucially, this post-**1922** regrouping occurred under the direct ideological impact of three transformative forces:
1. The triumph of the **1917 Russian Bolshevik Revolution** and the establishment of the **Soviet Union**, which demonstrated that an underground revolutionary vanguard could overthrow an autocratic empire by mobilizing workers and peasants;
2. The rise of an organized industrial **working-class trade union movement** (**AITUC**, founded **1920**) and early Marxist circles in Bombay, Calcutta, Cawnpore, and Lahore (**S.A. Dange**, **Muzaffar Ahmad**, **Singaravelu Chettiar**, **Ghulam Hussain**); and
3. The realization that Phase I secret societies had failed because they relied on Hindu religio-mystical oaths that alienated Muslims and treated the masses as passive spectators rather than active revolutionary agents.

#### Founding of the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA, October 1924) and *Bandi Jeevan*

The bridge between Phase I underground networks and Phase II socialist republicanism was **Sachindranath Sanyal** (released from the **Cellular Jail** in **1920**). In **1922**, **Sanyal** published his monumental Bengali/Hindi autobiographical treatise ***Bandi Jeevan*** (*A Life of Captivity*), which chronicled the **1912–15** **Rash Behari Bose–Ghadar** conspiracy and life inside the **Andaman Cellular Jail**, serving as the foundational textbook for every northern Indian revolutionary of the **1920s**.

* **The Cawnpore Conference (October 1924):** In **October 1924**, **Sachindranath Sanyal**, **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee** (dispatched from the **Dacca Anushilan Samiti** to reorganize the UP underground), and **Ramprasad Bismil** (the fearless poet-revolutionary of Shahjahanpur who had previously organized the **Matrivedi** society involved in the **1918 Mainpuri Conspiracy Case**), joined by young recruits **Chandrashekhar Azad**, **Sachendra Nath Bakshi**, and **Rajendra Nath Lahiri**, convened in **Cawnpore (Kanpur)** to found the **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)** (along with its armed wing, the **Hindustan Republican Army**).
* **Constitutional Architecture and *The Revolutionary* Manifesto (January 1, 1925):** Drafted by **Sachin Sanyal** (known as the *"Yellow Paper Constitution"* after a copy seized from **Jogesh Chatterjee** in Calcutta), the **HRA Constitution** declared its explicit objective: ***"to establish a Federated Republic of the United States of India by an organized and armed revolution."*** Between January 28 and 31, **1925**, the **HRA** simultaneously distributed its four-page English manifesto ***The Revolutionary*** (written by **Sanyal** under the signature *"Vijaykumar, President, Central Council, R.P. of India"*) across every major town from Peshawar to Rangoon. While retaining slight Neo-Vedantic philosophical undertones from **Sanyal’s** background, ***The Revolutionary*** marked a historic break toward socialism:
  1. The future **Federated Republic** would be founded on **universal adult suffrage**;
  2. All basic industries, railways, shipping, and mines enjoying public utility status would be **nationalized** by the legislature;
  3. It proclaimed the ***"abolition of all systems which make the exploitation of man by man possible"***; and
  4. It called for organizing **labourers and peasants** alongside educated youth.

#### The Kakori Train Action (August 9, 1925) and Martyrdom of the HRA Leadership

After **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee** and **Sachin Sanyal** were arrested in Bengal in late **1924** and early **1925**, operational command of the **HRA** in the United Provinces passed to **Ramprasad Bismil** and **Ashfaqulla Khan** (also of Shahjahanpur—whose unshakeable Hindu-Muslim brotherhood with Arya Samajist **Bismil** became legendary across India). Initially, **Bismil’s** units executed rural dacoities at **Bichpuri**, **Bamrauli**, and **Dwarkapur** to fund arms procurement from German smugglers via Calcutta, but **Bismil** and **Ashfaqulla** grew deeply troubled that raiding village wealthy houses killed innocent Indians and alienated public sympathy.

* **The August 9, 1925 Treasury Robbery:** At an emergency meeting in Shahjahanpur on August 8, **1925**, **Ramprasad Bismil** resolved that the **HRA** would henceforth seize only **British government treasury funds**. On the evening of **August 9, 1925**, ten **HRA** revolutionaries—**Ramprasad Bismil**, **Ashfaqulla Khan**, **Chandrashekhar Azad**, **Rajendra Nath Lahiri**, **Sachindra Nath Bakshi**, **Manmath Nath Gupta**, **Mukundi Lal**, **Keshab Chakravarty**, **Murari Lal Gupta**, and **Banwari Lal**—boarded the **8-Down Saharanpur–Lucknow Passenger Train** at **Kakori** station (14 km north of Lucknow). Shortly after the train departed Kakori, **Rajendra Lahiri** pulled the alarm chain in the second-class compartment; as the train ground to a halt in the darkness, **Azad** and **Manmath Nath Gupta** neutralized the British guards while **Ashfaqulla Khan** smashed open the heavy iron railway cash safe in the guard’s van with a sledgehammer, seizing leather bags containing **Rs 4,679 in government railway revenue** before vanishing into the Lucknow mango groves (during the firing to keep passengers inside compartments, one passenger, Ahmad Ali, was accidentally killed by a stray bullet from **Manmath Nath Gupta’s** Mauser pistol).
* **The Kakori Conspiracy Trial and Executions (1925–1927):** Stung by this audacious direct strike on state revenue, the colonial government appointed **R.A. Horton** to head a massive special CID task force. On September 26, **1925**, police raided 40 locations across northern India, arresting **Ramprasad Bismil** and dozens of comrades; **Rajendra Lahiri** was captured at a bomb factory in **Dakshineswar** (Calcutta) in November **1925**, and **Ashfaqulla Khan** was betrayed and arrested in Delhi in December **1926** as he prepared to leave for engineering studies and revolutionary work in the Soviet Union. Only **Chandrashekhar Azad** eluded the police dragnet completely. Despite a spirited defense committee organized by **Govind Ballabh Pant**, **Chandra Bhanu Gupta**, and **Motilal Nehru**, the Special Sessions Court at Lucknow delivered ruthless sentences in **1927**:
  * **December 17, 1927:** **Rajendra Nath Lahiri** was hanged at **Gonda Jail** (two days ahead of schedule to prevent a suspected rescue raid by **Azad**).
  * **December 19, 1927:** **Ramprasad Bismil** (immortal creator of the Urdu patriotic ghazal *Sarfaroshi ki Tamanna ab Hamaare Dil Mein Hai*, authored originally by **Bismil Azimabadi** of Patna and popularized by **Ramprasad Bismil** in court) was hanged at **Gorakhpur Jail**; **Ashfaqulla Khan** (the first recorded Muslim revolutionary martyr hanged for conspiracy in Phase II, who went to the gallows kissing the rope with the Quran hung around his neck, declaring *"I have no soiled hands with human blood; our struggle was to free the Motherland"*) was hanged at **Faizabad Jail**; and **Thakur Roshan Singh** was hanged at **Naini Central Jail, Allahabad**.
  * **Sachindranath Sanyal**, **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee**, **Sachindra Nath Bakshi**, and **Govind Charan Kar** were sentenced to transportation for life, while **Manmath Nath Gupta** (spared the gallows solely due to being 17 years old) received 14 years’ rigorous imprisonment.

### 9. The HSRA Synthesis: Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Vanguard Marxism (1926–1931)

The **Kakori** hangings decimated the older generation of **HRA** leaders, passing the mantle to **Chandrashekhar Azad** (then operating underground from Jhansi) and a brilliant new cohort of Punjabi and UP student intellectuals led by **Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev Thapar**, **Bhagwati Charan Vohra**, **Bejoy Kumar Sinha**, **Shiv Verma**, **Jaidev Kapur**, and **Yashpal**. Educated at **Lala Lajpat Rai’s** **National College in Lahore** (founded during Non-Cooperation, where Professors **Jai Chandra Vidyalankar** and **Chhabil Das** taught the histories of the French, Irish, and Russian revolutions), **Bhagat Singh** and his comrades devoured the works of **Karl Marx**, **Friedrich Engels**, **Vladimir Lenin**, **Leon Trotsky**, **Mikhail Bakunin**, and **Prince Kropotkin** at the **Dwarkadas Library** in Lahore.

#### Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926) and the Ferozeshah Kotla Founding of the HSRA (September 1928)

Before reconstructing the armed underground, **Bhagat Singh** recognized that a revolutionary vanguard required an open mass platform to educate youth, peasants, and workers in secular scientific socialism.

* **Naujawan Bharat Sabha (March 1926):** In **March 1926**, **Bhagat Singh** (as General Secretary), along with **Ram Krishan** (President), **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** (Propaganda Secretary), **Sukhdev**, and **Dhanwantri**, founded the **Naujawan Bharat Sabha (Youth Society of India)** in Lahore, modeled externally on **Mazzini’s** *Young Italy* and internally on Bolshevik mass fronts. The **Sabha** opened branches across Punjab districts, collaborated closely with the **Kirti Kisan Party** (founded by **Sohan Singh Josh** and **Santokh Singh** around the Marxist Punjabi journal ***Kirti*** in Amritsar, to which **Bhagat Singh** contributed under the pseudonym *Vidrohi*), organized magic-lantern slide lectures on **Kartar Singh Sarabha** and **Madan Lal Dhingra**, and held public **inter-caste and inter-religious community dinners** combining pork and beef dishes to smash Hindu-Muslim-Sikh communalism and untouchability at a stroke.
* **The Ferozeshah Kotla Conclave (September 8–9, 1928):** On **September 8 and 9, 1928**, ten young revolutionary delegates from Punjab, the United Provinces, Bihar, and Rajputana—**Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev**, **Bejoy Kumar Sinha**, **Shiv Verma**, **Jaidev Kapur**, **Surendranath Pandey**, **Brahm Dutt Mishra**, **Phanindranath Ghosh**, **Manmohan Banerjee**, and **Kundan Lal**—gathered secretly amidst the medieval ruins of **Ferozeshah Kotla** in **Delhi** (while **Chandrashekhar Azad** remained outside for security). At **Bhagat Singh’s** insistence, the delegates unanimously voted to insert the word **"Socialist"** into the party’s name, transforming the **HRA** into the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)** (with its military arm designated the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Army**).
  * **Organizational Structure:** A seven-member **Central Committee** was established alongside separated political and military wings; **Chandrashekhar Azad** was appointed **Commander-in-Chief** in absentia; **Bhagat Singh** and **Bejoy Kumar Sinha** were made inter-provincial coordinators; and **Sukhdev**, **Shiv Verma**, **Phanindranath Ghosh**, and **Kundan Lal** were appointed provincial organizers for Punjab, UP, Bihar, and Rajputana respectively.
  * **Ideological Rupture:** The **Ferozeshah Kotla** resolutions formally abolished all religious oaths and rituals, excluded members of communal organizations from the party, decided to stop robbing private citizens, and declared **Scientific Socialism**—the capture of state power by a **dictatorship of the proletariat and peasantry** to abolish both British imperialism and indigenous capitalist/landlord exploitation—as the official goal of the revolution.

#### The Saunders Assassination (December 17, 1928)

The **HSRA’s** first major action was forced upon it by colonial police brutality during the **1928 anti-Simon Commission boycotts**. On **October 30, 1928**, when the all-white **Simon Commission** arrived at Lahore Railway Station, the sixty-three-year-old veteran nationalist **Lala Lajpat Rai** (*Sher-e-Punjab*) led a massive, peaceful black-flag demonstration organized jointly with the **Naujawan Bharat Sabha**. On the orders of Lahore Superintendent of Police **James A. Scott**, police officers—including Assistant Superintendent **John Poyantz Saunders**—savagely beat **Lala Lajpat Rai** across the chest and head with iron-tipped *lathis*. That evening at Mori Gate, **Lajpat Rai** declared: *"Every blow aimed at me today is a nail in the coffin of British Imperialism."* When **Lajpat Rai** died of his injuries on **November 17, 1928**, **Basanti Devi** (widow of **C.R. Das**) issued an anguished public challenge asking whether the youth of India had become so dead that a national leader could be beaten to death by a petty British police officer with impunity.

Meeting in Lahore on December 10, **1928**, the **HSRA** Central Committee resolved to execute **James Scott** to redeem national honour. On the afternoon of **December 17, 1928**, **Bhagat Singh**, **Shivaram Rajguru** (a crack marksman from Khed, Poona, who had studied Sanskrit in Benares before joining the **HSRA**), and **Chandrashekhar Azad** took positions outside the District Police Headquarters on Court Road, Lahore, while **Jai Gopal** acted as spotter. Mistaking the twenty-one-year-old Assistant Superintendent **John P. Saunders** for **Scott** as he rode out of the gate on his red motorcycle, **Jai Gopal** gave the signal: **Rajguru** fired a single shot that knocked **Saunders** off his machine, and **Bhagat Singh** ran forward and emptied several bullets from his automatic pistol into **Saunders** to ensure his death. As they retreated through the DAV College compound pursued by Head Constable **Chanan Singh**, **Chandrashekhar Azad** shot **Chanan Singh** with his Mauser pistol to cover their escape. The next morning, pink handbills signed by **"Balraj, Commander-in-Chief, HSRA"** were pasted across Lahore’s walls:
> ***"Notice: Saunders is Dead; Lala Lajpat Rai is Avenged! ... Sorry for the bloodshed of a human being; but the sacrifice of individuals at the altar of the Revolution that will bring freedom to all and make the exploitation of man by man impossible, is inevitable."***

To slip through the tight police cordon sealing Lahore, **Durga Devi Vohra (*Durga Bhabhi*, wife of Bhagwati Charan Vohra)** bravely posed as the aristocratic Westernized wife of a cleanly-shaven, suit-and-hat-clad **Bhagat Singh** (carrying her infant son Sachindra), boarding the first-class compartment of the Calcutta Mail on December 20, **1928**, with **Rajguru** disguised as their family servant and **Azad** traveling in another coach rigged as a Hindu *panda* pilgrim. In Calcutta (where **Congress** was holding its December **1928** session), **Bhagat Singh** connected with Bengali revolutionary veteran **Jatindranath Das**, bringing him back to **Agra** and **Lahore** to manufacture high-grade bomb casings and train **HSRA** cadres.

#### The Central Legislative Assembly Bombing (April 8, 1929)

By early **1929**, the colonial state launched a two-pronged legislative and judicial offensive to crush the militant labour movement (which had just staged the massive six-month **1928 Girni Kamgar Union strike** in Bombay textile mills): in March **1929**, police arrested 31 top trade union and communist leaders across India in the **Meerut Conspiracy Case**, while in the **Central Legislative Assembly** in New Delhi, the government sought to enact two draconian statutes—the **Public Safety Bill** (empowering the executive to deport foreign socialist organizers and confiscate funds without trial) and the **Trade Disputes Bill** (outlawing sympathetic strikes and strikes in public utility services). When the elected Indian majority led by Speaker **Vitthalbhai Patel** and **Motilal Nehru** repeatedly blocked the **Public Safety Bill**, Viceroy **Lord Irwin** prepared to certify and enact the bills by arbitrary executive ordinance.

Inspired by the French anarchist **Auguste Vaillant** (who had thrown a bomb into the French Chamber of Deputies in **1893** declaring *"It takes a loud voice to make the deaf hear"*), **Bhagat Singh** convinced the **HSRA** that instead of escaping after an action, two revolutionaries should throw deliberately low-intensity, non-lethal smoke bombs onto the empty floor of the **Central Legislative Assembly** and voluntarily surrender, turning the subsequent trial into a nationwide megaphone to broadcast the **HSRA’s** socialist program. Overruling the Central Committee’s initial reluctance to lose **Bhagat Singh**, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** (of Burdwan/Cawnpore) were selected for the mission.

* **The April 8, 1929 Action:** At **12:30 PM on April 8, 1929**, inside the **Central Legislative Assembly Chamber** in New Delhi, just as Speaker **Vitthalbhai Patel** rose to deliver his ruling declaring the **Public Safety Bill** out of order (and immediately after the government forced through the **Trade Disputes Bill**), **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** stood up in the Visitors’ Gallery and threw two low-grade bombs (specifically engineered by **Jatin Das** and **Shiv Verma** with potassium chlorate and picric acid in weak cast-iron shells) onto the vacant floor space behind the Treasury Benches where **Sir George Schuster** (Finance Member) and **Bomanji Dalal** sat. As white smoke filled the chamber and members fled in panic, **Bhagat Singh** fired two shots into the air from his automatic pistol (the exact weapon used to kill **Saunders**), showered hundreds of red leaflets onto the floor, and thundered the slogans that instantly transformed the vocabulary of Indian nationalism:
  > ***"Inquilab Zindabad!" (Long Live the Revolution!)***
  > ***"Samrajyavad ka Nash Ho!" (Down with Imperialism!)***
  > ***"Duniya ke Mazdooro Ek Ho!" (Workers of the World, Unite!)***
* **The Red Leaflet and Courtroom Statement (June 6, 1929):** The red leaflet opened with **Vaillant’s** aphorism: *"It takes a loud voice to make the deaf hear."* Refusing to flee, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** laid down their pistol and surrendered quietly to Sergeant Terry. During their trial before the Delhi Sessions Judge in **June 1929** (defended formally by **Asaf Ali**), **Bhagat Singh** and **B.K. Dutt** read out a historic joint manifesto (drafted by **Bhagat Singh**) defining the scientific meaning of **Revolution (*Inquilab*)**:
  > *"Revolution does not necessarily involve sanguinary strife, nor is there any place in it for individual vendetta. It is not the cult of the bomb and the pistol. By 'Revolution' we mean that the present order of things, which is based on manifest injustice, must change. Producers or labourers, in spite of being the most necessary element of society, are robbed by their exploiters of the fruits of their labour and deprived of their elementary rights. The peasant who grows corn for all, starves with his family; the weaver who supplies the world market with textile fabrics, has not enough to cover his own and his children's bodies... This state of affairs cannot last; a radical change is therefore necessary, and it is the duty of those who realize it to reorganize society on the socialistic basis."*

#### The 63-Day Hunger Strike of Jatin Das, *Philosophy of the Bomb*, and the 1931 Martyrdoms

Sentenced to transportation for life in the **Assembly Bomb Case** on June 12, **1929**, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** were immediately transferred to Mianwali and Lahore jails, where police raids on **HSRA** bomb factories in **Lahore (Kashmiri Building, April 15, 1929)** and **Saharanpur (May 1929)** led to the arrest of **Sukhdev**, **Shiv Verma**, **Jaidev Kapur**, **Kishori Lal**, **Gaya Prasad**, **Jatin Das**, **Ajoy Ghosh**, and **Rajguru** (captured in Poona). With **Jai Gopal** and **Phanindranath Ghosh** turning Crown approvers, the state launched the **Second Lahore Conspiracy Case (1929–1930)**.

* **The 1929 Lahore Jail Hunger Strike and Martyrdom of Jatin Das (September 13, 1929):** Inside prison, **Bhagat Singh** and **B.K. Dutt** launched an indefinite hunger strike in **June 1929** (joined by **Jatin Das**, **Sukhdev**, **Shiv Verma**, and all Lahore Conspiracy prisoners in July) to abolish racial discrimination inside colonial jails and secure official recognition as **"Political Prisoners"**—demanding clean edible food, soap, newspapers, political books, exemption from humiliating convict labour, and equal treatment with European prisoners. When jail authorities attempted brutal nasal force-feeding with rubber tubes, **Jatindranath Das’s** lungs were punctured by milk entering his windpipe. Refusing all medical bail or compromise, **Jatin Das** died at **1:05 PM on September 13, 1929**, on the **63rd day of his hunger strike** at Borstal Jail, Lahore. His body was carried by train from Lahore to Calcutta, greeted by hundreds of thousands at every station—with **Jawaharlal Nehru** receiving the cortege at Allahabad and **Subhas Chandra Bose** Paying Rs 600 to bring the body to Calcutta, where **600,000 mourners** marched in his funeral procession, prompting Irish republicans (the family of **Terence MacSwiney**, who had died after a 74-day hunger strike in Brixton Prison in 1920) to wire their salute: *"Family of Terence MacSwiney have heard with grief and pride of the death of Jatin Das. Freedom will come."*
* ***The Philosophy of the Bomb* (January 1930) and *Why I Am an Atheist* (October 1930):** Outside prison, **Chandrashekhar Azad**, **Bhagwati Charan Vohra**, and **Yashpal** kept the **HSRA** active. On December 23, **1929**, **Yashpal** detonated a bomb under the Viceroy’s special train near Purana Qila, New Delhi. When **Mahatma Gandhi** narrowly condemned this act in an article titled *"The Cult of the Bomb"* in *Young India* (January 2, **1930**) and pushed a resolution through the **1929 Lahore Congress** criticizing the revolutionaries, **Bhagwati Charan Vohra**—in consultation with **Chandrashekhar Azad**—authored the brilliant counter-manifesto ***The Philosophy of the Bomb*** (distributed underground across India on **January 26, 1930**, Independence Day). **Vohra** dismantled the false binary between violence and non-violence, explaining that the **HSRA** did not worship violence for its own sake (*"Terrorism is a phase, a necessary inevitable phase of the revolution; Terrorism is not the complete revolution and the revolution is not complete without terrorism"*), criticized **Gandhi’s** 1922 Bardoli retreat, and declared that the revolutionaries sought to overthrow both foreign and Indian capitalism to establish the **Dictatorship of the Proletariat**. Tragically, on **May 28, 1930**, **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** died in the **Ravi River** forests near Lahore when a bomb he was testing for an audacious armed raid to rescue **Bhagat Singh** from prison exploded prematurely in his hands.
* Meanwhile, inside his condemned cell at Lahore Central Jail in **October 1930**, the twenty-three-year-old **Bhagat Singh** authored his celebrated philosophical treatise ***Why I Am an Atheist*** (in response to veteran Ghadarite **Baba Randhir Singh**, who had accused him of vanity for refusing to pray before death), alongside four Prison Notebooks filled with extracts from **Marx**, **Lenin**, **Trotsky**, and **Bukharin**, and his final political testament ***To Young Political Workers* (February 2, 1931)**. In *Why I Am an Atheist*, **Bhagat Singh** subjected all religious teleology and divine determinism to rigorous historical-materialist critique, asserting that a true revolutionary needed no mystical opiate of rebirth or afterlife to face the gallows with serene conviction.
* **Azad’s Martyrdom (February 27, 1931) and the March 23, 1931 Executions:** Because the **HSRA** defendants turned the open Magistrate’s court into a daily political theater—entering in handcuffs singing *Sarfaroshi ki Tamanna* and *Mera Rang De Basanti Chola*, celebrating **Lenin Day** (January 21, **1930**) by wearing red scarves and wiring greetings to the **Third International**, and exposing police torture—Viceroy **Lord Irwin** promulgated **Ordinance No. III of 1930 (May 1, 1930)** constituting an *ex-parte* **Special Tribunal** that dispensed with the presence of the accused, witnesses, defense lawyers, and press reporting. On **October 7, 1930**, the Tribunal sentenced **Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev Thapar**, and **Shivaram Rajguru** to death by hanging, and sentenced **Shiv Verma**, **Kishori Lal**, **Gaya Prasad**, **Jaidev Kapur**, **Bejoy Kumar Sinha**, **Mahabir Singh**, and **Kamalnath Tiwari** to transportation for life.
  * On **February 27, 1931**, while meeting **Sukhdev Raj** at **Alfred Park (Company Garden) in Allahabad** ( short days after meeting **Jawaharlal Nehru** at Anand Bhavan to discuss the impending **Gandhi-Irwin Pact**), **Chandrashekhar Azad** was betrayed by an informer and surrounded by a heavy police force led by SSP **Sir John Nott-Bower** and DSP **Bisheshwar Singh**. Wounded in the thigh after firing from behind a jamun tree to allow **Sukhdev Raj** to escape, **Azad** pinned down the police for over thirty minutes, wounding **Nott-Bower** in the wrist and **Bisheshwar Singh** in the jaw, before firing his **last remaining Colt bullet** into his own right temple—fulfilling his lifelong vow that the British police would never touch **"Azad" (The Free)** alive.
  * Despite massive public petitions and **Bhagat Singh’s** own letter to the Punjab Governor demanding that as prisoners of war they be **shot by a military firing squad** rather than hanged like common criminals, the colonial state carried out the executions at **7:30 PM on March 23, 1931** inside **Lahore Central Jail**—eleven hours ahead of the scheduled morning execution of March 24, on the very eve of the **Karachi Congress Session**—secretly chopping and burning the bodies of **Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev**, and **Rajguru** on the banks of the **Sutlej River at Hussainiwala**.

### 10. The Bengal Militant Resurgence: Surya Sen’s Chittagong Armoury Raid and Female Revolutionary Agency (1930–1934)

While the **HSRA** transformed revolutionary politics across northern India, Bengal witnessed a dramatic tactical and social metamorphosis between **1928** and **1934**. Released from detention after the **1924 Bengal Criminal Law Amendment Ordinance** expired, a younger generation of cadres within both the **Anushilan** and **Jugantar** networks rejected the old factionalism of their elders (**Dadaism**), forming **"Revolt Groups"** (*Advance Groups*) modeled directly on the **1916 Easter Rising in Dublin** and the **Irish Republican Army (IRA)** led by **Michael Collins** and **Dan Breen** (whose memoir *My Fight for Irish Freedom* was translated and read by every Bengali revolutionary). At the **December 1928 Calcutta Congress Session**, **Subhas Chandra Bose** organized a 2,000-strong uniformed **Bengal Volunteer Corps** (with **Jatin Das** as Major), out of which emerged the clandestine action group **Bengal Volunteers (BV)** led by **Hemchandra Ghosh** and **Bhupendra Kishore Rakshit-Roy**, alongside **Surya Sen’s** autonomous **Chittagong** organization and **Bina Das’s** all-women **Chhatri Sangha**.

#### Surya Sen (*Masterda*) and the Chittagong Armoury Raid (April 18, 1930)

The pinnacle of Phase II tactical military planning was the **Chittagong Armoury Raid** of **April 18, 1930**, planned and executed by **Surya Sen** (**1894–1934**), affectionately revered across Chittagong as ***Masterda*** (a mathematics teacher at the National School in Nandankanan and Secretary of the Chittagong District Congress Committee). Having participated in the **1920–22 Non-Cooperation Movement** before serving two years in detention (**1926–28**), **Surya Sen** built an extraordinary revolutionary core around five towering lieutenants (**"The Five Knights"**: **Ananta Singh**, **Ganesh Ghosh**, **Lokenath Bal**, **Nirmal Sen**, and **Ambika Chakrabarty**) and dozens of teenaged schoolboys (**Subodh Roy**, **Tegra [Harigopal] Bal**, **Naresh Roy**, **Ardhendu Dastidar**).

* **The Synchronized Military Operation (April 18, 1930 – Good Friday, 10:00 PM):** Operating under the formal banner of the **Indian Republican Army (IRA), Chittagong Branch**—deliberately commemorating the anniversary of the **1916 Dublin Easter Rising**—**Surya Sen** divided **65 khaki-uniformed revolutionaries** into five synchronized tactical columns striking simultaneously at **10:00 PM on April 18, 1930**:
  1. **Police Armoury Column (Led by Surya Sen, Ananta Singh, and Ganesh Ghosh):** Stormed the main Police Lines Armoury on Dampara hill, overwhelmed the sentries, broke open the armoury doors to seize hundreds of muskets and revolvers, soaked the building in petrol, hoisted the **Indian National Flag**, blew a bugle salute, and **proclaimed a Provisional Revolutionary Government** with **Surya Sen** as President.
  2. **Auxiliary Force of India (AFI) Armoury Column (Led by Lokenath Bal and Nirmal Sen):** Arrived in motor cars at Pahartali, with the fair-skinned **Lokenath Bal** dressed in a British officer’s uniform to deceive the sentry; shot dead Sergeant-Major Farrell and guards, and seized **Lewis machine guns**, **.303 magazine rifles**, and Colt revolvers. However, in the single critical tactical setback of the night, the revolutionaries failed to locate the separate bricked-up **ammunition magazine room** adjacent to the AFI Armoury, leaving them with advanced .303 rifles and Lewis guns but no matching .303 cartridges (forcing them to burn the AFI Armoury with petrol, during which **Himangshu Sen** was severely burned, prompting **Ananta Singh** and **Ganesh Ghosh** to drive away to tend to him, inadvertently splitting the force).
  3. **Telegraph and Telephone Exchange Column (Led by Ambika Chakrabarty):** Stormed the Central Telegraph and Telephone Office, chloroformed the telephone operator, smashed the switchboards with iron sledgehammers, and set the building ablaze, completely severing Chittagong’s telegraphic and telephonic communications with Calcutta and Dacca.
  4. **Railway Demolition Squads (Dhoom and Laksam):** Two sabotage teams traveled forty miles up the Assam-Bengal Railway line to **Dhoom (near Feni)** and **Laksam**, removing railway fishplates, derailing a goods train across the main track, and cutting overhead telegraph wires to prevent troop reinforcements from reaching Chittagong by rail.
  5. **European Club Column (Led by Naresh Roy):** Marched on the Chittagong Club to take British civilian and military officers hostage, only to find the club deserted because it was **Good Friday** night.
* **The Battle of Jalalabad Hills (April 22, 1930) and Four-Year Guerrilla Warfare:** Because British authorities managed to transmit an emergency radio signal via a wireless set aboard a ship anchored at Chittagong port (*SS Kistna*), troops of the **Eastern Frontier Rifles** and **Gurkha Rifles** arrived by sea within four days. Retreating with 57 young fighters armed only with single-shot police muskets into the scrub-covered ridges of the **Jalalabad Hills** north of Chittagong, **Surya Sen** and field commander **Lokenath Bal** fought a pitched, three-hour conventional battle on the afternoon of **April 22, 1930**, repelling wave after wave of British-led Gurkha and Jat infantry assaults and inflicting over 80 casualties on the Crown forces before darkness fell. Twelve teenaged revolutionaries—beginning with fourteen-year-old **Harigopal Bal (*Tegra*)**, **Naresh Roy**, **Tripura Sen**, **Bidhu Bhushan Bhattacharya**, and **Matilal Kanungo**—fell martyred on **Jalalabad Hill**.
* Rather than fighting a static last stand, **Surya Sen** dissolved the force into small mobile guerrilla squads across the villages of Chittagong district. For nearly **four years (1930–1934)**, **Masterda** and his core cadres waged an unprecedented rural guerrilla campaign—fighting the **Battle of Kalarpole**, the **Chandernagore gun battle** (where **Jiban Ghoshal [Makhan]** was killed and **Lokenath Bal** and **Ganesh Ghosh** were captured by Police Commissioner Charles Tegart in September **1930**), the **Dhalghat encounter** (June **1932**, where **Nirmal Sen** and **Apurba Sen** died covering **Surya Sen** and **Pritilata Waddedar’s** escape after killing Captain Cameron), and the **Gairala** and **Gahira** battles—sustained entirely by the shelter of poor Hindu and **Muslim peasants** (such as **Abdul Sattar** and **Mir Ahmed**), despite punitive collective fines, village burnings, and the deployment of entire military brigades across Chittagong. Betrayed at **Gairala village** by Netra Sen on **February 16, 1933** (Netra Sen was subsequently beheaded with a *dao* machete in front of his wife by revolutionary **Kiran Sen** on January 8, **1934**, the night before **Masterda’s** hanging), **Surya Sen** and **Tarakeshwar Dastidar** (who had taken over leadership after **Masterda’s** arrest and was captured with **Kalpana Dutt** at Gahira in May **1933**) were tried by a Special Tribunal. After brutal torture inside **Chittagong District Jail**—where British warders smashed **Surya Sen’s** teeth and finger joints with hammers and pulled out his nails—**Surya Sen (*Masterda*)** and **Tarakeshwar Dastidar** were hanged at midnight on **January 12, 1934**, and their bodies were weighted with iron and dumped into the Bay of Bengal from the cruiser *HMS Renown*. In his final letter smuggled out of his death cell (*"To My Followers"*), **Masterda** wrote: *"Ideal and Unity is my farewell message... Never forget the 18th of April 1930, the day of the Eastern Rebellion in Chittagong. Write in red letters in the core of your hearts the names of the patriots who have sacrificed their lives at the altar of India's freedom."*

#### Female Revolutionary Agency: Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Dutt, Santi Ghosh, Suniti Chaudhuri, and Bina Das

A defining structural breakthrough of the **1930–1934** Bengal militant resurgence was the transformation of **women’s agency**. In Phase I, women had been restricted to supportive domestic roles—sheltering fugitives, carrying messages, or hiding weapons—while simultaneously serving as passive symbolic icons of *Bharat Mata* or *Shakti*. By the late **1920s**, driven by expanding women’s university education (**Bethune College**, **Diocesan College**, and **Calcutta University**) and organized through **Leela Nag’s (Roy)** **Deepali Sangha** in Dacca (**1923**) and **Sucheta Kripalani / Kalyani Das’s** **Chhatri Sangha** in Calcutta (**1928**), young educated Bengali women demanded equal admission into the armed frontlines as combat commanders, bomb-makers, and assassins:

* **Pritilata Waddedar (1911–1932):** The daughter of a municipal clerk in Chittagong, **Pritilata Waddedar** graduated with distinction in Philosophy from **Bethune College, Calcutta** (her degree was withheld by the British University authorities until posthumously conferred in 2012) and returned to Chittagong as Headmistress of the **Nandankanan Aparna Charan English Girls’ High School**. Initiated into **Surya Sen’s** underground after meeting **Ramkrishna Biswas** in Alipore Jail (wearing forty disguises as his cousin before he was hanged for killing Inspector Tarini Mukherjee at Chandpur), **Pritilata** narrowly escaped with **Masterda** during the **June 1932 Dhalghat firefight**. On the night of **September 24, 1932**, dressed in Punjabi male attire (salwar, tahmat, and turban), twenty-one-year-old **Pritilata Waddedar** led an eight-member **IRA** combat squad (**Kalikinkar Dey**, **Bireshwar Roy**, **Prafulla Das**, **Shanti Chakrabarty**, **Mahendra Chowdhury**, **Sushil Dey**, and **Panna Sen**) in a direct armed assault on the **Pahartali European Club** in Chittagong (which notoriously bore a signboard reading *"Dogs and Indians Not Allowed"*). Throwing a bomb through the window into the ballroom where forty European railway officials and police officers were dancing and opening fire with pistols and rifles, **Pritilata’s** squad killed one European woman (Mrs. Sullivan) and wounded eleven others. Struck in the chest by a retaliatory revolver shot from a military officer as the squad withdrew outside the gate, **Pritilata** ordered her seven male comrades to retreat, handed her revolver to them, and **swallowed a capsule of potassium cyanide** to avoid capture by the British police. Found in her pocket was a printed revolutionary manifesto in her own handwriting calling upon the women of India to fight shoulder-to-shoulder with men and break the sex barrier in the armed struggle for liberation.
* **Kalpana Dutt (Joshi) (1913–1995):** A science student at **Bethune College** and member of the **Chhatri Sangha** who returned to Chittagong after the **April 1930 Armoury Raid**, **Kalpana Dutt** became the master chemist and courier of **Surya Sen’s** underground. In **May 1931**, she engineered the **Dynamite Conspiracy**—planting gun-cotton explosives fused with electric batteries underneath the Chittagong Courthouse and inside the jail walls to blow free **Ananta Singh** and **Ganesh Ghosh** during their trial (a plot discovered just hours before detonation). Going underground in male disguise alongside **Pritilata Waddedar**, **Kalpana** fought alongside **Surya Sen** when he was captured at **Gairala** in February **1933**, escaped through police gunfire, and was finally captured after a fierce gun-battle at **Gahira Beach** on **May 19, 1933** alongside **Tarakeshwar Dastidar**. Tried in the **Second Supplementary Chittagong Armoury Raid Case**, **Kalpana Dutt** was spared the gallows due to her age and gender and sentenced to **transportation for life** (released in **1939** after campaigns by **Rabindranath Tagore**, **C.F. Andrews**, and **Mahatma Gandhi**, subsequently joining the **Communist Party of India**, conducting relief work during the **1943 Bengal Famine**, and marrying CPI General Secretary **P.C. Joshi**).
* **Santi Ghosh and Suniti Chaudhuri (December 14, 1931):** In **Comilla (Tippera District)**, two eighth-class schoolgirls of Faizunnisa Girls’ High School—fifteen-year-old **Santi Ghosh** and fourteen-year-old **Suniti Chaudhuri**—were recruited through the *Chhatri Sangha* into the **Jugantar** group led by **Akhil Chandra Nandy** and trained in revolver shooting at Mainamati hills. On **December 14, 1931**, **Santi** and **Suniti** entered the bungalow of the tyrannical District Magistrate of Tippera, **Charles Geoffrey Buckland Stevens** (who had been using emergency ordinances to crush civil disobedience and student groups), under the pretext of submitting a petition to organize a girls’ swimming competition across the Gumti River. As **Stevens** stepped onto the veranda to sign their petition, **Santi Ghosh** and **Suniti Chaudhuri** drew two revolvers (.450 and .32 calibre) hidden beneath their shawls and shot **Stevens** dead on the spot. Because the law forbade hanging minors under sixteen, a Special Tribunal in Calcutta sentenced both girls to **transportation for life** in **January 1932**, where they showed complete fearlessness and defiance in court.
* **Bina Das (February 6, 1932):** Less than two months later, on **February 6, 1932**, at the annual Convocation Ceremony inside the Senate Hall of **Calcutta University**, twenty-one-year-old **Bina Das**—daughter of the eminent Brahmo Samaj teacher **Beni Madhab Das** (who had been **Subhas Chandra Bose’s** revered headmaster at Ravenshaw Collegiate School, Cuttack), sister of **Kalyani Das**, and an English Honours graduate of **Diocesan College** affiliated with **Hemchandra Ghosh’s** **Bengal Volunteers / Chhatri Sangha**—stepped out of her row in her academic gown and fired five shots from a revolver purchased via **Kamala Dasgupta** directly at the Governor of Bengal, **Sir Stanley Jackson**, as he stood at the podium delivering his convocation address. **Jackson** ducked behind the lectern and escaped unhurt while Vice-Chancellor **Sir Hassan Suhrawardy** pinned **Bina Das’s** arm. At her trial before the Calcutta High Court Special Tribunal on February 15, **1932** (where her written statement was suppressed by colonial censorship), **Bina Das** delivered a classic indictment of imperial rule before being sentenced to **nine years’ rigorous imprisonment**:
  > *"I fired at the Governor impelled by my love of my country which is being repressed... Can the measure of the ordinances crush the spirit of truth-seeking and liberty-loving youth of Bengal? I have no personal animosity against Sir Stanley Jackson, the man, who is just as good as my father; nor against Lady Jackson, who is just as good as my mother. Make no mistake: my target was the Governor of Bengal, who represents a system which has kept in slavery thirty crores of my countrymen and countrywomen."*
* **The Broader Bengal Volunteers Wave (1930–33):** Simultaneously, the **Bengal Volunteers** executed the famous **Writers’ Building Raid (December 8, 1930)** in Calcutta, where **Benoy Basu**, **Badal Gupta**, and **Dinesh Gupta** entered the secretariat headquarters dressed in European suits, assassinated the Inspector-General of Prisons **Colonel N.S. Simpson** (infamous for brutalizing political prisoners including **Subhas Chandra Bose** inside Alipore Jail), and fought a corridor battle with police (**Badal** took cyanide, **Benoy** shot himself and died in hospital, and **Dinesh** was hanged on July 7, **1931**), while across **Midnapore District**, **BV** cadres assassinated three successive British District Magistrates in three consecutive years—**James Peddie (April 1931)**, **Robert Douglas (April 1932)**, and **Bernard E.J. Burge (September 1933)**—making it virtually impossible for the Raj to find a European ICS officer willing to accept posting to Midnapore.

### 11. The Structural Legacy of the Revolutionary Underground and the Post-1934 Realignment

By **1934–1935**, the execution of **Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev**, **Rajguru**, and **Surya Sen**, the death in battle of **Chandrashekhar Azad** and **Pritilata Waddedar**, and the mass incarceration of thousands of **HSRA**, **Anushilan**, and **Jugantar** cadres in the **Cellular Jail** (during the second wave of Andaman deportations, **1932–1937**) and detention camps at **Deoli**, **Buxa Fort**, and **Hijli** (where unarmed detenus **Santosh Kumar Mitra** and **Tarakeshwar Sen** were shot dead inside the camp by British guards in September **1931**) brought the era of small-group underground armed actions to a close. Inside the **Cellular Jail** and mainland prisons during the **mid-1930s**, surviving revolutionaries (**Shiv Verma**, **Bejoy Kumar Sinha**, **Jaidev Kapur**, **Ajoy Ghosh**, **Ganesh Ghosh**, **Ananta Singh**, **Subodh Roy**, **Kalpana Dutt**, and **Harekrishna Konar**) formed the **"Communist Consolidation,"** conducting intense Marxist study circles and concluding that true socialist revolution could not be achieved by an isolated armed vanguard acting *on behalf of* the masses, but only through the direct organization of the peasantry and industrial proletariat into mass class struggles. Upon their release between **1937** and **1946**, the overwhelming majority of these revolutionaries entered the **Communist Party of India (CPI)**, the **Congress Socialist Party (CSP)**, the **Revolutionary Socialist Party (RSP, founded 1940 by Anushilan Marxists)**, or **Subhas Chandra Bose’s** **Forward Bloc** and the wartime **Indian National Army (INA, initially organized in East Asia by Ghadar veterans and Rash Behari Bose)**.

#### Outgoing Structural Bridge to Part 11

While the revolutionary underground of **1897–1935** permanently radicalized the horizon of Indian nationalism—injecting the non-negotiable demand for **Purna Swaraj**, popularizing the battle cry of ***Inquilab Zindabad***, pioneering secular socialist ideology, and shattering the psychological aura of colonial invincibility—its structural inability to mobilize millions of peasants and urban workers into sustained, open resistance highlighted the need for a radically different organizational technology. That mass-mobilizational breakthrough emerged simultaneously from a parallel historical trajectory beginning in **1915**: the return of **Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi** from his twenty-one-year **South African crucible (1893–1914)**. In **Part 11**, we examine how **Gandhi** synthesized the moral-political technology of **Satyagraha**, deployed it across the localized agrarian and industrial laboratories of **Champaran (1917)**, **Ahmedabad (1918)**, and **Kheda (1918)**, confronted the **1919 Constitutional-Coercive Rupture** (**Montagu-Chelmsford Dyarchy**, the **Rowlatt Act**, and the **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre**), and fundamentally restructured the **Indian National Congress** at the **1920 Nagpur Session** to launch the **Non-Cooperation and Khilafat Mass Mobilization (1920–1922)**.
