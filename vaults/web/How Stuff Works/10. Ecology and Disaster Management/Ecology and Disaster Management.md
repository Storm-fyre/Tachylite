### Part 1: Ecosystem Energetics, Biotic Interactions, Trophic Dynamics, and Biogeochemical Cycles
- **Levels of ecological organization and the ecosystem as an open thermodynamic system:** homeostasis, ecological resistance vs. resilience, and Holling’s adaptive cycle
- Habitat vs. Ecological Niche (Hutchinson’s fundamental vs. realized niche and Gause’s Competitive Exclusion Principle)
- Ecotone, ecocline, and Edge Effect
- **Interspecific biotic interactions:** Mutualism (lichens, mycorrhizae, coral-zooxanthellae), Protocooperation, Commensalism, Amensalism/Allelopathy, Predation, Parasitism, and Competition
- Ecological adaptations (halophytic vivipary and pneumatophores, xerophytes, Bergmann’s, Allen’s, and Gloger’s rules)
- **Trophic structure and energetics:** Grazing vs. Detritus food chains, Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) vs. Net Primary Productivity (NPP = GPP - R), global NPP distribution, and Raymond Lindeman’s 10% trophic efficiency law
- **Charles Elton’s Ecological Pyramids:** Pyramid of Numbers (upright vs. inverted tree pyramid), Pyramid of Biomass (upright terrestrial vs. inverted marine biomass pyramid), and Pyramid of Energy (always upright)
- **Ecological succession:** Primary vs. Secondary, pioneer to climax communities, autogenic vs. allogenic, and xerarch/lithosere vs. hydrarch/hydrosere succession
- **Biogeochemical cycling mechanics:** Gaseous cycles (Water, Carbon, and Nitrogen cycle: biological nitrogen fixation by Rhizobium/Azotobacter/Anabaena, ammonification, nitrification by Nitrosomonas/Nitrobacter, and denitrification by Pseudomonas) vs. Sedimentary cycles (Phosphorus and Sulphur cycles)
- Millennium Ecosystem Assessment services, NCAVES natural capital accounting, and Rockström’s Nine Planetary Boundaries framework.

### Part 2: Biodiversity Cartography, Indian Biogeographic Zones, Extinction Vortices, and In-Situ/Ex-Situ Conservation Networks
- **Biodiversity levels and R.H. Whittaker’s measurement metrics:** Alpha (α richness and evenness), Beta (β species turnover), and Gamma (γ landscape) diversity, Latitudinal Diversity Gradient, and Humboldt’s species-area relationship
- **Global cartography:** 17 Megadiverse Countries and Norman Myers’s Biodiversity Hotspots (criteria: >=1,500 endemic vascular plants and >=70% primary vegetation loss; the four Indian hotspots: Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, and Sundaland/Nicobar), plus Marine Hope Spots
- Rodgers and Panwar’s 10 Biogeographic Zones and 27 Biotic Provinces of India
- **Drivers of the Sixth Mass Extinction:** the HIPPO framework, island biogeography, extinction vortices, Minimum Viable Population, and IUCN Red List categories
- **In-Situ conservation networks:** National Parks (106+), Wildlife Sanctuaries (573+), Conservation Reserves, and Community Reserves
- Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZs under EPA 1986 and Supreme Court’s 2022–2023 modified 1-km buffer rulings) and the Western Ghats Ecologically Sensitive Area controversy (Gadgil vs. Kasturirangan reports and the 2024 Sixth Draft Notification)
- **UNESCO Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme:** 18 Indian Biosphere Reserves (12 in the UNESCO World Network) and Core-Buffer-Transition spatial zoning
- Sacred Groves (Kavu, Devrai, Law Kyntang, Orans) and the December 2024 Supreme Court Judgment directing legal notification of Rajasthan’s Orans as "Forests" under the FCA 1980
- OECMs, Biodiversity Heritage Sites, IBAs, and Ex-Situ networks (cryopreservation, Svalbard and Chang La seed vaults).

### Part 3: Keystone Species Recovery Doctrines, Project Tiger (58 Reserves), IBCA, Mega-Fauna Interventions, and Invasive Ecology
- **Functional ecological guilds:** Keystone, Umbrella, Flagship, Indicator, and Foundation/Ecosystem Engineer species
- **Project Tiger (1973) and India’s 58 Notified Tiger Reserves (up to the 56th Guru Ghasidas–Tamor Pingla, 57th Ratapani, and 58th Madhav Tiger Reserve notified in 2024–2025):** Core/Critical Tiger Habitat vs. Buffer demarcation, National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA), Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB), M-STrIPES monitoring, 5th Cycle Tiger Estimation (3,682 tigers), and CA|TS accreditation
- The International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA, HQ in India, Framework Agreement entered into force in Jan 2025 covering Tiger, Lion, Leopard, Snow Leopard, Puma, Jaguar, and Cheetah)
- **Wildlife translocation:** African Cheetah introduction in Kuno National Park and Gandhi Sagar Wildlife Sanctuary (carrying capacity, prey base, genetic bottlenecks) and Project Lion (Lion @ 2047 metapopulation expansion into Barda Sanctuary)
- **Project Elephant (1992):** 33 Elephant Reserves, MIKE sites, rail intrusion detection systems, and Project RE-HAB bee-fences
- Indian Rhino Vision 2.0, Project Snow Leopard (SPAI 2024 assessment of 718 snow leopards), Project Dolphin (Gangetic, Indus, Irrawaddy), Project Great Indian Bustard (and the March 2024 Supreme Court M.K. Ranjitsinh judgment balancing GIB diverters/corridors with renewable power lines), Vulture Action Plan (banning veterinary Diclofenac, Ketoprofen, Aceclofenac, and Nimesulide), Hangul, Sangai, Gharial, Olive Ridley Arribada, and Palk Bay Dugong Reserve
- Invasive Alien Species (Lantana camara, Prosopis juliflora, Senna spectabilis, Parthenium, Eichhornia).

### Part 4: Statutory Wildlife Regimes, Forest Jurisprudence, Biodiversity Laws, and Global Biodiversity Treaties (CBD COP16 and CITES COP20)
- **The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972:** institutional machinery (NBWL chaired by PM, CZA, NTCA, WCCB), protected area declarations, and the Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022 (rationalizing 6 schedules down to 4: Schedule I highest protection, Schedule II lesser protection, Schedule III protected plants, Schedule IV CITES specimens; inserting Chapter VB for CITES enforcement, Section 62A invasive alien species regulation, and Section 43 captive elephant transfer rules)
- The Indian Forest Act, 1927 (Reserved, Protected, and Village forests, and the 2017 Amendment exempting non-forest bamboo from the definition of "tree") and the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act (FRA), 2006 (Individual and Community Forest Resource - CFR rights, Critical Wildlife Habitats, and Gram Sabha consent)
- Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960 (AWBI and the 2023 5-Judge Supreme Court verdict upholding Jallikattu and Kambala)
- **The Biological Diversity Act, 2002:** three-tier grid (NBA, SBBs, BMCs), People’s Biodiversity Registers (PBRs), Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS), and the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 (decriminalizing offenses, exempting AYUSH practitioners and cultivated medicinal plants from ABS, and easing foreign investment rules)
- **Multilateral Biodiversity Agreements:** UN Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD 1992), Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (LMOs), Nagoya Protocol (ABS), Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF 30x30 target and India’s updated NBSAP 2024–30), and CBD COP16 (Cali 2024/2025: operationalizing the multilateral "Cali Fund" for Digital Sequence Information - DSI benefit-sharing and an Indigenous Peoples subsidiary body)
- CITES (Appendices I, II, III, COP19 Panama, COP20 Samarkand 2025), TRAFFIC, CMS Bonn Convention (COP14 Samarkand), ITPGRFA Seed Treaty, and IPBES.

### Part 5: Atmospheric Pollution, Ecotoxicology, Photochemical Smog, and Statutory Air Quality Regimes
- **Criteria and hazardous air pollutants:** Particulate Matter (PM10 and fine alveolar-penetrating PM2.5), Sulphur Dioxide (SO2), Nitrogen Oxides (NOx), Carbon Monoxide (carboxyhemoglobin affinity), Tropospheric Ozone (O3), Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), Lead, Ammonia (reacting to form secondary inorganic aerosols), Benzene, Benzo(a)pyrene, Arsenic, Nickel, Black Carbon vs. Brown Carbon, and Fly Ash utilization mandates
- Classical London Smog (cool, humid, sulphurous, chemically reducing) vs. Photochemical Los Angeles Smog (warm, sunny, vehicular NOx + VOCs, chemically oxidizing cocktail of Ozone and Peroxyacyl Nitrates - PAN)
- Acid rain deposition, Indoor Air Pollution, and Indo-Gangetic Plain winter boundary-layer temperature inversions and low ventilation index
- **Air quality measurement architectures:** National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS 2009: 12 pollutants) vs. 2021 WHO Air Quality Guidelines, National Air Quality Index (AQI: 8 pollutants across 6 color categories), and SAFAR
- **Statutory regimes:** Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1981 (including Noise Pollution Rules 2000 and Jan Vishwas Act 2023 decriminalization), Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) in NCR and Adjoining Areas Act 2021 (enforcing the 4-stage Graded Response Action Plan - GRAP), and the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP: 40% PM reduction target across 131 non-attainment cities by 2025–26, PRANA portal)
- **Sectoral abatement:** Thermal power plant Flue Gas Desulphurization (FGD) deadlines and biomass co-firing (SAMARTH), in-situ (Happy Seeder, Pusa Decomposer) vs. ex-situ (2G ethanol, pellets) crop-residue management, BS-VI Stage II vehicular norms, and CSIR-NEERI Green Crackers.

### Part 6: Hydrological Pollution, Aquatic Ecotoxicology, Coastal Zones, and Wetland Jurisprudence (101 Ramsar Sites and COP15)
- **Point vs. non-point aquatic pollution sources and water quality metrics:** Dissolved Oxygen (DO), Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD—biodegradable organic matter), and Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD—total biodegradable and non-biodegradable oxidation)
- Cultural eutrophication, nutrient runoff, Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs), hypoxic benthic "dead zones," and oligotrophic vs. eutrophic lakes
- Bioaccumulation (within an organism) vs. Biomagnification (amplification across trophic levels of lipophilic persistent toxins: DDT, PCBs, methylmercury)
- **Heavy metal and geogenic groundwater ecotoxicology:** Arsenic (Blackfoot disease), Fluoride (skeletal fluorosis), Uranium in NW alluvial aquifers, Nitrates (Blue Baby Syndrome), Mercury (Minamata disease), and Cadmium (Itai-Itai disease), plus Microplastics and PFAS ("forever chemicals")
- Bioremediation (Oilzapper) and Phytoremediation
- **Statutory water architecture:** Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974 (CPCB/SPCBs) and the Water (Amendment) Act, 2024 (decriminalizing minor violations via financial penalties and Adjudicating Officers)
- Namami Gange Mission (National Ganga Council, HAM sewage plants, environmental flows / e-flows)
- Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ 2018/2019 Notification: CRZ-I, II, IIIA/IIIB density split, IV) and Blue Flag beaches
- **Wetland jurisprudence:** Ramsar Convention (1971 "Wise Use" doctrine), Montreux Record (Keoladeo and Loktak Lake; Chilika removal), Wetlands Rules 2017, India’s expansion to 101 Ramsar Sites by 2026 (Surha Tal 100th, Glaw Lake 101st), Indore and Udaipur as India’s first Accredited Wetland Cities (2025), Ramsar COP15 (Victoria Falls, July 2025), Amrit Dharohar, and MISHTI mangrove restoration.

### Part 7: Solid, Plastic, E-Waste, Battery, and Hazardous Waste Statutory Regimes and Chemical Conventions
- Municipal solid waste mechanics and the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 (effective April 2026, replacing the 2016 Rules: mandatory four-stream source segregation into wet, dry, sanitary, and special-care waste, Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility, CPCB digital portal, graded Refuse-Derived Fuel industrial mandates, higher landfilling fees, and legacy dumpsite biomining)
- **Thermal waste treatment:** incineration, pyrolysis, gasification, plasma arc, and anaerobic biomethanation (GOBARdhan)
- Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 (2021–2024 Amendments: single-use plastic ban, 120-micron carry-bag norm, and statutory Extended Producer Responsibility - EPR tradable recycling certificates across four plastic packaging categories) and the UN Global Plastics Treaty (INC-5) negotiations
- E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 (expanded to 106 EEE items across 7 categories including solar PV panels, digital EPR recycling certificates, urban mining, and RoHS hazardous substance restrictions)
- Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 (mandatory material recovery and minimum recycled content in EV/industrial batteries)
- Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (four color-coded bins: Yellow, Red, White, Blue), C&D Waste Rules, Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules (Used Oil, Waste Tyres, Non-Ferrous Scrap EPR), Vehicle Scrappage Policy, and Ecomark Rules, 2024
- **International Chemical and Waste Conventions:** Basel Convention (transboundary hazardous/plastic waste PIC and Ban Amendment), Rotterdam Convention (Annex III pesticides/chemicals PIC), Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs: "Dirty Dozen", Endosulfan, UV-328, PFAS), and Minamata Convention on Mercury.

### Part 8: Atmospheric Climate Mechanics, Stratospheric Ozone Depletion, Cryospheric Instability, and Planetary Tipping Points
- Thermodynamics of the greenhouse effect and radiative forcing (W/m2)
- **The greenhouse gas basket, atmospheric lifetimes, and 100-year Global Warming Potentials (GWP100):** Water Vapor (positive amplifying feedback), Carbon Dioxide (Mauna Loa Keeling Curve crossing 425+ ppm), Methane (CH4), Nitrous Oxide (N2O—also the top current ozone-depleting emission), and high-GWP fluorinated gases (HFCs, PFCs, SF6, NF3)
- Short-Lived Climate Pollutants (Black Carbon, Methane, Tropospheric Ozone, HFCs) and aerosol cloud-albedo cooling masking
- **Stratospheric ozone depletion chemistry:** Dobson Units, Molina-Rowland catalytic chlorine/bromine free-radical cycle, and Antarctic Austral Spring ozone hole mechanics (circumpolar Polar Vortex isolation, Type-I/II Polar Stratospheric Clouds converting inactive chlorine into Cl2 photolyzed by spring sunlight)
- **The Vienna Convention (1985) and Montreal Protocol (1987):** phase-out of CFCs, Halons, and HCFCs (India’s HPMP Stage-II/III), and the Kigali Amendment (2016, ratified by India in 2021: phasing down zero-ODP, high-GWP HFCs with India in Group 2: 2024–26 baseline, 2028 freeze, 85% cut by 2047) alongside the India Cooling Action Plan
- **Cryospheric instability and feedbacks:** Arctic Polar Amplification, ice-albedo feedback, Greenland and West Antarctic (Thwaites Glacier) melt, Himalayan "Third Pole" glacial retreat, and permafrost thawing
- Global sea-level rise (steric thermal expansion vs. eustatic land-ice melt)
- Planetary climate tipping points (AMOC collapse, Amazon dieback, coral reef die-off)
- Climate geoengineering (Carbon Dioxide Removal / DAC / Biochar vs. Solar Radiation Management / Stratospheric Aerosol Injection risks).

### Part 9: The International Climate and Ocean Commons Architecture: From Rio to Paris, Article 6 Carbon Markets, COP29/COP30, and the High Seas Treaty (BBNJ 2026)
- **The scientific anchor:** Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report, Remaining Carbon Budget, and five Shared Socioeconomic Pathways - SSPs)
- **United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC, 1992):** CBDR-RC principle and Annex I, Annex II, and Non-Annex I differentiation
- **The Kyoto Protocol (1997, Doha Amendment):** top-down Annex I binding targets and three flexibility mechanisms (Emissions Trading, Joint Implementation, and Clean Development Mechanism - CDM)
- **The Paris Agreement (2015):** bottom-up Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), progression ratchet, long-term goal (well below 2.0°C and pursuing 1.5°C), and the five-yearly Global Stocktake (COP28 Dubai first GST calling for transitioning away from fossil fuels and tripling renewables by 2030)
- **Article 6 Global Carbon Markets (finalized at COP29 Baku, Nov 2024):** Article 6.2 bilateral Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs with Corresponding Adjustments) and Article 6.4 centralized Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM), plus REDD+ and Blue Carbon ecosystems
- Global Methane Pledge (and India’s non-signatory survival-emissions rationale)
- **Climate finance architecture:** Green Climate Fund (GCF), Loss and Damage Fund (FRLD), the COP29 Baku New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG: $300B/year by 2035 within an aspirational $1.3T roadmap, critiqued by India), and UNFCCC COP30 at Belém, Brazil (Nov 2025: Global Mutirão, NDC 3.0 2035 targets, $125B Tropical Forest Forever Facility - TFFF, Just Transition Mechanism, and Trade-and-Climate Dialogues)
- **Governance of the Marine Commons:** the UN High Seas Treaty (BBNJ Agreement, officially entered into force on January 17, 2026 across four pillars: Marine Genetic Resources, High-Seas MPAs, EIAs, and capacity building).

### Part 10: Subcontinental Decarbonization Pathways, NAPCC, Green Hydrogen, Domestic Carbon Markets (CCTS), and Grid-Storage Transitions
- **India’s climate vulnerability profile, Updated NDCs (August 2022), Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment), and Panchamrit pledges:** 45% reduction in emissions intensity of GDP by 2030 (2005 base), 50% cumulative electric power installed capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030, 2.5 to 3 billion tonnes CO2e additional forest/tree carbon sink by 2030, 500 GW non-fossil capacity, and the Net-Zero 2070 commitment (LT-LEDS and NDC 3.0)
- **The National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC, 2008):** the eight National Missions (Solar—including PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana for 1 crore rooftop households and PM-KUSUM; Enhanced Energy Efficiency—BEE PAT scheme; Sustainable Habitat—ECSBC building code; Water; Sustaining Himalayan Ecosystem; Green India; Sustainable Agriculture; Strategic Knowledge)
- **The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 and the Indian Carbon Market (ICM):** statutory launch of the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS, operationalized 2024–2026 under BEE, Grid Controller of India, and CERC trading Carbon Credit Certificates across a mandatory Compliance Mechanism for 9 hard-to-abate sectors and a voluntary Offset Mechanism)
- The Green Credit Programme (GCP Rules, 2023 under EPA 1986)
- **Clean-tech and industrial decarbonization missions:** National Green Hydrogen Mission (5 MMT target by 2030, SIGHT incentives for electrolyzers and green ammonia, BIS green hydrogen standard), PM E-DRIVE and PM-eBus Sewa electromobility, 20% Ethanol Blending (E20), Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) targets, National Coal Gasification Mission, Bharat Small Nuclear Reactors, Union Budget 2026–27 ₹20,000 crore Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS) Mission, and grid-scale Battery Energy Storage (BESS) and Pumped Hydro Storage (PSP).

### Part 11: Environmental Jurisprudence, EIA 2006 vs. Ex-Post Facto Clearances (2026 SC Verdict), Forest Conservation Act 2023, CAMPA, ISFR 2023, and UNCCD COP16
- **Constitutional foundations (Articles 48A, 51A(g), and Article 21 right against climate change in M.K. Ranjitsinh 2024) and core doctrines:** Absolute Liability (M.C. Mehta Oleum Gas Leak case superseding Rylands v. Fletcher strict liability), Polluter Pays, Precautionary Principle, Public Trust Doctrine (Kamal Nath), and Intergenerational Equity
- The National Green Tribunal (NGT Act, 2010: natural justice, Ankita Sinha 2021 suo motu power, and Schedule I 7-Act jurisdiction excluding WPA 1972, IFA 1927, and FRA 2006)
- The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (and Jan Vishwas Act 2023 decriminalization via Environmental Protection Fund) and the EIA Notification, 2006 (Category A central EAC vs. Category B1/B2 state SEIAA appraisal, Screening, Scoping, Public Consultation, Appraisal on PARIVESH 2.0)
- The landmark July 2026 Supreme Court 3-Judge Bench Verdict on Ex-Post Facto Environmental Clearances (Vanashakti / CREDAI case: quashing the 2021 Office Memorandum amnesty regime and affirming mandatory Prior EC, while limiting any future one-time regularization strictly to a narrow statutory Notification under Section 3 of the EPA 1986 for projects of supervening public interest)
- The Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, the 1996 T.N. Godavarman dictionary definition of forest, the Forest (Conservation) Amendment Act, 2023 (exempting strategic border projects within 100 km of LAC/borders and unrecorded deemed forests), and the February 2024 Supreme Court interim order mandating continued application of the Godavarman dictionary definition (plus the 2026 Great Nicobar mega-project litigation)
- CAMPA Act 2016 (Net Present Value)
- India State of Forest Report (ISFR 2023: 25.17% total forest and tree cover—21.76% forest + 3.41% tree cover)
- UNCCD (Land Degradation Neutrality 2030, COP16 Riyadh 2024, and the Aravalli Green Wall Project).

### Part 12: Disaster Mechanics I: Seismic Zonation (BIS IS 1893:2025/2026 Controversy), Tsunami Early Warning, Himalayan/Western Ghats Landslides, and Subsidence
- Disaster risk equation (Hazard x Exposure x Vulnerability / Capacity) and India’s multi-hazard profile
- **Earthquake disaster mechanics:** Himalayan plate-boundary thrust accumulation (MCT, MBT, HFT, and the Central Seismic Gap), intra-plate cratonic fault ruptures (Bhuj, Latur), and Indo-Gangetic alluvial soil liquefaction
- **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Seismic Design Code evolution:** traditional four-zone map (IS 1893:2016 Zones II, III, IV, V) vs. the November 2025 release of Revised IS 1893:2025 based on Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Assessment (introducing a new highest-risk Zone VI across the entire Himalayan Arc and placing 61% of India in moderate-to-high risk), followed by the March 2026 BIS/MoHUA suspension and review due to construction/retrofitting cost escalations
- Vernacular earthquake-resistant architectures (Dhajji-Dewari, Kath-Kuni, Koti Banal, Ikra)
- **Tsunami risk management:** Andaman-Sumatra and Makran subduction sources, Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC at INCOIS Hyderabad: BPR/DART buoys, RTSP status), UNESCO-IOC "Tsunami Ready" coastal communities, and mangrove bio-shields
- **Landslide disaster mechanics:** structural contrast between tectonic/road-cut Himalayan landslides and pore-water-pressure lateritic regolith piping/debris-avalanche failures in the Western Ghats (exemplified by the July 2024 Wayanad Mundakkai-Chooralmala catastrophe), National Landslide Risk Management Strategy, GSI’s National Landslide Forecasting Centre (NLFC, Kolkata 2024), and Bhusanket/Bhooskhalan systems
- Subterranean land subsidence (2023 Joshimath crisis), Himalayan tunneling safety SOPs (post-Silkyara collapse), and Barren Island volcanic monitoring.

### Part 13: Disaster Mechanics II: Cyclones, Riverine/Urban Floods, GLOFs, Droughts, Heatwaves, Forest Fires, and Lightning
- **Tropical Cyclone disaster management:** Bay of Bengal vs. intensifying Arabian Sea cyclone dynamics, IMD four-stage color-coded alerts, Multi-Purpose Cyclone Shelters, and the National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project (NCRMP EWDS and GEMINI devices)
- **Riverine Flood disasters:** catchment siltation, the Embankment Paradox (raising riverbeds above floodplains in the Kosi and Brahmaputra), CWC FloodWatch, and chronic non-enactment of the 1975 Model Flood Plain Zoning Bill by states
- **Urban Inundation (Urban Flooding):** impervious concretization, encroachment of urban wetlands, lakes, and natural stormwater drains (Rajakaluves, Pallikaranai marsh, Mithi river), obsolete drainage capacity vs. cloudbursts, "Sponge City" nature-based drainage solutions, X-band Doppler radars, and the ₹2,500 crore Urban Flood Risk Management Programme
- **Cryospheric catastrophes:** Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs: moraine dam failure mechanics, October 2023 South Lhonak Lake GLOF destroying the Teesta-III dam in Sikkim, CWC/NRSC monitoring of 190+ high-risk lakes, HDPE siphoning, and the 2024 National GLOF Risk Mitigation Programme), avalanches, and cloudbursts
- **Drought disasters:** Meteorological, Hydrological, Agricultural, and Socio-Economic droughts, and the 2016 Drought Manual objective triggers
- **Extreme Heatwave hazards:** IMD thresholds, the 35°C physiological Wet-Bulb Temperature survival limit, Urban Heat Islands, and municipal Heat Action Plans (HAPs)
- Forest Fires (Chir Pine resin flammability, FSI SNPP-VIIRS alerts) and Lightning strikes (Damini app and SDRF notification debates).

### Part 14: Disaster Mechanics III: Chemical, Industrial, CBRN Threats, Dam Safety, and Anthropogenic Infrastructure Failures
- **Chemical and industrial disasters:** legacy of the 1984 Bhopal Methyl Isocyanate (MIC) gas leak and contemporary industrial leaks (2020 Visakhapatnam Styrene leak)
- **The post-Bhopal statutory triad:** Factories (Amendment) Act 1987 (Chapter IVA On-Site Emergency Plans), Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals (MSIHC) Rules 1989 and Chemical Accidents Rules 1996 (identifying 1,861+ Major Accident Hazard - MAH units and four-tier Central/State/District/Local Crisis Groups with Off-Site Emergency Plans), and the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 (mandatory no-fault insurance and Environmental Relief Fund)
- **Chemical, Biological, Radiological, and Nuclear (CBRN) disasters:** nerve/blister chemical agents, CDC Category A–C bioterror and zoonotic threats (BSL-1 to BSL-4 containment, Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 vs. DM Act 2005), radiological dispersal devices ("dirty bombs" and orphan sources like Mayapuri Cobalt-60), and nuclear core meltdowns
- **Nuclear power safety architecture:** Emergency Planning Zones (1.6-km Exclusion, 5-km Sterilized, and 16-km Off-Site zones), Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB autonomy deficits), Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act (CLNDA 2010: ₹1,500 crore operator cap and Section 17(b) supplier recourse), and specialized NDRF CBRN battalions
- **Critical infrastructure failures:** structural aging of 230+ century-old large dams (Mullaperiyar dispute), the Dam Safety Act, 2021 (NCDS, National Dam Safety Authority - NDSA, SCDS, SDSO), and DRIP Phases I–III
- Maritime oil spills (NOS-DCP), fire safety audits, and NDMA Crowd/Stampede Management guidelines.

### Part 15: Institutional Disaster Governance, the Disaster Management (Amendment) Act 2024/2025, Mitigation Financing, and the Sendai Framework
- Paradigm shift from reactive post-disaster relief to proactive pre-disaster prevention, mitigation, and preparedness
- **The Disaster Management Act, 2005 and its three-tier institutional matrix:** National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA chaired by the PM) and National Executive Committee (NEC chaired by the Home Secretary), State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMAs chaired by CMs), and District Disaster Management Authorities (DDMAs chaired by the District Collector with the Zila Parishad Chair as Co-Chair), supported by the National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM) and 16 specialized NDRF battalions
- **The Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2024/2025 overhaul:** statutory creation of Urban Disaster Management Authorities (UDMAs headed by the Municipal Commissioner) for all state capitals and municipal corporation cities, empowering NDMA/SDMAs to directly draft disaster plans, mandating statutory National and State Disaster Databases, and conferring statutory status on the National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC) and High-Level Committee (HLC)
- **Disaster Risk Financing architecture (15th and 16th Finance Commissions):** creation of the National and State Disaster Risk Management Funds (NDRMF/SDRMF) with a statutory 80:20 bifurcation into Response Funds (NDRF/SDRF: relief, recovery, preparedness) and dedicated Mitigation Funds (NDMF/SDMF) for pre-disaster structural mitigation, across 12 centrally notified disasters (plus 10% flexi-funds for state-notified local disasters)
- Common Alerting Protocol (Sachet cell broadcast), CDRI/IRIS, Aapda Mitra community volunteers, "Build Back Better" recovery, the Sendai Framework (2015–2030: 7 targets, 4 priorities), and the PM’s 10-Point Agenda.
