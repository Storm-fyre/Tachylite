# Part 2: Left-Wing Extremism (LWE): Ideological Genesis, Protracted People's War, and Counter-Insurgency Doctrines

## I. Ideological Genesis, Organizational Evolution, and the Doctrine of Protracted People's War

### 1. From Naxalbari (1967) to the 2004 CPI (Maoist) Unification: Historical Trajectory and Organizational Synthesis

#### The 1967 Naxalbari Spark and the Charu Mazumdar–Kanu Sanyal Line
The structural genesis of Left-Wing Extremism (LWE) in India represents a violent ideological rupture within the Indian communist movement over the nature of the post-colonial Indian state and the legitimate method of seizing sovereign power. Whereas mainstream parliamentary communist parties transitioned toward constitutional electoral participation, radical factions diagnosed the Indian state as a "semi-colonial, semi-feudal" formation controlled by an alliance of big landlordism and comprador bureaucratic capitalism. In **May 1967**, an armed peasant uprising erupted in the **Naxalbari** police station area of Darjeeling district in West Bengal, led by **Charu Mazumdar**, **Kanu Sanyal**, and **Jangal Santhal**. Triggered by the violent eviction of a sharecropper (*adhiar*) despite a judicial land order, the **1967 Naxalbari uprising** rejected parliamentary trade-unionism in favor of armed agrarian revolution modeled strictly on Mao Zedong's Chinese revolution.

*   **The Eight Historic Documents (1965–1967):** Authored by **Charu Mazumdar**, these foundational ideological texts argued that the Indian parliamentary system was a structural illusion designed to pacify revolutionary class consciousness, and that power could only be seized through an armed agrarian insurgency encircling urban centers from the countryside.
*   **Formation of CPI (Marxist-Leninist) (April 1969):** Following the Sino-Soviet ideological split and the endorsement of the Naxalbari line by the Chinese Communist Party's mouthpiece (*People's Daily*, calling it "Spring Thunder over India"), **Kanu Sanyal** announced the formation of the **Communist Party of India (Marxist-Leninist) [CPI (ML)]** in Calcutta on **April 22, 1969** (Vladimir Lenin's birth anniversary), with **Charu Mazumdar** as General Secretary.
*   **The "Annihilation of Class Enemies" Line:** Mazumdar instituted a secretive, conspiratorial tactical doctrine prioritizing the physical assassination ("khatam" line) of individual feudal landlords, moneylenders, police informers, and state officials by small underground guerrilla squads (*dalams*), rejecting mass open peasant organizations as "revisionist economism."
*   **Collapse of Phase I (1967–1972):** Because the first phase relied on urban student radicalism (particularly in Calcutta and Srikakulam) and lacked deep rural mass mobilization or sustainable topographical sanctuaries, a coordinated counter-insurgency campaign by the Indian Army and state police (**Operation Steeplechase**, 1971), followed by **Charu Mazumdar's** arrest and death in custody in **July 1972**, fractured the movement into over forty competing ideological splinter groups.

Through this initial collapse, the surviving insurgent theoreticians deduced a fundamental strategic lesson: isolated urban terror and premature tactical confrontation against the Westphalian state's military apparatus without hardened rural-forest sanctuaries and mass peasant front organizations inevitably results in rapid kinetic destruction.

#### Fragmentation and Parallel Tactical Streams: People's War Group (PWG) vs. Maoist Communist Centre (MCC)
During the Second Phase of the insurgency (late 1970s to 2004), the gravitational center of Left-Wing Extremism migrated southward and eastward from West Bengal into the agrarian plains of Bihar and the forested tribal hinterlands of Andhra Pradesh (Telangana), Maharashtra (Gadchiroli), Madhya Pradesh (Bastar), and Odisha. Two dominant armed formations emerged from this ideological reorganization, operating across distinct socio-ecological theaters.

*   **The People's War Group (PWG — Founded 1980):** Established on **April 22, 1980**, in Andhra Pradesh by **Kondapalli Seetharamaiah**, the **CPI (ML) People's War** corrected Charu Mazumdar's rejection of mass organizations. While retaining armed guerrilla squads (*dalams*), the **PWG** built an extensive ecosystem of student, peasant, cultural (such as *Jana Natya Mandali* led by Gaddar), and civil-liberties front organizations in Telangana and North Andhra. When state police pressure mounted in the Andhra plains during the early 1980s, the **PWG** took the visionary strategic decision to send Reconnaissance Squads across the Godavari River into the dense, unpoliced **Dandakaranya** forests of Bastar, establishing a permanent strategic rear base.
*   **The Maoist Communist Centre (MCC — Founded 1969 as Dakshin Desh):** Originating in **October 1969** under the leadership of **Kanhai Chatterjee** and **Amulya Sen** as the *Dakshin Desh* group (which never joined Mazumdar's CPI-ML, criticizing its neglect of mass peasant mobilization) and renamed the **Maoist Communist Centre (MCC)** in **1975**, this stream entrenched itself in central Bihar and the Chota Nagpur plateau (modern Jharkhand). Operating across acute caste-feudal fault lines, the **MCC** organized landless Dalit and lower-backward caste peasantry into the *Lal Raksha Dal* (Red Defense Corps), engaging in brutal, large-scale retaliatory massacres against upper-caste private landlord militias (such as the **Ranvir Sena**, *Bhumi Sena*, and *Sunlight Sena*).
*   **Tactical Convergence and Fratricidal Conflict (1990s):** Throughout the late 1990s, the **PWG** (expanding northward from Telangana/Dandakaranya into Bihar and Jharkhand via its merger with CPI-ML Party Unity in 1998) and the **MCC** (expanding southward into Odisha and Chhattisgarh) engaged in bloody turf wars over extortion territories ("levy zones") and village allegiances in Jharkhand and Bihar, resulting in hundreds of cadre deaths before ideological mediators brokered a peace pact in early 2003.

This dual evolution fused two complementary insurgent capabilities: the **PWG's** sophisticated military engineering, mobile guerrilla warfare, and urban intellectual-front architecture, and the **MCC's** ruthless agrarian mobilization, "Jan Adalat" (People's Court) summary justice mechanisms, and command over the mineral-rich coal and iron-ore belts of eastern India.

#### The September 2004 Unification into CPI (Maoist) and the Red Corridor Architecture
On **September 21, 2004** (publicly announced on October 14, 2004), the **People's War Group (PWG)** and the **Maoist Communist Centre of India (MCCI)** merged to form a single, unified pan-Indian insurgent party: the **Communist Party of India (Maoist) [CPI (Maoist)]**. **Muppala Lakshmana Rao (alias Ganapathi)** of the erstwhile PWG was appointed the first General Secretary, while **Prashanta Bose (alias Kishanda)** of the MCC became the second-in-command within the unified Politburo.

*   **Ideological Synthesis (Marxism-Leninism-Maoism):** The **2004 unification** adopted five foundational strategic documents—including the *Strategy and Tactics of the Indian Revolution (STIR)* and the *Party Programme*—codifying **Marxism-Leninism-Maoism (MLM)** as its guiding ideology and declaring the "annihilation of state power through **Protracted People's War**" as its non-negotiable objective.
*   **Command-and-Control Centralization:** The unified **CPI (Maoist)** established a strictly hierarchical Leninist-military structure headed by a **Politburo** (the apex executive ideological body), a **Central Committee (CC)**, and a **Central Military Commission (CMC)** (commanded for nearly two decades by **Nambala Keshava Rao alias Basavaraju**, who subsequently succeeded Ganapathi as General Secretary in **November 2018**). Beneath the central bodies operated Regional Bureaus (Central, Eastern, Northern, Southern/South-Western), State/Special Zonal Committees ( notably the **Dandakaranya Special Zonal Committee — DKSZC** and the **Bihar-Jharkhand Special Area Committee — BJSAC**), Divisional Committees, and Area Committees.
*   **The "Red Corridor" Geostrategic Contiguity:** By unifying the northern Bihar-Jharkhand-West Bengal theater with the southern Chhattisgarh-Telangana-Maharashtra-Odisha theater, the **2004 merger** created a contiguous insurgent corridor stretching across the forested heartland of peninsular and eastern India—colloquially termed the **"Red Corridor"** (envisioned ideologically as linking "Pashupati in Nepal to Tirupati in Andhra Pradesh")—peaking around **2008–2010** when LWE violence affected over **180 to 200 districts** (with **126 districts** officially notified under the Central SRE security grid by 2010–2013).

The **2004 unification** transformed fragmented regional peasant rebellions into what then-Prime Minister Manmohan Singh characterized in **2006** as the "single biggest internal security challenge ever faced by our country," confronting the Indian state with a unified command capable of coordinating simultaneous multi-state guerrilla offensives, industrial extortion, and weapons standardization.

### 2. Strategic Anatomy of Mao Zedong's Protracted People's War (PPW) and Area Denial

#### The Three-Phase Escalation Ladder of Guerrilla Warfare
The military-political doctrine of the **CPI (Maoist)** is anchored in **Mao Zedong's** classic treatise *On Protracted War* (1938), adapted to Indian conditions in the party's *Strategy and Tactics of the Indian Revolution (STIR)*. Because the Westphalian state possesses overwhelming conventional superiority in firepower, logistics, air mobility, and fiscal revenue at the outset of an insurgency, the insurgent organization cannot survive a direct Clausewitzian battle of annihilation. Instead, **Protracted People's War (PPW)** weaponizes *time*, *space*, and *popular mobilization* to invert the asymmetry of power across three sequential strategic phases:

1.  **Phase I — The Strategic Defensive (Preservation and Base-Building):** When the state is overwhelmingly strong and the insurgent force is nascent, the guerrilla movement avoids positional defense of territory. It dispatches armed propaganda teams (*dalams*) into remote, topographically inaccessible hinterlands where the state's administrative footprint is limited to seasonal revenue/forest extraction. By resolving local grievances (increasing wages for minor forest produce, redistributing disputed forest land, expelling corrupt petty officials), the insurgents establish clandestine village-level revolutionary committees (*Sanghams*) and transform "White Areas" (state-controlled) into contested **"Guerrilla Zones."**
2.  **Phase II — The Strategic Stalemate (Equilibrium and Parallel Governance):** As the insurgent movement expands its cadre strength and weapons inventory (exclusively through looting state police armories and ambush seizures), it escalates tactical offensives to expel the remaining civil administration and police posts from the interior. Contested Guerrilla Zones are consolidated into **"Base Areas"** or **"Liberated Zones"** governed by parallel insurgent administrations (**Janathana Sarkars**). Guerrilla Warfare transitions toward **Mobile Warfare** (*운동전*), where company- and battalion-sized **PLGA** formations execute concentrated "Tactical Counter-Offensive Campaigns" (TCOCs) during the dry summer months (February to June) to inflict mass casualties on security forces (exemplified by the **April 2010 Tadmetla massacre** of 76 CRPF personnel and the **April 2021 Tekulagudem/Tarrem ambush** of 22 jawans).
3.  **Phase III — The Strategic Offensive (Conventional Encirclement of Cities):** Once the rural Base Areas are linked across regional theaters and the state's security forces are demoralized and bled white in the countryside, the guerrilla army is envisioned to transform into a regular **People's Liberation Army (PLA)** conducting **Positional Warfare**, cutting off national highways, railways, and mineral supply chains, and encircling urban centers to overthrow the central state.

Crucially, throughout its 59-year history in India, Left-Wing Extremism never progressed beyond the threshold between **Phase I (Strategic Defensive)** and localized pockets of **Phase II (Strategic Stalemate)** inside deep forest enclaves like **Abujhmad**, as the Indian state's fiscal and military capacity prevented the insurgents from ever holding a single district headquarters or urban center.

#### Area Denial Mechanics and the Destruction of the State's Physical Footprint
To prevent the Indian state from projecting either kinetic force or welfare governance into its Guerrilla Zones and Base Areas, the **CPI (Maoist)** systematically operationalized the doctrine of **Area Denial**. In Maoist strategic calculus, a paved road, a telecommunication tower, a village panchayat भवन, or a residential school is not a neutral public good; it is a vector of state penetration that simultaneously enables troop mobility, human/technical intelligence collection, and the erosion of tribal dependence on the insurgent apparatus.

*   **Physical Sabotage of Infrastructure:** Maoist formations systematically blew up rural roads, culverts, and bridges using command-detonated and pressure-plate **Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs)**, burned road-construction machinery (JCBs and rollers), assassinated contractors, and demolished hundreds of school buildings and health centers across Bastar, Dantewada, and Sukma on the pretext that security forces might use concrete roofs as temporary camping bases.
*   **Information and Electoral Blackout:** By destroying Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL) and private mobile towers, the insurgents imposed an information vacuum that blinded security agencies to troop movements while isolating tribal youth from mainstream economic integration. Simultaneously, the **CPI (Maoist)** enforced violent electoral boycotts—threatening to amputate inked fingers and assassinating local elected representatives (such as the **May 2013 Jiram Ghati massacre** in Darbha Valley, which wiped out the senior leadership of the Chhattisgarh Congress, and the **April 2019 assassination of BJP MLA Bhima Mandavi** in Dantewada)—to paralyze grassroots constitutional democracy.
*   **Coercive Counter-Intelligence ("Informer" Executions):** To deny the state **Human Intelligence (HUMINT)**, Maoist *Jan Adalats* conducted public kangaroo-court trials and brutal executions of local villagers, *Sarpanchs*, and *Shiksha Karmis* accused of being police informers (*mukhbir*), creating a pervasive climate of psychological terror that silenced dissent within tribal hamlets.

Ultimately, the **Area Denial** doctrine contained a fatal internal contradiction: by violently obstructing roads, electricity, schools, healthcare, and PDS ration delivery over four decades, the **CPI (Maoist)** alienated the younger generation of Adivasis whose socio-economic aspirations increasingly demanded connectivity and mobility rather than perpetual isolation inside an insurgent garrison.

### 3. Topographical Determinism: The Dandakaranya–Abujhmad–Bastar Revolutionary Base Area Strategy

#### Tri-Junction Sanctuary Mechanics and Forest Canopy Exploitation
In classic asymmetric warfare theory (from Mao Zedong's Jinggangshan mountains to Che Guevara's *foco* doctrine), guerrilla survivability is directly proportional to topographical friction and administrative fragmentation. Beginning in **1980**, the **PWG** (and post-2004, the **CPI (Maoist)**) selected the **Dandakaranya** physiographic region—spanning approximately **92,000 sq km** across the Bastar division of southern Chhattisgarh, Gadchiroli and Chandrapur in Maharashtra, Malkangiri and Koraput in Odisha, and Mulugu/Bhadradri Kothagudem in Telangana—as its primary **Revolutionary Base Area**.

*   **Inter-State Tri-Junction Exploitation:** Under Schedule VII of the Indian Constitution, "Public Order" and "Police" (Entries 1 and 2 of the State List) are exclusively state subjects. The Maoists deliberately located their regional headquarters and logistics hubs along inter-state **tri-junctions**—such as the **Chhattisgarh–Telangana–Maharashtra** junction along the Indravati and Pranhita rivers, the **Andhra–Odisha Border (AOB)** in the Eastern Ghats (*Cut-Off Area* of Malkangiri/Chitrakonda reservoir), and the **Bihar–Jharkhand–Chhattisgarh** tri-junction at **Budha Pahar**. Whenever state police launched a kinetic operation in one state, the insurgents simply crossed an unmarked riverine or hilly state boundary to exploit jurisdictional inertia, radio-frequency incompatibility, and intelligence silos among neighboring state police forces.
*   **Multi-Layered Canopy and Lateritic Terrain:** Dominated by dense tropical moist and dry deciduous **Sal (*Shorea robusta*)** and **Teak** forests, steep undulating hills, seasonal streams (*nallahs*), and caves, the Dandakaranya ecology offered natural overhead concealment against aerial reconnaissance (UAVs and satellite electro-optics) while channeling wheeled security vehicles onto predictable dirt tracks ideal for V-shaped and L-shaped directional IED ambushes.

By fusing natural topographical barriers with the federal administrative seams of the Indian Union, the **Dandakaranya Special Zonal Committee (DKSZC)** converted southern Bastar into an insurgent fortress that withstood conventional policing for over three decades.

#### The Strategic Pivot to Abujhmad and the Karreguttalu Ridge
Within the broader Dandakaranya theater, two micro-topographies served as the ultimate inner sanctum of the **CPI (Maoist)** Politburo and Central Military Commission: the **Abujhmad** massif and the **Karreguttalu Hills**.

*   **The Abujhmad ("The Unknown Hill") Citadel:** Spanning roughly **4,000 sq km** across Narayanpur, Bijapur, and Dantewada districts of Chhattisgarh (adjoining Gadchiroli in Maharashtra), **Abujhmad** is a labyrinth of steep hills, deep gorges, and thick forest inhabited by the **Abujhmadia** tribe (a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group). Because the British colonial administration and post-independence state governments left the region **administratively unsurveyed** since 1909 (lacking land revenue records or cadastral maps), the **CPI (Maoist)** transformed Abujhmad into its *de facto* capital after **2005**, housing its central Politburo headquarters, technical weapons research and development (R&D) foundries, communication hubs, and central training academies.
*   **The Karreguttalu Hills (KGH) Sanctuary:** Situated along the south-western edge of Bijapur district (Chhattisgarh) adjoining Mulugu district (Telangana), the **Karreguttalu Hills** form a rugged, 60-km-long and 20-km-wide forested mountain ridge watered by the **Indravati River** and honeycombed with natural caves and rock shelters. As security forces systematically captured former Maoist strongholds in **Budha Pahar** (Jharkhand-Chhattisgarh border, cleared via **Operation Octopus** in 2022–2023) and **Chakarbandha** (Bihar), the core military apparatus of the insurgency—specifically **PLGA Battalion No. 1** and the **Telangana State Committee**—retreated into the heavily mined **Karreguttalu Hills** and core **Abujhmad**, setting the stage for the decisive kinetic showdowns of **2024–2026**.

Thus, the spatial history of Left-Wing Extremism from **2004 to 2026** exhibits a relentless geographical contraction: from a sprawling 18,000+ sq km multi-state corridor down to a fortified, 4,000-sq-km topographical redoubt in **Abujhmad** and **Karreguttalu** where the movement's final military reserves were ultimately cornered and neutralized.

## II. Structural Socio-Economic Drivers, Fifth Schedule Friction, and Parallel Insurgent Governance

### 4. The Fifth Schedule Paradox: Tribal Land Alienation, Mining Displacement, and Forest Bureaucracy Friction

#### Resource Curse Economics vs. Adivasi Subsistence Ecologies
While Maoist leadership derives its strategic doctrine from transnational Marxist-Leninist-Maoist ideology, the insurgency's capacity to recruit thousands of Adivasi foot soldiers over four decades rested upon a profound structural contradiction in India's political economy: the **Fifth Schedule Resource Paradox**. The geographical map of India's most severe LWE-affected districts overlaps almost identically with the country's **Fifth Schedule tribal belt**, greatest **forest cover**, and richest deposits of **coal, iron ore, bauxite, and limestone** (concentrated across Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Maharashtra, and Telangana).

*   **Asymmetric Extractive Development ("Internal Colonialism" Narrative):** Under the Land Acquisition Act of 1894 and the Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition and Development) Act of 1957, vast tracts of ancestral tribal land (*Jal, Jungle, Zameen*) were acquired under the doctrine of **eminent domain** for public and private mining corporations, steel plants, and large hydroelectric dams (such as the Bailadila iron ore mines in Dantewada). While these capital-intensive extractive enclaves generated massive mineral royalties and industrial output for urban centers, the indigenous tribal populations suffered acute **mining-induced displacement** without commensurate rehabilitation, skilled employment, or equity share.
*   **Ecological Degradation and Livelihood Collapse:** Open-cast mining polluted perennial tribal water sources (such as the *Shankhini* river in Dantewada, known locally as the "red river" due to iron-ore tailing runoff) and destroyed communal grazing and shifting-cultivation lands, reducing self-sufficient Adivasi cultivators to precarious daily-wage laborers at the bottom of the informal economy.
*   **Human Development Deficits:** Prior to the post-2014 infrastructure saturation drive, Schedule V tribal blocks exhibited maternal and infant mortality rates, illiteracy levels, and malnutrition indices comparable to Sub-Saharan Africa, creating a profound crisis of state legitimacy that Maoist propagandists exploited to portray the Indian state as an extractive predator serving corporate interests.

This divergence between subterranean mineral wealth and surface-level tribal destitution provided the objective socio-economic grievances (*jal, jungle, zameen*) that allowed an predominantly upper-caste, non-tribal Maoist leadership (largely from the Andhra/Telangana plains) to mobilize indigenous Gond, Madia, Muria, and Santhal youth into an armed war against the republic.

#### Colonial Continuity in Forest Governance and the "Encroacher" Legal Fiction
Compounding mining displacement was the structural friction between traditional Adivasi forest-dwelling communities and the state's forest bureaucracy. Historically, the **Indian Forest Act of 1927** and the **Wildlife (Protection) Act of 1972** were engineered around a state-monopoly conservation and timber-extraction model that extinguished customary communal tenures.

*   **Criminalization of Subsistence Practices:** Overnight, centuries-old tribal agricultural and foraging practices—collecting **Minor Forest Produce (MFP)** such as *tendu* leaves, *mahua* flowers, tamarind, and bamboo, grazing cattle, or cultivating ancestral forest clearings—were legally reclassified as "forest offenses" and "encroachments" on Reserve and Protected Forests.
*   **Petty Bureaucratic Extortion:** Across the remote interiors of Bastar, Gadchiroli, and Saranda, the Indian state had virtually no welfare presence (no doctors, teachers, or development officers); the *only* visible face of the sovereign state was the armed **Forest Guard (*Nakedar*)**, the **Patwari** (revenue official), and the local **Police Constable**. The routine harassment, arbitrary fines, forced unpaid labor (*begar*), and petty bribes extracted by this lower-rung bureaucracy generated intense daily humiliation among Adivasi communities.
*   **The Insurgent Tactical Wedge:** When the first **PWG** guerrilla *dalams* entered Bastar in **1980**, their immediate tactical intervention was not teaching Marxist theory, but physically expelling forest guards, halting state timber felling, redistributing **Reserve Forest** land to landless tribals for cultivation, and forcing *tendu* leaf contractors to double collection wages—thereby winning immediate social sanctuary among the local populace.

By turning the state's own regulatory rigidity into a recruitment engine, the Maoists established a protective human shield across the Fifth Schedule forest belt.

### 5. Statutory Implementation Deficits: PESA Act (1996) and Forest Rights Act (2006) Bottlenecks

#### Hollowing Out of Gram Sabha Consent under PESA (1996)
To rectify the historical alienation of tribal communities under the **Fifth Schedule (Article 244(1))** of the Constitution, Parliament enacted two landmark pieces of emancipatory legislation following the recommendations of the **Bhuria Committee (1995)**: the **Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)** and the **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA)**. However, As documented by the **D. Bandyopadhyay Expert Group Report (Planning Commission, 2006 — *Development Challenges in Extremist Affected Areas*)** and the **Xaxa Committee (2014)**, systemic bureaucratic dilution neutralized the counter-insurgency potential of both statutes for nearly two decades.

*   **Erosion of the Gram Sabha's Statutory Primacy:** **Section 4 of PESA (1996)** vests the **Gram Sabha** (the assembly of all adult voters in a natural tribal hamlet/habitation) with statutory ownership over **Minor Forest Produce (MFP)**, the power to prevent alienation of tribal land and restore unlawfully alienated land, control over village markets and local money-lending, and mandatory consultation prior to land acquisition for development projects or granting prospecting licenses for minor minerals.
*   **Delayed and Diluted State PESA Rules:** Several Fifth Schedule states delayed framing state-specific **PESA Rules** for over a decade or two (for instance, Chhattisgarh notified its PESA Rules only in **August 2022**, 26 years after the central Act), and even where notified, state panchayat and forest laws were never amended to align with PESA.
*   **Consultation vs. Consent Engineering:** In mineral-rich districts, district administrations routinely bypassed Section 4(i) of PESA by substituting "consultation with the Panchayat" for informed **Gram Sabha consent**, or by fabricating Gram Sabha resolutions under heavy administrative pressure to clear coal and iron-ore blocks—directly validating the Maoist propaganda narrative that constitutional safeguards were mere paper promises.

#### Rejection of Individual and Community Forest Rights under FRA (2006)
Enacted explicitly to undo the "historical injustice" committed against forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes whose ancestral rights were went unrecorded during colonial and post-colonial forest consolidations, the **Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006** recognizes both **Individual Forest Rights (IFR)** (up to 4 hectares of self-cultivated forest land occupied prior to December 13, 2005) and **Community Forest Resource (CFR)** rights (empowering the Gram Sabha to protect, regenerate, and manage community forest landscapes).

*   **High Administrative Rejection Rates:** Across LWE-affected states, Sub-Divisional and District Level Committees (dominated by Forest Department officials) rejected over **40% to 45% of initial IFR claims** on arbitrary technical grounds—such as demanding satellite imagery proof or multiple documentary evidences that illiterate forest-dwellers could not possess—without communicating written reasons for rejection to claimants to enable appeals.
*   **Stagnation of Community Forest Resource (CFR) Titles:** While states periodically distributed **IFR** pattas as individual welfare handouts, the recognition of **Community Forest Resource (CFR)** rights—which structurally transfers governance and revenue over forest landscapes from the Forest Department to the **Gram Sabha**—remained severely neglected until recent saturation drives under **Niyad Nellanar** (2024–2026) integrated forest-rights distribution directly into the counter-insurgency stabilization matrix.
*   **Inability of Governors to Exercise Fifth Schedule Powers:** Although **Paragraph 5 of the Fifth Schedule** empowers the **Governor** of a state to direct that any parliamentary or state law shall not apply to a Scheduled Area (or shall apply with specified modifications) and to make regulations for peace and good government in consultation with the **Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)**, no Governor in post-independence history proactively utilized these plenary legislative powers to halt tribal land alienation or override forest bureaucracy overreach.

### 6. Operational Anatomy of the Insurgent State: PLGA, Janathana Sarkar, Extortion Economies, and Urban Fronts

#### The Three-Tier Military Architecture of the People's Liberation Guerrilla Army (PLGA) and IED Warfare
Founded on **December 2, 2000** (initially as the People's Guerrilla Army by the PWG on the first death anniversary of three Central Committee members killed at Koyyuru, and renamed the **People's Liberation Guerrilla Army — PLGA** upon the **2004 merger**), the armed wing of the **CPI (Maoist)** was structured under the **Central Military Commission (CMC)** into a highly disciplined, three-tier force pyramid designed to execute simultaneous guerrilla and mobile warfare.

*   **Tier 1 — The Main Force (Battalions and Companies):** Comprising hardcore, full-time, uniformed combatants armed with looted military-grade weapons (AK-47s, INSAS rifles, SLRs, LMGs, 51mm/81mm mortars, and indigenous Rocket Launchers/BGLs), the **Main Force** was organized into **Military Companies** and elite **Battalions**—most notably **PLGA Battalion No. 1** in South Bastar (commanded for years by **Madvi Hidma** and later **Barse Deva**), which spearheaded every major mass-casualty ambush in Dandakaranya.
*   **Tier 2 — The Secondary Force (Guerrilla Squads / *Dalams* and Platoons):** Operating at the Divisional and Area Committee levels, these mobile *dalams* (15 to 40 cadres) carried a mix of automatic rifles and .303/12-bore weapons, enforcing area domination, collecting extortion levies, and providing flanking support to the Main Force during **Tactical Counter-Offensive Campaigns (TCOCs)**.
*   **Tier 3 — The Base Force (*Jan Militia* / People's Militia and *Sangham* Members):** Numbering in the tens of thousands at the movement's peak, the **Jan Militia** consisted of part-time village youth living in their hamlets without uniforms. Armed with bows and poisoned arrows, axes (*tangia*), muzzle-loading rifles (*bharmars*), and directional **IEDs**, the **Jan Militia** functioned as the eyes, ears, logistical porters, and outer defensive perimeter of the Main Force—tracking security force movements from hilltops, planting pressure IEDs along patrol tracks, and melting back into the civilian population after an ambush.
*   **Asymmetric IED and Booby-Trap Engineering:** Directed by its **Central Regional Command / Technical R&D Wing**, the **PLGA** compensated for its lack of artillery and airpower through sophisticated **Improvised Explosive Device (IED)** warfare. Utilizing Ammonium Nitrate Fuel Oil (ANFO), slurry explosives looted from mining quarries, and gelatin sticks, Maoist sappers deployed command-wire IEDs (buried 4–6 feet beneath black-top roads to defeat deep-search metal detectors and flip 12-tonne Mine-Protected Vehicles, as in the **2023 Aranpur Dantewada blast**), carbon-rod/syringe **pressure-mechanism IEDs** targeting dismounted foot patrols, and rudimentary **spike-hole booby traps** (*punjis*) around forest camps.

#### Janathana Sarkar (Revolutionary People's Committees) as Parallel Counter-States
In areas where the Indian state's civil and police administration was completely expelled, the **CPI (Maoist)** established embryonic parallel governments termed **Janathana Sarkars** ("People's Governments") in Dandakaranya and **Revolutionary People's Committees (RPCs)** in Bihar-Jharkhand.

*   **Organizational Structure of a Janathana Sarkar:** Each **Janathana Sarkar** administered a cluster of 15 to 30 tribal villages (population ~5,000 to 15,000) through an elected committee overseen by the local Maoist Area Committee. It operated **eight specialized administrative departments**: Financial, Defense, Agriculture, Judicial (*Jan Adalat*), Education-Culture (*Chetna Natya Manch*), Health, Public Relations, and Forest Protection.
*   **Socio-Political Indoctrination and Welfare Substitution:** The Agriculture Department organized cooperative village ponds and collective grain banks (requiring households to contribute a portion of their harvest to feed the **PLGA**); the Health Department trained barefoot medical cadres in basic malaria and anti-venom treatment; the Education Department ran mobile forest schools teaching a Maoist syllabus; and the Judicial Department resolved village land and marital disputes while executing suspected state informers.

By embedding the **Janathana Sarkar** directly into the daily agrarian reproduction of the tribal village, the Maoists ensured that a kinetic strike against the insurgency was perceived by locals as an attack on their community institutions—a bond broken only when the state's **Niyad Nellanar** scheme (2024–2026) replaced these rudimentary parallel bodies with vastly superior constitutional welfare delivery.

#### The Extortion Economy and Urban Intellectual/Front Networks
Unlike cross-border terrorist outfits in Jammu and Kashmir or ethno-insurgencies in the Northeast that rely heavily on external state sponsorship, the **CPI (Maoist)** built an indigenous, self-financing **extortion economy** estimated by intelligence agencies at **₹1,500 crore to ₹2,000 crore annually** during its peak years (2008–2016).

*   **The Four Pillars of the Maoist "Levy" (Revolutionary Tax) Economy:**
    1.  **Mining and Mineral Transport Contractors:** Systematic extortion of a fixed percentage levy per tonne of coal, iron ore, and bauxite extracted or transported by private mining companies and truckers in Jharkhand (CCL/BCCL belts), Odisha, and Chhattisgarh (Bailadila/Kanker).
    2.  **Tendu Patta and Minor Forest Produce Contractors:** Seasonal extortion of millions of rupees in cash from *tendu* leaf (used for *bidi* rolling) and bamboo contractors across Bastar, Gadchiroli, and Telangana in exchange for permitting collection operations during the critical May–June harvest window.
    3.  **Public Infrastructure Contractors:** Demanding a 5% to 15% protection levy from PWD road, bridge, telecom, and irrigation contractors operating in LWE-affected districts, burning the equipment of non-compliant firms.
    4.  **Illicit Opium and Ganja Cultivation:** In pockets of Jharkhand (Chatra, Latehar) and the **Andhra–Odisha Border (AOB / Malkangiri-Koraput)**, Maoist cadres taxed illicit poppy and cannabis (*ganja*—notably the *Sheelavathi* strain) cultivation and narco-trafficking syndicates to compensate for shrinking mining revenues.
*   **Urban Movement and Front Organizations (*Urban Perspective: Our Work in Urban Areas*, 2004):** Recognizing that rural guerrilla bases cannot triumph without urban support, the **CPI (Maoist)** adopted a formal **Urban Perspective Plan** in **2004**. Rather than engaging in urban armed action, the urban network operates through clandestine **Fractional Committees** embedded within seemingly legal **Front Organizations** (cultural troupes, radical student unions, legal-aid collectives, and pseudo-human rights fronts). These urban networks fulfill four critical strategic functions:
    1.  **Lawfare and Information Warfare:** Filing coordinated public interest litigations and amplifying atrocity narratives to paralyze kinetic security operations and tie down counter-insurgency commanders in judicial inquiries.
    2.  **Ideological Recruitment:** Identifying, radicalizing, and funneling scientifically and technically educated urban university students into the forest leadership cadre to replenish aging Politburo and Central Committee ranks (such as engineering graduates like **Basavaraju**).
    3.  **Logistical and Medical Supply Chains:** Procuring restricted dual-use materials—electronic detonators, gelatin sticks, ammonium nitrate, satellite phones, VHF communication sets, lathe machines for rocket launchers, and specialized surgical/medical supplies—from urban markets and providing safe houses for injured senior leaders in cities like Pune, Hyderabad, Ranchi, Delhi, and Kolkata.
    4.  **Financial Laundering:** Converting cash extortion levies collected in forest camps into white assets through urban real-estate investments, shell companies, transport businesses, and benami bank accounts—a financial pipeline systematically dismantled post-2017 by the **National Investigation Agency (NIA)** Terror Funding and Fake Currency (TFFC) Cell and the **Enforcement Directorate (ED)** under the **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA)** and the **Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA)**.

## III. State Counter-Insurgency Architecture: The SAMADHAN Doctrine, Specialized Forces, and Constitutional Guardrails

### 7. The "Security and Development" Paradigm and the SAMADHAN Operational Doctrine

#### Deconstructing the Eight Pillars of the SAMADHAN Doctrine (2017–2026)
From **1967 until the early 2000s**, India's response to Left-Wing Extremism suffered from a severe doctrinal schizophrenia: state governments alternated between treating the insurgency as a routine "law-and-order problem" (relying on poorly trained, under-equipped district police armed with bolt-action .303 rifles) and viewing it as a purely socio-economic welfare issue that could be solved without kinetic area domination. Following the **2006 National Policy and Action Plan** and the **2010 Chidambaram "Clear, Hold, and Build" pivot**, the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) crystallized a comprehensive, intelligence-driven operational doctrine in **May 2017** under the acronym **SAMADHAN**, which subsequently anchored the **2024–2026 final eradication offensive**:

*   **S — Smart Leadership:** Instituting clear political resolve at the Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) and Chief Ministerial levels alongside accountable operational command among IG/DIG/SP-level police and CAPF officers in LWE theaters, eliminating ambiguity over the state's monopoly of force.
*   **A — Aggressive Strategy:** Transitioning security forces from a reactive posture (waiting inside fortified police stations to be ambushed during road-opening tasks) to an **offensive area-domination strategy**—pushing **Forward Operating Bases (FOBs)** continuously deeper into previously unentered Maoist core zones.
*   **M — Motivation and Training:** Upgrading jungle-warfare training capacity through **Counter-Insurgency and Anti-Terrorism (CIAT) Schools** and the famed **Counter-Terrorism and Jungle Warfare (CTJW) College at Kanker** (established by Brig. B.K. Ponwar under the motto *"Fight a Guerrilla like a Guerrilla"*), alongside enhanced hardship allowances (at par with Siachen/J&K deployments) and immediate air-ambulance casualty evacuation via IAF/BSF Mi-17 helicopters.
*   **A — Actionable Intelligence:** Shifting from vague general alerts to pinpoint **Technical Intelligence (TECHINT)** (intercepting VHF/LoRa signals, tracking mobile metadata, and deploying **NTRO/CRPF long-endurance UAVs** with foliage-penetrating thermal imaging) fused with localized **Human Intelligence (HUMINT)** gathered by surrendered cadres in the **District Reserve Guard (DRG)**.
*   **D — Dashboard-Based KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) and KRAs (Key Result Areas):** Monitoring real-time spatial progress—number of new FOBs constructed, road kilometers black-topped, 4G towers energized, NIA/ED financial attachments executed, and top-bounty commanders neutralized—through centralized MHA and state GIS dashboards.
*   **H — Harnessing Technology:** Deploying **Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR)** against deep-buried command IEDs, satellite-based GIS mapping of unsurveyed terrains like **Abujhmad**, biometric tracking of surrendered cadres, and AI-enabled financial trail analysis.
*   **A — Action Plan for Each Theater:** Rejecting a one-size-fits-all blueprint in favor of customized inter-state operational plans for distinct micro-theaters—such as **Operation Octopus** for **Budha Pahar** (Jharkhand-Chhattisgarh), **Operation Prahar** and **Operation Black Forest** for **South Bastar/Karreguttalu**, and targeted sweeps in the **AOB** and **MMC (Maharashtra-Madhya Pradesh-Chhattisgarh)** zones.
*   **N — No Access to Financing:** Operationalizing **Multi-Disciplinary Groups (MDGs)** comprising the **NIA**, **ED**, **Income Tax Department**, **FIU-IND**, and State Special Branches to prosecute extortion-paying contractors, attach assets of Maoist front operatives under **UAPA Section 25**, and choke off cash, detonator, and weapon supply chains.

#### Fiscal and Infrastructure Instruments: SRE, SCA, Eklavya Schools (EMRS), Road Connectivity (RCPLWEA), and 4G Telecom Saturation
To operationalize the **"Security and Development" holistic paradigm**—ensuring that every square kilometer cleared by security forces is immediately integrated into the constitutional welfare grid—the Union Government deployed a synchronized matrix of fiscal and physical infrastructure schemes across LWE-affected districts:

*   **Security Related Expenditure (SRE) Scheme:** A central reimbursement mechanism under the umbrella *Modernisation of Police Forces (MPF)* scheme through which the Union Government reimburses state governments (typically on a 60:40 or 90:10 basis) for operational expenditures incurred on training, logistics, insurance, ex-gratia compensation for security personnel and civilian victims of LWE violence, community policing, and **Surrender-cum-Rehabilitation** incentives (providing immediate financial grants in fixed deposits of ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh plus monthly stipends and vocational training for surrendered cadres).
*   **Special Central Assistance (SCA) for Most LWE-Affected Districts:** Launched in **2017** to fill critical public infrastructure and livelihood gaps that cannot be met through regular sector schemes, **SCA** provides untied/flexible block grants (₹30 crore per annum for "Most Affected Districts" and ₹10 crore per annum for "Districts of Concern") managed by a District-Level Committee headed by the District Collector alongside the Superintendent of Police and District Forest Officer, ensuring zero bureaucratic delay in building last-mile culverts, solar micro-grids, health sub-centers, and Anganwadis.
*   **Fortified Police Stations (FPS) Scheme:** To eliminate the vulnerability of isolated rural police stations being overrun by Maoist **Main Force** columns for weapon looting, the MHA funded the construction of over **586 Fortified Police Stations** featuring blast-resistant perimeter walls, interlocking corner bunkers, overhead anti-grenade wire meshes, and secure communication rooms.
*   **Road Requirement Plan (RRP-I) and Road Connectivity Project for LWE Affected Areas (RCPLWEA):** Because road construction converts insurgent sanctuaries into accessible administrative zones, over **14,000+ km** of all-weather black-top roads and critical bridges (such as the **Gurupriya Bridge** in Malkangiri, Odisha, which ended the 50-year isolation of 151 villages in the *Cut-Off Area*, and the **Palli-Barsoor** road cutting directly into the heart of **Abujhmad**) were constructed under security cover provided by CAPF Road Opening Parties (ROPs).
*   **Telecom Saturation (Mobile Connectivity Phase-I & II / 4G Saturation):** Funded via the *Digital Bharat Nidhi* (formerly Universal Service Obligation Fund — USOF), over **6,500+ mobile towers** (upgraded to high-speed 4G) were erected inside fortified security camps across Bastar and other LWE pockets, enabling both real-time tactical data transmission for security patrols and **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) / UPI banking** for tribal citizens.
*   **Educational and Financial Inclusion (Eklavya Model Residential Schools — EMRS):** To break the Maoist monopoly over tribal youth indoctrination (*Bal Sanghams*), the Union Government accelerated the establishment of **Eklavya Model Residential Schools (EMRS)** in every block with more than 50% ST population and at least 20,000 tribal persons, complemented by Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs), Skill Development Centers (*Roshni* scheme), and the opening of hundreds of brick-and-mortar bank branches, ATMs, and **India Post Payments Bank (IPPB)** touchpoints within a 5-km radius of every village.

### 8. Asymmetric Counter-Guerrilla Force Design: Greyhounds, CoBRA, DRG, and the Bastarriya Battalion

#### The Andhra Pradesh Greyhounds Model: Small-Team Pseudo-Guerrilla Hound Tactics
Conventional infantry and standard paramilitary battalions—trained to move in large, road-bound columns with heavy logistical tails—suffered severe attrition when deployed into the dense jungles of Dandakaranya. The paradigm shift in Indian counter-LWE force structure originated in **1989**, when visionary IPS officer **K.S. Vyas** founded the **Greyhounds** in undivided Andhra Pradesh as an elite, self-contained, commando-style jungle-warfare force specifically engineered to hunt **PWG** *dalams* on their own terrain.

*   **Organizational and Selection Rigor:** The **Greyhounds** operate as an independent, non-conventional force recruiting the fittest volunteer officers and jawans from the state police on a fixed deputation tenure (typically 3 to 5 years to maintain a youthful age profile under 30–35 years), paying them a **50%–60% basic pay risk allowance** and subjecting them to grueling jungle-survival, night-firing, and reflex-shooting regimens at their 500-acre campus near Hyderabad.
*   **Small-Team "Hound" Operational Geometry:** Instead of deploying clumsy company-sized sweeps that alert Maoist **Jan Militia** lookouts miles in advance, the **Greyhounds** operate in small, agile, self-sustained **Assault Teams of 15 to 30 commandos**. Carrying 5 to 7 days of dry rations, night-vision devices, GPS trackers, and high-firepower automatic weapons on their backs, these teams infiltrate the forest silently at night, move off-track along ridgelines (avoiding mined dirt paths), live inside the jungle exactly like a Maoist *dalam*, and strike insurgent camps with overwhelming surprise firepower based on pinpoint **Special Intelligence Branch (SIB)** inputs.
*   **Strategic Victory in the Andhra-Telangana Theater:** By coupling the **Greyhounds** (the kinetic sword) with the **Special Intelligence Branch — SIB** (a dedicated, single-mandate intelligence agency tracking every Maoist cadre's biography, family links, and communication signatures), Andhra Pradesh systematically decimated the **PWG/CPI (Maoist)** state committee between **1998 and 2008**, culminating in the **October 2016 Ramaguda encounter** in the AOB *Cut-Off Area* that wiped out 30 top Maoists and rendered Andhra Pradesh and Telangana virtually free of resident insurgent formations.

#### Federal and Local Indigenous Force Multiplication: CRPF CoBRA, Chhattisgarh DRG, and the Bastarriya Battalion
Drawing directly from the **Greyhounds** operational blueprint, both the Union Government and the worst-affected states engineered specialized federal and indigenous counter-guerrilla forces tailored to the human and physical geography of the **Red Corridor**:

*   **CRPF CoBRA (Commando Battalion for Resolute Action — Established 2008–2009):** Raised as a specialized jungle-warfare wing within the **Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF)**, the **10 CoBRA Battalions (Battalions 201 to 210)** are permanently stationed across the primary LWE theaters (Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Bihar, Maharashtra, and Assam). Equipped with Israeli X-95/Tavor assault rifles, sniper systems, thermal sights, and micro-UAVs, **CoBRA** units serve as the federal spearhead for high-risk deep-forest heliborne insertions and surgical strikes alongside state forces.
*   **Chhattisgarh District Reserve Guard (DRG — Institutionalized 2008–2015):** Recognizing that external central forces initially struggled with the **Gondi, Halbi, Dorli, and Madia** tribal dialects, hyper-local forest micro-topography, and extreme heat/malaria in Bastar, the Chhattisgarh Police raised the **District Reserve Guard (DRG)** across all seven districts of Bastar division. Recruiting local tribal youth alongside **surrendered former Maoist cadres** (*"turning the insurgent's knowledge against the insurgency"*), the **DRG** proved to be the single most decisive tactical game-changer in **Chhattisgarh**. Because DRG jawans know the exact forest trails, hidden waterholes, cave systems, and tactical SOPs of the **PLGA**, they neutralized the Maoists' foundational advantage of terrain familiarity and local linguistic anonymity—spearheading virtually every major breakthrough from **2020 to 2026**, including the **May 2025 neutralization of General Secretary Basavaraju** in Abujhmad.
*   **CRPF Bastarriya Battalion (241 Battalion — Commissioned May 2018):** To integrate local tribal representation directly into the federal paramilitary architecture while providing formal central government employment to alienated youth, the **CRPF** raised the **241 Bastarriya Battalion** in **2018**, recruiting **100% of its combat personnel** (including a **33% female commando contingent**) exclusively from the local Scheduled Tribes of the four worst-hit districts of Bastar (**Bijapur, Dantewada, Narayanpur, and Sukma**), after relaxing physical height/weight norms to match indigenous anthropometry.
*   **Parallel State Special Forces:** Complementing these formations are Maharashtra's elite **C-60 Commandos** (raised in **1990** by K.P. Raghuvanshi in Gadchiroli with local tribal recruits, famed for the **2018 Kasnasur operation** and the **November 2021 Mardintola encounter** that eliminated Central Committee member Milind Teltumbde alongside 25 Maoists), Odisha's **Special Operations Group (SOG)**, and Jharkhand's **Jharkhand Jaguar (STF)**.

### 9. Constitutional Boundaries of Counter-Insurgency: The Salwa Judum Experiment and *Nandini Sundar v. State of Chhattisgarh* (2011)

#### The Pathology of State-Armed Civilian Vigilantism (Salwa Judum and SPOs)
In **June 2005**, confronted with expanding Maoist **Janathana Sarkars** in Dantewada and Bijapur districts and lacking trained counter-guerrilla police units like the modern DRG, the Chhattisgarh state government endorsed and armed a controversial counter-insurgency militia known as **Salwa Judum** (a Gondi term translating to *"Purification Hunt"* or *"Peace March"*), led politically by local MLA Mahendra Karma.

*   **Outsourcing the Monopoly of Violence via SPO Appointments:** Under **Section 17 of the Chhattisgarh Police Act, 2007** (and earlier under the colonial Police Act of 1861), the state administration appointed thousands of poorly educated, minimally trained local tribal youth (including minors and victims of Maoist violence) as **Special Police Officers (SPOs)**—colloquially called *Koya Commandos*—handing them .303 rifles and a meager monthly honorarium of ₹1,500 to ₹3,000 without formal police service conditions, accountability, or legal training.
*   **Strategic Hamleting and Spiral of Retaliatory Violence:** **Salwa Judum** militias, accompanied by security forces, marched through interior forest villages forcing inhabitants to abandon their ancestral hamlets and relocate into 23 roadside relief camps (a controversial "strategic hamleting" model that displaced over 50,000 Adivasis), treating any villager who remained in the forest as a Maoist militant. While the campaign temporarily disrupted Maoist village networks, undisciplined **Salwa Judum** vigilantes committed widespread arson and human rights abuses, which in turn triggered ferocious retaliatory massacres by the **PLGA** against relief camps (such as the **2006 Errabore** and **2007 Rani Bodli** attacks) and culminated in the Maoist assassination of Mahendra Karma at **Jiram Ghati in May 2013**.

#### Constitutional Jurisprudence of *Nandini Sundar v. State of Chhattisgarh* (2011): Articles 14 and 21 vs. Counter-Insurgency Outsourcing
In a landmark constitutional intervention on **July 5, 2011**, a two-judge Bench of the Supreme Court of India (comprising **Justice B. Sudershan Reddy** and **Justice Surinder Singh Nijjar**) delivered its judgment in ***Nandini Sundar & Ors. v. State of Chhattisgarh* [(2011) 7 SCC 547]**, striking down the state-sponsored arming of civilian vigilantes and **SPOs** in counter-insurgency operations as unconstitutional.

*   **Violation of Article 14 (Equality Before Law):** The Supreme Court held that appointing poorly educated tribal youth with minimal training as **SPOs** to perform frontline counter-insurgency duties alongside regular police officers—while paying them a fraction of regular wages, denying them statutory service protections, and exposing them to vastly higher mortality risks without commensurate training—constituted an arbitrary, irrational classification violating **Article 14** of the Constitution.
*   **Violation of Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Dignity):** The Court ruled that equipping inadequately trained youths with lethal firearms and unleashing them in counter-insurgency operations endangered both the **Article 21** right to life of the **SPOs** themselves (turning them into "cannon fodder" against battle-hardened **PLGA** guerrillas) and the **Article 21** rights of unarmed civilian villagers subjected to vigilante excesses.
*   **Prohibition of State abdication of Constitutional Duty:** Framing its ratio within Westphalian constitutionalism, the Bench declared that the State cannot outsource its sovereign monopoly of force to private vigilante groups like **Salwa Judum** or *Koya Commandos*, Ordered the immediate disarming of all SPOs used in counter-insurgency operations, halted central **SRE** funding for such irregular militias, and directed the vacation of all school buildings and hostels occupied by security forces.
*   **Institutional Evolution from SPOs to the Constitutional DRG:** Crucially, the ***Nandini Sundar (2011)*** judgment forced the Chhattisgarh government and the MHA to abandon informal vigilantism and institutionalize a legally compliant, professionally trained indigenous force structure. Eligible former auxiliary personnel were systematically absorbed into regular, statutory police cadres with full constitutional accountability, pay scales, and intensive training at the Kanker CTJW College—giving birth to the professionalized **District Reserve Guard (DRG)** and *Bastar Fighters*, which achieved through constitutional professionalism what **Salwa Judum** could never achieve through lawless vigilantism.

## IV. The 2024–2026 Decisive Endgame: FOB Saturation, Niyad Nellanar, Operation Kagar, and the March 2026 Eradication Milestone

### 10. Spatial Re-Conquest: Forward Operating Bases (FOBs) and the Niyad Nellanar Saturation Model

#### The Geometry of Forward Operating Base (FOB) Carpet-Grid Saturation
Between **2019 and 2026**, the Indian security establishment executed a decisive spatial transformation in counter-insurgency strategy: replacing seasonal, hit-and-withdraw jungle raids with the permanent, year-round physical occupation of Maoist core areas through a dense carpet grid of **Forward Operating Bases (FOBs)**.

*   **The Tactical Mechanics of FOB Construction:** Historically, security forces operated only during the dry months and retreated before the monsoon when flooded rivers (*Indravati*, *Chintavagu*) isolated the interior, allowing the **PLGA** to regroup and re-mine the terrain. Beginning in **2019** and accelerating dramatically after **December 2023**, combined **CRPF, CoBRA, BSF, ITBP, and Chhattisgarh Police** columns pushed directly into the heart of **South Bastar (Sukma, Bijapur, Dantewada)** and **Abujhmad (Narayanpur)**, establishing **over 300+ new Forward Operating Bases** (including 46+ camps in 2024 and dozens more in 2025) within a distance of **4 to 6 km** of one another.
*   **24-Hour Rapid Camp Fortification:** Utilizing prefabricated HESCO bastions, concertina wire, thermal-imaging watchtowers, drone-jamming grids, helipads, and mechanized earthmovers under heavy **CoBRA/DRG** outer-ring protection, security engineers erected fully defensible **FOBs** inside former Maoist tactical headquarters—such as **Puvarti** (the native village of dreaded PLGA Commander **Madvi Hidma** in Sukma, captured in **February 2024**), **Tekulagudem**, **Gundrem**, **Rekhapalli**, and **Ghalgam** (near the Karreguttalu Hills)—within 24 to 48 hours.
*   **Shattering the Insurgent Logistics and Mobility Matrix:** Each **FOB** projects a permanent **5-km to 10-km kinetic dominance and UAV surveillance radius**. By overlapping these radii across the **Dandakaranya** and **Abujhmad** map, the FOB grid severed the Maoists' traditional north-south and east-west movement corridors, denied them access to village grain and medicine supplies, and forced large **PLGA** formations to fracture into small, exhausted bands constantly on the run from UAV thermal sensors.

#### Niyad Nellanar ("Your Good Village") and Niyad Nellanar 2.0: Converting Security Camps into Developmental Nodes
A kinetic **Forward Operating Base** surrounded by a hostile or alienated civilian population remains a besieged garrison. To fuse the **FOB** grid with immediate constitutional welfare saturation, the Chhattisgarh government (in coordination with the MHA) launched the flagship **Niyad Nellanar** scheme (a local Dandami Madia/Gondi phrase meaning *"Your Good Village"*) in **February 2024**, modeled on the Union Government's **PM-JANMAN** mission for Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs).

*   **TheIntegrated "Security Camp as Development Camp" Architecture:** Under **Niyad Nellanar**, every newly established **Forward Operating Base (FOB)** functions simultaneously as a **multi-faceted integrated development hub** for all tribal villages located within a **5-km radius** of the camp—a coverage radius formally expanded from **5 km to 10 km** in **April 2025** following a high-level review chaired by the Union Home Minister in Raipur.
*   **100% Saturation of 25 Basic Infrastructural Amenities and 32 Individual Entitlements:** Rather than waiting for villagers to travel to distant block headquarters, district administration teams camp inside the **FOB** perimeter to deliver **over 25 community amenities** and **32 individual beneficiary schemes** on a mission-mode saturation basis:
    *   *Identity and Financial Inclusion:* Instant camp-based issuance of **Aadhaar cards**, voter IDs, ration cards, caste/domicile certificates, and zero-balance Jan Dhan bank passbooks.
    *   *Housing, Energy, and Nutrition:* Replacement of kutcha mud huts with pucca houses under **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY)**, free **PM Ujjwala** LPG cooking gas connections, solar home lighting/grid electricity, and *Har Ghar Jal* piped drinking water.
    *   *Land Tenures and Livelihoods:* Immediate verification and distribution of **Forest Rights Act (FRA) certificates**, MGNREGA wage employment, minor irrigation pumps, and **₹1 crore special development grants** for villages declared completely "Naxal-free" through 100% cadre surrender.
    *   *Social & Physical Connectivity:* Opening of primary schools, **Anganwadi centers**, and **Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** (health clinics), alongside linking isolated interior villages via the **Chief Minister's Bus Service Scheme** and **4G telecom towers**.
*   **Empirical Transformation and Niyad Nellanar 2.0 (May 2026):** By **May 2026**, administrative reviews across Bastar division confirmed that **85% of remote settlements** were connected by all-weather roads, **3,056 villages** gained mobile connectivity, **425 previously inaccessible interior villages** were integrated into public bus transport routes, and **5,016 primary schools** plus **8,947 Anganwadi centers** were operationalized—prompting the rollout of **Niyad Nellanar 2.0** in **May 2026** to transition from basic amenity saturation to long-term tribal entrepreneurship and livelihood consolidation.

### 11. Kinetic Decapitation: Operation Kagar, Operation Black Forest in Abujhmad/Karreguttalu, and Politburo Collapse

#### Operation Kagar (2024–2026) and the Penetration of the Unsurveyed Abujhmad Citadel
With the **FOB** and **Niyad Nellanar** grid constricting the insurgents' spatial periphery, the Union Ministry of Home Affairs and state security forces launched **Operation Kagar** (Hindi for *"The Brink / Final Edge"*) in **early 2024**—mobilizing an integrated multi-force theater deployment of **60,000 to 100,000 personnel** across the **DRG, STF, CRPF, CoBRA, BSF, and ITBP** to dismantle the last surviving military and ideological strongholds of the **CPI (Maoist)** ahead of the **March 31, 2026 eradication deadline**.

*   **Dismantling the Abujhmad Myth:** For four decades, the 4,000-sq-km **Abujhmad** forest was treated as an impenetrable fortress. Under **Operation Kagar**, joint **DRG, STF, and BSF/ITBP/CRPF** columns—guided by foliage-penetrating UAV thermal scans, satellite GIS terrain models, and surrendered cadre HUMINT—launched continuous, multi-axis deep-penetration strikes directly into the core of **Abujhmad** (spanning Narayanpur, Kanker, Bijapur, and Dantewada):
    *   **Kanker (Chhotebethiya / Kalper) Encounter (April 16, 2024):** A surgical joint strike by the **DRG** and **BSF** neutralized **29 hardcore Maoists**, including senior DKSZC commander Shankar Rao (carrying a ₹25 lakh bounty), with zero security force fatalities.
    *   **Thulthuli–Gawadi (Abujhmad, Narayanpur-Dantewada Border) Operation (October 4, 2024):** In the largest single-day counter-LWE blow in Chhattisgarh's history up to that point, **DRG** and **STF** commandos encircled a combined Company No. 6 and East Bastar Division formation inside Abujhmad, neutralizing **38 uniformed PLGA cadres** (carrying a cumulative bounty of ₹2.62 crore) and recovering a massive cache of AK-47s, INSAS rifles, LMGs, and BGLs.

#### Operation Black Forest (April–May 2025) in the Karreguttalu Hills and the Neutralization of General Secretary Basavaraju
As relentless pressure in **Abujhmad** and **South Bastar** uprooted the Maoists' traditional camps, intelligence inputs in early **2025** revealed that over 100 to 120 elite fighters—comprising **PLGA Battalion No. 1**, the **Telangana State Committee**, and top Politburo/Central Committee leaders—had consolidated inside the heavily mined caves and rock shelters of the **Karreguttalu Hills (KGH)** along the **Chhattisgarh (Bijapur)–Telangana (Mulugu)** border, while the supreme commander of the movement remained in deeper **Abujhmad**.

*   **Execution of Operation Black Forest (April 21 – May 11, 2025):** Launched at first light on **April 21, 2025**, using the **Ghalgam FOB** as the forward tactical launchpad, roughly **10,000 personnel** from **CRPF, CoBRA, Chhattisgarh DRG, and Chhattisgarh/Telangana STF**—supported by IAF/CRPF Mi-17 helicopters, micro-UAVs, and real-time GIS IED mapping—executed a grueling **21-day continuous high-summer siege** (in 40°C+ temperatures) across the 60 km × 20 km **Karreguttalu mountain ridge**. By its termination on **May 11, 2025**, **Operation Black Forest** had completely cleared the **Karreguttalu Hills**, neutralizing **31 hardcore Maoists**, arresting 22 cadres, and unearthing **450 IEDs, 2 tonnes of explosives**, and underground weapon factories.
*   **The Decapitation Strike — Elimination of General Secretary Basavaraju (May 21, 2025):** Ten days after the conclusion of the Karreguttalu sweep, on **May 21, 2025**, **Chhattisgarh DRG** units executed the ultimate decapitation strike of the 58-year insurgency inside the **Abujhmad** forests (Narayanpur district): neutralizing **Nambala Keshava Rao (alias Basavaraju / Gaganna)**—the **General Secretary of the CPI (Maoist)**, head of its **Politburo** and **Central Committee**, former chief of the **Central Military Commission (CMC)** for nearly two decades, and mastermind of virtually every major Maoist ambush since 2004 (carrying a ₹10 crore+ cumulative bounty)—alongside **27 other Maoist cadres**.

#### The Late-2025 Politburo Surrender Cascade and the Societal Fault Lines of the CPI (Maoist)
The elimination of **General Secretary Basavaraju** in **May 2025**, combined with the loss of both **Abujhmad** and the **Karreguttalu Hills**, shattered the psychological, military, and organizational cohesion of the **CPI (Maoist)**, triggering an unprecedented cascade of surrenders across the surviving leadership and rank-and-file throughout late **2025** and early **2026**:

*   **Collapse of the Politburo and Central Committee:** On **October 14, 2025**, senior Politburo and Central Committee member **Mallojula Venugopal Rao (alias Bhupati / Sonu)**—the brother of slain Maoist leader Kishenji and chief ideologue of the Dandakaranya/Maharashtra theater—surrendered before Maharashtra Police in **Gadchiroli** along with **60–61 armed cadres and 54 weapons**. Weeks later, in **November 2025**, Central Committee member **Pulluri Prasad Rao (alias Chandranna)** and Telangana State Committee member **Bandi Prakash (alias Prabhath)** surrendered in Hyderabad, while senior commander **Thakkallapally Vasudeva Rao (alias Ashanna)** surrendered in Chhattisgarh with roughly 200 cadres and a massive cache of weapons.
*   **Exposure of the Internal Leadership–Cadre Fault Line:** This terminal collapse exposed a deep sociological cleavage within the movement: for four decades, the ideological Politburo and Central Committee had been overwhelmingly dominated by ageing, upper- and dominant-caste leaders from the Andhra/Telangana plains, whereas **over 95% to 99% of the fighting and dying PLGA foot soldiers** were local Gond, Madia, and Koya Adivasis from Bastar. Once the kinetic shield of the **PLGA** was broken by **DRG** and **CoBRA** operations and attractive state **Surrender-cum-Rehabilitation policies** (reinforced by the ₹1 crore "Naxal-free village" incentive) offered a viable exit, nearly **2,000 Maoist cadres surrendered in 2025 alone**, followed by over **350+ cadre neutralizations**, reducing the active Central Committee to scattered, non-operational remnants.

### 12. The March 2026 LWE Eradication Milestone, Post-Insurgency Consolidation, and Structural Bridge

#### Empirical Assessment of the March 31, 2026 Eradication Milestone: From 126 Districts to Zero Territorial Sanctuaries
In **August 2024**, at an inter-state security coordination summit in Raipur, the Union Home Minister established **March 31, 2026** as the hard national deadline to completely eradicate armed Left-Wing Extremism from India. Driven by the synchronization of **Operation Kagar**, **Operation Black Forest**, **FOB** saturation, and **Niyad Nellanar**, the spatial contraction of the insurgency accelerated at an exponential rate between **2024 and March 2026**:

*   **Rapid De-Notification of LWE-Affected Districts:** From a historical peak of **126 SRE-notified districts** in **2013–2014** (and 106 in 2015), the number of LWE-affected districts dropped to **38 in April 2024**, plummeted to **18 in April 2025** (with "Most Affected Districts" halving from 12 to 6: Bijapur, Kanker, Narayanpur, Sukma, West Singhbhum, and Gadchiroli), and contracted further in **October 2025** to just **11 districts total**, with only **3 districts in the entire country** (**Bijapur, Sukma, and Narayanpur** in Chhattisgarh) remaining in the "Most Affected" category as Andhra Pradesh and Telangana exited the LWE list entirely.
*   **Formal Achievement of the March 2026 Milestone:** On **March 30–31, 2026**, the Union Government announced in Parliament that India had achieved its scheduled target of eradicating armed Left-Wing Extremism, with the Ministry of Home Affairs formally communicating to the states that **no district in the country remained under active Maoist territorial control or the standard LWE-affected classification**—marking the end of a 59-year armed insurgency that had claimed approximately 17,000 lives since 1967.

#### The Post-Kinetic Challenge: Preventing Ideological Resurgence and Securing Constitutional Governance
While the **March 2026 milestone** marks the definitive military defeat of the **PLGA** and the territorial eradication of Maoist **Base Areas**, strategic first principles dictate that the kinetic suppression of an insurgency is only durable if the structural conditions that incubated it are permanently transformed. In the post-March 2026 era, Indian internal security planners confront three non-kinetic consolidation imperatives:

1.  **Preventing Extractive Backsliding in Reclaimed Fifth Schedule Areas:** As previously inaccessible mineral-rich hills in **Bastar (Bailadila, Rowghat, Abujhmad)** and **Gadchiroli (Surjagarh)** open up to large-scale iron-ore and industrial mining following the dismantling of Maoist **Area Denial**, the state must scrupulously enforce **PESA (1996) Gram Sabha consent**, **Forest Rights Act (2006) CFR titles**, and equitable **District Mineral Foundation (DMF)** revenue sharing. Any return to predatory land acquisition or forest bureaucracy high-handedness risks re-igniting localized tribal alienation.
2.  **Sustainable Socio-Economic Reintegration of Surrendered Cadres and DRG Auxiliaries:** With thousands of former Maoist cadres having surrendered between **2024 and 2026**, ensuring their long-term physical security against residual vendettas, disbursing promised rehabilitation packages without bureaucratic corruption, and transitioning them into formal livelihoods under **Niyad Nellanar 2.0** is vital to prevent criminalization or splinter band formation.
3.  **Monitoring Residual Urban Ideological Networks:** Although the rural guerrilla army (**PLGA**) has collapsed, the **urban ideological and front ecosystem** remains an active counter-intelligence concern. Continuous vigilance by the **IB**, **NIA**, and State Special Branches—Strictly bounded by constitutional due process under **Articles 19 and 21**—remains essential to prevent ideological remnants from exploiting urban labor, agrarian, or environmental fault lines.

#### Outgoing Structural Bridge to Part 3: From Domestic Ideological Insurgency to Cross-Border State-Sponsored Proxy Warfare
The neutralization of Left-Wing Extremism demonstrates how the Indian state successfully resolved a **domestic, endogenously financed ideological insurgency** by combining indigenous counter-guerrilla force design (**Greyhounds, DRG, CoBRA**), spatial infrastructure saturation (**FOBs** and **Niyad Nellanar**), and financial choking under **SAMADHAN** and **Operation Kagar**. However, when an asymmetric conflict is no longer confined to domestic jungles and indigenous extortion, but is instead fueled, armed, and directed by a **hostile, nuclear-armed neighboring state apparatus** utilizing cross-border terrain, global terror-financing networks, and sub-conventional plausible deniability, the strategic calculus changes fundamentally.
