### Part 1: Prehistoric Foundations, Lithic Technologies, Rock Art, and Neolithic-Chalcolithic Agrarian Enclaves
- Geological transitions (Pleistocene to Holocene) and paleoclimatic shifts
- Lower Paleolithic industries (Soan pebble-core and Acheulian handaxe-cleaver traditions at Attirampakkam, Hunsgi, Didwana)
- Middle Paleolithic flake-tool industries
- Upper Paleolithic blade-and-burin industries (Kurnool bone tools, Patne ostrich eggshell beads)
- Mesolithic foraging economies, animal domestication, and geometric/non-geometric microlithic toolkits (Bagor, Adamgarh, Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, Langhnaj)
- **Prehistoric rock art as early cognitive surplus:** petroglyphs and cave paintings at Bhimbetka, Adamgarh, Lakhudiyar, and Kupgallu
- Zooarchaeological analysis of hunting rituals, x-ray animal motifs, fertility symbols, and communal dance
- **The Neolithic Revolution:** agrarian domestication at Mehrgarh (wheat, barley, cotton, mud-brick granaries, early dentistry), Vindhyan/Gangetic rice domestication (Lahuradewa, Koldihwa, Chopani Mando), Kashmir pit-dwellings and bone tools (Burzahom, Gufkral), Bihar/Northeastern bone and stone celts (Chirand, Daojali Hading), and South Indian Neolithic pastoral ash-mounds (Utnur, Piklihal, Maski, Brahmagiri, Hallur)
- **Chalcolithic agrarian enclaves and early copper metallurgy:** Ahar-Banas (Gilund), Kayatha, Malwa (Navdatoli), Jorwe (Inamgaon, Daimabad), Copper Hoard Culture, and the Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) horizon.

### Part 2: The Harappan Bronze Age: Urban Spatial Morphology, Metrology, Craft Specialization, and Systemic Collapse
- Material genesis from Early Harappan regional cultures (Amri-Nal, Kot Diji, Sothi-Siswal, Damb Sadaat)
- **Urban spatial morphology:** citadel-lower town dichotomy (and tripartite division at Dholavira), gridiron street networks, standardized burnt-brick metrology (1:2:4 ratio), granaries, and hydraulic engineering (Mohenjo-daro Great Bath and covered drainage, Dholavira rock-cut water reservoirs and storm drains, Lothal tidal dockyard basin, Kalibangan ploughed field and fire altars, Banawali radial streets, Rakhigarhi, Surkotada)
- **Agrarian base:** rabi multi-cropping, Shortugai canal irrigation, plough agriculture, and cotton cultivation
- **Harappan aesthetics, metrology, and craft specialization:** bronze lost-wax (cire perdue) casting (Dancing Girl, Daimabad bronzes), stone statuary (Bearded Priest-King, Male Torso), steatite glyptic art (Pashupati and unicorn seals), undeciphered boustrophedon script, binary-decimal chert weights, terra-cotta figurines, Black-on-Red wheel-turned pottery, and bead-making/shell-working factories (Chanhudaro, Lothal, Nageshwar, Balakot)
- Internal resource procurement (Khetri copper, Kolar gold, Shortugai lapis lazuli) and maritime trade with Mesopotamia (Meluhha, Dilmun, Magan)
- **De-urbanization dynamics:** 4.2-kiloyear aridification event, hydro-climatic shifts, Ghaggar-Hakra desiccation, disappearance of script/weights, and localization into Late Harappan horizons (Cemetery H, Jhukar, Rangpur, Lustrous Red Ware).

### Part 3: The Vedic Corpus, Iron Metallurgy, Pastoral-Agrarian Transitions, and the Sacrificial Ritual Economy (1500–600 BCE)
- **Indo-Aryan linguistic migrations, the Sapta Sindhu geography, and the Rigvedic pastoral economy:** cattle-wealth dynamics (Gaveshana, Gavishti), kinship units (Kula, Grama, Vis, Jana), tribal assemblies (Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha, Gana), the Battle of Ten Kings (Dasarajna), and voluntary tribute (Bali)
- **The Later Vedic agrarian shift (1000–600 BCE):** Iron metallurgy (Shyama/Krishna Ayas at Atranjikhera, Noh, Jakhera), clearing of the Kuru-Panchala and Kosala-Videha Gangetic plains, plough agriculture (Sira), and the Painted Grey Ware (PGW) culture
- **Evolution of the Vedic literary canon:** Shruti vs. Smriti (the four Samhitas: Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva; ritualistic Brahmanas; forest Aranyakas; and the six auxiliary Vedangas: Shiksha, Kalpa, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Chhanda, Jyotisha)
- The socio-fiscal mechanics of sacrificial ritualism (Yajna: Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha) as instruments of wealth consumption, priestly Dakshina redistribution, and monarchical legitimacy (Janapada)
- Institutionalization of the fourfold Varna hierarchy, Gotra exogamy, Ashrama stages, and patriarchal subordination
- Early Upanishadic (Vedanta) epistemological revolts (Brihadaranyaka, Chhandogya: Atman-Brahman monism and Karma-Samsara) against ritual dominance.

### Part 4: The Second Urbanization, Gangetic Monetization, Mahajanapada Statecraft, and Heterodox Epistemological Revolutions (600–321 BCE)
- **Material base of the middle Gangetic plain:** iron-share wet-rice transplantation (Vrihi/Sali), Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW), urban fortifications, monetization via silver/copper punch-marked coinage (Karshapana, Pana, Shatamana), merchant-financiers (Setthis), peasant proprietors (Gahapatis), and autonomous craft/trade guilds (Shreni, Nigama, Puga)
- **The 16 Mahajanapadas:** structural conflict between monarchical states (Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti) and oligarchic tribal republics (Gana-Sanghas: Vajji/Lichchhavi, Malla, Shakya)
- **Magadhan imperial expansion (Haryanka, Shishunaga, and Nanda dynasties):** control over Chotanagpur iron ores, Gangetic river trade, war-elephant forests, and strategic capitals (Rajagriha to Pataliputra)
- Achaemenid Persian and Alexandrian Macedonian invasions of the North-West
- **The Heterodox (Shramana) philosophical rupture:** Jainism (Vardhamana Mahavira and the 24 Tirthankaras, Triratna, five Mahavratas, Anekantavada, Syadvada, Nayavada, karmic materialism, Pataliputra and Vallabhi Councils, Agama canon, and Digambara-Svetambara schism)
- Buddhism (Siddhartha Gautama, Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path, Pratityasamutpada, Anatta, Anicca, the Pali Tripitaka: Sutta, Vinaya, Abhidhamma, monastic Sangha rules, and the Four Buddhist Councils driving the Theravada vs. Mahasanghika splits)
- **Materialist and determinist radicalism:** Charvaka/Lokayata empiricism (Brihaspati), Ajivika fatalistic determinism (Makkhali Gosala, Niyati), Purana Kassapa, Ajita Kesakambali, and Pakudha Katyayana.

### Part 5: The Mauryan Imperial State, Kautilyan Bureaucracy, Ashokan Epigraphy, and Monumental State Art (321–185 BCE)
- Imperial consolidation under Chandragupta Maurya and Bindusara, and the Seleucid diplomatic interface (Megasthenes’ Indica, Deimachus, Dionysius)
- **Kautilya’s Arthashastra:** the Saptanga theory of the state, the 18 Tirthas and 27 Adhyakshas (state regulation of mines, mints, tolls, agriculture, textiles, shipping), crown Sita lands, revenue extraction (Bhaga, Bali, Udaka-bhaga, Pranaya, Pindakara, Vishti), centralized espionage (Gudhapuruṣas), and civil/criminal courts (Dharmasthiya and Kantakashodhana)
- Ashoka’s territorial statecraft and the Kalinga War (261 BCE, Major Rock Edict XIII)
- Ashokan Dhamma as an ideological instrument of multi-ethnic imperial cohesion (Dhamma Mahamattas, Rajukas, Pradeshikas, Yuktas)
- **Epigraphic distribution:** Major and Minor Rock Edicts, Pillar Edicts, and Cave Inscriptions across Prakrit, Greek, and Aramaic languages in Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, and Aramaic scripts
- **Mauryan courtly vs. popular art:** monolithic mirror-polished Chunar sandstone pillars, animal capitals (Sarnath Lion Capital, Rampurva Bull, Lauriya Nandangarh, Sankissa Elephant), early stupa engineering (Sanchi core, Bharhut, Bairat, Dhauli rock-cut elephant), rock-cut cave architecture (Barabar and Nagarjuni caves with Lomas Rishi chaitya arch gifted to the Ajivikas), and folk Yaksha/Yakshini sculptures (Didarganj Yakshi, Parkham Yaksha) and ring-stones
- Structural causes of Mauryan disintegration.

### Part 6: Post-Mauryan Mercantile Networks, Silk Road Polities, and the Sculptural and Dramaturgical Renaissance (185 BCE–300 CE)
- **Polycentric regional polities:** Shungas (Pushyamitra Shunga, Patanjali’s Mahabhashya, Vidisha Heliodorus Garuda pillar, Bharhut and Sanchi Torana expansions), Kanvas, Chedis of Kalinga (Kharavela’s Hathigumpha inscription, Udayagiri-Khandagiri Jain caves), Indo-Greeks (Menander and the Milinda Panha, die-struck portrait/bilingual coins), Shakas/Western Kshatrapas (Nahapana, Rudradaman I’s Junagadh Sanskrit inscription and Sudarshana Lake repair), Parthians (Gondophernes), and Kushanas (Kujula, Vima Kadphises, and Kanishka I’s Devaputra kingship, Rabatak inscription, Silk Road tolls, and gold Dinar coinage)
- Roman maritime commerce (Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, Pliny’s bullion drain, Barygaza and Barbaricum ports) and Central Asian trade corridors
- Mahayana Buddhist philosophical schools (Fourth Buddhist Council, Bodhisattva doctrine, Nagarjuna’s Madhyamaka/Shunyavada, Asanga and Vasubandhu’s Yogachara/Vijnanavada, Ashvaghosha’s Buddhacharita)
- **Foundational dramaturgy and aesthetics:** Bharata’s Natyashastra (Rasa and Bhava theory, Abhinaya) and Bhasa’s Sanskrit plays
- **The tri-regional sculptural revolution:** the Gandhara school (Greco-Roman-Buddhist synthesis, grey-blue schist and stucco, realistic musculature and drapery), the Mathura school (indigenous spotted red sandstone, headless Kanishka statue, earliest anthropomorphic Buddha, Jain Tirthankaras/Ayagapattas, and Brahmanical icons), and the Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda (Ikshvaku) schools (white Palnad limestone narrative reliefs, Tribhanga postures, and Ikshvaku dual patronage)
- **Early Buddhist rock-cut monasteries of the Western Ghats:** Chaityas and Viharas at Bhaja, Karle, Bedsa, Nasik (Pandavleni), Kanheri, and early Ajanta.

### Part 7: Early Peninsular Statehood: Satavahana Deccan, the Sangam Literary Horizon, and Megalithic Architectures (300 BCE–300 CE)
- **Peninsular Iron Age and Megalithic substratum:** Black-and-Red Ware (BRW), iron implements, and megalithic burial architectures (dolmens, cists, cairn-circles, menhirs, urn-burials at Adichanallur, Kodumanal, Hallur, Brahmagiri, Maski, Porkalam) as markers of social stratification
- **Satavahana statecraft in the Deccan (Simuka, Satakarni I, Naganika’s Naneghat inscription, Gautamiputra Satakarni, Vasishthiputra Pulumavi, Yajna Sri Satakarni):** matronymics, Ahara districts, Gaulmika rural garrisons, lead/potin coinage, Hala’s Prakrit Gaha Sattasai, Gunadhya’s Brihatkatha, and agrarian expansion through tax-free royal religious land grants (Brahmadeya and Buddhist monastic grants with Parihara immunities)
- **Sangam age polities in Tamilakam—the Muvendar (Cheras of Vanji/Muziris, Cholas of Uraiyur/Puhar, Pandyas of Madurai/Korkai) and Velir chieftains:** Karikala Chola’s Kallanai (Grand Anicut) hydraulic dam, Senguttuvan, and Nedunjeliyan
- **The Sangam literary and spatial taxonomy:** Tolkappiyam, Akam (love) vs. Puram (war/statecraft) poetics, and the Tinai concept (five ecological landscapes: Kurinji, Mullai, Marudam, Neydal, Palai)
- **The Sangam and post-Sangam canon:** Ettuthokai, Pattuppattu, Patinenkilkanakku (Tiruvalluvar’s Tirukkural), and the twin epics (Silappadikaram by Ilango Adigal and Manimekalai by Sattanar)
- Indo-Roman trade emporia and craft archaeology at Arikamedu, Pattanam (Muziris), Kaveripattinam, Korkai, Alagankulam, and Kodumanal.

### Part 8: The Gupta-Vakataka Classical Age: Agrarian Feudalization, Sanskrit Literary Zenith, and Structural Temple Genesis (300–550 CE)
- **Gupta imperial expansion:** Chandragupta I, Samudragupta’s geopolitical strategy in Harishena’s Prayagraj Pillar Prashasti (Aryavarta, Dakshinapatha, Atavika, Pratyanta), Chandragupta II’s conquest of the Western Kshatrapas, Kumaragupta I (Nalanda Mahavihara foundation), and Skandagupta (Bhitari inscription, Huna repulsion, Sudarshana Lake restoration), alongside Faxian’s travelogue
- The Vakataka alliance in the Deccan (Prabhavatigupta’s regency, Nandivardhana and Vatsagulma branches under Harishena)
- Institutional transition toward agrarian land grants (Agrahara, Brahmadeya, Devadana) transferring fiscal/judicial rights, Vishti forced labor, Samanta feudalism, gold Dinar debasement, and the "Indian Feudalism" debate (R.S. Sharma’s model vs. B.D. Chattopadhyaya’s integrative paradigm)
- **Legal and social codification (Yajnavalkya, Narada, Brihaspati, Katyayana Smritis):** Jati proliferation, Kayasthas, untouchability (Chandalas), Stridhana, and early Sati epigraphy (Eran inscription, 510 CE)
- **The classical Sanskrit literary zenith:** Kalidasa (Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghadutam, Raghuvamsham, Kumarasambhavam), Shudraka (Mrichchhakatika), Vishakhadatta (Mudrarakshasa), Bharavi, Amarasimha’s Amarakosha, Vatsyayana’s Kamasutra, and Puranic redactions
- **Morphological birth of the structural Hindu temple (Panchayatana plan) and classical sculpture/murals:** Udayagiri rock-cut caves at Vidisha (Varaha relief), flat-roofed shrines (Tigawa, Sanchi Temple 17) to towered Shikharas (Deogarh Dashavatara stone temple, Bhitargaon brick temple, Nachna Kuthara, Bhumara), Sarnath Buddha sculptures, Sultanganj copper Buddha, and the classical mural traditions of the Vakataka Ajanta Caves (Caves 1, 2, 16, 17, 19) and Bagh Caves.

### Part 9: Post-Gupta Northern and Eastern Polities: Harshavardhana, Kashmir’s Hydraulic State, the Pratiharas, and the Pala Monastic Complex (550–1000 CE)
- **North Indian transition:** Maukharies, Later Guptas, Shashanka of Gauda, and Harshavardhana’s imperial center at Kannauj (Banabhatta’s Harshacharita and Kadambari, Harsha’s Sanskrit plays Ratnavali, Priyadarshika, Nagananda, the Kannauj/Prayag assemblies, and Xuanzang’s Si-Yu-Ki and Yijing’s travelogues)
- **The Himalayan Kingdom of Kashmir (Karkota, Utpala, and Lohara Dynasties):** Durlabhavardhana, Lalitaditya Muktapida’s campaigns and the Martand Sun Temple (peristyle courtyard architecture), Avantivarman and minister Suyya’s hydraulic engineering (Jhelum dredging, flood canals, and wet-rice reclamation), Queen Didda, and Kalhana’s Rajatarangini
- **The Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj (Northern and Eastern poles):** the Gurjara-Pratiharas (Nagabhata I & II, Mihira Bhoja’s Adivaraha coinage and Sulaiman’s account, Mahendrapala I, court poet Rajashekhara’s Kavyamimamsa, and early Maha-Maru/Gurjara-Pratihara temples at Osian, Bateshwar, Baroli, and Gwalior Teli ka Mandir) and the Palas of Bengal and Bihar (Gopala, Dharmapala, Devapala’s diplomacy with Sailendra Srivijaya, Mahipala I, Ramapala and Sandhyakar Nandi’s Ramacharita; state patronage of Mahayana-Vajrayana Buddhist monastic universities at Nalanda, Vikramashila, Odantapuri, Somapura, and Jagaddala; Atisha Dipankara; Pala black-basalt sculpture and bronze casting under Dhiman and Bitpalo; and Pala palm-leaf manuscript painting Astasahasrika Prajnaparamita)
- **Early medieval Kamarupa (Assam):** Varman, Mlechchha, and Pala dynasties (Bhaskaravarman, Nidhanpur copper plates, Ambari sculptures).

### Part 10: Peninsular Cratonic Sovereignty, Early Tamil Bhakti, and Monolithic Rock-Cut Architecture (550–973 CE)
- **Deccan and Southern transitional polities:** the Kadambas of Banavasi (Mayurasharma, Talagunda inscription) and the Western Gangas of Talakad (minister Chavundaraya and the monolithic 57-foot Jain Gommateshwara/Bahubali statue at Shravanabelagola)
- **The Chalukyas of Badami (Pulakeshin I, Pulakeshin II’s Narmada victory in Ravikirti’s Aihole Prashasti, Vikramaditya II):** rock-cut cave temples at Badami and structural Vesara experimentation at Aihole (Lad Khan, apsidal Durga temple, Meguti Jain temple) and Pattadakal (Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna Dravida temples alongside Papanatha Nagara temple)
- **The Pallavas of Kanchi (Simhavishnu, Mahendravarman I’s Mattavilasa Prahasana and Kudumiyanmalai music inscription, Narasimhavarman I Mamalla, Narasimhavarman II Rajasimha):** Mamallapuram’s monumental political theater (monolithic rock-cut Pancha Rathas, open-air bas-relief of Descent of the Ganges / Arjuna’s Penance, structural Shore Temple) and the Kanchipuram Kailasanatha and Vaikuntha Perumal temples establishing the foundational grammar of Dravida architecture
- **The Rashtrakutas of Manyakheta (Dantidurga’s Hiranyagarbha ritual, Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha I’s Kannada Kavirajamarga, Indra III, Krishna III, Al-Masudi’s account, Kannada poets Pampa and Ponna):** Krishna I’s monolithic top-down rock-cut engineering of the Kailasanatha Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora, multi-religious Ellora caves, and the Elephanta Island caves (Trimurti/Maheshamurti)
- **The first Tamil Bhakti revolution:** the 12 Vaishnava Alvars (Nalayira Divya Prabandham, Andal, Nammalvar) and 63 Shaiva Nayanars (Tevaram/Tirumurai, Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar, Manikkavacakar, Karaikkal Ammaiyar).

### Part 11: The Imperial Cholas: Kaveri Hydraulic Agrarianism, Sabha Governance, Temple Economies, and Sacred Bronzes (850–1279 CE)
- **Imperial trajectory and maritime projection:** Vijayalaya, Parantaka I, Rajaraja I, Rajendra I (Gangetic expedition, conquest of Sri Lanka and Maldives, and the 1025 CE naval raid on the Srivijaya Empire across the Malacca Strait), and Kulottunga I (embassy to Song China)
- **Agrarian foundation of the Kaveri delta:** hydraulic sluices, stone weirs, Valanadu, Nadu (Nattar assemblies), Vellanvagai peasant villages, Brahmadeya grants, Nagaram market towns, cadastral land surveys, and tax grades (Kadamai, Kudimai, Vetti)
- **Historiographical debate:** Burton Stein’s "Segmentary State" model vs. Nilakanta Sastri and Y. Subbarayalu’s centralized-integrative fiscal model
- **Village administrative assemblies:** functional anatomy of the non-Brahmanical Ur and the Brahmanical Sabha/Mahasabha—electoral qualifications, Kudavolai lottery system, and Variyam (Eri-variyam) committees recorded in Parantaka I’s Uttaramerur Inscriptions
- **The High Dravidian temple as state bank, employer, grain repository, and royal deification:** Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur, Gangaikondacholapuram, and Airavatesvara Temple at Darasuram
- **Temple performing arts and bronze metallurgy:** institutionalization of the Devadasi (Tali-cheri-pendugal) and Nattuvanar land-grant system (Sadir / early Bharatanatyam) and Oduvar hymn singers
- Chola Panchaloha bronze casting via the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique and the cosmic iconography of Shiva Nataraja, Somaskanda, and Karaikkal Ammaiyar
- **Chola Tamil literary canon:** Kamban’s Ramavataram, Sekkizhar’s Periyapuranam, Jayamkondar’s Kalingattupparani, and Ottakoothar.

### Part 12: Late Peninsular Polities, Tank Networks, Virashaiva Radicalism, and Transoceanic Merchant Cartels (973–1336 CE)
- **The Western Chalukyas of Kalyani (Tailapa II, Someshvara I, Vikramaditya VI):** Bilhana’s Vikramankadeva Charita, Vijnaneshwara’s Mitakshara legal commentary (contrasted with Jimutavahana’s Dayabhaga in Bengal), and Gadag/Kalyani architectural evolution (Itagi Mahadeva, Lakkundi, Dodda Basappa at Dambal)
- **The 12th-century Virashaiva / Lingayat social and philosophical movement:** Basavanna, Akka Mahadevi, and Allama Prabhu—the Anubhava Mantapa, Kannada Vachana poetry, Ishtalinga egalitarianism, and rejection of caste pollution, Vedic ritualism, and temple landlordism
- **The Hoysalas of Dwarasamudra (Vishnuvardhana, Ballala II):** stellate star-shaped Jagati platforms, lathe-turned soapstone/chloritic schist pillars, and ornate Madanika bracket sculptures (Mallitamma) at Belur (Chennakesava), Halebidu (Hoysaleswara), and Somanathapura (Keshava)
- **The Kakatiyas of Warangal (Rudradeva, Ganapatideva, Rani Rudrama Devi, Prataparudra):** early Nayankara tenures, dryland agrarian transformation through cascading hydraulic tank networks (Cheruvus: Pakhal, Ramappa, Lakhnavaram), the Motupalli Abhaya Shasana maritime trade charter (Marco Polo’s account), and the Thousand Pillar (Hanamkonda) and Ramappa (Palampet) temples (sandbox seismic foundations, floating bricks, and Perini Shivatandavam dance)
- **The Yadavas of Devagiri (Singhana II, Ramachandra):** Hemadpanti architecture and Sarangadeva’s foundational musicological treatise Sangita Ratnakara
- The Second Pandyan Empire (Sundara Pandyan, Kayal horse trade, outer Gopurams)
- **Southern transoceanic merchant corporations:** corporate charters (Samaya), private mercenary armies (Virakkoiyar, Mummuridandas), and Southeast Asian trade cartels of the Ayyavole 500 (Ainnurruvar / Nanadesi), Manigramam, and Anjuvannam.

### Part 13: Pre-Sultanate Rajput and Eastern Regional Polities, Nagara Temple Morphologies, Stepwells, and Fortification Engineering (800–1250 CE)
- **Rajput state formation, Agni-kula origin myths, and clan-based Bhaibant land tenures:** Chahamanas (Chauhans) of Shakambhari/Ajmer-Delhi (Vigraharaja IV’s Harikeli Nataka, Prithviraj III), Tomaras of Delhi (Anangpal, Lal Kot, Surajkund), Gahadavalas of Kannauj (Govindachandra, Turushka-danda tax, Jayachandra), Kalachuris of Tripuri, Paramaras of Malwa (Raja Bhoja’s Samarangana Sutradhara treatise on architecture/automata, Bhojeshwar Temple, Bhojshala, and the massive Bhojtal hydraulic lake), Chandelas of Jejakabhukti, and Chaulukyas/Solankis of Gujarat (Siddharaja Jayasimha, Kumarapala, Jain scholar Hemachandra’s Parishishtaparvan)
- **Eastern regional polities:** Senas of Bengal (Vijayasena, Ballala Sena’s Kulinism caste hierarchy, Lakshmana Sena’s court: Jayadeva’s Gita Govinda, Dhoyi, Halayudha) and Eastern Gangas of Odisha (Anantavarman Chodaganga, Narasimhadeva I—the Rauta royal deputyship under Lord Jagannath and Mahari/Gotipua temple dance economies)
- **North Indian Nagara temple morphologies (Latina, Phamsana, Valabhi, Shekhari, Bhumija):** the Odisha/Kalinga sub-school (Rekha deula, Pidha deula/Jagamohana, Khakhara deula at Mukteshvara, Rajarani, and Lingaraja in Bhubaneswar, Jagannath Puri, and the Konark Sun Temple solar chariot), the Central Indian/Chandela sub-school at Khajuraho (Sandhara/Nirandhara plans, clustered Urushringas, Tantric-Maithuna friezes at Kandariya Mahadeva, Lakshmana, and Vishvanatha, plus circular hypaethral Chausath Yogini temples at Mitaoli, Bhedaghat, and Hirapur), and the Western Indian/Solanki (Maru-Gurjara) sub-school (Modhera Sun Temple Surya Kund, Dilwara Jain marble temples at Mount Abu: Vimala Vasahi and Luna Vasahi, and Western Indian/Jain Apabhramsa palm-leaf and paper manuscript paintings Kalpasutra and Kalakacharya Katha)
- Subterranean stepwells (Baolis/Vavs: Rani ki Vav at Patan, Chand Baori at Abhaneri, Adalaj) and strategic hill/desert fortifications (Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Ranthambore, Gwalior, Gagron, Jaisalmer, Kalinjar).

### Part 14: Subcontinental Epistemological Systems (Shad-Darshana), Vedantic Metaphysics, and Indigenous Scientific, Medical, and Metallurgical Canons
- **The Six Orthodox (Astika) Epistemological Systems (Shad-Darshana), their Pramanas, and theories of causation (Satkaryavada vs. Asatkaryavada):** Samkhya (Kapila, Ishvarakrishna’s Samkhya Karika—dualism of Purusha and Prakriti and the three Gunas), Yoga (Patanjali’s Yoga Sutras), Nyaya (Gautama, Gangesha’s Navya-Nyaya—formal logic and four Pramanas), Vaisheshika (Kanada, Prashastapada—Paramanuvada atomism), Purva Mimamsa (Jaimini, Kumarila Bhatta, Prabhakara—ritual hermeneutics and Apurva), and Uttara Mimamsa / Vedanta (Badarayana’s Brahma Sutras)
- **Metaphysical battles within Vedanta:** Adi Shankara’s Advaita (absolute non-dualism, Maya, Vivartavada, and the four cardinal Mathas at Sringeri, Puri, Dwaraka, and Jyotirmath), Ramanuja’s Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism, Prapatti), Madhvacharya’s Dvaita (strict dualism, Pancha-bheda), Nimbarka’s Dvaitadvaita, Vallabhacharya’s Shuddhadvaita (Pushtimarg), and Chaitanya’s Achintya Bheda Abheda
- **Non-dual Tantric and Shaiva systems:** Kashmir Shaivism (Trika system of Vasugupta, Utpaladeva, and Abhinavagupta’s Tantraloka and Abhinavabharati establishing Shanta Rasa), Pashupata, Kapalika, Kalamukha, and Shakta traditions
- **Mathematical and astronomical canons:** Baudhayana and Apastamba Sulba Sutras (ritual geometry), Pingala’s binary combinatorics, the Bakhshali Manuscript, Aryabhata I (Aryabhatiya: decimal place-value, $\pi$, axial rotation, eclipse mechanics, Jya trigonometry), Varahamihira (Panchasiddhantika, Brihat Samhita), Brahmagupta (Brahmasphutasiddhanta: zero and negative rules, Kuttaka equations), Mahavira (Ganita Sara Sangraha), Bhaskara II (Siddhanta Shiromani: Lilavati, Bijaganita, Goladhyaya—Chakravala method and calculus precursors), the Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics (Madhava of Sangamagrama, Nilakantha Somayaji, Jyesthadeva’s Yuktibhasha—infinite series expansions), and Sawai Jai Singh II’s Jantar Mantar observatories
- **Indigenous medical, chemical, and botanical treatises:** Charaka Samhita (internal medicine, Tridosha theory), Sushruta Samhita (surgical instruments, rhinoplasty, cataract surgery), Vagbhata’s Ashtanga Hridaya, Siddha and Unani medicine, Nagarjuna’s Rasaratnakara (mercury alchemy), Vrikshayurveda, Hastyayurveda, and Al-Biruni’s Kitab-ul-Hind
- **Metallurgical mastery:** corrosion-resistant forge-welded phosphorus iron (Mehrauli and Dhar Iron Pillars), South Indian crucible high-carbon Wootz (Ukku) steel, and downward-distillation zinc smelting retorts at Zawar.

### Part 15: The Delhi Sultanate I: Military Slave Institutions, the Iqta Extraction Grid, Khalji Centralization, and Early Arcuated Engineering (1192–1320)
- **Socio-political and military mechanics of the Ghurid conquest (First and Second Battles of Tarain, 1191–92; Chandawar, 1194):** Central Asian mounted archery, iron stirrups, and Tulghuma tactics vs. fragmented Rajput feudal levies
- **The Mamluk/Ilbari Sultanate:** Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish (diplomatic evasion of Chinggis Khan, Abbasid Caliphal Manshur, the Turkan-i-Chahalgani / Corps of Forty, and standardized bimetallic Silver Tanka and Copper Jital coinage), Razia Sultan, and Ghiyas-ud-din Balban (destruction of the Chahalgani, Iranian Sasanian divine monarchy doctrine: Zill-i-Ilahi, Niyabat-i-Khudai, Sijda, Paibos, Nauroz, the Diwan-i-Arz military department, and Meo/Katehar forest clearance)
- **The Iqta system:** administrative mechanics of transferable revenue assignments (Muqti/Wali), military centralization, and Fawazil surplus remittance
- **The Khalji revolution (Jalal-ud-din and Alauddin Khalji, 1296–1316):** broadening the nobility, repelling Chagatai Mongol invasions, and Malik Kafur’s Deccan/Peninsular tribute raids
- **Alauddin’s agrarian and market interventions:** confiscating Inam/Waqf grants into crown Khalisa, crushing rural intermediaries (Khuts, Muqaddams, Chaudharis), direct land measurement (Masahat/Biswa) fixing Kharaj at 50%, Ghari and Charai taxes, Diwan-i-Mustakhraj, cash-paid standing cavalry with Dagh (horse branding) and Chehra (descriptive rolls), and the Four Market Regulations (Diwan-i-Riyasat, Shahna-i-Mandi, Sarai-i-Adl)
- **Technological diffusions (Irfan Habib’s thesis):** geared Persian wheel (Saqiya), bow-string cotton carder (Naddaf), spinning wheel (Charkha), paper manufacturing, and lime-gypsum mortar
- **Foundational Indo-Islamic architectural engineering:** structural introduction of the true voussoir arch, true dome on squinches, minarets, and reuse of temple spolia (Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque, Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra, Qutb Minar, Sultan Ghari, Tomb of Balban, Alai Darwaza, Siri Fort, Hauz Khas).

### Part 16: The Delhi Sultanate II and the 15th-Century Provincial Sultanates: Tughlaq Statecraft, Hydraulic Networks, and Regional Architectures (1320–1526)
- **Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq (postal relay system) and Muhammad bin Tughlaq’s (1325–1351) administrative experiments (documented in Ziauddin Barani’s Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi and Ibn Battuta’s Rihla):** Doab taxation and famine, the 1327 Daulatabad capital relocation, the 1329–32 copper-brass token currency crisis, Khurasan/Qarachil expeditions, state agricultural loans (Sondhar/Taqavi) under the Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi, and Amiran-i-Sada rebellions
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq’s (1351–1388) statecraft:** hereditary Iqtas, separate Jizya extended to Brahmins, Haqq-i-Shurb (10% irrigation tax), canal networks in the Sutlej-Yamuna divide, urban foundations (Firozabad, Hissar, Fatehabad, Jaunpur), Dar-ul-Shafa hospitals, Diwan-i-Khairat, and the slave bureaucracy (Diwan-i-Bandagan)
- The 1398 Timurid invasion and regional splintering
- **The Sayyid and Lodi dynasties (Bahlul, Sikandar’s Gaz-i-Sikandari and 1504 Agra foundation, Ibrahim Lodi):** Afghan tribal confederation kingship model
- **Tughlaq, Sayyid, and Lodi architecture:** grey quartzite rubble masonry, battered sloping walls, arch-and-beam synthesis (Tughlaqabad, Ghiyas-ud-din’s Tomb, Kotla Firoz Shah), Telangani’s octagonal tomb plan, and Lodi Garden double-dome prototypes (Bara Gumbad, Shish Gumbad, Tomb of Sikandar Lodi)
- **The 15th-century Provincial Sultanates—political economy and regional Indo-Islamic architectures:** Jaunpur (Sharqi dynasty: sloping pylon facades without minarets at Atala Masjid and Jama Masjid), Bengal (Ilyas Shahi and Hussain Shahi dynasties: terracotta brick masonry, curved Do-chala/Char-chala Bangla roofs at Adina Mosque Pandua, Dakhil Darwaza, Kadam Rasul, and Bengali vernacular patronage), Gujarat (Muzaffarid dynasty: Ahmad Shah I and Mahmud Begada’s Champaner-Pavagadh urban-hydraulic complex, Sidi Saiyyed Mosque stone Jali screens, and Cambay/Surat maritime commerce), Malwa (Mandu ridge-and-lake architecture: Jahaz Mahal, Hindola Mahal, Hoshang Shah’s marble tomb, and the illustrated Nimatnama manuscript), and Kashmir (Shah Mir dynasty under Sultan Zain-ul-Abidin Budshah: abolition of Jizya, canal networks, Zaina Lank, Samarkand craft imports, and wooden pagoda-style Rishi mosque architecture at Khanqah-e-Moula and Jama Masjid Srinagar).

### Part 17: The Bhakti and Sufi Synthesis: Khanqah Institutions, Vernacular Subversion, Sikh Institutionalization, and Folk Performative Traditions (1200–1700)
- **Institutional network of Sufism (Tasawwuf):** Pir-Murid lineage, Wilayat spiritual territories, socio-spatial function of the Khanqah and Langar, Sama musical audition, Ziyarat dargah pilgrimage, Malfuzat/Maktubat literary sources, and the philosophical debate between Ibn Arabi’s Wahdat-ul-Wujud (Unity of Being) and Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi’s Wahdat-ush-Shuhud
- **Major Sufi Silsilahs:** Chishti (Muinuddin Chishti, Bakhtiyar Kaki, Baba Farid, Nizamuddin Auliya, Chiragh-i-Dehlavi, Gesudaraz, Salim Chishti), Suhrawardi (Bahauddin Zakariya), Firdausi (Yahya Maneri), Kashmir Rishi order (Shaikh Nuruddin Nund Rishi and Shaiva poetess Lal Ded), Qadiri (Miyan Mir, Dara Shikoh’s Majma-ul-Bahrain and Sirr-i-Akbar), Shattari, and Naqshbandi
- Amir Khusrau’s linguistic and musical synthesis (Sabaq-i-Hindi, Hindavi, Qawwali, Khayal, Tarana, Sitar/Tabla)
- **The Northern and Regional Bhakti explosion:** Nirguna anti-caste syncretism (Ramananda, Kabir’s Bijak and Ulbansi, Ravidas’s Begumpura, Dadu Dayal) vs. Saguna devotion (Vallabhacharya and Surdas’s Sur Sagar and Ashtachhap, Mirabai, Tulsidas’s Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi, Narsi Mehta, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu’s Sankirtana, and Srimanta Sankaradeva’s Ekasarana Dharma, Naamghar, Sattra monasteries, Borgeet, and Ankiya Nat / Sattriya dance in Assam)
- Sikhism from Guru Nanak (Kartarpur, Langar) through Guru Angad (Gurmukhi), Guru Amar Das (Manji), Guru Ram Das (Amritsar), Guru Arjan Dev (Adi Granth 1604, Harmandir Sahib), Guru Hargobind (Miri-Piri, Akal Takht), and Guru Tegh Bahadur to Guru Gobind Singh (the Khalsa Panth 1699, Dasam Granth)
- The Varkari and Dharakari movements of Maharashtra (Jnaneshwar’s Jnaneshwari, Namdev, Eknath’s Bharuds, Tukaram’s Abhangas, Bahinabai, Samarth Ramdas’s Dasbodh)
- Regional folk theatre (Jatra, Ramlila, Raslila, Bhavai, Tamasha, Yakshagana, Therukoothu, Koodiyattam, Krishnattam), traditional puppetry (String: Kathputli, Kundhei, Gombeyatta, Bommalattam; Shadow: Togalu Gombeyatta, Tholu Bommalata, Ravanachhaya; Rod: Putul Nautch, Yampuri; Glove: Pavakoothu), and ritual folk/scroll paintings (Madhubani, Patachitra, Kalamkari, Phad, Pichwai, Cheriyal, Warli, Thangka).

### Part 18: The Vijayanagara Empire and the Deccan Sultanates: Nayankara Militarism, Hampi Urban Morphology, and Courtly Syncretism (1336–1687)
- Geopolitical conflict over the Raichur Doab and coastal horse-import ports
- **Dynastic sequence of Vijayanagara:** Sangama (Harihara I, Bukka I, Deva Raya I & II), Saluva, Tuluva (Krishnadevaraya’s Amuktamalyada and Ashtadiggajas led by Allasani Peddana; Achyuta Deva Raya), and Aravidu (Rama Raya, Battle of Talikota 1565, Venkata II)
- Foreign traveler accounts (Niccolò de' Conti, Abdur Razzaq, Athanasius Nikitin, Duarte Barbosa, Domingo Paes, Fernão Nuniz)
- **Vijayanagara fiscal-military statecraft:** the Amara-Nayaka (Nayankara) military land-tenure system, the 12-functionary Ayagar village system, tank irrigation (Kere), and gold Varaha (Pagoda) monetization
- **Urban morphology and architecture of Hampi:** seven concentric fortifications enclosing agricultural fields, Kamalapuram tank and Hiriya canal, Mahanavami Dibba, Lotus Mahal, Elephant Stables, Virupaksha and Hazara Rama Temples, Vitthala Temple (monolithic Stone Chariot and 56 musical pillars), soaring Rayagopurams, Kalyana Mandapas, Lepakshi Veerabhadra murals, and Siddhendra Yogi’s Kuchipudi dance-drama
- The Bahmani Kingdom (Firuz Shah Bahmani, Mahmud Gawan’s 8 Tarafs, Khalisa expansion, and Bidar Madrasa), the Deccani vs. Afaqi factional split, and fragmentation into the five Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar, Berar)
- Malik Ambar’s guerrilla warfare and Ryotwari-prototype revenue settlement in Ahmadnagar
- **Deccani courtly aesthetics and engineering:** Ibrahim Adil Shah II’s Kitab-i-Nauras, Ibrahim Rauza, and Gol Gumbaz (whispering gallery dome) at Bijapur
- Quli Qutb Shah’s Charminar, Golconda Fort acoustics and diamond mines, and Masulipatnam Kalamkari
- Bidriware metal inlays
- and the Deccani miniature painting schools (Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda).

### Part 19: The Mughal Imperium I: Timurid Gunpowder Tactics, Sur Administrative Precedents, the Mansabdari-Jagirdari Grid, and Akbari Synthesis (1526–1605)
- Babur’s tactical innovations at Panipat (1526) and Khanwa (1527) (Ottoman Rumi/Araba gun-carts, matchlockmen, and Timurid Tulghuma flanking cavalry), Baburnama, and Persian Charbagh gardens
- Humayun’s setbacks and return with Safavid painters Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd us-Samad
- **The Sur interregnum:** Sher Shah Suri’s Sarkar/Pargana administration, hemp-rope land measurement (Jarib), crop-yield schedules (Ray/Rai), peasant Patta and Qabuliyat deeds, standardized 178-grain Silver Rupiya and Copper Dam, highway networks (Sadak-e-Azam / Grand Trunk Road and Sarais), Purana Qila, and Sher Shah’s octagonal tomb at Sasaram
- **Akbar’s institutional consolidation (documented in Abul Fazl’s Akbarnama / Ain-i-Akbari and Badauni’s Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh):** central and provincial (Suba) checks and balances (Wazir/Diwan-i-Kul, Mir Bakhshi, Mir Saman, Sadr-us-Sudur)
- Raja Todar Mal’s Ain-i-Dahsala (Zabti) land revenue system (1580: bamboo-and-iron Tanab measurement, 10-year Dastur-ul-Amal cash rates, Polaj, Parauti, Chachar, Banjar land classifications, Batai/Kankut/Nasaq variants, and agrarian classes: Khud-kashta, Pahi-kashta, Muzarian, and Zamindars holding 10% Milkiyat/Malikana)
- **The Mansabdari-Jagirdari apparatus:** numerical dual ranks of Zat and Sawar, Dah-Bisti rule, transferable Tankhwah Jagirs vs. hereditary Rajput Watan Jagirs, Jama vs. Hasil audits, and noble escheat (Zabt)
- Rajput matrimonial-military alliances, abolition of Jizya (1564), Ibadat Khana debates (1575), the Mahzar decree (1579), Tauhid-i-Ilahi, and the state doctrine of Sulh-i-Kul (universal peace)
- **Akbari cultural and architectural synthesis:** Maktab Khana Persian translation bureau (Razmnama), Miyan Tansen and courtly Dhrupad, the imperial painting atelier (Tasvir Khana: Hamzanama, Akbarnama, Daswanth, Basawan), Humayun’s Tomb (double dome, Charbagh, Hasht-Bihisht layout), Agra Fort, and Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza, Diwan-i-Khas, Panch Mahal, Salim Chishti’s Tomb).

### Part 20: The Mughal Imperium II: Imperial Karkhanas, Miniature Portraiture, Marble Monumentalism, and the Jagirdari Agrarian Crisis (1605–1707)
- **Jahangir’s reign (1605–1627):** Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri, Du-Aspa Sih-Aspa Mansabdari modification, Nur Jahan’s junta, English East India Company envoys (William Hawkins, Thomas Roe), and the zenith of the Mughal miniature painting atelier (Muraqqa albums, Hashiya borders, European chiaroscuro/halo influences, portraiture, and botanical/zoological naturalism under Ustad Mansur, Abul Hasan, Bishandas, and Manohar)
- Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah at Agra (white Makrana marble and Pietra Dura / Parchin Kari floral inlays)
- **Shah Jahan’s reign (1628–1658):** the Month-Scale (Mahwar) Mansabdari adjustment, Balkh/Qandahar campaigns, and European traveler accounts (François Bernier, Tavernier, Manucci, Peter Mundy—describing state Karkhanas, the global silver bullion sink into India, Dadni merchant advances, and Sarraf Hundi banking networks of Virji Vora)
- **Shahjahani architectural zenith:** bilateral symmetry (Qarina), multifoil engrailed arches, bulbous double domes, the Taj Mahal (Ustad Ahmad Lahori, riverfront Charbagh), and Shahjahanabad (Red Fort and Jama Masjid)
- **Aurangzeb’s reign (1658–1707) and structural imperial collapse:** war of succession (Samugarh), compilation of the Fatawa-i-Alamgiri, Muhtasibs, temple destruction directives vs. selective grants, 1679 reimposition of Jizya, and Bibi-ka-Maqbara / Badshahi Mosque
- Agrarian peasant rebellions (Jats under Gokula, Rajaram, and Churaman; Satnamis; Bundelas under Chhatrasal; Rathore-Sisodia Rajputs; and Sikhs)
- Aurangzeb’s Deccan campaign (annexation of Bijapur 1686 and Golconda 1687), the shortage of assignable Paibaqi land (Be-jagiri / the Jagirdari crisis—Satish Chandra and Irfan Habib’s theses), rise of Ijara revenue-farming, and imperial fiscal exhaustion.

### Part 21: The Maratha Swarajya, the Ahom Paik State, 18th-Century Courtly Decentralization, Regional Miniature Schools, and Classical Performing Arts Lineages (1630–1761)
- **Chhatrapati Shivaji (1630–1680) and the Maratha Swarajya:** Mavali peasant mobilization, hill and coastal fortress warfare (Ganimi Kava; Raigad, Pratapgad, Sindhudurg, and the Maratha Navy under Kanhoji Angre), Treaty of Purandar (1665), 1674 Raigad coronation, the Ashtapradhan council, Annaji Datto’s Kathi land revenue settlement curbing Deshmukh/Watandar intermediaries, and fiscal extractions (Chauth and Sardeshmukhi)
- **The Peshwa ascendancy (1713–1761: Balaji Vishwanath, Baji Rao I, Balaji Baji Rao):** hereditary Saranjam fiefs, the Maratha Confederacy (Scindias, Holkars, Gaekwads, Bhonsles), North Indian expansion, and the Third Battle of Panipat (1761)
- **The Ahom Kingdom of the Brahmaputra Valley (1228–1826):** wet-rice reclamation, the non-monetized Paik corvée labor and Khel military mobilization system, the riverine Battle of Saraighat (1671) under Lachit Borphukan, Buranji historical chronicles, Sivasagar masonry architecture (Rang Ghar, Talatal Ghar), and the royal mound-burials of the Charaideo Maidams
- **18th-century decentralization of patronage and regional miniature painting schools:** Rajasthani schools (Mewar under Sahibdin, Bundi, Kota, Marwar, Amber-Jaipur, and Kishangarh’s Bani Thani by Nihal Chand and Sawant Singh), Pahari schools (Basohli, Guler, and Kangra under Sansar Chand), and Tanjore/Mysore gesso-and-gold paintings
- **Divergence and codification of classical music:** Hindustani (Dhrupad, Khayal under Sadarang/Adarang, Thumri, Tappa, and the Gharana system: Gwalior, Agra, Kirana, Jaipur-Atrauli, Patiala, Banaras) vs. Carnatic music (Purandara Dasa, Venkatamakhin’s 72 Melakarta scheme, and the 18th-century Carnatic Trinity at Thanjavur: Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri)
- **Codification of the eight canonical classical dance traditions and martial lineages:** Bharatanatyam (Tanjore Quartet), Kathak (Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras Gharanas), Kathakali (Kerala Ramanattam and Kalaripayattu), Mohiniyattam (Travancore), Kuchipudi, Odissi, Sattriya, and Manipuri (Bhagyachandra’s Ras Lila, Pung Cholom, and Thang-Ta).
