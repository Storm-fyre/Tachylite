### Part 1: The Intellectual and Political Ruptures: The Enlightenment, the Seven Years’ War, and the American Constitutional Revolution (1688–1789)
- Transition from the Scientific Revolution and the 1688 English Glorious Revolution to the European Enlightenment (Francis Bacon, René Descartes, Isaac Newton to John Locke’s social contract, Montesquieu’s separation of powers, Voltaire, Jean-Jacques Rousseau’s General Will, Diderot’s Encyclopédie, and Adam Smith’s critique of mercantilism)
- Enlightened Absolutism (Frederick the Great of Prussia, Catherine the Great of Russia, Joseph II of Austria) vs. the epistemological breakdown of monarchical absolutism and the Divine Right of Kings
- The Seven Years’ War (1756–1763) as the first global conflict across European, North American, Caribbean, and Indian (Carnatic/Plassey) theaters, and the fiscal exhaustion of the British and French crowns
- **The American War of Independence (1775–1783):** mercantilist trade restrictions (Navigation Acts, Sugar Act, Stamp Act 1765, Townshend Acts, Tea Act), "No Taxation Without Representation", the Boston Tea Party (1773), Intolerable Acts, First and Second Continental Congresses, Thomas Paine’s Common Sense, and Thomas Jefferson’s Declaration of Independence (1776)
- Military trajectory (Saratoga, French-Spanish-Dutch intervention, Cornwallis’s surrender at Yorktown 1781, and the Treaty of Paris 1783)
- **Global-subcontinental entanglement:** British pivot from the First British Empire (Atlantic settler-mercantilism) to the Second British Empire (Asian territorial revenue extraction, transferring Cornwallis from Yorktown to Bengal)
- **The United States Constitution (1787 Philadelphia Convention):** replacement of the Articles of Confederation, federal separation of powers, checks and balances, Connecticut Compromise bicameralism, the Bill of Rights (1791), and the Three-Fifths Compromise preserving institutionalized chattel slavery.

### Part 2: The Radical Ruptures: The French and Haitian Revolutions, the Napoleonic Imperium, and the Vienna Settlement (1789–1815)
- **The French Revolution (1789–1799):** structural bankruptcy of the Bourbon Ancien Régime (Seven Years’ War and American War debts, tax exemptions of the First and Second Estates), agrarian subsistence crises, and the convening of the Estates-General (May 1789)
- Abbé Sieyès’ What is the Third Estate?, the Tennis Court Oath, storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789), the Great Fear, August Decrees abolishing feudalism, Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, Women’s March on Versailles, and the Civil Constitution of the Clergy
- Girondin vs. Jacobin (Montagnard) radicalization, the Sans-Culottes, execution of Louis XVI, the Committee of Public Safety and Robespierre’s Reign of Terror (1793–94), Levée en masse conscription, and the Thermidorian Reaction (1794) and Directory
- **The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804):** Saint-Domingue’s sugar-and-coffee slave plantation economy, racial caste hierarchy, slave insurrection led by Toussaint Louverture and Jean-Jacques Dessalines, defeat of British, Spanish, and Napoleonic armies, birth of the first independent Black anti-slavery republic, and France’s punitive 1825 indemnity debt
- **The Napoleonic statehood (1799–1815):** 18 Brumaire coup, the Napoleonic Code (Civil Code of 1804), administrative centralization (Prefects, Bank of France, Lycées), Concordat of 1801, dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire, the Continental System blockade (and Anglo-French Jacobin/maritime spillover into the Indian Ocean and Egypt/Mysore), Peninsular War guerrilla ulcer, invasion of Russia (1812), Leipzig (1813), and the Battle of Waterloo (1815)
- **The Congress of Vienna (1814–15):** Klemens von Metternich, Castlereagh, Talleyrand, principles of Legitimacy, Compensation, and Balance of Power, the Concert of Europe, and the Holy and Quadruple Alliances.

### Part 3: The Industrial Transformation, Comparative Capitalist Industrialization, and Socialist Counter-Movements (1760–1914)
- **The First Industrial Revolution in Great Britain (1760–1840):** Agricultural Revolution and Enclosure Acts, demographic transition, navigable canals, coal-and-iron endowments, colonial primitive accumulation (Atlantic slave trade and Bengal Diwani transfers), textile mechanization (flying shuttle, spinning jenny, water frame, Crompton’s mule, power loom, cotton gin), coke iron smelting, and steam power (Newcomen, James Watt, Stephenson’s locomotive)
- **The Second Industrial Revolution (1870–1914):** Bessemer and open-hearth steel, synthetic chemicals, electrical grids, internal combustion engines, petroleum, and Fordist assembly-line production
- **Comparative late-industrialization pathways:** Imperial Germany (the Zollverein customs union, railway integration, universal investment banks, corporate Kartells/Konzerns), the United States (continental resource scale, transcontinental railroads, protective tariffs, monopoly trusts of Carnegie, Rockefeller, and Morgan), Tsarist Russia (Sergei Witte’s state-led industrialization, Trans-Siberian Railway, foreign loans), and Meiji Japan (state-incubated pilot enterprises transferred to Zaibatsu conglomerates)
- **Social transformations and working-class resistance:** explosive urbanization, factory floor exploitation, child/female labor, urban disease environments, Luddism, Peterloo Massacre (1819), the Chartist Movement, and Bismarck’s 1880s State Socialism (health, accident, and old-age social insurance laws)
- **Ideological counter-movements:** Utopian Socialism (Saint-Simon, Charles Fourier, Robert Owen), Anarchism and Syndicalism (Proudhon, Bakunin, Kropotkin), and Scientific Socialism (Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels: Dialectical and Historical Materialism, Surplus Value, The Communist Manifesto 1848, Das Kapital)
- The First International (1864), the Paris Commune (1871), the Second International (1889), and Eduard Bernstein’s Revisionist Social Democracy.

### Part 4: 19th-Century European Nationalism, Italian and German Unification, the Eastern Question, and the American Civil War (1815–1871)
- **Breakdown of the Metternich Congress System:** Greek War of Independence, the July 1830 Revolution in France (Louis-Philippe) and Belgian independence, and the Revolutions of 1848 ("Springtime of the Peoples" across the French Second Republic, Habsburg Vienna/Hungary/Prague, the Frankfurt Parliament in Germany, and Italian states)
- **The Eastern Question and the Crimean War (1853–1856):** Ottoman decline ("Sick Man of Europe"), Russian expansion toward the Turkish Straits, Anglo-French-Piedmontese intervention at Sevastopol, the Treaty of Paris (1856), and Tsar Alexander II’s 1861 Emancipation of the Russian Serfs
- **The Unification of Italy (Il Risorgimento, 1848–1870):** Giuseppe Mazzini’s Young Italy, Count Cavour’s Piedmont-Sardinia modernization and Plombières diplomacy with Napoleon III, the 1859 Franco-Austrian War (Lombardy), Giuseppe Garibaldi’s Expedition of the Thousand (Red Shirts) in Sicily and Naples (1860), King Victor Emmanuel II (1861), annexation of Venetia (1866), and seizure of Rome (1870)
- **The Unification of Germany (1862–1871):** Kleindeutschland vs. Großdeutschland debate, Otto von Bismarck’s "Blood and Iron" realpolitik, Prussian military reforms (Moltke, Roon), and the three wars of unification—the Danish War (1864), the Austro-Prussian Seven Weeks’ War (1866, Battle of Sadowa and North German Confederation), and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71: Ems Dispatch, Sedan, annexation of Alsace-Lorraine, and proclamation of the German Empire at Versailles)
- **The American Civil War and Reconstruction (1861–1877):** Westward expansion (Manifest Destiny, Mexican-American War), collision between Northern free-labor industrialism and Southern plantation chattel slavery, Missouri Compromise, Kansas-Nebraska Act, Dred Scott verdict, election of Abraham Lincoln (1860), Union naval blockade (triggering the global Lancashire Cotton Famine and the Indian Deccan/Egyptian cotton boom), Emancipation Proclamation (1863), Gettysburg, Appomattox, the 13th, 14th, and 15th Constitutional Amendments, and the 1877 Compromise and Jim Crow segregation.

### Part 5: New Imperialism, the Scramble for Africa, the Subjugation of Qing China and Southeast Asia, and the Rise of Imperial Japan (1800–1914)
- **Economic transition from free-trade commerce to monopoly finance imperialism:** theories of J.A. Hobson, Rudolf Hilferding (Finance Capital), Rosa Luxemburg, and Vladimir Lenin (Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism)
- **The Scramble for Africa:** the Berlin West Africa Conference (1884–85: "Effective Occupation" doctrine and artificial cartographic borders), King Leopold II’s rubber regime in the Congo Free State, British "Cape to Cairo" vs. French East-West axis (1882 occupation of Egypt/Suez, 1898 Fashoda Incident), Ethiopian victory over Italy at the Battle of Adwa (1896), Herero and Nama genocide, and the Anglo-Boer Wars (1899–1902: Witwatersrand gold/diamonds, scorched-earth concentration camps, and 1910 Union of South Africa)
- **Subjugation of Qing China:** the Canton System, the British-Indian opium trade triangle, First (1839–42) and Second (1856–60) Opium Wars, Treaty of Nanking and "Unequal Treaties" (Hong Kong cession, treaty ports, extraterritoriality), the Taiping Rebellion (1850–64, Hong Xiuquan), Self-Strengthening Movement, European/Russian/Japanese "spheres of influence," US Open Door Policy (1899), and the Boxer Uprising (1899–1901)
- **Colonial regimes in Southeast Asia:** Dutch Cultuurstelsel (Cultivation System) and Ethical Policy in Indonesia, British tin-and-rubber plantation economy and tri-ethnic plural society in Malaya, French Indochina, and the 1898 Spanish-American War transferring the Philippines to US rule
- Latin American independence wars (Simón Bolívar, José de San Martín), caudillo oligarchies, and US informal empire (Monroe Doctrine 1823, 1898 Platt Amendment, Panama Canal, Theodore Roosevelt’s Corollary and Dollar Diplomacy)
- **The Meiji Restoration in Japan (1868):** Commodore Perry’s expedition, overthrow of the Tokugawa Shogunate, Fukoku Kyohei defensive modernization, abolition of feudal domains and the samurai class, 1889 Meiji Constitution, First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95, Treaty of Shimonoseki), 1902 Anglo-Japanese Alliance, Russo-Japanese War (1904–05: Port Arthur, Mukden, Tsushima), and 1910 annexation of Korea.

### Part 6: The Great War, Imperial Collapse, the Paris Peace Settlement, and Interwar Diplomacy (1914–1928)
- **Structural causes of World War I:** Bismarckian alliances collapsing under Kaiser Wilhelm II’s Weltpolitik, rigid alliance blocs (Triple Alliance vs. Triple Entente: 1894 Franco-Russian, 1904 Entente Cordiale, 1907 Anglo-Russian), Anglo-German Dreadnought naval arms race, Moroccan Crises (1905, 1911), the Balkan powder keg (1908 Bosnian crisis, 1912–13 Balkan Wars), the July Crisis of 1914 (Sarajevo assassination), and the Schlieffen Plan
- **Industrial total war and global mobilization (1914–1918):** Western Front trench stalemates (Marne, Verdun, Somme, Passchendaele), machine guns, heavy artillery, poison gas, tanks, German U-boat warfare vs. British naval blockade, Eastern Front (Tannenberg, Brusilov Offensive, March 1918 Treaty of Brest-Litovsk), US entry (1917), war economies, and mobilization of 1.5 million Indian troops across Ypres, Gallipoli, East Africa, and Mesopotamia
- **Collapse of four continental empires:** Romanov, Hohenzollern, Habsburg, and Ottoman dynasties
- **The Paris Peace Conference (1919–20):** Woodrow Wilson’s Fourteen Points vs. Clemenceau and Lloyd George
- **The Treaty of Versailles (June 1919):** Article 231 (War Guilt Clause), 132-billion gold mark reparations, territorial losses (Polish Corridor, Danzig, Alsace-Lorraine), Mandate colonies, Rhineland demilitarization, and Reichswehr caps
- Minor peace treaties (Saint-Germain with Austria, Trianon with Hungary, Neuilly with Bulgaria, Sèvres with the Ottoman Empire) and multi-ethnic successor states (Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Poland)
- Structural design defects of the League of Nations (US Senate non-ratification, unanimity rule, absence of military enforcement)
- **1920s interwar diplomacy:** Washington Naval Conference (1921–22: Four-Power, Five-Power 5:5:3 ratio, and Nine-Power Treaties), 1923 Franco-Belgian occupation of the Ruhr and Weimar hyperinflation, Dawes Plan (1924) and Young Plan (1929) circular debt-reparations loop, Locarno Treaties (1925), and Kellogg-Briand Pact (1928).

### Part 7: The Dismantling of the Ottoman Empire, the Mandate System, Kemalist Turkey, and the Remaking of the Modern Middle East (1914–1948)
- Ottoman Tanzimat reforms, the 1908 Young Turk Revolution (CUP), 1914 Ottoman-German alliance, Gallipoli campaign (1915), Armenian Genocide, and Mesopotamian/Palestine campaigns (Kut-al-Amara, Baghdad, and Jerusalem with Indian Army deployments)
- **Contradictory British wartime promises:** the Hussein-McMahon Correspondence (1915–16) and the Arab Revolt, the secret Anglo-French Sykes-Picot Agreement (1916) partitioning the Levant and Mesopotamia, and the Balfour Declaration (1917) supporting a Jewish national home in Palestine
- **The Turkish War of Independence (1919–1923):** Mustafa Kemal Atatürk’s nationalist resistance against the punitive Treaty of Sèvres (1920), defeat of Allied/Greek forces, and the Treaty of Lausanne (1923)
- Kemalist secular modernization ("Six Arrows": abolition of the Sultanate 1922 and the Ottoman Caliphate 1924, Swiss civil code, Latin alphabet, women’s suffrage, Étatism) alongside Reza Shah Pahlavi’s modernization in Iran (1925)
- **The League of Nations Class-A Mandate System:** French mandates (Syria and Lebanon) and British mandates (Iraq, Palestine, Transjordan)
- Discovery of Middle Eastern petroleum, the 1928 Red Line Agreement, the 1933 Saudi-ARAMCO concession, and the "Seven Sisters" Western oil cartel
- **The Palestine Mandate crisis:** rising Jewish Aliyahs during the Nazi era, the 1936–39 Arab Revolt, 1937 Peel Commission partition proposal, 1939 British White Paper, post-WWII Zionist insurgency, UNGA Resolution 181 (November 1947 Partition Plan), establishment of the State of Israel (May 1948), the 1948–49 First Arab-Israeli War, 1949 Green Line armistice, and the Palestinian Nakba.

### Part 8: The Russian Bolshevik Revolution, Stalinist Command Industrialization, and the Chinese Revolutionary Arc (1911–1976)
- **Structural crisis of Tsarist autocracy under Nicholas II:** agrarian land hunger, rapid industrial concentration, the 1905 Revolution (Bloody Sunday, Petrograd Soviet, October Manifesto, Dumas), Stolypin’s agrarian reforms, and WWI military collapse
- The February Revolution (March 1917) and "Dual Power" between the Provisional Government (Lvov, Kerensky) and the Petrograd Soviet
- **Lenin’s April Theses and the Bolshevik October Revolution (November 1917):** Decrees on Peace and Land, Russian Civil War (1918–21: Trotsky’s Red Army vs. White Armies and Allied intervention), War Communism (Prodrazvyorstka grain requisitioning, Kronstadt rebellion) transitioning to Lenin’s New Economic Policy (NEP, 1921–28), formation of the USSR (1922), and the Comintern (including the 1920 Lenin–M.N. Roy Colonial Theses debate on bourgeois-democratic vs. peasant-communist anti-imperial strategy)
- **Stalin’s ascent ("Socialism in One Country" vs. Trotsky’s "Permanent Revolution"):** forced agrarian collectivization (Kolkhoz/Sovkhoz, liquidation of the Kulaks, and the 1932–33 Holodomor famine), Gosplan Five-Year Plans for rapid heavy industrialization (Magnitogorsk, Ural industrial base), the Great Purges/Terror (1936–38 Moscow Trials), and the GULAG system
- **The Chinese Revolutionary Arc (1911–1976):** Sun Yat-sen’s Three Principles of the People and the 1911 Xinhai Revolution overthrowing the Qing dynasty, Yuan Shikai and the Warlord Era, the May Fourth Movement (1919), 1921 founding of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), First United Front, Chiang Kai-shek’s 1927 Shanghai purge, Mao Zedong’s pivot to peasant guerrilla revolution, and the Long March (1934–35)
- Second United Front against Japan (1937–45), the 1945–49 Chinese Civil War (KMT vs. CCP), and the October 1949 proclamation of the People’s Republic of China
- **Maoist transformations:** agrarian land reform, First Five-Year Plan, Hundred Flowers Campaign, the Great Leap Forward (1958–62: People’s Communes and the Great Chinese Famine), and the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution (1966–76: Red Guards, purge of Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping).

### Part 9: The 1929 Capitalist Collapse, the Keynesian New Deal, and the Rise of Totalitarian Fascism, Nazism, and Japanese Militarism (1919–1939)
- **The Great Depression of 1929:** structural fragility of the Gold Exchange Standard, agricultural/industrial overproduction, US Dawes/Young loan dependence, the October 1929 Wall Street Crash, banking panics, Federal Reserve contraction, the Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act (1930), debt deflation, and competitive devaluations
- **Global-subcontinental entanglement:** transmission of commodity price crashes into peripheral colonies (50%+ collapse in Indian agricultural prices and distress gold exports from India to London post-1931)
- Keynesian macroeconomics (John Maynard Keynes’s General Theory, 1936) and Franklin D. Roosevelt’s New Deal (1933–39: Relief, Recovery, Reform—Glass-Steagall Act/FDIC, abandoning the Gold Standard, TVA, CCC, WPA, AAA, Wagner Act, Social Security Act)
- **Rise of Italian Fascism (Benito Mussolini):** post-WWI "mutilated victory" and Biennio Rosso, Blackshirt Squadrismo, October 1922 March on Rome, Matteotti crisis, the Corporative State, 1929 Lateran Treaty, and 1935 invasion of Ethiopia
- **Collapse of the Weimar Republic and rise of German National Socialism (Adolf Hitler):** "Stab-in-the-Back" myth, 1923 Beer Hall Putsch, Article 48 decrees, Depression unemployment, Mein Kampf racial-territorial ideology (Lebensraum), Jan 1933 appointment as Chancellor, Reichstag Fire Decree, the Enabling Act (March 1933), Gleichschaltung, Night of the Long Knives (1934), 1935 Nuremberg Laws, Kristallnacht (1938), and Schacht’s Mefo-bill deficit rearmament
- **Japanese militarism:** Depression silk crash, the 1931 Mukden Incident and invasion of Manchuria (Manchukuo), and withdrawal from the League of Nations
- **The slide to global war:** 1936 Rhineland remilitarization, Rome-Berlin Axis and Anti-Comintern Pact, the Spanish Civil War (1936–39: Franco vs. Second Republic, Guernica), failure of Appeasement (March 1938 Anschluss of Austria, September 1938 Munich Agreement partitioning the Sudetenland), and the August 1939 Molotov-Ribbentrop Nazi-Soviet Non-Aggression Pact.

### Part 10: World War II, the Holocaust, Total War Mobilization, the Atomic Era, and the Yalta-Potsdam Settlement (1939–1945)
- **Phase I Axis ascendancy (1939–41):** invasion of Poland, Blitzkrieg combined-arms doctrine, Fall of France (May–June 1940, Dunkirk, Vichy regime), Battle of Britain (1940), Battle of the Atlantic, and North African/Balkan campaigns
- **Phase II global expansion (1941–42):** Operation Barbarossa (June 1941 Nazi invasion of the USSR), the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937, Rape of Nanking), Japanese Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, US oil embargo, Pearl Harbor (Dec 7, 1941), and rapid Japanese conquest of Malaya, Singapore, Philippines, Dutch East Indies, and Burma
- **Phase III turning points and industrial total war (1942–44):** Battle of Midway (June 1942), Battle of El Alamein (Oct–Nov 1942), and the decisive Soviet victories at Stalingrad (1942–43), Kursk (1943), and Operation Bagration (1944), alongside Operation Overlord (D-Day, June 1944)
- **Allied economic and scientific mobilization:** US Lend-Lease ("Arsenal of Democracy"), Soviet Ural factory relocation, female industrial labor, Enigma cryptography (Bletchley Park), and the 2.5-million-strong Indian Army across North Africa, Italy, the China-Burma-India (CBI) theater, and the 1944 Battles of Imphal and Kohima (plus wartime Sterling Balance accumulation)
- **The Holocaust (Shoah):** Einsatzgruppen, the January 1942 Wannsee Conference ("Final Solution"), industrial extermination camps (Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka), and Japanese Unit 731 war crimes
- The Manhattan Project, atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (August 1945), and Soviet invasion of Manchuria
- **Wartime summits and post-war order:** Atlantic Charter (1941), Tehran (1943), Yalta (Feb 1945), Potsdam (July–Aug 1945), division of Germany/Europe, 1944 Bretton Woods Conference, 1945 San Francisco UN Charter, and the Nuremberg and Tokyo War Crimes Trials.

### Part 11: Decolonization in Asia and Africa, the Anti-Apartheid Struggle, Dependency Theory, and the Third World Project (1945–1994)
- **Structural causes of post-war European imperial retreat:** economic exhaustion, Sterling debt, shattered colonial prestige in Asia, and bipolar superpower dynamics
- **Asian decolonization trajectories:** Partition of India (1947), Indonesian National Revolution against Dutch rule (1945–49 under Sukarno and Hatta), British counter-insurgency in the Malayan Emergency (1948–60, Briggs Plan, 1957 Merdeka, and 1965 separation of Lee Kuan Yew’s Singapore), and the First Indochina War against France (Ho Chi Minh and Vo Nguyen Giap’s Viet Minh victory at Dien Bien Phu 1954 and the Geneva Accords)
- **African decolonization trajectories:** Kwame Nkrumah’s constitutional mobilization in Ghana (1957) and Pan-Africanism, Julius Nyerere’s Ujamaa in Tanzania, the Mau Mau uprising in settler Kenya (1952–60, Jomo Kenyatta), the Algerian War of Independence (1954–62: FLN vs. French state and Pieds-Noirs, Frantz Fanon, collapse of the French Fourth Republic, Charles de Gaulle, and the 1962 Évian Accords), the Congo Crisis (1960–65: Katanga secession, assassination of Patrice Lumumba 1961, Mobutu), and the Portuguese colonial wars in Angola, Mozambique, and Guinea-Bissau (1961–74, Amílcar Cabral, triggering Lisbon’s 1974 Carnation Revolution)
- **The Anti-Apartheid struggle in South Africa (1948–1994):** 1948 National Party codification of Apartheid (Group Areas Act, Pass Laws, Bantustans), African National Congress (ANC) mobilization, 1955 Freedom Charter, 1960 Sharpeville Massacre, Umkhonto we Sizwe, 1963–64 Rivonia Trial (Nelson Mandela, Walter Sisulu, Oliver Tambo), Steve Biko and the 1976 Soweto Uprising, global sanctions, F.W. de Klerk’s 1990 unbanning, and the 1994 democratic transition and Truth and Reconciliation Commission
- **Political economy of neo-colonialism:** Dependency Theory (Raúl Prebisch and Hans Singer’s declining terms of trade thesis, Andre Gunder Frank, Samir Amin, Walter Rodney’s How Europe Underdeveloped Africa), Bandung Conference (1955), Non-Aligned Movement (Belgrade 1961), G77, UNCTAD (1964), and the UN New International Economic Order (NIEO, 1974).

### Part 12: The Cold War Architecture: Bipolarity, European Integration, Nuclear Brinkmanship, and Global Proxy Warfare (1945–1985)
- **Consolidation of bipolarity:** George Kennan’s Long Telegram (1946), Churchill’s "Iron Curtain" speech, Truman Doctrine (1947), Containment, Marshall Plan (1948), and Soviet Cominform (1947) and COMECON (1949)
- **Division of Germany (FRG and GDR), the Berlin Blockade and Airlift (1948–49), construction of the Berlin Wall (1961), and military alliances:** NATO (1949) vs. the Warsaw Pact (1955)
- **Post-war Western European economic recovery (Wirtschaftswunder, Monnet Plan) and European Integration:** 1950 Schuman Declaration, European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC, 1951), Treaty of Rome (1957 creating the EEC Common Market and EURATOM), and 1973 enlargement
- **Nuclear arms race and space race:** hydrogen bomb, Sputnik (1957), ICBM/SLBM triads, Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD), Khrushchev’s 1956 de-Stalinization speech and Hungarian intervention, and the Cuban Missile Crisis (October 1962)
- **Major Cold War proxy conflicts:** the Korean War (1950–53: 38th Parallel, Inchon, Chinese PVA intervention, Panmunjom Armistice), the Vietnam War (1955–75: Gulf of Tonkin, Viet Cong, Ho Chi Minh Trail, 1968 Tet Offensive, Vietnamization, 1973 Paris Accords, 1975 Fall of Saigon), and US interventions in Latin America (1954 Guatemala coup, 1959 Cuban Revolution under Fidel Castro and Che Guevara, 1961 Bay of Pigs, 1973 Chilean coup against Salvador Allende, Operation Condor, and Nicaraguan Contras)
- The Sino-Soviet Split (1960–69: ideological clash, 1968 Brezhnev Doctrine in Prague, 1969 Ussuri River border war), Nixon-Kissinger-Zhou Enlai triangular diplomacy (1972 Shanghai Communiqué), Willy Brandt’s Ostpolitik, Détente, PTBT (1963), NPT (1968), SALT I & II, the 1972 ABM Treaty, 1975 Helsinki Accords (CSCE), and the 1979 collapse of Détente.

### Part 13: The West Asian Crucible: Pan-Arabism, Arab-Israeli Wars, the 1973 Oil Shock, the Iranian Revolution, and the Afghan Jihad (1952–1990)
- **Rise of Pan-Arab nationalism:** Gamal Abdel Nasser’s 1952 Egyptian revolution, Ba'athism in Syria and Iraq, nationalization of the Suez Canal, and the 1956 Suez Crisis (Tripartite Aggression by Israel, Britain, and France halted by US-Soviet pressure)
- **The Arab-Israeli Wars:** the Six-Day War (June 1967: Israeli occupation of Sinai, Gaza, West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Golan Heights; UNSC Resolution 242), rise of the PLO (Yasser Arafat, Black September 1970, 1982 Lebanon War), the Yom Kippur / October War (1973 under Anwar Sadat), the Camp David Accords (1978) and 1979 Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty, and the First Intifada (1987)
- **The 1971 Nixon Shock (ending Bretton Woods Gold-Dollar convertibility) and the 1973 OPEC Oil Embargo:** quadrupling of oil prices, petrodollar recycling through Western banks into Third World debt, end of the post-war Western boom, global stagflation, and the Gulf infrastructure boom triggering the South Asian labor-remittance corridor
- **The Iranian Revolution of 1979:** legacy of the 1953 CIA-MI6 Operation Ajax coup against Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh, Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi’s White Revolution and SAVAK repression, overthrow of the monarchy by Ayatollah Khomeini, establishment of the Islamic Republic (Velayat-e Faqih), and the 1979–81 US Embassy hostage crisis
- **The Iran-Iraq War (1980–1988):** Saddam Hussein’s invasion, trench attrition, chemical weapon deployment, and the Tanker War
- **The Soviet-Afghan War (1979–1989):** 1978 Saur Revolution, Dec 1979 Soviet intervention, CIA Operation Cyclone (US, Saudi, and Pakistani ISI funding and Stinger missiles for the Afghan Mujahideen), 1988 Geneva Accords, 1989 Soviet withdrawal, and the genesis of transnational jihadist militancy (Al-Qaeda, the Taliban, and weaponization of the Af-Pak frontier).

### Part 14: The Neoliberal Turn, Deng’s Market Reforms, the Collapse of the Soviet Imperium, and the Unipolar Moment (1978–2001)
- **The Neoliberal counter-revolution:** crisis of 1970s Keynesian stagflation, rise of Chicago School monetarism (Milton Friedman, Friedrich Hayek), Margaret Thatcher (1979) and Ronald Reagan (1981) deregulation/privatization, the 1979–82 Volcker interest-rate shock triggering the 1982 Latin American Sovereign Debt Crisis ("Lost Decade"), and the Washington Consensus (IMF/World Bank Structural Adjustment Programs)
- **Divergent Communist reform trajectories:** Deng Xiaoping’s post-1978 "Reform and Opening Up" in China (Four Modernizations, Household Responsibility System dismantling communes, Township and Village Enterprises, coastal Special Economic Zones like Shenzhen, and the June 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown preserving CCP monopoly) vs. structural contradictions of the Soviet command economy (Brezhnev stagnation, microelectronics lag, grain import dependency, post-1985 oil price collapse, 1986 Chernobyl disaster, and military overstretch)
- **Mikhail Gorbachev’s reforms (1985–91):** Perestroika (economic restructuring), Glasnost (political openness unleashing republic nationalisms), 1987 INF Treaty, and the Sinatra Doctrine
- **The Revolutions of 1989 across Central and Eastern Europe:** Poland’s Solidarity (Lech Wałęsa), Hungary, fall of the Berlin Wall (Nov 9, 1989), Czechoslovakia’s Velvet Revolution (Václav Havel), overthrow of Ceaușescu in Romania, and the 1990 2+4 Treaty for German Reunification
- The August 1991 Moscow coup attempt, Boris Yeltsin, the Belovezha Accords, and the December 1991 dissolution of the USSR into 15 republics (collapsing the Soviet Rupee-Ruble trade bloc and accelerating market liberalization across non-aligned economies)
- **The Unipolar Moment (1990–2001):** Fukuyama’s "End of History" vs. Huntington’s "Clash of Civilizations", the First Gulf War (1990–91: Operation Desert Storm), the 1992 Maastricht Treaty creating the European Union and Eurozone, violent disintegration of Yugoslavia (Bosnian War, 1995 Srebrenica genocide, Dayton Accords, 1999 Kosovo War), the 1994 Rwandan Genocide, ICTY/ICTR and the "Responsibility to Protect" (R2P) doctrine, creation of the WTO (1995), the 1997–98 Asian Financial Crisis, 1999 NATO eastward expansion, and the September 11, 2001 attacks.
