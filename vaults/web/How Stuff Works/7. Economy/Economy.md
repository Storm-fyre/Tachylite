### Part 1: National Income Accounting, Growth Dynamics, Inclusive Growth, and the Structural Transformation Paradox
- **Conceptual and accounting foundations:** Gross Domestic Product (GDP), Gross Value Added (GVA), Net Domestic Product (NDP), Net National Product (NNP), and Gross National Income (GNI)
- Factor Cost vs. Basic Prices vs. Market Prices (production vs. product taxes and subsidies)
- Nominal vs. Real GDP, GDP Deflator, and Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
- The February 2026 MoSPI GDP Base-Year Revision from 2011–12 to FY 2022–23 (incorporating MCA-21 corporate filings, GSTN transaction data, PLFS, and ASUSE)
- **Savings and investment dynamics:** Gross Capital Formation (GCF), Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF), Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR), Potential GDP determinants, and the private corporate Capex cycle
- **The anomalous structural transformation of the Indian economy:** premature de-industrialization and bypass of labor-intensive manufacturing, transitioning directly from low-productivity agriculture to high-skill services
- Labor productivity divergences across primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors
- **Growth vs. Inclusive Growth and development:** limitations of GDP, Green GDP and the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA), Human Development Index (HDI), Inequality-adjusted HDI, Lorenz Curve, Gini Coefficient, Kuznets Curve, Palma Ratio, Poverty Estimation Committees (Alagh, Lakdawala, Tendulkar, Rangarajan) and the Multidimensional Poverty Index (Global MPI vs. NITI Aayog National MPI), the Middle-Income Trap, financial inclusion architecture (PMJDY), and the Economic Survey 2025–26 doctrine of Disciplined Swadeshi.

### Part 2: The Developmental State, Five-Year Plans, and the 1991 Structural Adjustment Watershed
- **Historical evolution of economic planning:** Visvesvaraya Plan (1934), National Planning Committee (1938), Bombay Plan (1944), People’s Plan, Gandhian Plan, Sarvodaya Plan, and creation of the Planning Commission (1950) and National Development Council (1952)
- First Five-Year Plan (1951–56: Harrod-Domar model, agrarian and river-valley dam focus)
- Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61: P.C. Mahalanobis two-sector/four-sector heavy-industrialization strategy, Import-Substitution Industrialization - ISI, capital-goods priority, and the Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 Schedule A/B/C public-sector commanding heights)
- Trajectory from the Third to the Twelfth Five-Year Plans (Gadgil-Mukherjee formula, Plan Holidays, Rolling Plans, Hindu Rate of Growth, Minimum Needs Programme)
- The License-Permit-Quota Raj (IDRA 1951), monopolies regulation (MRTP Act 1969), FERA (1973), and nationalization of commercial banks (1969 and 1980)
- **Structural causes of the 1991 Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis:** 1980s fiscal deficits, twin deficits, short-term external debt vulnerabilities, collapse of the Soviet trade bloc, and the 1990–91 Gulf War shock
- **The July 1991 Structural Adjustment Program:** IMF conditionalities, Washington Consensus, Rupee devaluation, Industrial Policy Statement 1991, Raja Chelliah tax reforms, Narasimham banking reforms, and transition to NITI Aayog (2015) and the market-led regulatory state.

### Part 3: Monetary Policy Architecture, Central Banking, Money Supply, and Liquidity Frameworks
- **Institutional foundation:** Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Act, 1934, central bank operational autonomy vs. Section 7 government directions, and the Bimal Jalan Committee on the Economic Capital Framework (Contingent Risk Buffer and RBI surplus transfers)
- **Money supply aggregates:** Reserve / High-Powered Money (M0), Narrow Money (M1, M2), Broad Money (M3, M4), velocity of circulation, and the Money Multiplier
- **Evolution of monetary policy frameworks:** Monetary Targeting (Chakravarty Committee) to Multiple Indicator Approach to Urjit Patel Committee and Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT)
- The Monetary Policy Framework Agreement (2016), statutory creation of the 6-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC: 4% ± 2% headline CPI target), and the March 2026 Second Statutory 5-Year FIT Review retaining the 4% ± 2% target for the 2026–2031 cycle
- **Quantitative instruments:** CRR, SLR, Bank Rate, Policy Repo Rate, Fixed Reverse Repo Rate, Standing Deposit Facility (SDF), and Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)
- **Qualitative and macroprudential tools:** moral suasion, LTV caps, and risk-weight tightening
- **Liquidity management frameworks:** LAF corridor, VRR/VRRR, Open Market Operations (OMO), Operation Twist, LTRO/TLTRO, Market Stabilization Scheme (MSS), and Forex swaps
- Monetary transmission (BPLR to Base Rate to MCLR to EBLR), Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC / e₹ Wholesale and Retail pilots), and Project Nexus.

### Part 4: The Banking Sector, Basel III Standards, Expected Credit Loss (ECL), and the IBC Resolution Ecosystem
- **Structure of the Indian banking sector:** Public Sector Banks (PSBs), Private Sector Banks, Foreign Banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Differentiated Banks (Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks), and Urban/Rural Cooperative Banks
- P.J. Nayak Committee, Indradhanush Plan, PSB mega-mergers, the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2024/2025, and the Union Budget 2026–27 High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat
- **Evolution of Non-Performing Assets (NPAs):** Special Mention Accounts (SMA-0, 1, 2), 90-day overdue norm, Twin Balance Sheet problem transitioning to the Four Balance Sheet challenge, Asset Quality Reviews (AQR), and Prompt Corrective Action (PCA)
- Transition from Incurred-Loss (IRACP) provisioning to the April 2026 RBI Final Directions on the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Provisioning Framework (effective April 2027: IFRS-9 three-stage model using Probability of Default, Loss Given Default, and Exposure at Default)
- **Basel III regulatory standards:** CRAR/CAR, CET-1, AT-1 perpetual bonds, Tier-2 capital, Capital Conservation Buffer, Countercyclical Capital Buffer, LCR, NSFR, D-SIBs, and DICGC deposit insurance
- Historical resolution regimes (DRTs, Lok Adalats, SARFAESI Act 2002, CDR, S4A)
- **The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016:** institutional pillars (IBBI, NeSL, IPs, NCLT/NCLAT), CIRP (Committee of Creditors, Sections 12, 12A, 29A, 53 waterfall), Supreme Court jurisprudence (Swiss Ribbons, Essar Steel, Vidarbha Industries, Rainbow Papers), Pre-Packaged Insolvency (PPIRP), Cross-Border Insolvency, and NARCL/IDRCL bad bank architecture.

### Part 5: Fiscal Architecture, the Income-Tax Act 2025, GST Mechanics, and Public Debt Management
- **Components of the Union Budget (Article 112):** Revenue Account vs. Capital Account and capital expenditure fiscal multipliers
- **Taxonomy of budget deficits:** Revenue, Effective Revenue, Fiscal, Primary, and Monetized Deficits
- **Direct taxation and the Income-Tax Act, 2025 (effective April 2026, replacing the Income-Tax Act, 1961):** unified "Tax Year" concept eliminating Previous Year vs. Assessment Year, simplified schedules, New vs. Old Personal Income Tax regimes, Corporate Tax cuts, OECD Pillar Two 15% Global Minimum Corporate Tax, Angel Tax abolition, Capital Gains rationalization, Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT reduced from 15% to 14% in Budget 2026–27), STT, Equalization Levy, Virtual Digital Assets taxation, and the Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme 2026
- Tax Buoyancy, Tax Elasticity, and the Laffer Curve
- **Indirect taxation:** Customs tariffs, inverted duty structures, and Goods and Services Tax (GST) architecture (Destination-Based VAT, Input Tax Credit chain, e-Way Bills, e-Invoicing, rate rationalization, and GST Appellate Tribunals - GSTAT)
- **The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003:** N.K. Singh Committee review, escape clauses, off-budget borrowings, and the Post-FY26 Fiscal Consolidation shift to a Central Government Debt-to-GDP Ratio Anchor (targeting 50 ± 1% by March 2031)
- Zero-Based, Outcome, and Gender Budgeting, Ways and Means Advances (WMA), Sovereign Green Bonds, New PSE Policy (2021), DIPAM disinvestment, National Monetization Pipeline (NMP), and Dedicated CPSE REITs (Budget 2026–27).

### Part 6: Inflation Dynamics, Statistical Base-Year Revisions (2026), and Household Financial Savings
- **Conceptual taxonomy of inflation:** Demand-Pull, Cost-Push, and Built-In (wage-price spiral) inflation
- Headline vs. Core Inflation
- Phillips Curve (short-run trade-off vs. long-run NAIRU), Stagflation, Skewflation, Shrinkflation, Reflation, Disinflation, and Deflation
- **Structural drivers of inflation in the subcontinent:** imported crude oil and edible-oil shocks, food price volatility (TOP crops: Tomato, Onion, Potato; pulses and cereals), monsoon vagaries, El Niño shocks, and supply-chain markups
- **Statistical measurement architectures and the 2026 MoSPI Base-Year Revisions:** Consumer Price Index (CPI-Combined, CPI-Rural, CPI-Urban compiled by NSO: February 2026 CPI Base-Year Revision from 2012 to 2024 based on the 2022–24 HCES, re-weighting the Food and Beverages basket downward), CPI-IW, CPI-AL, and CPI-RL (compiled by Labour Bureau)
- Wholesale Price Index (WPI compiled by OEA, DPIIT: Primary Articles, Fuel & Power, Manufactured Products, absence of services, and CPI-WPI divergence mechanics)
- Index of Industrial Production (IIP: May 2026 Base-Year Revision from 2011–12 to 2022–23, Mining, Manufacturing, Electricity, Eight Core Industries Index, and Use-Based classification), and Annual Survey of Industries (ASI)
- **Macroeconomic impacts of persistent inflation:** real interest rate suppression, fiscal drag, currency depreciation pressures, erosion of net household financial savings toward physical assets, and unsecured retail consumer credit dynamics.

### Part 7: Capital Markets, Global Bond Index Inclusion, NBFC Shadow Banking, and Pension/Insurance Architectures
- Money market vs. Capital market instruments (Call/Notice Money, Treasury Bills, Commercial Papers, Certificates of Deposit, Tri-Party Repo)
- **Equity capital markets:** Primary Market (IPOs, FPOs, Rights Issues, QIPs) vs. Secondary Market
- Market Infrastructure Institutions (BSE, NSE, Clearing Corporations, Depositories NSDL/CDSL) and T+1 and T+0 instant settlement cycles
- **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI Act, 1992):** insider trading, front-running, Related Party Transactions, LODR corporate governance norms, Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs), and SEBI’s regulatory curbs on retail Futures & Options (F&O) derivatives
- **Structural underdevelopment of the Indian Corporate Bond Market:** bank-lending dominance, credit rating opacity, and Corporate Debt Market Development Fund (CDMDF)
- **Sovereign Bond Market internationalization:** inclusion of Indian FAR G-Secs in Global Bond Indices (JP Morgan GBI-EM 2024, Bloomberg EM 2025, FTSE Russell 2025) and RBI Retail Direct
- GIFT City International Financial Services Centre (IFSCA)
- **Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs):** shadow banking credit intermediation, Asset-Liability Mismatch (ALM) liquidity crises (IL&FS and DHFL), RBI Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) four-layer framework, Bank-NBFC Co-Lending Model, and Microfinance Institutions
- **Insurance and Pension markets:** IRDAI, 100% Insurance FDI, Bima Sugam, PFRDA, National Pension System (NPS) vs. Old Pension Scheme (OPS), and the Unified Pension Scheme (UPS, 2025).

### Part 8: The External Sector, Balance of Payments, Currency Trilemma, and Foreign Trade Policy
- **Balance of Payments (BoP) accounting framework:** Current Account (Merchandise Trade Balance; Invisibles Balance: net software/GCC services exports, Secondary Income $125B+ diaspora remittances, and Primary Investment Income) vs. Capital and Financial Account (Foreign Direct Investment - FDI, Press Note 3 screening, Foreign Portfolio Investment - FPI, External Commercial Borrowings - ECBs, and NRI Deposits: FCNR-B, NRE, NRO)
- Structural Current Account Deficits (CAD) and Twin Deficit mechanics
- **Foreign Exchange Reserves:** composition (Foreign Currency Assets, Gold, IMF SDRs, Reserve Tranche Position) and adequacy metrics
- **Exchange rate determination and central bank intervention:** NEER vs. REER, Marshall-Lerner Condition, J-Curve effect, managed floating, and the "Impossible Trinity" (Mundell-Fleming Trilemma)
- **Currency convertibility and FEMA:** Current Account Convertibility (IMF Article VIII, 1994) vs. Capital Account Convertibility (First and Second Tarapore Committees), replacement of FERA with the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA, 1999), Liberalized Remittance Scheme (LRS), 2026 FEMA Non-Debt Instruments (NDI) Rules review permitting Individual Persons Resident Outside India (PROIs) equity investment, and Rupee internationalization (Special Rupee Vostro Accounts)
- **Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) 2023:** RoDTEP, Advance Authorization, EPCG, Districts as Export Hubs, SCOMET streamlining, E-Commerce Export Hubs, and Budget 2026–27 removal of the ₹10 lakh courier export cap
- Macroeconomics of New-Generation FTAs (UAE CEPA, Australia ECTA, EFTA TEPA, UK CETA), EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), and CAROTAR Rules of Origin.

### Part 9: Physical and Digital Infrastructure, PPP Investment Models, Logistics, and the Power Sector Quagmire
- Infrastructure deficit, Gross Capital Formation constraints, National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP), and the PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan
- **Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Investment Models:** EPC, BOT-Toll, BOT-Annuity, Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM), Toll-Operate-Transfer (TOT), DBFOT, Swiss Challenge Method, Viability Gap Funding (VGF), Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs), Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs), Vijay Kelkar Committee on PPPs, and the Union Budget 2026–27 Infrastructure Risk Guarantee Fund
- **Development Finance Institutions:** NaBFID, IIFCL, and NIIF
- **Logistics economics:** National Logistics Policy (2022), ULIP, Multi-Modal Logistics Parks (MMLPs), and rail-road freight modal rebalancing
- **Transportation networks:** Bharatmala highways, Dedicated Freight Corridors (EDFC, WDFC, and Budget 2026–27 East-West Dankuni-Surat DFC), Railways modernization (Kavach and 7 New High-Speed Rail Corridors in Budget 2026–27), Maritime and Ports (Sagarmala, Major Port Authorities Act 2021, Vadhavan and Galathea Bay Mega Transshipment Ports, Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme, and 20 New National Waterways), UDAN aviation, and City Economic Regions (CERs)
- **Energy infrastructure and the power sector structural quagmire:** Electricity Act 2003, Open Access, Indian Energy Exchange (IEX), Green Energy Corridors, and financial distress of State DISCOMs (AT&C losses, cross-subsidization, UDAY failure, and Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme - RDSS smart prepaid metering)
- Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) as a macroeconomic multiplier.

### Part 10: The Industrial Policy Landscape, Frontier Manufacturing, PLI Schemes, and MSME Structural Bottlenecks
- Historical trajectory of manufacturing in India (stagnation at 14–17% of GDP), National Manufacturing Policy (2011 NIMZs), and Make in India
- **Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Schemes across 14 sectors:** incremental output incentives, domestic value-addition targets, and Capex commitments
- **Union Budget 2026–27 Seven Strategic and Frontier Manufacturing Sectors:** Biopharma SHAKTI (biologics and biosimilars), India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) 2.0, Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme, Dedicated Rare Earth Corridors (Odisha, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu), Chemical Parks, Capital Goods and Container Manufacturing, and the Integrated Textile Programme (National Fibre Scheme and PM MITRA Mega Textile Parks) alongside the Revival of 200 Legacy Industrial Clusters
- Industrial Corridors (NICDC: DMIC, CBIC, AKIC) and Special Economic Zones (SEZ Act 2005, Baba Kalyani Committee, Sunset Clause, and SEZ domestic sales reforms)
- **Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs):** revised 2025–26 composite investment-and-turnover thresholds, Udyam Registration, the "Dwarfism" and "Missing Middle" pathology, U.K. Sinha Committee, RAMP programme, Samadhaan delayed-payment portal and Section 43B(h) tax rule, and the Union Budget 2026–27 "Champion SMEs" package (SME Growth Fund, Self-Reliant India Fund, CGTMSE credit guarantees, and TReDS overhaul: mandatory CPSE onboarding, invoice credit guarantees, GeM linkage, and secondary-market securitization of TReDS receivables).

### Part 11: The Services Sector Hegemony, Global Capability Centers, the Startup Ecosystem, and the Platform Economy
- Macroeconomic dominance of the tertiary sector (>54% of GVA, FDI inflows, and urban growth)
- **Structural evolution of Indian IT-BPM services:** from low-cost coding and back-office outsourcing to 1,700+ Global Capability Centers (GCCs) driving global R&D, semiconductor chip design, AI engineering, and enterprise transformation
- **Balance of Payments stabilization:** net software and business services surplus offsetting merchandise trade deficits
- **Union Budget 2026–27 Services Reforms:** the High-Powered 'Education to Employment and Enterprise' Standing Committee (targeting a 10% global services share by 2047 and assessing AI impact on service jobs) and the Unified Safe Harbour Tax Framework for IT, ITeS, KPO, and Contract R&D
- **The Indian Startup and Deep-Tech Ecosystem:** Startup India (DPIIT recognition), Unicorns, domestic Venture Capital / AIF mobilization, 2024 Abolition of Angel Tax, the ₹1 Lakh Crore Anusandhan R&D / Deep-Tech Fund of Funds, and reverse-flipping of overseas startups back to India
- **High-growth service verticals:** Tourism and Hospitality (Heal in India medical value travel), AVGC-XR (Indian Institute of Creative Technology), Financial/Professional services, and Space/Telecom services
- **The Digital Platform, E-Commerce, and Gig Economy:** platform capitalism, network effects, Quick-Commerce (dark stores) disrupting traditional unorganized Kirana retail, algorithmic management, ONDC unbundling, and state-level gig-worker welfare interventions.

### Part 12: Labor Economics, Employment Statistical Architectures, the Four Labor Codes, and the Demographic Dividend Window
- **Statistical measurement architectures of employment:** Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) by NSO (Usual Status ps+ss vs. Current Weekly Status, LFPR, WPR, and Unemployment Rate metrics), Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE), ASI, EPFO payroll data, and the e-Shram portal
- **Structural informality and dualism of the Indian labor market:** ~85–90% informal/unorganized employment without written contracts, disguised unemployment in agriculture, contractualization of formal jobs, and real wage stagnation
- "Jobless Growth" and AI/Generative AI white-collar displacement dynamics
- Employment-Linked Incentive (ELI) Schemes (EPFO-linked first-time formal workforce entry and manufacturing job-creation subsidies)
- **The Female Labour Force Participation Rate (FLFPR) conundrum:** Claudia Goldin’s U-shaped feminization curve, rising rural unpaid family helper participation vs. low urban formal absorption, Time-Use Survey unpaid care-economy burdens, and safety/creche deficits
- **The Four Labor Codes (consolidating 29 central labor statutes):** Code on Wages 2019 (statutory National Floor Wage), Industrial Relations Code 2020 (retrenchment threshold raised from 100 to 300 workers, fixed-term employment), Code on Social Security 2020 (extension to Gig, Platform, and Unorganized Workers), and OSHWC Code 2020 (inter-state migrant portability and women’s night-shift employment)
- The Demographic Dividend Window, sharp North-South fertility divergence, closing dependency ratio window (~2040–2050), and the risk of "growing old before growing rich."
