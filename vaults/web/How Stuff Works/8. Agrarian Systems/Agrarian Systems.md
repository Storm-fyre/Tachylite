### Part 1: Agrarian Structural Foundations: Land Tenure, Land Leasing, LARR 2013, and Digital Conclusive Titling
- **Historical lineages of land tenure:** colonial inheritance (Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari, Mahalwari) and creation of parasitic rentier classes
- **Post-Independence Four-Pillar Land Reforms:** Abolition of Intermediaries (Zamindars, Jagirdars, Inamdars; First Amendment 1951 and Ninth Schedule vs. Khudkasht loopholes), Tenancy Reforms (fair rent, security of tenure, ownership rights—Operation Barga in West Bengal and Kerala reforms), Land Ceiling Legislation (family caps and Benami evasions), and Consolidation of Holdings (Chakbandi)
- **Voluntary land redistribution:** Vinoba Bhave’s Bhoodan and Gramdan movements
- **Contemporary land realities (Agriculture Census):** extreme land fragmentation, declining average operational holding size (<1.08 hectares), and preponderance of Small and Marginal Farmers (>86% of holdings)
- **The informal tenancy trap:** restrictive tenancy laws driving unwritten oral sharecropping, excluding actual cultivators from KCC credit, PMFBY insurance, and MSP procurement
- NITI Aayog’s Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act, 2016 (T. Haque Committee) legalizing land leasing while protecting landowner title
- The LARR Land Acquisition Act, 2013 (Social Impact Assessment, 70%/80% consent, 2x–4x compensation)
- **Digital land modernization:** DILRMP, SVAMITVA Scheme (drone mapping of rural Abadi property cards), ULPIN (Bhu-Aadhaar 14-digit parcel ID), AgriStack Farmer Registries, and transition from Presumptive to Conclusive Land Titling (Torrens system).

### Part 2: Agro-Ecological Regionalization, Cropping Patterns, 2025–2026 Diversification Missions, and Irrigation Hydro-Agronomy
- Agro-Climatic Zones (15 Planning Commission zones) and Agro-Ecological Regions (20 NBSS&LUP / ICAR zones based on physiography, soils, bioclimate, and Length of Growing Period)
- Major cropping seasons (Kharif, Rabi, Zaid) and Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)
- **Monoculture traps:** hydro-ecologically unsustainable Rice-Wheat rotation in the semi-arid Indo-Gangetic plains and water-depleting Sugarcane shifts in drought-prone peninsular Maharashtra
- **Flagship 2025–2026 Crop Diversification and Productivity Missions:** PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (launched Oct 2025 targeting 100 low-productivity, moderate-crop-intensity, and low-credit-access districts), Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (Dalhan Aatmanirbharta Mission 2025–2031 with 100% assured procurement of Tur, Urad, and Masoor via NAFED/NCCF), National Mission on Edible Oils – Oilseeds (NMEO-Oilseeds 2024–2031) and NMEO-Oil Palm, and Mission for Cotton Productivity (Kapas Kranti 2025–2030 promoting Extra-Long Staple cotton and the 5F Vision)
- **Irrigation hydro-agronomy:** IPC vs. IPU gap, canal command areas, and tubewell over-extraction of alluvial and hard-rock aquifers
- **Groundwater depletion dynamics:** free agricultural electricity, CGWB critical/over-exploited assessments, Atal Bhujal Yojana, and PM-KUSUM solar pumps
- **Micro-irrigation economics:** drip and sprinkler fertigation, Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY: AIBP, Har Khet Ko Pani, Per Drop More Crop, Watershed Development), and Virtual Water trade.

### Part 3: The Technological Input Package: Seed Systems, Fertilizer Regimes, Farm Mechanization, and Digital Agriculture (E-Technology)
- The Green Revolution biological (HYV seeds), chemical (fertilizers/pesticides), and mechanical (tractorization/tubewells) package
- **The Indian seed ecosystem:** Breeder, Foundation, and Certified seeds, Seed Replacement Rate (SRR) and Varietal Replacement Rate (VRR) bottlenecks, public breeding (ICAR/SAUs) vs. multinational corporations, National Mission on High Yielding Seeds (2025), and Bharatiya Beej Sahakari Samiti (BBSSL)
- **Intellectual Property in agriculture:** Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights (PPV&FR) Act, 2001 (balancing plant breeder rights and farmers' statutory right to save, exchange, and sell unbranded farm-saved seed)
- **The fertilizer subsidy and soil health regime:** NPK application imbalances (deviating from the optimal 4:2:1 ratio), Urea price controls vs. Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS, 2010) for P&K fertilizers, Neem-Coated Urea, Sulphur-Coated Urea (Urea Gold), PM-PRANAM, One Nation One Fertilizer (Bharat brand), Liquid Nano Urea and Nano DAP, Soil Health Card Scheme, micronutrient deficiencies (Zinc, Boron, Sulphur), and Soil Organic Carbon (<0.5%) depletion
- **Farm mechanization:** Sub-Mission on Agricultural Mechanization (SMAM), Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs), Happy Seeder, and Namo Drone Didi
- **Digital Agriculture ("E-Technology in the aid of farmers"):** Digital Agriculture Mission (2024–2026), AgriStack (Farmer Registry, Digital Crop Survey), Krishi-DSS, AI-based National Pest Surveillance System (NPSS), and Union Budget 2026–27 Bharat-VISTAAR multilingual AI advisory platform.

### Part 4: Agri-Biotechnology, GM Mustard Jurisprudence, Genome Editing, Natural/Organic Farming, and Climate-Resilient Agro-Ecologies
- **Science and politics of Genetically Modified (GM) transgenic crops:** recombinant DNA technology, commercial adoption of Bt Cotton (2002 Cry1Ac and Cry2Ab genes), yield jumps vs. Pink Bollworm resistance, seed pricing control, and illegal HTBt cotton
- **The GM food crop impasse:** 2010 Bt Brinjal moratorium, Genetically Modified Mustard (DMH-11 Barnase-Barstar-Bar system), and the July 2024 Supreme Court Judgment on GM Mustard (Gene Campaign v. Union of India: split verdict on environmental release coupled with a unanimous binding directive to formulate a National Policy on GM Crops)
- **Statutory regulatory framework:** Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC) under the 1989 Rules of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and FSSAI GM food regulations
- **Genome-edited crops (CRISPR-Cas9):** 2022 regulatory exemption of Site-Directed Nuclease (SDN-1 and SDN-2) categories from transgenic GEAC scrutiny and ICAR biofortified crops
- **Sustainable, natural, and organic agro-ecologies:** Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF: Jeevamrutha, Beejamrutha, Acchadana, Whapasa), National Mission on Natural Farming (NMNF, 2024), BPKP, bio-fertilizers, Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY), MOVCDNER, and NPOP (export) vs. PGS-India (domestic) organic certification
- The Millet Renaissance (Shree Anna nutri-cereals, C4 climate resilience, IIMR global hub)
- **Climate-smart agriculture:** NICRA, Direct Seeded Rice (DSR), Alternate Wetting and Drying, Biochar, and agricultural carbon credit markets.

### Part 5: Agrarian Pricing Economics, the CACP-MSP Framework, PM-AASHA, Sugarcane-Ethanol Economics, FCI Procurement, and the Public Distribution System
- **The price support regime:** Minimum Support Price (MSP) for 22 mandated crops and Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) for sugarcane
- **Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP, 1965):** pricing determinants and cost concepts—Cost A2 (actual paid-out input costs), Cost A2 + FL (A2 plus imputed value of unpaid family labor), and Cost C2 (comprehensive cost including A2+FL plus imputed rental value of owned land and interest on fixed capital)
- Swaminathan Commission recommendation (C2 + 50%) vs. current policy (1.5x A2+FL), and the statutory legal guarantee debate
- **Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM-AASHA, revamped 2024–2026):** Price Support Scheme (PSS physical procurement), Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS / Bhavantar), and Market Intervention Scheme (MIS) and Price Stabilization Fund (PSF) for TOP perishables
- **Sugarcane and ethanol blending economics:** FRP vs. State Advised Prices (SAP), Rangarajan Committee 70% revenue-sharing formula, cane arrears, and the Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) Programme achieving the 20% E20 blending target in 2025–26 via B-heavy molasses, sugarcane juice, maize, and surplus FCI rice
- **Operational procurement and food security apparatus:** Food Corporation of India (FCI), regional procurement skew in Punjab/Haryana, Decentralized Procurement (DCP), Economic Cost of foodgrains, buffer stocking norms, Open Market Sale Scheme (OMSS), Shanta Kumar Committee (2015) recommendations, Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS), National Food Security Act (NFSA, 2013), PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (PMGKAY), One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC), SMART-PDS, Rice Fortification, Direct Income Transfers (PM-KISAN, Rythu Bharosa, KALIA), and the Ashok Dalwai Committee on Doubling Farmers’ Income.

### Part 6: Agricultural Marketing, APMC Monopolies, e-NAM, Contract Farming, and Post-Harvest Storage Logistics
- **Physical infrastructure of agricultural marketing:** Rural Primary Markets (Haats / GrAMs—Gramin Agricultural Markets), Secondary Wholesale Mandis, and Terminal Markets
- **State Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) Acts:** historical intent vs. contemporary failure modes (market fragmentation, mandatory first-sale restrictions, Arhtiya commission-agent cartelization, licensing entry barriers, and cascading mandi fees/cesses)
- **Reform blueprints:** Model APMC Act (2003), Model Agricultural Produce and Livestock Marketing (APLM) Act (2017), and Model Contract Farming Act (2018)
- **The 2020–2021 Three Farm Laws crisis:** Farmer’s Produce Trade and Commerce Act, Farmers Agreement on Price Assurance Act, and Essential Commodities (Amendment) Act—economic intent, Seventh Schedule Entry 28 (State List) vs. Entry 33 (Concurrent List) federalism controversies, corporate monopsony fears, and repeal
- **The National Agriculture Market (e-NAM, 2016):** pan-India electronic trading portal networking 1,389+ mandis, Platform of Platforms (PoP), and persistent inter-state trade hurdles (lack of scientific assaying/grading labs at mandis, absence of third-party dispute resolution, and high farm-to-fork price spreads)
- **Post-harvest storage, warehousing, and pledge financing:** Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority (WDRA Act, 2007), electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipts (e-NWRs) integrated with KCC to prevent distress sales, the ₹1 Lakh Crore Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF), the World’s Largest Decentralized Grain Storage Plan in the Cooperative Sector, and FCI steel silos.

### Part 7: The Food Processing Sector, Cold-Chain Logistics, Value Addition, and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs)
- **Macroeconomics of the Indian Food Processing Industry:** high labor-to-capital employment multiplier, upstream and downstream supply-chain linkages, and the structural processing deficit (processing <10% of output compared to 60–80% in developed economies, and dominance of primary milling over secondary/tertiary value addition)
- **Post-harvest losses and cold-chain skewness:** NABCONS wastage assessments in perishables, >70% cold-storage capacity skewed toward potatoes in UP and West Bengal, and acute deficits in farm-gate pack-houses, pre-cooling units, ripening chambers, and refrigerated (reefer) transport
- **State policy and infrastructure interventions:** 100% FDI under the Automatic Route in food processing and 100% FDI under Government Route for retail trading (including e-commerce) of food products produced in India
- Pradhan Mantri Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (Mega Food Parks, Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition Infrastructure, CEFPPC, Agro-Processing Clusters, Operation Greens expanded from TOP to 22 perishable TOTAL crops, and Food Safety Quality Assurance Labs)
- Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Food Processing Industry (PLISFPI: Ready-to-Eat, marine, organic, and millet-based products)
- PM Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises (PM-FME) Scheme and One District One Product (ODOP) focus
- **Collective aggregation and rural women's enterprise:** Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) under Part IXA of the Companies Act and Cooperative Societies, the 10,000 FPO Central Sector Scheme (CBBOs, matching equity grants, credit guarantees), direct ONDC/e-NAM market linkage, Feminization of Agriculture, Lakhpati Didi, and Union Budget 2026–27 Self-Help Entrepreneur (SHE-Marts) community-owned retail outlets.

### Part 8: Agrarian Financial Intermediation, Priority Sector Lending, KCC, PACS Overhaul, and Rural Credit Pathologies
- **Historical trajectory of rural credit:** colonial moneylender indebtedness, All India Rural Credit Survey (1954), bank nationalization (1969/1980), Lead Bank Scheme, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs, 1975: 50:35:15 shareholding), and NABARD (1982 Sivaraman Committee)
- **The three-tier Short-Term Rural Cooperative Credit Structure:** Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS) at village level, District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs) at district level, and State Cooperative Banks (StCBs) at state level (Vaidyanathan Committee revival and the 2022–2026 computerization of 63,000+ PACS and Model Bye-Laws turning PACS into multi-service hubs)
- **Priority Sector Lending (PSL) mandates:** mandatory 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC), 18% Agriculture target with a mandatory 10% sub-target for Small and Marginal Farmers, regional weight adjustments for credit-starved vs. credit-saturated districts, and Priority Sector Lending Certificates (PSLCs)
- The Kisan Credit Card (KCC, 1998) and Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS: 7% interest reduced to effective 4% upon prompt repayment, collateral-free limit up to ₹2 lakh, the Union Budget 2025–26 enhancement of the KCC subsidized limit from ₹3 lakh to ₹5 lakh, and the Kisan Rin Portal)
- Long-term investment credit via NABARD’s Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)
- **Structural pathologies of rural credit:** short-term vs. investment loan skew, Southern regional concentration vs. Eastern credit starvation, diversion of subsidized loans, survival of informal usurious moneylenders due to exclusion of oral tenants, and the moral hazard of farm loan waivers.

### Part 9: Agrarian Risk Mitigation, Crop Insurance (PMFBY), Economics of Animal-Rearing, and High-Value Allied Sectors
- **Systemic risk profile of Indian agriculture and evolution of crop insurance from CCIS/NAIS to Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY, 2016) and Restructured Weather Based Crop Insurance Scheme (RWBCIS):** uniform farmer premium (2% Kharif, 1.5% Rabi, 5% commercial/horticultural crops), post-harvest and localized risk coverage
- **PMFBY 2.0 revamp and tech integration:** voluntary enrollment, Beed Model (80–110 Cup and Cap risk-sharing), YES-TECH satellite/AI yield estimation, WINDS weather stations, CROPIC, and DigiClaim to eliminate Crop-Cutting Experiment (CCE) delays
- **"Economics of Animal-Rearing" and the Livestock/Dairy Sector (>30% of agricultural GVA):** Operation Flood, the AMUL cooperative model, White Revolution 2.0, Rashtriya Gokul Mission (indigenous breeds, sex-sorted semen, IVF, genomic chips), National Programme for Dairy Development, AHIDF, National Livestock Mission (poultry, small ruminants, and fodder deficit management), and National Animal Disease Control Programme (FMD, Brucellosis, and Lumpy Skin Disease vaccination)
- **Fisheries and the Blue Economy:** Inland vs. Marine fisheries, Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY), PM-MKSSY, FIDF, shrimp export dominance, and Budget 2026–27 Integrated Development of 500 Reservoirs and Amrit Sarovars
- **Horticulture and High-Value Agriculture (HVA):** Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture (MIDH), Clean Plant Programme (2024), Sericulture (Silk Samagra-2), Apiculture (Sweet Revolution), Makhana Board in Bihar, Coconut Promotion Scheme, and Dedicated Cashew, Cocoa, Sandalwood, Agarwood, and Temperate Nut Self-Reliance Programmes.

### Part 10: Multilateral Trade in Agriculture, the WTO Agreement on Agriculture, MC13/MC14 Deadlocks, and Agrarian Export Policy
- **International rules-based trading regime in agriculture:** the WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA, 1995) and its Three Structural Pillars—Market Access (tariffication and the Special Safeguard Mechanism - SSM demanded by G-33 nations), Export Subsidies (2015 Nairobi abolition and WTO sugar panel ruling), and Domestic Support (Traffic-Light Box Framework: Green Box Annex 2 non-trade-distorting support including research, infrastructure, and decoupled PM-KISAN; Blue Box Article 6.5 production-limiting payments; Development Box Article 6.2 exempting agricultural input subsidies—fertilizer, electricity, irrigation—provided to low-income or resource-poor farmers; and Amber Box trade-distorting price supports subject to Aggregate Measurement of Support - AMS and 10% De Minimis limit for developing nations)
- **The structural clash over Public Stockholding (PSH) for Food Security:** the archaic 1986–88 External Reference Price base-year calculation flaw, the 2013 Bali / 2014 Perpetual Peace Clause invoked by India for rice, and the WTO 13th (Abu Dhabi, 2024) and 14th (Yaoundé, Cameroon, March 2026) Ministerial Conferences (MC13 and MC14) deadlocks: US/Cairns Group block against a permanent solution on PSH, entry into force of the Phase-I WTO Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (2025) and MC14 Phase-II collapse over Overcapacity and Overfishing (India defending 9 million artisanal fishers), and the March 2026 MC14 lapse of the 1998 E-Commerce Customs Duty Moratorium
- India’s Agriculture Export Policy, APEDA/MPEDA, SPS/TBT non-tariff barriers (EU Maximum Residue Limits and EUDR), and sudden domestic price-control export bans/MEPs.
