### Part 1: Historical Antecedents, Ideological Consensus, and the Constituent Assembly (1773–1950)
- **Colonial statutory lineage:** Regulating Act of 1773, Pitt’s India Act of 1784, Charter Acts of 1793, 1813, 1833, and 1853, Government of India Act 1858, Indian Councils Acts of 1861, 1892, and 1909 (Morley-Minto communal electorates), Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford provincial dyarchy and bicameralism), and Government of India Act 1935 (three legislative lists, provincial autonomy, federal court, emergency provisions)
- **The Constituent Assembly (1946–1950):** Cabinet Mission Plan composition, indirect election by provincial assemblies, sovereignty under the Indian Independence Act 1947, working committees (Drafting Committee, Union Powers, Fundamental Rights and Minorities), and Jawaharlal Nehru’s Objectives Resolution (Dec 1946)
- **Core philosophical cleavages:** Gandhian decentralized village republics (Gram Swaraj) vs. B.R. Ambedkar’s modernist liberal constitutionalism vs. Nehruvian centralized developmentalism
- Minority rights bargains, rejection of separate electorates, language compromises (Munshi-Ayyangar formula), preventive detention debates, and property/land-reform bargains
- **The Constitution as a transformative social document:** Granville Austin’s "seamless web" (national unity, democracy, social revolution), Upendra Baxi’s subaltern critique, and the Twelve Schedules overview.

### Part 2: The Philosophical Architecture: The Preamble, Union and its Territory (Part I), and Citizenship Mechanics (Part II)
- **The Preamble:** ideological lineage, sovereign matrix (Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic; Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity), 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) additions (Socialist, Secular, Integrity upheld in Dr. Balram Singh v. Union of India, 2024), and legal status/amendability (In Re: Berubari Union 1960, Kesavananda Bharati 1973, LIC of India 1995)
- **The Union and its Territory (Part I, Articles 1–4):** Article 1 ("India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"—indestructible union of destructible states), Article 2 (admission/establishment of new states) vs. Article 3 (internal boundary/name alteration without state consent under Babulal Parate 1960), Article 4 (simple parliamentary majority outside Article 368), and cession of sovereign territory requiring an Article 368 amendment (Berubari Union 1960, 9th Amendment, and 100th Amendment 2015 Indo-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement)
- **Citizenship jurisprudence (Part II, Articles 5–11):** constitutional criteria at commencement, single citizenship and termination upon foreign acquisition (Article 9), and parliamentary plenary power (Article 11)
- **The Citizenship Act, 1955:** acquisition (birth—jus soli to jus sanguinis tightening, descent, registration, naturalization, incorporation) and loss (renunciation, termination, deprivation)
- Section 6A Assam Accord jurisprudence (In Re: Section 6A of the Citizenship Act, October 2024 5-Judge Bench upholding the March 24, 1971 cut-off date), National Register of Citizens (NRC), and Foreigners Tribunals (Sarbananda Sonowal)
- The Citizenship (Amendment) Act (CAA), 2019 and 2024 Rules (Article 14 challenges and Sixth Schedule/ILP exemptions)
- Legal status of Overseas Citizens of India (OCI, Sections 7A–7D), refugees, and stateless persons.

### Part 3: Fundamental Rights I: Definition of "The State," Judicial Review, Equality Jurisprudence, and Affirmative Action (Articles 12–18)
- Vertical vs. horizontal enforceability of Fundamental Rights (Kaushal Kishor v. State of UP, 2023 5-Judge Bench)
- **Concept of "The State" under Article 12:** statutory corporations and agency/instrumentality tests (R.D. Shetty, Ajay Hasia 1981, 7-Judge Pradeep Kumar Biswas 2002, Zee Telefilms, and judiciary under Art 12 in Rupa Ashok Hurra)
- **Article 13 and judicial review:** Doctrine of Severability (R.M.D. Chamarbaugwalla), Doctrine of Eclipse (Bhikaji Narain, Dularey Lodh), Doctrine of Waiver (Basheshar Nath), personal laws exclusion (Narasu Appa Mali vs. Sabarimala critique), and Article 13(4) (24th Amendment)
- **Article 14:** Equality before Law (Dicey’s Rule of Law) and Equal Protection of Laws
- transition from the reasonable classification/intelligible differentia nexus test (Anwar Ali Sarkar) to the doctrine of non-arbitrariness (E.P. Royappa 1974, Maneka Gandhi) and Manifest Arbitrariness (Shayara Bano 2017)
- **Articles 15 and 16:** formal vs. substantive equality (N.M. Thomas 1976), special provisions for women/children (Art 15(3)), educational reservations (Art 15(4)–(5), 93rd Amendment, Pramati Educational Trust 2014)
- Public employment equality and OBC affirmative action (Indra Sawhney 1992 9-Judge Bench: 27% OBC quota, 50% ceiling, Creamy Layer exclusion)
- SC/ST reservations in promotions and consequential seniority (77th, 81st Carry Forward Art 16(4B), 82nd, and 85th Amendments; M. Nagaraj 2006 quantifiable data test; Jarnail Singh 2018 applying Creamy Layer to SCs/STs; B.K. Pavitra II 2019)
- 10% EWS Reservation (103rd Amendment Articles 15(6) and 16(6), upheld in Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India, 2022 5-Judge Bench)
- Sub-classification of SCs, STs, and OBCs (Justice Rohini Commission and the landmark 7-Judge Bench in State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh, August 2024 overruling E.V. Chinnaiah 2004)
- Local domicile quotas (Art 16(3))
- Abolition of Untouchability (Article 17, Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955, SC/ST PoA Act 1989) and Abolition of Titles (Article 18, Balaji Raghavan 1996).

### Part 4: Fundamental Rights II: Democratic Freedoms, Criminal Due Process, and the Expanding Frontiers of Article 21 (Articles 19–22)
- The six democratic freedoms under Article 19(1)(a)–(g) and exhaustive reasonable restrictions under Article 19(2)–(6)
- **Freedom of speech and expression (Art 19(1)(a)):** freedom of press (Romesh Thappar, Bennett Coleman, Sakal Papers), voter’s right to know (PUCL), commercial speech, right to silence (Bijoe Emanuel), internet shutdowns and the Four-Pronged Proportionality Doctrine (Modern Dental College 2016, Anuradha Bhasin 2020, Telecommunications Act 2023), online speech (Shreya Singhal 2015 striking down Section 66A IT Act), and sedition jurisprudence (Kedar Nath Singh 1962, S.G. Vombatkere stay, and Section 152 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023)
- Assembly (Himat Lal Shah, Shaheen Bagh), Association (97th Amendment cooperatives, no right to strike in T.K. Rangarajan), Movement, Residence, and Profession (res extra commercium)
- **Article 20 criminal protections:** ex-post facto penal laws (Art 20(1)), double jeopardy (Art 20(2), Maqbool Hussain), and self-incrimination (Art 20(3), Kathi Kalu Oghad 1961, Nandini Satpathy, Selvi v. State of Karnataka 2010 barring involuntary narco/polygraph tests, and the Criminal Procedure Identification Act 2022)
- **Jurisprudential evolution of Article 21:** from A.K. Gopalan (1950 watertight compartments) to the Golden Triangle substantive due process synthesis in R.C. Cooper (1970) and Maneka Gandhi (1978)
- **The expansive Article 21 rights matrix:** Right to Privacy (K.S. Puttaswamy I 2017 9-Judge Bench, Puttaswamy II 2018 Aadhaar judgment, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023), gender and bodily autonomy (NALSA 2014, Navtej Singh Johar 2018, Joseph Shine 2018, X v. Principal Secretary Health 2022, and Supriyo v. Union of India 2023 5-Judge marriage equality case), Right to Die with Dignity (passive euthanasia and living wills in Common Cause 2018 and 2023 modification), clean environment and Right against Climate Change (M.C. Mehta and M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India 2024), speedy trial/bail (Hussainara Khatoon, D.K. Basu, Satender Kumar Antil, BNSS Section 479), and the November 2024 Supreme Court Pan-India Judgment against punitive "Bulldozer Justice" demolitions
- Right to Education (Article 21A, 86th Amendment 2002)
- **Article 22:** punitive arrest safeguards (Pankaj Bansal 2023 and Prabir Purkayastha 2024 written grounds of arrest) and preventive detention jurisprudence (3-month advisory board limit, NSA, UAPA, Ameena Begum 2023).

### Part 5: Fundamental Rights III: Exploitation, Freedom of Conscience, Minority Rights, Constitutional Remedies, and Martial Law (Articles 23–30, 32–35)
- **Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24):** prohibition of human trafficking, begar, and forced labor (PUDR Asiad Workers 1982 minimum-wage ruling, Bandhua Mukti Morcha, Bonded Labour Abolition Act 1976) and prohibition of child labor in hazardous employment (Article 24, M.C. Mehta Sivakasi case, Child and Adolescent Labour Amendment Act 2016)
- **Freedom of conscience and religion (Articles 25–28):** individual freedom to profess, practice, and propagate (Art 25, Rev. Stainislaus 1977 conversion ruling and state anti-conversion statutes) vs. collective denominational autonomy (Art 26, S.P. Mittal criteria)
- **The Essential Religious Practices (ERP) doctrine:** origins in 7-Judge Shirur Mutt (1954), evolution (Durgah Committee, Tandava Dance, Shayara Bano), and constitutional morality critiques (Indian Young Lawyers Association Sabarimala 2018, Hijab split verdict, and the pending 9-Judge Constitution Bench reference)
- Places of Worship (Special Provisions) Act, 1991 (M. Siddiq Ayodhya 2019 and Gyanvapi/Mathura litigation) and the Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025 (UMEED Act)
- Freedom from religious taxation (Art 27) and religious instruction in schools (Art 28)
- **Cultural and educational rights of minorities (Articles 29–30):** state-level minority unit determination (11-Judge T.M.A. Pai Foundation 2002), regulatory autonomy (St. Stephen’s, Islamic Academy, P.A. Inamdar), and the landmark 7-Judge Constitution Bench verdict in Aligarh Muslim University v. Naresh Agarwal (November 2024 overruling Azeez Basha 1967 on the indicia of "established and administered" under Art 30(1))
- **Constitutional remedies under Article 32:** five prerogative writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto) compared with High Court Article 226
- **Exceptions to Fundamental Rights (Articles 33–35):** Armed Forces/Police restrictions (Art 33), Martial Law indemnity vs. National Emergency (Art 34), and exclusive parliamentary legislative competence (Art 35).

### Part 6: Directive Principles of State Policy, Fundamental Duties, and the Property-Socialism Constitutional Dialectic (Articles 31–31C, 36–51A, 300A)
- **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51):** Irish and Sapru Committee origins, non-justiciable yet fundamental character (Article 37), and functional classification—Socialistic (Articles 38, 39(a)–(f), 39A free legal aid, 41, 42, 43, 43A), Gandhian (Articles 40 village panchayats, 43, 43B cooperatives, 46, 47, 48 cow slaughter jurisprudence in 7-Judge Mirzapur Moti Kureshi 2005), and Liberal-Intellectual (Articles 44 Uniform Civil Code, 45, 48A environment, 49, 50 separation of judiciary from executive, 51 international peace)
- **The Uniform Civil Code (Article 44):** Shah Bano (1985), Sarla Mudgal (1995), Shayara Bano (2017), 21st Law Commission report, and the Uttarakhand Uniform Civil Code Act (2024/2025) implementation
- **The 75-year judicial war between Fundamental Rights, DPSPs, and the Right to Property:** Champakam Dorairajan (1951), First Amendment (1951 inserting Articles 31A, 31B, and the Ninth Schedule), Bella Banerjee and Fourth Amendment (1955), Golaknath (1967), 25th Amendment (1971 inserting Article 31C), Kesavananda Bharati (1973), 42nd Amendment expansion of Art 31C struck down in Minerva Mills (1980 harmonious construction doctrine), and the 44th Amendment (1978) shifting Property from Art 19(1)(f)/31 to Constitutional Right Article 300A (Kolkata Municipal Corporation 2024 seven procedural sub-rights)
- **The landmark 9-Judge Constitution Bench Verdict in Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (November 2024):** upholding unamended Article 31C while overruling Ranganatha Reddy (1977) and Sanjeev Coke (1983) to hold that NOT every privately owned property constitutes a "material resource of the community" under Article 39(b)
- **Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A):** Swaran Singh Committee (42nd Amendment, 1976), 86th Amendment (11th Duty, 2002), Verma Committee (1999), and role in statutory interpretation (AIIMS Students Union).

### Part 7: The Union and State Executives: President, Vice-President, Governors, Prime Minister, and Council of Ministers
- **The President of India (Articles 52–78, 123):** Electoral College and proportional representation vote-value calculus (Articles 54–55, 1971 Census freeze), impeachment for "violation of the Constitution" (Article 61 two-thirds total membership majority), Westminster aid and advice (Articles 53, 74, 75, 78; Ram Jawaya Kapur 1955, Shamsher Singh 1974, 42nd and 44th Amendments), Veto powers (Article 111: absolute, suspensive, pocket veto; 24th Amendment bar on Constitutional Amendment Bills), Ordinance-making power (Article 123 and Governor’s Article 213: R.C. Cooper, D.C. Wadhwa 1987, and 7-Judge Krishna Kumar Singh 2017 against re-promulgation), and Pardoning power (Article 72 vs. Governor’s Article 161: Maru Ram, Kehar Singh, Epuru Sudhakar 2006, Shatrughan Chauhan 2014 mercy-delay commutation, and Bilkis Bano 2024 remission quashing)
- **The Vice-President of India (Articles 63–71):** electoral college and removal via Rajya Sabha effective majority
- **Prime Minister and Council of Ministers:** collective responsibility (Article 75(3)), individual responsibility, 91st Constitutional Amendment Act (2003) (15% ministerial cap and anti-defection bar), and Cabinet Committees
- **The Governor (Articles 153–167, 200–201):** dual constitutional role (Hargovind Pant 1979), appointment and removal jurisprudence (Sarkaria/Punchhi Commissions and 5-Judge B.P. Singhal 2010), discretionary powers (Article 163, 5-Judge Nabam Rebia 2016, Shivraj Singh Chouhan 2020), and the 2023–2026 Article 200 & 201 Legislative Assent Jurisprudence (State of Punjab 2023, State of Tamil Nadu and State of Kerala 2024–2025 rulings, and Presidential Reference dynamics) barring gubernatorial pocket vetoes and mandating assent on re-passed bills.

### Part 8: The Union and State Legislatures I: Bicameralism, Legislative Procedures, Money Bills, and Budgetary Architecture
- **Bicameralism (Articles 79–122, 168–212):** Rajya Sabha federal asymmetric representation (Fourth Schedule, Kuldip Nayar 2006 open ballot and non-domicile ruling, exclusive powers under Articles 249 and 312), Lok Sabha composition, 104th Amendment (2019) (abolishing Anglo-Indian nomination), 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam—Articles 330A, 332A, 334A) reserving 33% seats for women post-delimitation, and State Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishads, Article 169 creation/abolition by simple parliamentary majority and 4-month maximum delaying power under Art 197)
- Sessions, prorogation, dissolution, and lapsing of bills (Article 107)
- Ordinary Bills and Joint Sittings (Article 108: simple majority mechanics, inapplicable to Money and Constitutional Amendment Bills)
- **Money Bills (Articles 109–110) vs. Financial Bills Category I and II (Article 117):** Speaker’s certification under Article 110(3) and judicial review over bicameral bypass (Puttaswamy II 2018 Aadhaar majority vs. 5-Judge Rojer Mathew 2019 reference to a 7-Judge Bench)
- **The Parliamentary Budgetary Process (Articles 112–117):** Annual Financial Statement, post-2017 Railway Budget merger, Charged vs. Voted expenditure, Cut Motions (Policy, Economy, Token), Guillotine, Appropriation Bill (Art 114), Vote on Account, and Vote of Credit
- **The Three Constitutional Funds:** Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266(1)), Public Account of India (Article 266(2)), and Contingency Fund of India (Article 267).

### Part 9: The Union and State Legislatures II: Presiding Officers, Parliamentary Privileges, Committee Oversight, and the Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule)
- **Presiding Officers (Articles 89–97, 178–186):** Speaker of the Lok Sabha/Assembly (tenure survival post-dissolution under Art 94, removal by effective majority under Art 96, casting vote under Art 100, and partisan convention vs. Westminster neutrality), Deputy Speaker vacancy crisis (Art 93), Rajya Sabha Chairman/Deputy Chairman, and independent Parliamentary Secretariat (Art 98)
- **Parliamentary accountability devices:** Question Hour, Zero Hour, No-Confidence Motion (Rule 198), Censure Motion, Adjournment Motion, and Calling Attention
- **Parliamentary Privileges (Articles 105 and 194):** individual and collective privileges, non-codification vs. Fundamental Rights (M.S.M. Sharma Searchlight Case 1959, In Re: Keshav Singh 1965), cash-for-query expulsions (Raja Ram Pal 2007 and 2023 Ethics Committee precedent), destruction of public property on the House floor (State of Kerala v. K. Ajith 2021), and the landmark 7-Judge Constitution Bench Verdict in Sita Soren v. Union of India (March 2024) overruling P.V. Narasimha Rao (JMM Bribery, 1998) to hold that legislators enjoy zero immunity under Articles 105(2)/194(2) for accepting bribes
- **The Parliamentary Committee System:** Financial Committees (Public Accounts Committee - PAC, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings - COPU), 24 Department-Related Standing Committees (DRSCs), and Joint Parliamentary Committees (JPCs)
- **The Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule, 52nd Amendment 1985 and 91st Amendment 2003):** grounds of disqualification (voluntarily giving up membership in Ravi S. Naik, defying party whip, independent/nominated rules), repeal of one-third split and retention of two-thirds merger, Kihoto Hollohan (1992 declaring the Speaker a Tribunal subject to judicial review), Shrimanth Balasaheb Patil (2019), Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020 3-month timeline and independent tribunal recommendation), and the 5-Judge Constitution Bench Verdict in Subhash Desai v. Governor of Maharashtra (May 2023) distinguishing the Political Party Whip from the Legislature Party and referring Nabam Rebia (2016) to a 7-Judge Bench.

### Part 10: The Higher Judiciary: Jurisdiction, Collegium Appointments, Judicial Independence, and Accountability
- Unified judicial architecture (Supreme Court Articles 124–147; High Courts Articles 214–231)
- **Supreme Court jurisdictions:** Original and Exclusive Federal Jurisdiction (Article 131, state suits against central laws/borrowing caps), Extraordinary Writ Jurisdiction (Article 32 vs. wider High Court Article 226 scope), Appellate Jurisdiction (Articles 132–134), Special Leave to Appeal (Article 136 plenary discretion and National Court of Appeal debate), and Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143 Presidential References)
- Court of Record and Contempt of Court (Articles 129 and 215, Contempt of Courts Act 1971)
- Stare Decisis (Article 141), Review Petitions (Article 137), Curative Petitions (Rupa Ashok Hurra 2002), and Complete Justice powers under Article 142 (Vishaka, Supreme Court Bar Association 1998 limits, and 5-Judge Shilpa Sailesh 2023 irretrievable breakdown of marriage)
- **Judicial Appointments and the Collegium System (Articles 124(2) and 217):** evolution across the Four Judges Cases—First Judges Case (S.P. Gupta 1981 executive primacy), Second Judges Case (SCARA 1993 9-Judge Bench establishing Collegium and CJI primacy), Third Judges Case (In Re: Special Reference 1998 9-Judge Bench expanding Collegium to CJI + 4), and Fourth Judges Case (SCARA 2015 5-Judge Bench striking down the 99th Constitutional Amendment and NJAC Act as violating Basic Structure)—alongside Memorandum of Procedure (MoP) friction and executive pocket-veto delays
- **Independence safeguards and accountability:** security of tenure, Consolidated Fund charge, post-retirement practice bar (Art 124(7)) vs. post-retirement political/tribunal appointments, removal procedure under the Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968, Master of the Roster prerogative (Shanti Bhushan 2018), and RTI applicability to the CJI’s office (Subhash Chandra Agarwal 2019 5-Judge Bench).

### Part 11: Constitutional Amendment Mechanics (Article 368), the Basic Structure Doctrine, Judicial Review, and Public Interest Litigation
- **Constitutional Amendment Mechanics (Part XX, Article 368):** three tiers of amendment—(1) Simple Majority outside Article 368 (Articles 2, 3, 4, 169), (2) Special Majority of each House under Article 368(2) (majority of total membership + 2/3 present and voting), and (3) Special Majority PLUS Ratification by half of the State Legislatures under the Proviso to Article 368(2) for entrenched federal provisions (enforced in Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah 2021)
- Procedural rules of Article 368 (initiation only in Parliament, no joint sitting under Art 108, and mandatory Presidential assent after the 24th Amendment 1971)
- **Genealogy of the Basic Structure Doctrine:** Sankari Prasad (1951) and Sajjan Singh (1965) unlimited amending power, I.C. Golaknath (1967 11-Judge Bench prospective overruling), 24th and 25th Amendments, and the watershed 13-Judge Constitution Bench in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (April 24, 1973, 7:6) distinguishing "amendment" from "destruction" of foundational identity
- **Consolidation of Basic Structure:** Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975 39th Amendment strike-down), Minerva Mills (1980 striking down 42nd Amendment Clauses 4 and 5 of Art 368), Waman Rao (1981 fixing April 24, 1973 cut-off), S.R. Bommai (1994 secularism/federalism), L. Chandra Kumar (1997 judicial review), M. Nagaraj (2006 width and identity tests), and the 9-Judge Bench in I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007) subjecting all post-1973 Ninth Schedule (Article 31B) entries to the Direct Impact and Effect test
- **Judicial Review, Judicial Activism vs. Overreach, and Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** relaxation of locus standi (Hussainara Khatoon, S.P. Gupta 1981, Bhagwati and Krishna Iyer’s epistolary jurisdiction), Continuing Mandamus (Vineet Narain, Godavarman), and separation-of-powers guidelines against frivolous PILs (Balwant Singh Chaufal 2010).

### Part 12: The Subordinate Judiciary, Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR), and the Tribunalization Regime (Part XIV-A)
- **Subordinate Judiciary (Part VI, Chapter VI, Articles 233–237):** appointment of District Judges (Art 233, Dheeraj Mor 2020) and High Court administrative control over district courts (Art 235)
- The All-India Judicial Service (AIJS, Article 312(3) added by 42nd Amendment) federal debate
- Post-July 2024 criminal and civil trial hierarchy under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023 (mandatory forensic investigation, audio-video search recording, Zero FIR, e-FIR, statutory trial timelines)
- **Systemic pathologies:** >5 crore case pendency tracked on the National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG), judge-to-population deficit, Gram Nyayalayas Act (2008), e-Courts Phase III, and the proposed National Judicial Infrastructure Authority (NJIAI)
- **Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) ecosystem:** Section 89 CPC, Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 (NALSA and statutory Lok Adalats with non-appealable decrees), the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 (2015–2021 Amendments, 7-Judge In Re: Interplay between Arbitration Agreements and Indian Stamp Act Dec 2023, 5-Judge Cox and Kings 2023 Group of Companies doctrine, and 5-Judge CORE II Nov 2024 striking down unilateral PSU arbitrator appointments), Commercial Courts Act 2015, and the Mediation Act, 2023
- **Administrative Adjudication and the Tribunalization Regime (Part XIV-A, 42nd Amendment 1976):** Article 323A (Administrative Tribunals / CAT) vs. Article 323B (specialized tribunals: NGT, NCLT/NCLAT, Armed Forces Tribunal, ITAT, SAT)
- **Separation-of-powers jurisprudence:** the landmark 7-Judge Bench in L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997) restoring High Court Division Bench writ scrutiny under Articles 226/227, the R. Gandhi (2010) and Madras Bar Association I–IV (2014–2021) rulings against executive control of appointments/tenures, and the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 deadlock.

### Part 13: The Federal Architecture I: Legislative, Administrative, and Inter-State Water and Trade Dynamics (Parts XI and XIII)

### Part 14: The Federal Architecture II: Fiscal Federalism, Mineral and GST Jurisprudence, the Finance Commission, and Borrowing Constraints
- **Constitutional distribution of tax powers (Part XII, Articles 264–293):** Article 265 (no tax without authority of law; Tax vs. Fee vs. Cess vs. Surcharge), strict separation of Union and State tax entries, and the five-category revenue distribution matrix (Articles 268, 269, 269A IGST, 270 Divisible Pool post-80th Amendment, and 271 non-shareable Union Cesses and Surcharges)
- The landmark 9-Judge Constitution Bench Verdict in Mineral Area Development Authority (MADA) v. Steel Authority of India (July–August 2024) overruling India Cement (1989) to hold that mining royalty is not a tax and that States possess plenary constitutional power under Entries 49 and 50 of List II to tax mineral rights and mineral-bearing lands
- **The Goods and Services Tax (GST) architecture (101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016):** Article 246A simultaneous legislative power, The GST Council (Article 279A) weighted voting mechanics (1/3 Union weight, 2/3 States weight, 75% majority threshold), Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022) ruling that GST Council recommendations are non-binding cooperative persuasion, Compensation Cess sunset, and exclusion of petroleum and potable alcohol
- **The Finance Commission of India (Article 280):** 15th Finance Commission (N.K. Singh: 41% vertical devolution) and the 16th Finance Commission (chaired by Dr. Arvind Panagariya for the 2026–2031 cycle)
- Horizontal devolution criteria friction (Income Distance, 2011 Population, Demographic Performance, Forest/Ecology) and the North-South fiscal disparity debate
- Erosion of the divisible pool via Union cesses/surcharges and Article 282 Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) vs. Article 275 statutory grants
- **Public Borrowing and Article 293(3) Federal Litigation:** central consent for indebted states, inclusion of off-budget State PSE borrowings in Net Borrowing Ceilings, and the 2024 State of Kerala v. Union of India reference to a 5-Judge Constitution Bench.

### Part 15: Emergency Provisions, Union Territories (Part VIII), and Asymmetrical Federalism (Fifth and Sixth Schedules, Articles 370–371J)
- **Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII, Articles 352–360):** National Emergency (Article 352: War, External Aggression, or Armed Rebellion; 44th Amendment 1978 safeguards requiring written Union Cabinet advice, Special Majority within 1 month, 6-month renewals, and Lok Sabha simple-majority revocation; impact on Fundamental Rights under Article 358 automatic suspension of Art 19 during External Emergency vs. Article 359 presidential suspension excluding Articles 20 and 21)
- President’s Rule (Articles 356 and 365: 2-month approval, 3-year cap, and the 9-Judge Bench in S.R. Bommai 1994 and Rameshwar Prasad 2006 mandating floor tests, judicial review, and power to restore dissolved assemblies in Harish Rawat and Nabam Rebia 2016)
- Financial Emergency (Article 360)
- **Union Territories and the NCT of Delhi Constitutional Battle (Part VIII, Articles 239–241):** Article 239AA (69th Amendment 1991 excluding Public Order, Police, and Land), the 5-Judge NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2018 and May 2023 Services verdicts), and the GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023 creating the National Capital Civil Service Authority (NCCSA)
- **Asymmetrical Federalism:** the landmark 5-Judge Constitution Bench Verdict in In Re: Article 370 of the Constitution (December 2023) upholding the August 2019 Presidential Orders and the Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act 2019 (affirming that J&K retained zero internal sovereignty, Art 370 was temporary, and directing Assembly elections held in 2024 alongside statehood restoration)

### Part 16: Grassroots Democratic Decentralization: PRIs (73rd Amendment), ULBs (74th Amendment), PESA, and Co-operative Societies (97th Amendment)
- **Historical evolution of local self-government:** Article 40, Community Development Programme (1952), and the Balwant Rai Mehta (1957), Ashok Mehta (1977), G.V.K. Rao (1985), and L.M. Singhvi (1986) Committees
- **The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Part IX, Articles 243 to 243-O and Eleventh Schedule—29 subjects):** compulsory vs. voluntary provisions, Gram Sabha (Art 243A), uniform three-tier Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs), mandatory SC/ST and minimum 1/3rd (50% in 21+ states) Women reservations (Art 243D), OBC reservation "Triple Test" (Vikas Kishanrao Gawali 2021), 5-year fixed tenure (Art 243E), minimum age 21, two-child/educational disqualification validity (Javed 2003, Rajbala 2015), State Finance Commission (Art 243I), and independent State Election Commission (Art 243K, Kishan Singh Tomar 2006)
- **The Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act (PESA), 1996:** Bhuria Committee origins, radical Gram Sabha empowerment over Minor Forest Produce, minor minerals, village markets, and mandatory consultation before land acquisition (Niyamgiri Vedanta Case 2013)
- **The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Part IXA, Articles 243P to 243ZG and Twelfth Schedule—18 subjects):** Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Councils, Municipal Corporations, Ward Committees, District Planning Committees (Article 243ZD), and Metropolitan Planning Committees (Article 243ZE)
- Structural crisis of the "Three Fs" (Functions, Finances, Functionaries), Sarpanch Pati proxy syndrome, and parastatal bypass
- **Co-operative Societies (97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011):** Articles 19(1)(c), 43B, and Part IXB (Articles 243ZH–243ZT), and the 2021 Supreme Court Verdict in Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah striking down Part IXB for state cooperatives for lacking Article 368(2) state ratification while upholding it for Multi-State Co-operative Societies.

### Part 17: Constitutional Public Services, Public Service Commissions, National Commissions for SCs/STs/BCs, and Official Language Jurisprudence
- **Public Services under the Constitution (Part XIV, Articles 308–314):** legislative regulation of service conditions (Art 309), Doctrine of Pleasure (Article 310), and Constitutional Safeguards for Civil Servants under Article 311—Art 311(1) no dismissal by a subordinate authority, Art 311(2) mandatory inquiry and reasonable opportunity of being heard (42nd Amendment abolition of second representation on penalty), and the three summary dismissal exceptions under the Second Proviso to Art 311(2) (Union of India v. Tulsiram Patel 1985)
- All-India Services (Article 312: IAS, IPS, IFoS; creation via 2/3 Rajya Sabha resolution and dual-control mechanics)
- **Public Service Commissions (Articles 315–323):** UPSC, SPSCs, and JSPSCs—appointment, removal of SPSC members exclusively by the President after a Supreme Court Reference (Article 317), Consolidated Fund charge (Art 322), post-retirement employment bars (Art 319), and advisory functions (Art 320)
- **Constitutional Commissions for Marginalized Sections (Part XVI):** NCSC (Article 338), NCST (Article 338A—89th Amendment 2003), and NCBC (Article 338B—102nd Amendment 2018)
- civil court investigation powers, the Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil (Maratha Quota, 2021) ruling, and the 105th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021 amending Article 342A to restore State Governments’ power to maintain their own State OBC/SEBC Lists
- **Official Language Jurisprudence (Part XVII, Articles 343–351 and Eighth Schedule—22 languages):** Union official language (Art 343, Official Languages Act 1963/1967), mandatory English in Supreme Court/High Court proceedings and authoritative statutory texts (Article 348), linguistic minority safeguards (Articles 350A and 350B), and the October 2024 Expansion of Classical Languages of India to 11 (adding Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, and Bengali).

### Part 18: Electoral Jurisprudence: The Election Commission, Representation of the People Acts, Campaign Finance, Delimitation, and Simultaneous Elections
- **Constitutional architecture of elections (Part XV, Articles 324–329):** Article 324 plenary superintendence vested in the Election Commission of India (Mohinder Singh Gill 1978), universal adult suffrage (Article 326, 61st Amendment voting age 18), and bar on judicial interference during elections (Art 329(b))
- **Appointment and security of tenure of the ECI:** T.N. Seshan (1995) majority parity, removal protection limited to the CEC while ECs are removable on CEC recommendation (Art 324(5)), the 5-Judge Constitution Bench in Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (March 2023) vs. Parliament’s enactment of the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023 (replacing the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister on the selection committee)
- **The Representation of the People Acts (RPA, 1950 and RPA, 1951):** voter registration, political party registration (Section 29A and lack of de-registration power), party symbol split adjudication under Para 15 of the Symbols Order 1968 (Sadiq Ali), and Corrupt Practices (Section 123(3) religious/caste appeals in 7-Judge Abhiram Singh 2017)
- **Disqualification of Legislators (Articles 102/191 and RPA Sections 8–11A):** Office of Profit (Jaya Bachchan), criminal conviction for 2+ years (Lily Thomas v. Union of India, 2013 striking down Section 8(4) automatic appeal protection), voter’s right to candidate affidavits (ADR 2002, PUCL 2013 NOTA, Resurgence India 2013), and the April 2024 Supreme Court EVM-VVPAT Judgment
- Campaign Finance and the landmark 5-Judge Constitution Bench Verdict in Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (February 2024) striking down the Electoral Bonds Scheme as violating Article 19(1)(a) and Article 14
- **Delimitation and Simultaneous Elections:** 84th Amendment (2001) freeze on inter-state Lok Sabha seat allocation until the first Census after 2026 (and the impending North-South demographic representation crisis), and the Ram Nath Kovind Committee Report and 129th Constitutional Amendment Bill, 2024 (One Nation, One Election—Article 82A "Appointed Date" and unexpired-term synchronization).

### Part 19: Fiscal and Legal Constitutional Watchdogs, Statutory Rights Commissions, and Comparative Constitutionalism
- **The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG, Articles 148–151 and the CAG DPC Act, 1971):** constitutional independence safeguards (6-year/65-year tenure, removal equivalent to an SC Judge, post-retirement office bar, Consolidated Fund charge), structural distinction from the UK Comptroller (purely ex-post auditor lacking exchequer control), regulatory, propriety, and performance audits, Supreme Court affirmation of CAG power to audit private PPP/Telecom revenue-sharing firms (Association of Unified TeleServices Providers 2014), and symbiosis with the Public Accounts Committee
- **Constitutional Law Officers:** Attorney General for India (Article 76) and Advocate General for the State (Article 165) (Doctrine of Pleasure tenure, right of audience, and right to speak/participate in Parliament and Committees without voting under Article 88)
- **Specialized Statutory Rights and Anti-Corruption Watchdogs:** National Human Rights Commission (NHRC—Protection of Human Rights Act 1993, 2006 and 2019 Amendments, Paris Principles, and 1-year limitation/Armed Forces jurisdiction constraints), National Commission for Women (NCW Act 1990), National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR Act 2005), National Commission for Minorities (NCM Act 1992), Central Information Commission (RTI Act 2005 and 2019/2023 Amendments), Central Vigilance Commission (CVC Act 2003), and Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act (2013)
- **Comparative Constitutionalism:** rigorous structural contrast of the Indian Constitution with the United Kingdom (unwritten constitution, absolute parliamentary sovereignty, unitary devolution, shadow cabinet, non-partisan Speaker), the United States (strict presidential separation of powers, "indestructible union of indestructible states," equal Senate representation, state residuary powers, rigid Article V amendment, Due Process), France (Fifth Republic semi-presidential dual executive, Conseil Constitutionnel ex-ante review, negative Laïcité secularism vs. Indian Principled Distance), Germany (Basic Law Ewigkeitsklausel Eternity Clause Art 79(3) inspiring Basic Structure, Constructive Vote of No-Confidence Art 67), Canada (strong center with residuary powers), Australia (Concurrent List, Section 92 trade freedom, Joint Sitting), Japan (Art 31 procedure established by law), and South Africa (post-apartheid transformative constitutionalism and justiciable socio-economic rights).
