### Part 2: Plate Tectonics, Paleomagnetism, Diastrophism, and Crustal Deformation Mechanics
- **Historical lineage:** Alfred Wegener’s Continental Drift hypothesis (1912: Pangaea, jigsaw fit, Glossopteris and Mesosaurus fossils, Talchir tillite glaciation, gold placer continuity, and polar-fleeing/tidal force flaws)
- Arthur Holmes’s Mantle Convection Current theory (1929) and Kober’s Geosynclinal Orogenic theory (Kratogen and Orogen)
- **Seafloor Spreading theory (Harry Hess and Robert Dietz, 1960–62):** mid-oceanic ridge magma upwelling, symmetric Vine-Matthews-Morley geomagnetic marine anomaly striping, radiometric youth of oceanic crust, and trench subduction
- **Modern Plate Tectonic theory (Tuzo Wilson, Dan McKenzie, Jason Morgan, 1967):** 7 major and 20+ minor lithospheric plates
- **Lithospheric driving forces:** slab-pull, ridge-push, basal mantle drag, and deep mantle plumes/hotspots (Hawaii, Réunion)
- **Plate boundary kinematics:** Divergent margins (Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift system), Convergent margins (Ocean-Ocean Mariana island arcs, Ocean-Continent Andean subduction, Continent-Continent Himalayan orogenesis), and Transform faults (San Andreas, Dead Sea faults)
- The Wilson Cycle of supercontinental assembly and breakup
- **Diastrophism and crustal deformation:** Epeirogenic vs. Orogenic forces, structural taxonomy of Folds (anticline, syncline, symmetrical, asymmetrical, monoclinal, isoclinal, recumbent, overturned, nappe) and Faults (normal, reverse, thrust, strike-slip, step faults, Horst block mountains, and Graben rift valleys).

- **Spatial shifts of core industries:** Iron and Steel (shift from coalfield Ruhr/Pittsburgh/Damodar and iron-ore Ural-Kuznetsk/Bhilai locations to coastal port-based Tokyo-Osaka/POSCO/Visakhapatnam/Paradip locations), Cotton Textiles (footloose shift from Mumbai/Ahmedabad/Lancashire to Coimbatore/Tiruppur and interior markets), Jute concentration along the Hooghly river (and 1947 Partition raw-jute split), Sugar industry’s structural shift from subtropical UP/Bihar to tropical peninsular Maharashtra/Karnataka (higher sucrose, longer crushing season, cooperative mills), Aluminum smelting near cheap power (Korba, Renukoot, Angul), Petrochemicals/Fertilizers along gas pipelines (HBJ, Urja Ganga), and high-tech/semiconductor clusters (Silicon Valley, Hsinchu, Shenzhen, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Dholera/Sanand/Morigaon fabs)
- Global and Indian Industrial Regions
- **Transport geography:** Kansky’s network indices, Transcontinental Railways (Trans-Siberian, Canadian Pacific, Union-Pacific, Trans-Andean), Ocean Shipping Lanes and Canal Chokepoints (North Atlantic, Suez Canal, Panama Canal Gatun lock drought, Cape of Good Hope, Malacca, Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb), Dedicated Freight Corridors, National Waterways, and Mega-Ports.

### Part 19: Spatial Development Theories, Regional Planning Models, and Subcontinental Regional Disparities
- **Conceptual foundations of regional geography:** Formal/Uniform, Functional/Nodal (city-region umland), and Planning/Programming regions (Damodar Valley Corporation modeled on the US TVA)
- **Theoretical models of spatial economic divergence:** François Perroux’s Growth Pole Theory (1955: propulsive industries and input-output linkages in abstract economic space), Jacques Boudeville’s geographical Growth Centres, and R.P. Misra’s five-tier Indian Growth Foci hierarchy (Central Villages, Service Centres, Growth Points, Growth Centres, Growth Poles)
- Gunnar Myrdal’s Theory of Circular and Cumulative Causation (1957: centripetal Backwash Effects draining capital/skill from the periphery vs. centrifugal Spread Effects)
- Albert Hirschman’s Unbalanced Growth Model (Polarization vs. Trickle-Down effects, SOC vs. DPA)
- John Friedmann’s four-stage Core-Periphery Model (Core, Upward-Transitional, Resource Frontier, and Downward-Transitional regions)
- and Rostow’s vs. Wallerstein’s spatial models
- **Structural anatomy of regional disparities in India:** colonial port-enclave bias, the 1952–1993 Freight Equalization Policy (eroding the Weberian mineral advantage of Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh), Green Revolution skewness, and post-1991 coastal/southern agglomeration
- **Target-area and target-group regional planning programmes:** Hill Area (HADP), Tribal Sub-Plan (ITDPs), Drought Prone/Desert (DPAP/DDP), Command Area (CADWM Warabandi), Western Ghats, Border Areas/Vibrant Villages, DoNER/PM-DevINE/UNNATI, Budget 2024–26 Purvodaya Eastern India plan, Island Development Agency, Aspirational Districts/Blocks, and Constitutional District/Metropolitan Planning Committees (Articles 243ZD and 243ZE).
