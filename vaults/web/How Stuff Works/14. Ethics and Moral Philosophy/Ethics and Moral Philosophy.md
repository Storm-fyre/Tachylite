### Part 1: Foundational Moral Philosophy, Normative Epistemology, and the Anatomy of Human Values
- **Philosophical essence of ethics:** moral realism vs. anti-realism, cognitivism vs. non-cognitivism, and ethical objectivism/universalism vs. cultural moral relativism
- **The four branches of moral inquiry:** Meta-ethics (David Hume’s Is-Ought problem and G.E. Moore’s Naturalistic Fallacy), Normative ethics, Descriptive ethics, and Applied ethics
- **Determinants and consequences of morality in human actions:** Human Action (Actus Humanus) vs. Acts of Man (Actus Hominis), conditions of moral agency (knowledge, voluntariness, free will), impediments to culpability (ignorance, passion, fear, habit, coercion), and the four determinants of morality (object/nature of the act, intention/motive of the agent, situational circumstances, and teleological consequences)
- Distinctions between Ethics, Morality, Law, Religion, and Social Etiquette
- **Ethics in private vs. public relationships:** moral role differentiation, Michael Walzer and Machiavelli’s problem of "Dirty Hands" in statecraft, and private conscience vs. public constitutional duty
- **Human Values:** intrinsic vs. instrumental values
- **Psychology of moral development:** Lawrence Kohlberg’s six stages of moral development (pre-conventional, conventional, post-conventional) vs. Carol Gilligan’s feminist Ethics of Care critique
- Structural agencies of value socialization (family, school hidden curricula, peer groups, media, religion).

### Part 2: Western Normative Ethical Traditions: Virtue Ethics, Deontology, Consequentialism, and Justice
- **Classical Greek Virtue Ethics:** Socrates (Socratic intellectualism: "virtue is knowledge" and "the unexamined life is not worth living"), Plato (The Republic, the Ring of Gyges, and the Four Cardinal Virtues: Wisdom/Prudence, Courage/Fortitude, Temperance, and Justice), and Aristotle’s Nicomachean Ethics (Eudaimonia human flourishing, teleology, the Golden Mean between deficiency and excess, Phronesis practical wisdom, and character habituation)
- Hellenistic Stoicism (Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus, Seneca: dichotomy of control and Apatheia) and St. Thomas Aquinas (Natural Law and Doctrine of Double Effect)
- **Deontological Moral Philosophy:** Immanuel Kant’s Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals—Good Will, Duty for Duty’s Sake, autonomy vs. heteronomy, Hypothetical vs. Categorical Imperatives (Formula of Universal Law, Formula of Humanity as an End in Itself, and Kingdom of Ends), and W.D. Ross’s pluralistic Prima Facie duties
- **Consequentialism and Utilitarianism:** Ethical Egoism (Ayn Rand) vs. Jeremy Bentham’s Quantitative Utilitarianism (felicific calculus) and John Stuart Mill’s Qualitative Utilitarianism (Harm Principle, higher intellectual vs. lower bodily pleasures, and Act vs. Rule Utilitarianism)
- **Social Contract and Justice Theories:** Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, John Rawls’s A Theory of Justice (Original Position, Veil of Ignorance, Liberty Principle, Fair Equality of Opportunity, and the Difference Principle), Robert Nozick’s libertarian entitlement critique, Amartya Sen (Niti vs. Nyaya) and Martha Nussbaum’s Capability Approach, Communitarianism (MacIntyre, Sandel), and Sartre’s Existentialism.

### Part 3: Indian and Non-Western Ethical Traditions: Dharma, Nishkama Karma, and Emancipatory Morality
- **Foundational Vedic and Upanishadic moral order:** Rta (cosmic/moral order), Satya, Dharma (contextual duty and righteousness), Law of Karma, Rina (moral debts), Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam, and the four Purusharthas (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha)
- **Heterodox ethical revolutions:** Buddhist ethics (Majjhima Patipada Middle Path, Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path, Karuna compassion, Mudita, and Ahimsa), Jain ethics (Pancha Mahavratas, Ahimsa Paramo Dharma, Anekantavada intellectual humility, and Aparigraha non-possession), and Charvaka materialism
- **Ethics of the Bhagavad Gita:** Nishkama Karma (detached performance of duty without craving for fruits), Svadharma, Lokasamgraha (welfare of the world), and the Sthitaprajna (equanimous moral agent)
- Realist statecraft in Kautilya’s Arthashastra (Rajadharma: "In the happiness of his subjects lies the king’s happiness") vs. the universal secular ethics of Tiruvalluvar’s Tirukkural (Aram, Porul, Inbam)
- **Medieval Bhakti-Sufi egalitarian ethics:** Kabir, Guru Nanak (Kirat Karo, Naam Japo, Vand Chhako), and Basavanna (Kayaka—work as worship and Dasoha)
- **Modern Indian moral thinkers and reformers:** Raja Rammohan Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, Jyotirao and Savitribai Phule, Swami Vivekananda (Practical Vedanta and Daridra Narayana), Rabindranath Tagore, Sri Aurobindo, Mahatma Gandhi (unity of means and ends, Satyagraha, Sarvodaya, Antyodaya, Trusteeship, Gandhi’s Talisman, and Seven Social Sins), B.R. Ambedkar (Constitutional Morality and the 2026 9-Judge Supreme Court debate), S. Radhakrishnan, and A.P.J. Abdul Kalam
- **Non-Western traditions:** Confucianism (Ren, Li, Junzi) and African Ubuntu.

### Part 4: Attitude, Behavioral Psychology, Cognitive Biases, Moral Disengagement, and Administrative Evil
- **Psychological anatomy of Attitude:** the CAB/ABC Model (Cognitive belief, Affective emotion, Behavioral predisposition), Ambivalence, and Daniel Katz’s four functional properties of attitudes (utilitarian, knowledge, ego-defensive, value-expressive)
- **Attitude-behavior consistency models:** Icek Ajzen and Martin Fishbein’s Theory of Reasoned Action and Theory of Planned Behavior (perceived behavioral control)
- **Attitude change and persuasion mechanics:** Leon Festinger’s Cognitive Dissonance Theory, Elaboration Likelihood Model (central vs. peripheral routes), and Robert Cialdini’s six weapons of social influence (reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity)
- Prejudice, stereotyping, implicit bias, and Gordon Allport’s Intergroup Contact Hypothesis
- **Behavioral economics in public policy:** Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky’s System 1 (fast/intuitive) vs. System 2 (slow/deliberative) thinking, heuristic biases, and Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein’s Nudge Theory (libertarian paternalism and choice architecture in Swachh Bharat and tax compliance)
- **Social psychology of Administrative Evil and Moral Failure:** Hannah Arendt’s "Banality of Evil" (Adolf Eichmann’s bureaucratic thoughtlessness), Stanley Milgram’s Obedience to Authority experiments (agentic shift), Philip Zimbardo’s Stanford Prison Experiment (Lucifer Effect and deindividuation), Albert Bandura’s eight mechanisms of Moral Disengagement, Darley and Latané’s Bystander Effect (diffusion of responsibility), and Irving Janis’s Groupthink.

### Part 5: Emotional Intelligence (EI) in Public Governance, Neurobiology, and Conflict Resolution
- **Conceptual evolution of Emotional Intelligence:** Howard Gardner’s intrapersonal/interpersonal intelligences, Peter Salovey and John Mayer’s four-branch ability model (perceiving, using, understanding, and managing emotions), Reuven Bar-On’s EQ-i model, and Daniel Goleman’s five-component competency model (Self-Awareness, Self-Regulation, Internal Motivation, Empathy, and Social Skills)
- Distinction between IQ (cognitive intelligence), EQ (emotional intelligence), SQ (social intelligence), and Spiritual Quotient
- **Neurobiological foundations of emotion:** Joseph LeDoux’s Amygdala Hijack (thalamo-amygdala fast survival pathway bypassing the rational Prefrontal Cortex), mirror neurons in cognitive/affective empathy, and chronic cortisol burnout
- **Indian philosophical models of emotional self-regulation:** the Bhagavad Gita’s Sthitaprajna (mastery over Kama and Krodha), Patanjali’s Chitta-Vritti-Nirodha, and Buddhist Vipassana mindfulness and Upekkha (equanimity)
- Distinction between Empathy, Sympathy, and Compassion (and preventing Compassion Fatigue / Vicarious Trauma in frontline civil servants)
- **Applications of Emotional Intelligence in public administration:** de-escalating violent mob protests and communal tensions without lethal force, empathetic communication during disaster evacuations and land acquisition, managing political pressure from elected representatives, ethical leadership, and the Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument (competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating).

### Part 6: Aptitude and Foundational Values for Civil Service, Administrative Discretion, and Codes of Ethics
- Structural distinction between Aptitude (innate or acquired potential to master specific cognitive/operational skills, e.g., quantitative, verbal, spatial, and administrative aptitude) and Attitude (evaluative psychological disposition determining how aptitude is deployed)
- **Core foundational values of civil services:** Integrity (intellectual and moral consistency when no one is watching), Impartiality, Non-Partisanship, Objectivity (evidence-based decision-making vs. neutrality), Dedication to Public Service, Empathy, Tolerance, and Compassion toward weaker sections (Antyodaya)
- **Evolution of bureaucratic ethos:** Max Weber’s Sine Ira et Studio (without anger or bias), Paul Appleby’s democratic morality, and the tension between Political Neutrality (serving any elected government loyally), Constitutional Anonymity, and the pathology of "Committed Bureaucracy"
- The UK Lord Nolan Committee’s Seven Principles of Public Life (1995: Selflessness, Integrity, Objectivity, Accountability, Openness, Honesty, Leadership)
- **Ethical dilemmas of administrative discretion:** balancing rigid Weberian rule-bound compliance (Red Tape) with equity and substantive justice
- Code of Ethics (aspirational values recommended by the Hota Committee 2004, 2nd ARC 4th Report Ethics in Governance 2007, and Draft Public Services Bill) vs. Code of Conduct (enforceable punitive rules under the CCS Conduct Rules 1964 and AIS Conduct Rules 1968)
- Conflicts of Interest (actual, potential, perceived; cooling-off periods and recusal).

### Part 7: Probity in Governance, Social Contract, RTI, Citizen’s Charters, Utilization of Public Funds, and Anti-Corruption Architecture
- **Philosophical basis of governance and probity:** Social Contract theory, Public Office as a Public Trust, Aristotle’s civic virtue, and Kautilyan fiscal accountability
- Conceptual anatomy of Probity (procedural uprightness transcending mere absence of corruption)
- **Taxonomy and economics of corruption:** Robert Klitgaard’s equation (Corruption = Monopoly + Discretion - Accountability), Principal-Agent-Client asymmetric information model, and the distinction between Coercive (Extortionary) Corruption (harassing citizens for statutory entitlements like rations or FIRs) and Collusive Corruption (mutual quid pro quo between bribe-giver and official in spectrum/mining/procurement)
- **Information sharing and transparency as philosophical antidotes to bureaucratic secrecy:** Right to Information (RTI Act 2005 Section 4 proactive disclosure and Section 8 exemptions vs. colonial Official Secrets Act 1923 mentality and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 privacy-transparency balance)
- **Statutory anti-corruption and whistleblower architecture:** Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 (and 2018 Amendment: Section 17A prior sanction shield for bona fide policy decisions and criminalization of supply-side bribe-giving), Whistle Blowers Protection Act (2014), Lokpal, and CVC
- Citizen’s Charters (1991 UK John Major initiative, 6 principles, Indian DARPG failures, and the Sevottam Model integrating Service Delivery Standards, Public Grievance Redressal CPGRAMS, and Citizen Empowerment)
- **Ethics of Utilization of Public Funds:** fiduciary trusteeship of taxpayer money, the Canons of Financial Propriety, parliamentary and CAG propriety audit ethics, pathologies of misutilization (under-utilization, March Rush fiscal dumping, leakages, and ghost assets), and the normative debate over Irrational Electoral Freebies / Competitive Populism vs. Capability-Enhancing Welfare Investments
- **Work Culture and Quality of Service Delivery:** Chalta Hai feudal apathy vs. Kaizen, Karma Yoga, psychological safety, and Mission Karmayogi behavioral transformation.

### Part 8: Corporate Governance, Stakeholder Capitalism, CSR, Greenwashing, and Global Probity Frameworks
- **Philosophical paradigms of corporate purpose:** Milton Friedman’s Shareholder Primacy Doctrine (1970: the sole social responsibility of business is to increase profits within the rules of the game) vs. R. Edward Freeman’s Stakeholder Theory (fiduciary and moral duties toward employees, suppliers, customers, local communities, and the environment), John Elkington’s Triple Bottom Line (People, Planet, Profit), Michael Porter’s Creating Shared Value (CSV), and Mahatma Gandhi’s Doctrine of Trusteeship
- **Anatomy of corporate governance failures and white-collar crime:** accounting fraud (Enron, Satyam Computer Services), conflict of interest and insider trading (Rajat Gupta, Chanda Kochhar), related-party siphoning, crony capitalism, and startup valuation/governance collapses (Byju’s, BharatPe)
- **Evolution of Indian corporate governance frameworks:** Kumar Mangalam Birla (1999), Naresh Chandra (2002), Narayana Murthy (2003), and Uday Kotak Committee (2017) recommendations codified in SEBI (LODR) Regulations (separation of Chairperson and MD/CEO, Independent Directors, Audit/Whistleblower committees, and NFRA auditor oversight)
- Corporate Social Responsibility (Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013: mandatory 2% net-profit spend—ethical critique of compliance tokenism vs. genuine community empowerment)
- ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) investing, Greenwashing deception, and SEBI’s Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR Core) value-chain assurance
- **International probity regimes:** UN Convention against Corruption (UNCAC, 2003) and OECD Anti-Bribery Convention.

### Part 9: Ethics in International Relations, Just War Theory, Refugee Ethics, International Funding, and Global Distributive Justice
- **Competing normative paradigms in international relations:** Political Realism (Thucydides, Machiavelli, Morgenthau: international anarchy, moral skepticism, and state survival as supreme moral duty) vs. Liberal Internationalism (Kantian cosmopolitan law and human rights) vs. Moral Cosmopolitanism (Peter Singer, Martha Nussbaum, Charles Beitz, and Thomas Pogge)
- **Just War Theory (Bellum Justum—St. Augustine, Aquinas, Hugo Grotius, Michael Walzer):** Jus ad bellum (justice of resorting to war: legitimate authority, just cause, right intention, last resort, reasonable probability of success, and macro-proportionality), Jus in bello (justice in the conduct of war: Principle of Distinction / non-combatant immunity, military necessity, micro-proportionality, and prohibition of malum in se weapons), and Jus post bellum (justice of post-war reconstruction, reparations, and war-crimes tribunals), alongside Michael Walzer’s "Supreme Emergency" exception
- Ethics of Humanitarian Intervention and the Responsibility to Protect (R2P) vs. neo-imperial regime change
- **Ethics of Borders, Forced Displacement, and Refugees:** Non-Refoulement as a peremptory moral norm, Garrett Hardin’s "Lifeboat Ethics" vs. hospitality duties
- **Ethical Issues in International Funding and Foreign Aid:** donor-driven conditionalities (IMF/World Bank structural adjustment austerity), "tied aid" commercial procurement, neo-colonial debt-trap diplomacy, politicization of humanitarian aid, climate finance grants vs. loans, and FCRA regulation of transnational NGO funding
- **Global Distributive and Climate Justice:** John Rawls’s The Law of Peoples vs. Thomas Pogge’s Global Resources Dividend
- Historical carbon debt, climate refugees, "vaccine apartheid," unilateral economic sanctions, and cyber/drone warfare on civilian infrastructure.

### Part 10: Applied and Frontier Ethics: Bioethics, Environmental Philosophy, Media/Sports Ethics, and AI Alignment
- **Biomedical and Clinical Ethics:** Tom Beauchamp and James Childress’s Four Principles of Biomedical Ethics (Autonomy / informed consent, Beneficence, Non-Maleficence / Primum non nocere, and Distributive Justice)
- **Ethical controversies at the beginning and end of life:** reproductive autonomy vs. fetal personhood (MTP Act 2021), commercial vs. altruistic surrogacy (Surrogacy Regulation Act 2021), organ transplantation allocation and cadaver donation ethics, clinical trials on vulnerable populations (Declaration of Helsinki), Active vs. Passive Euthanasia (Common Cause Living Wills and the Doctrine of Double Effect in palliative care), and Human Germline Gene Editing (CRISPR "designer babies," somatic vs. heritable enhancement, and new eugenics)
- **Environmental and Ecological Philosophy:** Anthropocentrism (shallow ecology / instrumental value of nature) vs. Biocentrism (Paul Taylor, Albert Schweitzer’s Reverence for Life, and Peter Singer’s Animal Liberation critique of Speciesism) vs. Ecocentrism (Aldo Leopold’s Land Ethic and Arne Næss’s Deep Ecology intrinsic value of whole ecosystems), Ecofeminism (Vandana Shiva), and Intergenerational Stewardship
- **Media, Journalism, and Sports Ethics:** Fourth Estate yellow journalism, paid news, trial by media, algorithmic outrage monetization, and doping/match-fixing
- **Ethics of Frontier Technologies:** AI alignment, algorithmic bias, black-box opacity, Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) moral responsibility gap, Shoshana Zuboff’s Surveillance Capitalism, Neurorights, and the 2026 Supreme Court Draft AI Guidelines for the Judiciary ("human-in-the-loop" mandate).

### Part 11: Ethical Dilemmas in Public Administration, Case-Study Frameworks, and Moral Decision-Making Models
- **Structural anatomy of an Ethical Dilemma:** a clash not between right and wrong, but between two or more competing moral imperatives where satisfying one necessarily violates another
- **Rushworth Kidder’s Four "Right vs. Right" Dilemma Paradigms:** Truth vs. Loyalty (whistleblowing on departmental corruption vs. organizational/peer fidelity), Individual vs. Community (protecting an individual’s rights/land vs. majoritarian public infrastructure/epidemic quarantine), Short-Term vs. Long-Term (populist fiscal relief vs. long-term fiscal/ecological sustainability), and Justice vs. Mercy (strict uniform application of law vs. compassionate relaxation for an indigent/juvenile offender)
- **Core Public Administration Conflict Matrices:** Law/Rules vs. Personal Conscience (Antigone dilemma), Administrative Efficiency vs. Due Process Equity, Development vs. Tribal/Ecological Displacement, Transparency vs. National Security/Privacy, and Rule of Law vs. Extrajudicial "Encounter Justice"
- **Systematic Administrative Decision-Making Models:** Terry L. Cooper’s Responsible Administrator Ethical Decision-Making Model, the Potter Box Framework (Facts, Values, Principles, Loyalties), and the Five-Step Integrated Synthesis Protocol—Stakeholder and Fact Mapping, Statutory and Constitutional Boundary Check, Multi-Philosophical Interrogation (Kantian Universalizability/Dignity Test, Millian Rule-Utilitarian Test, Rawlsian Difference Principle / Gandhi’s Talisman Test, and Aristotelian/Gita Virtue Test), Short-Term and Long-Term Second-Order Consequence and Risk Mitigation Analysis, and Actionable, Institutionalized, and Accountable Resolution.
