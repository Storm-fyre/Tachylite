### Part 1: Theoretical Foundations of International Relations and Classical Geopolitical Doctrines
- **Classical Realism:** Thucydides’ Melian Dialogue and the Peloponnesian security dilemma, Kautilya’s Rajamandala interstate geometry, Niccolò Machiavelli, Thomas Hobbes, E.H. Carr (The Twenty Years' Crisis), and Hans Morgenthau’s six principles of political realism
- **Structural Realism / Neorealism:** Kenneth Waltz’s Theory of International Politics (systemic anarchy, distribution of capabilities, bipolarity vs. multipolarity), John Mearsheimer’s Offensive Realism (Tragedy of Great Power Politics) vs. Defensive Realism (Stephen Walt’s balance-of-threat theory, Robert Jervis’s security dilemma), and Neoclassical Realism (domestic transmission belts)
- **Liberalism and Neoliberal Institutionalism:** Kantian Perpetual Peace, Democratic Peace Theory (Michael Doyle), Robert Keohane and Joseph Nye’s Complex Interdependence, Stephen Krasner’s Regime Theory, and G. John Ikenberry’s liberal institutional order
- **Social Constructivism and the English School:** Alexander Wendt’s social theory of international politics (Hobbesian, Lockean, and Kantian cultures of anarchy; identities and norm life-cycles of Finnemore and Sikkink) and Hedley Bull’s Anarchical Society
- **Marxist, Critical, Post-Colonial, and Feminist IR theories:** Immanuel Wallerstein’s World-Systems analysis (Core-Semi-Periphery-Periphery extraction), Dependency critique, Robert Cox’s Neo-Gramscian hegemony, Amitav Acharya’s Global IR / Multiplex World, and J. Ann Tickner’s Feminist IR
- **Classical Geopolitical Doctrines:** Halford Mackinder’s Heartland Theory (Eurasian pivot), Nicholas Spykman’s Rimland Theory, Alfred Thayer Mahan’s Sea Power doctrine, and Eurasianism.

### Part 2: The Architecture of Global Governance, International Courts, Specialized Agencies, and the Crisis of Multilateralism
- **The United Nations System:** structural mechanics of the UN Security Council (P5 veto hegemony, Chapter VI pacific settlement vs. Chapter VII coercive enforcement, UNSC 1267 Sanctions Committee listing holds, and India’s proposed Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism - CCIT), UN General Assembly (Uniting for Peace Resolution 377A), ECOSOC, and Peacekeeping mandate/funding crises
- **UNSC reform impasse:** the G4 coalition (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan), Uniting for Consensus (Coffee Club) resistance, the African Union Ezulwini Consensus, L.69 Group, and Intergovernmental Negotiations (IGN)
- **International Judicial and Arbitral Institutions:** International Court of Justice (ICJ: contentious vs. advisory jurisdiction; Kulbhushan Jadhav, Chagos, and Palestine opinions), International Criminal Court (ICC: 2002 Rome Statute jurisdiction and structural rationale for non-signatory stance of India, the US, China, and Russia), Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA), and ITLOS (Enrica Lexie and South China Sea rulings)
- **The Bretton Woods Institutions vs. Non-Western Multilateral Banks:** International Monetary Fund (IMF quota/voting-share distortions, US veto power, Special Drawing Rights - SDRs, structural adjustment conditionalities) and World Bank Group (IBRD, IDA, IFC, MIGA) vs. the New Development Bank (NDB / BRICS Bank) and Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)
- **The World Trade Organization (WTO):** 1994 Marrakesh Agreement, MFN and National Treatment, Special and Differential Treatment (S&DT), Doha Round stagnation, paralysis of the Appellate Body, Joint Statement Initiatives (plurilaterals), and e-commerce moratorium debates
- **Specialized Agencies, Humanitarian Law, and Global Commons:** WHO (International Health Regulations and the WHO Pandemic Agreement), ILO, UNESCO, UNRWA, UNHCR (1949 Geneva Conventions and the 1951 Refugee Convention / 1967 Protocol—India’s non-signatory strategic rationale), UNCLOS (1982), the Antarctic Treaty System (1959) and Madrid Protocol (Maitri, Bharati, Maitri-II), and the 1967 Outer Space Treaty.

### Part 3: International Security, WMD Non-Proliferation, Strategic Export Control Cartels, and Arms Control Erosion
- **The Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT, 1968):** the three pillars (non-proliferation, disarmament under Article VI, peaceful use), structural division between P5 Nuclear-Weapon States (NWS) and Non-Nuclear-Weapon States (NNWS), and India’s principled non-signatory stance
- Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (CTBT, 1996: Annex-2 ratification requirements, zero-yield threshold vs. sub-critical computer simulations, CTBTO verification grid)
- Fissile Material Cut-off Treaty (FMCT) deadlock at the Conference on Disarmament, Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW, 2017), Regional Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zones (Tlatelolco, Rarotonga, Bangkok, Pelindaba, Semipalatinsk), IAEA Safeguards and Additional Protocol, and the Hague Code of Conduct (HCOC)
- Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions (BWC 1972 verification vacuum and CWC 1993 / OPCW inspection regime)
- **The Four Multilateral Strategic Export Control Cartels:** Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG: 1974 origins post-Pokhran-I, Trigger List, the September 2008 India-specific clean waiver, and ongoing Chinese procedural veto blocking full Indian membership), Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR: Category I 500 kg / 300 km thresholds and India’s 2016 accession), Wassenaar Arrangement (dual-use goods and conventional arms, India’s 2017 accession), and Australia Group (chemical/biological precursors, India’s 2018 accession)
- **Collapse of the global strategic arms control architecture:** demise of the ABM Treaty (2002), INF Treaty (2019), and Open Skies Treaty, Russian CTBT de-ratification, the February 2026 expiration of the New START Treaty (ending bilateral US-Russia strategic warhead caps), and unconstrained triad/hypersonic/MIRV competition.

### Part 4: Geo-Economics, Weaponized Interdependence, Global Tax/Debt Architectures, and Supply Chain Warfare
- **Mechanics of weaponized interdependence (Farrell and Newman):** asymmetric network topologies, the "Panopticon effect" (surveillance) and the "Chokepoint effect" (coercive exclusion)
- **Weaponization of international clearing and finance:** US Dollar primacy, CHIPS clearing, the SWIFT messaging network, unilateral and secondary extraterritorial sanctions (OFAC, CAATSA), G7 price caps, and freezing of sovereign central bank reserves
- **De-dollarization counter-strategies:** bilateral Local Currency Settlement (LCS) mechanisms, Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVAs), central bank currency swap lines, SPFS and CIPS alternatives, central bank gold accumulation, and BRICS cross-border CBDC/payment settlement initiatives (BRICS Bridge / Pay)
- **Supply chain geopolitics and industrial policy:** semiconductor value-chain chokepoints (US EDA design tools, Dutch ASML EUV lithography monopoly, Japanese chemicals, Taiwanese TSMC and South Korean Samsung fabrication concentration; US CHIPS and Science Act, export controls, and Chip 4 Alliance), critical minerals cartels (Rare Earth Elements, Lithium, Cobalt, Nickel, Graphite, Gallium, Germanium export restrictions), and friend-shoring networks (Supply Chain Resilience Initiative - SCRI and IPEF Supply Chain Pillar)
- **Geopolitical infrastructure lending:** China’s Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), debt-trap diplomacy debates, and Western/multilateral alternatives (G7 PGII and EU Global Gateway)
- **Sovereign Debt and Global Tax Governance:** the G20 Common Framework for Debt Treatments (Paris Club vs. Chinese bilateral sovereign lending in Sri Lanka, Zambia, and Ghana) and the OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS 2.0) Two-Pillar Framework (Pillar One digital taxing rights and Pillar Two 15% Global Minimum Corporate Tax).

### Part 5: Indian Strategic Culture, Foreign Policy Doctrines, Nuclear Deterrence, Soft Power, and the Global Diaspora
- **Ideational foundations of Indian strategic culture:** Kautilyan realism (Mandala theory, Shadgunya six-fold policy, four Upayas) synthesized with Ashokan/Buddhist moral internationalism (Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam)
- **Evolution of Indian foreign policy doctrines:** Phase I Nehruvian non-alignment (1947–62), Phase II post-1962 realpolitik balancing and the 1971 Indo-Soviet Treaty (1962–91), Phase III post-Cold War economic pragmatism (1991 Look East, 1996 Gujral Doctrine of non-reciprocal accommodation, 1998 nuclear overtness, 2005–08 Civil Nuclear Deal), and Phase IV contemporary Strategic Autonomy, Multi-Alignment, and Vishwamitra plurilateral bridging (2014–present)
- **India’s Nuclear Doctrine (January 2003 CCS codification):** Credible Minimum Deterrence (CMD), No First Use (NFU) pledge, massive and unacceptable punitive retaliation, the chemical and biological attack exception, and strict civilian political control via the Nuclear Command Authority (NCA: Political Council chaired by the PM and Executive Council chaired by the NSA) and Strategic Forces Command (SFC) across the nuclear triad
- **Cultural, Soft Power, and HADR Statecraft:** Project Mausam, International Buddhist Confederation, ICCR, Yoga/AYUSH Global Centre, Vaccine Maitri, and Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (Operation Dost, Operation Karuna, Operation Sadbhav)
- **The 35-million-strong Global Indian Diaspora (NRIs and OCIs) and Consular Statecraft:** historical waves (19th-century Girmitiya indentured diaspora in Mauritius, Fiji, Guyana, Trinidad, Suriname vs. Western tech/financial elites in the US, UK, Canada, Australia vs. 9-million Gulf contractual workers), the world’s largest inward remittance economy ($125B+/year), OCI statutory rights vs. exclusions, diaspora political leverage vs. transnational Khalistani separatist friction (India-Canada diplomatic rupture and Five Eyes intelligence dynamics), and Non-Combatant Evacuation Operations (Operation Rahat, Vande Bharat, Operation Devi Shakti, Operation Ganga, Operation Kaveri, Operation Ajay, Operation Indravati).

### Part 6: The Subcontinental Security Matrix I (The Western Flank): Pakistan, Indus Hydropolitics, Afghanistan, and SAARC Paralysis
- **Structural drivers of the India-Pakistan rivalry:** conflicting state identities, the Rawalpindi military-ISI garrison state (Milbus), the Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan territorial dispute, four conventional wars (1947–48, 1965, 1971, Kargil 1999), Siachen (Operation Meghdoot 1984), and Sir Creek
- **Nuclear escalation dynamics and the Stability-Instability Paradox:** Pakistan’s asymmetric proxy terror strategy under a nuclear umbrella and low-yield tactical nuclear weapons (Nasr)
- **Evolution of Indian counter-doctrines:** from Operation Parakram and Cold Start / Integrated Battle Groups (IBGs) to the 2016 cross-LoC surgical strikes, the February 2019 Balakot pre-emptive non-military airstrike, the August 2019 constitutional reorganization of J&K, FATF grey-listing coercion, and punitive cross-border deterrence
- **Transboundary Hydropolitics—The Indus Waters Treaty (IWT, 1960):** World Bank-brokered allocation of Eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej to India; Shahpurkandi and Ujh completion) vs. Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab to Pakistan with Indian run-of-the-river hydroelectric rights), Pakistani technical lawfare against Baglihar, Kishanganga (330 MW), and Ratle (850 MW) projects, the Article IX dispute-resolution crisis (parallel Neutral Expert and Court of Arbitration proceedings), and India’s formal notices under Article XII(3) (2023–2026) to modify/review the treaty and suspend/place routine mechanisms in abeyance
- **Afghanistan and the Taliban 2.0 Emirate (post-August 2021):** India’s $3B+ infrastructure footprint (Salma Dam, Zaranj-Delaram Highway, Afghan Parliament), reopening of India’s "Technical Mission" in Kabul (2022) and Chabahar wheat/medical aid, the Pakistan-Taliban rupture over the Durand Line and Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) insurgency, and ISKP terror threats
- Structural paralysis of SAARC (1985–present) and India’s pivot to BIMSTEC.

### Part 7: The Subcontinental Security Matrix II (The Himalayan and Eastern Flanks): Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, and BIMSTEC
- **India-Nepal relations:** the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship (Articles 6 and 7 asymmetry debates and the Eminent Persons Group report), open-border Roti-Beti ties, cartographic disputes (Kalapani, Lipulekh, Limpiyadhura stemming from the 1816 Treaty of Sagauli, and Susta), Agnipath Gurkha recruitment friction, and Chinese BRI encroachment vs. India’s cross-border energy and digital integration (Motihari-Amlekhgunj petroleum pipeline, Arun-III hydel project, the 2024 Long-Term Power Trade Agreement to import 10,000 MW over 10 years, tripartite Nepal-India-Bangladesh power flow, and UPI integration)
- **India-Bhutan relations:** transition from the 1949 Treaty to the revised 2007 Friendship Treaty, turnkey hydropower interdependency (Chukha, Kurichhu, Tala, Mangdechhu, Punatsangchhu-I & II), support for Bhutan’s 13th Five-Year Plan and the Gelephu Mindfulness City project, the 2017 Doklam tri-junction standoff overlooking the Siliguri Corridor, and China-Bhutan boundary negotiations ("Three-Step Roadmap" and Jakarlung/Pasamlung vs. Doklam swap pressure)
- **India-Bangladesh relations:** 2009–2024 gains (elimination of Northeast insurgent camps, 2015 Land Boundary Agreement 100th Amendment, Chittagong/Mongla transit, Akhaura-Agartala rail, Friendship Pipeline, Maitri power plant) vs. the August 2024 political regime transition (ouster of Sheikh Hasina, Muhammad Yunus’s interim administration and post-transition dynamics, minority security tensions, Teesta water deadlock, and the 2026 expiration/renewal negotiations for the 1996 Ganga Water Sharing Treaty)
- **India-Myanmar relations:** navigating the post-2021 Tatmadaw military junta vs. Ethnic Armed Organizations (Operation 1027, Arakan Army capture of Rakhine/Paletwa, Chin National Front), security risks to the Kaladan Multi-Modal Project (Sittwe port) and IMT Trilateral Highway vs. China’s CMEC (Kyaukpyu port and Coco Islands), Manipur/Mizoram refugee spillover, and India’s 2024 termination of the Free Movement Regime (FMR) and border-fencing mandate
- Sub-regional integration via BIMSTEC (2022 Colombo Charter, Maritime Transport Agreement, and India’s Security Pillar lead) and BBIN.

### Part 8: The Indian Ocean Littoral, Island Geopolitics, and Regional Maritime Security Grids
- **India-Sri Lanka relations:** post-civil war reconciliation and the 13th Amendment Tamil devolution question, Palk Bay and Katchatheevu Island fishermen disputes, Chinese dual-use footprints (99-year Hambantota port lease, Colombo Port City, spy/research vessel dockings) vs. India’s $4B emergency financial lifeline during the 2022 sovereign default, IMF debt restructuring co-chairmanship, and strategic infrastructure integration under the post-2024 Dissanayake administration (Colombo West Container Terminal, Trincomalee energy hub, Kankesanthurai port, power-grid and petroleum-pipeline links)
- **India-Maldives relations:** strategic location across the Eight and Nine Degree Channels, electoral oscillations from Solih’s "India First" to Muizzu’s 2023 "India Out" campaign (replacing military aviation crews with civilians) and the 2024–2025 pragmatic reset driven by Maldivian debt distress (Indian currency swaps, Treasury-bill rollovers, Greater Malé Connectivity Project, and Comprehensive Economic and Maritime Security Partnership)
- **Western Indian Ocean Island Neighbors:** Mauritius (Girmitiya ties, 2021 CECPA, and the February 2024 inauguration of the Agaléga Island dual-use airstrip and jetty), the historic 2024–2025 UK-Mauritius Sovereignty Transfer Agreement over the Chagos Archipelago (restoring Mauritian sovereignty while securing a 99-year lease for the US-UK Diego Garcia military base), Seychelles (Assumption Island radar grid), Madagascar, Comoros, and joint EEZ patrols with France (Réunion)
- **Indian Ocean Region (IOR) doctrinal and institutional architecture:** evolution of SAGAR (2015) into the upgraded MAHASAGAR Doctrine (2025: Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions), the Colombo Security Conclave (CSC: India, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Mauritius, Bangladesh), Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA), Indian Ocean Naval Symposium (IONS), and the Information Fusion Centre – Indian Ocean Region (IFC-IOR, Gurugram).

### Part 9: The Sino-Indian Rivalry: Contested Borders, Strategic Encirclement, Economic Asymmetry, and Hydrological Leverage
- **Historical trajectory and >5x GDP/defense asymmetry:** 1950 Tibet annexation, 1954 Panchsheel Agreement, 1959 Dalai Lama asylum, and the 1962 border war
- **Cartographic anatomy of the 3,488-km Line of Actual Control (LAC):** Western Sector in Ladakh/Aksai Chin (1865 Johnson-Ardagh Line vs. 1899 Macartney-MacDonald Line, plus Pakistan’s illegal 1963 cession of Shaksgam Valley to China), Middle Sector (Himachal/Uttarakhand), and Eastern Sector in Arunachal Pradesh/Sikkim (1914 Simla Convention and McMahon Line, Chinese "renaming" warfare under its 2022 Land Borders Law)
- **Collapse of the 1993–2013 border management agreements (1993, 1996, 2005, 2013):** standoff flashpoints at Depsang (2013), Chumar (2014), Doklam (2017), the May–June 2020 Eastern Ladakh intrusions (triggered by India’s DSDBO Road construction), the lethal June 15, 2020 Galwan Valley clash, Tawang/Yangtse clash (2022), and the October 2024 Depsang and Demchok patrolling disengagement understanding amid permanent winter militarization
- Chinese strategic encirclement ("String of Pearls": CPEC/Gwadar traversing Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, Djibouti naval base, Ream in Cambodia, Kyaukpyu, Hambantota)
- **The bilateral trade paradox:** trade exceeding $118B with an $85B+ Indian trade deficit due to structural reliance on Chinese APIs, electronics, solar wafers, and heavy machinery despite Press Note 3 FDI screening
- **Transboundary Hydropolitics of the Tibetan Plateau:** absence of a formal water-sharing treaty, Chinese upstream dams on the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra), China’s approval of the 60,000 MW Medog mega-dam at the Great Bend, hydrological data-sharing leverage, and India’s counter-balancing Upper Siang multipurpose storage project.

### Part 10: The Indo-Pacific Strategic Theater, Maritime Chokepoints, Minilateral Grids, ASEAN Centrality, and East Asian Pillars
- Geopolitical transition from "Asia-Pacific" to "Indo-Pacific" (Shinzo Abe’s 2007 "Confluence of the Two Seas" and India’s 2018 Shangri-La vision and 2019 Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative - IPOI)
- **Maritime spatial realities and global chokepoints:** China’s "Malacca Dilemma", Strait of Malacca, Sunda, Lombok, and Makassar Straits, Taiwan and Luzon Straits, and China’s Nine-Dash/Ten-Dash Line militarization in the South China Sea (defying the 2016 UNCLOS Arbitral Award) and East China Sea (Senkaku/Diaoyu)
- **The Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (QUAD: India, US, Japan, Australia):** evolution from the 2004 Tsunami Core Group and 2017 revival to leader-level summits, Malabar naval exercises, Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness (IPMDA), critical technology/semiconductor supply-chain networks, and biosecurity
- AUKUS (Australia, UK, US: Pillar I nuclear-powered attack submarines and IAEA non-proliferation debates; Pillar II advanced technologies) and the US-Japan-Philippines-Australia ("Squad") lattice
- **India’s Act East Policy and ASEAN:** 2022 Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, East Asia Summit (EAS), ADMM-Plus, India’s 2019 exit from RCEP, review of the 2009 ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement (AITIGA), and South China Sea defense diplomacy (export of BrahMos supersonic cruise missiles to the Philippines, naval partnerships with Vietnam, Indonesia/Sabang, and Singapore/Changi)
- **East Asian Bilateral Pillars and Pacific Islands:** India-Japan Special Strategic and Global Partnership (2+2 dialogue, 2020 ACSA logistics pact, Shinkansen/Northeast ODA, Unicorn stealth radar co-production), India-South Korea Special Strategic Partnership (K9 Vajra artillery, shipbuilding, electronics), India-Australia ECTA and MLSA, and the Forum for India-Pacific Islands Cooperation (FIPIC).

### Part 11: The Indo-US Strategic Partnership: Defense Interoperability, Critical Technologies, and Bilateral Frictions
- Historical evolution from Cold War "estranged democracies" to 21st-century strategic convergence
- The 2005–2008 Civil Nuclear Deal (Hyde Act, 123 Agreement, IAEA safeguards, 2008 NSG Clean Waiver, and lingering supplier liability friction under India’s CLNDA 2010)
- **Defense and intelligence interoperability architecture:** Major Defense Partner (2016) and STA-1 (2018) designations, 2+2 Ministerial Dialogue, and the Four Foundational Defense Agreements—GSOMIA (2002) and ISA (2019), LEMOA (2016 logistics exchange), COMCASA (2018 encrypted communications), and BECA (2020 geospatial intelligence)—supplemented by the 2024 Security of Supply Arrangement (SOSA), Indian liaison officers at INDOPACOM/NAVCENT, and Master Ship Repair Agreements (MSRA) at Indian shipyards
- **Strategic high-technology co-production:** evolution from iCET (2023) to the 2025 U.S.-India COMPACT and TRUST (Transforming the Relationship Utilizing Strategic Technology) frameworks, GE Aerospace–HAL F414 jet engine co-production in India, acquisition of 31 MQ-9B HALE armed drones, Stryker/Javelin co-production, INDUS-X defense startup bridge, and NASA-ISRO NISAR and Artemis Accords space collaboration
- **Bilateral friction points under the 2025–2026 US administration (Trump 2.0):** reciprocal tariff threats and trade deficit pressures, 2019 GSP revocation, USTR Special 301 IPR critiques, H-1B skilled migration politics, extraterritorial sanctions friction over Russia and Iran, and diplomatic/legal tensions over transnational Khalistani separatist investigations.

### Part 12: The Indo-Russian Axis, Eurasian Balancing, European Realignments, and the Arctic Geopolitical Frontier
- **The India-Russia "Special and Privileged Strategic Partnership":** historical legacy of Soviet UNSC vetoes and the 1971 Treaty
- **Defense and nuclear interdependency:** licensed manufacturing of Su-30MKI fighters, T-90S tanks, and AK-203 rifles, the BrahMos joint venture, S-400 Triumf air-defense squadrons, nuclear submarine leases, and the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant (Units 1–6)
- **Post-2022 Ukraine war geo-economic shifts:** surge of discounted Russian crude oil to ~35–40% of Indian oil imports, Rupee-Ruble trade imbalance ($50B+ deficit), and India’s balancing act against the deepening Sino-Russian "no-limits" axis via Act Far East and the Chennai–Vladivostok Eastern Maritime Corridor
- **European Union engagement:** India-EU Strategic Partnership, the India-EU Trade and Technology Council (TTC, 2023), 2025–2026 FTA and Investment Protection negotiations, and friction over EU green protectionism (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism - CBAM and EU Deforestation Regulation - EUDR)
- The landmark India-EFTA Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA, 2024 with Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein) featuring a legally binding $100B investment and 1-million-job commitment
- **Key European bilateral partners:** France (Horizon 2047 roadmap, 36 Rafale + 26 Rafale-M jets, Scorpène submarines, Safran jet engine co-development, Jaitapur nuclear project, and trilateral Indo-Pacific frameworks), Germany (Green and Sustainable Development Partnership, Project-75I submarine bids), and the United Kingdom (Roadmap 2030, 2025–2026 India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement / CETA FTA, and electric naval propulsion co-development)
- **The Arctic Geopolitical Frontier:** Arctic Council paralysis, Russia’s Northern Sea Route (NSR), China’s "Polar Silk Road", and India’s Observer status, 2022 Arctic Policy, Himadri station at Svalbard, and winter Arctic expeditions.

### Part 13: West Asia and the Extended Western Neighborhood: Energy, De-Hyphenation, Minilateral Corridors, and Regional Ruptures
- **Strategic shift from transactional "Look West" to "Think West":** 9-million Indian diaspora in the Gulf, $50B+ annual remittances, 50–60% crude oil and LNG supply dependencies, and counter-terrorism intelligence sharing
- **Diplomatic de-hyphenation doctrine:** simultaneously balancing deep strategic ties across the Arab Gulf monarchies (UAE, Saudi Arabia), Israel, and the Islamic Republic of Iran alongside Palestine
- **The GCC transformation:** 2022 India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), Rupee-Dirham local currency settlement, Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth investments, Strategic Petroleum Reserves, IIT Delhi-Abu Dhabi, and the Saudi-India Strategic Partnership Council
- **Israel and minilateral grids:** defense and agricultural ties (Barak-8 MR-SAM, Phalcon AWACS, Heron drones, drip-irrigation Centres of Excellence), the Abraham Accords (2020), and the I2U2 Group (India, Israel, UAE, United States)
- **Iran and Eurasian connectivity:** India’s May 2024 10-year operational contract for Chabahar Port (Shahid Beheshti terminal) bypassing Pakistan to Afghanistan and Central Asia, and the 7,200-km International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC)
- The India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC, launched at the 2023 New Delhi G20 Summit)
- **The 2023–2026 West Asian geopolitical ruptures:** the 2023 Beijing-brokered Saudi-Iran détente, the post-October 2023 Gaza-Israel-Lebanon war and direct Iran-Israel missile exchanges stalling IMEC, the December 2024 collapse of the Assad regime in Syria, and the Red Sea / Bab-el-Mandeb Houthi maritime crisis prompting the Indian Navy’s massive Operation Sankalp anti-piracy and merchant-escort deployment.

### Part 14: Central Asia, the African Continent, Latin America, and Global South Leadership
- **Central Asia ("Connect Central Asia" policy and the 2022 India–Central Asia Summit mechanism):** strategic stakes across Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Tajikistan, and Kyrgyzstan (Kazakh uranium supplies, secular buffer against Afghan extremism, Ashgabat Agreement, INSTC integration, and TAPI pipeline hurdles)
- **The Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO: 2017 Astana accession of India and Pakistan, 2023 Iran, 2024 Belarus):** Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS), Eurasian balancing, and India’s refusal to endorse China’s BRI
- **The African Continent:** PM Modi’s 10 Kampala Principles (2018), India-Africa Forum Summits, $12B+ in concessional Lines of Credit across 42 nations, Pan-African e-Network (e-VidyaBharati / e-ArogyaBharati), affordable generic pharmaceuticals, Western Indian Ocean maritime security (AIKEYME 2025 naval exercise, Mozambique LNG investments), vs. Chinese debt-diplomacy and Russian Africa Corps footprints
- **Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC):** India-CELAC and CARICOM dialogues, expansion of the India-MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement, crude oil and edible-oil security (Brazil, Guyana, Venezuela, Argentina), and KABIL’s 2024 acquisition of five lithium brine blocks in Argentina’s Catamarca province ("Lithium Triangle")
- **Institutionalizing Global South Leadership:** "Voice of the Global South" Summits, the 2023 New Delhi G20 Presidency consensus declaration and permanent induction of the 55-nation African Union (G21), and exporting India’s Digital Public Infrastructure (India Stack, UPI, DigiLocker, ONDC) via the Global DPI Repository.

### Part 15: Global Climate Diplomacy, Energy Transition Geopolitics, and Critical Mineral Cartographies
- **International politics of UNFCCC climate negotiations:** historical carbon debt (>70% cumulative emissions by developed nations vs. India’s ~4% share and ~2-tonne per-capita footprint), equitable carbon space, and the principle of Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)
- Coalitional diplomacy (BASIC and LMDCs), COP26 Glasgow coal "phase-down" vs. "phase-out" intervention, operationalization of the Loss and Damage Fund (COP27/COP28), the COP29 Baku New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG: $300B/year by 2035 vs. the Global South’s $1.3T demand), and the COP30 Belém trajectory
- **Weaponized green trade barriers:** EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), and US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) subsidies
- **India’s proactive climate statecraft:** Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment) and Indian-founded multilateral institutions—the International Solar Alliance (ISA, 2015: One Sun, One World, One Grid), Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI, 2019: IRIS for Small Island States), Global Biofuels Alliance (GBA, 2023), LeadIT, and the International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA)
- **Energy transition geopolitics:** shift from hydrocarbon geopolitics (OPEC+) to critical mineral cartels (Lithium, Cobalt, Nickel, Copper, Graphite, and 17 Rare Earth Elements dominated 60–90% by Chinese refining), India’s identification of 30 Critical Minerals and 2023 MMDR Act reform, KABIL overseas equity acquisitions, and India’s 2023 entry into the US-led Minerals Security Partnership (MSP).

### Part 16: The Emerging Multipolar Order: Great Power Rivalry, Minilateralism, BRICS+ Expansion, and Frontier Norm Generation
- Structural transition from the post-1991 unipolar liberal order to contested multipolarity
- **The US-China systemic rivalry:** technological containment, the "Thucydides Trap" debate, Taiwan Strait "Porcupine Strategy" and First Island Chain flashpoints, and the South China Sea
- Proliferation of issue-based minilateral groupings (QUAD, AUKUS, I2U2, India-France-UAE, India-France-Australia, US-Japan-ROK Camp David Trilateral, Chip 4) bypassing multilateral paralysis, and India’s unique bridge position across Western and Eurasian platforms
- **The expansion of BRICS (BRICS+ 2024–2025 inducting Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, and Indonesia):** internal strategic divergence between Sino-Russian attempts to forge an anti-Western bloc vs. Indian, Brazilian, and Emirati insistence on a "Non-Western, Not Anti-Western" multipolar reform platform
- **Global norm generation in frontier strategic domains:** Outer Space governance (obsolescence of the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, competition between the US-led Artemis Accords signed by India in 2023 and the Sino-Russian International Lunar Research Station - ILRS, and UN PAROS debates)
- Global Cyber governance (clash between the Western multi-stakeholder "Open Internet" / 2001 Budapest Convention model and the Sino-Russian "Cyber Sovereignty" model at the UN OEWG and 2024 UN Cybercrime Convention)
- Global Artificial Intelligence governance (2023 Bletchley Declaration, 2023 New Delhi GPAI Summit, 2025 Paris AI Action Summit, 2026 India AI Impact Summit, EU AI Act vs. US voluntary model vs. India’s DPI-based inclusive sovereign AI framework, and Geneva CCW negotiations on Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems - LAWS).
