### Part 1: The Social Matrix: Cultural Pluralism, Sociological Perspectives, and Linguistic Geographies
- **Foundational sociological perspectives on Indian society:** Indological/textual view (G.S. Ghurye, Louis Dumont), Structural-Functional field-view (M.N. Srinivas, S.C. Dube, André Béteille), Marxist historical materialism (D.P. Mukerji, A.R. Desai, Ramkrishna Mukherjee), and Subaltern/Civilizational perspectives (Ranajit Guha, B.R. Ambedkar, N.K. Bose, Surajit Sinha)
- Colonial orientalism (static village republics) vs. nationalist integrationism
- Robert Redfield, Milton Singer, and McKim Marriott’s Little Tradition and Great Tradition paradigm (orthogenetic vs. heterogenetic change, universalization and parochialization)
- **Linguistic taxonomy and geographical distribution:** Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic, Tibeto-Burman, and Andamanese language families
- Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, three-language formula friction, linguistic identity assertions, and the October 2024 expansion of Classical Languages of India to 11
- Colonial racial/morphological classifications (Herbert Risley, B.S. Guha) and modern population-genomic refutations (Ancestral North and South Indian admixture)
- Religious pluralism, spatial demographic distribution, and minority community profiles
- Cultural dialectics of assimilation, acculturation, integration, and composite syncretism (Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb).

### Part 2: The Caste System (Varna and Jati): Structural Hierarchy, Untouchability, and the 2027 Caste Census
- **Conceptual foundations of caste:** the scriptural-ritual model (Varna) vs. the empirical-functional reality (Jati)
- **Sociological theories of caste:** Louis Dumont’s structuralist paradigm of purity and pollution (Homo Hierarchicus), Celestin Bouglé’s defining traits (hereditary specialization, hierarchy, mutual repulsion), André Béteille’s Caste, Class, and Power (shift from cumulative to dispersed inequality), and B.R. Ambedkar’s materialist critique (Annihilation of Caste: unnatural division of laborers and graded inequality)
- Colonial Census codification (Herbert Risley, 1901–1931) hardening fluid identities
- **Post-Independence transformations:** M.N. Srinivas’s Sanskritization, Westernization, Secularization, and Dominant Caste paradigms
- Rajni Kothari’s politicization of caste, Dipankar Gupta’s transition from vertical hierarchy to horizontal competition ("discrete castes"), and Dalit Capitalism (DICCI)
- **Affirmative action and reservation politics:** Creamy Layer, Rohini Commission OBC sub-categorization, the August 2024 7-Judge Supreme Court verdict in State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh permitting SC/ST sub-classification, the 2023 Bihar Caste Survey, and the April 2025 Union Cabinet approval of statutory Caste Enumeration in the digital Population Census 2027
- Persistence of endogamy (and digital matrimonial-app endogamy), Khap panchayats and honor killings (Shakti Vahini 2018), the December 2025 Supreme Court ruling on matrilineal caste certificates, and the October 2024 Supreme Court judgment (Sukanya Shantha) abolishing caste-based labor and segregation in state prison manuals.

### Part 3: Stratification within Religious Minorities and Subaltern Identity Discourses
- Sociological myth of monolithic egalitarian minority social structures and Imtiaz Ahmad’s empirical framework on caste analogues among non-Hindus
- **Social stratification among Indian Muslims:** the structural tripartite divide across Ashraf (noble lineages claiming foreign extraction: Syed, Sheikh, Mughal, Pathan), Ajlaf (clean occupational artisanal converts: Ansari, Qureshi, Mansoori, Idrisi), and Arzal (severely marginalized, historically "untouchable" convert groups: Halalkhor, Lalbegi, Hela, Dhobi)
- **Political and sociological emergence of the Pasmanda Muslim movement (Ali Anwar):** demands for social justice, internal democratization, and affirmative action inclusion
- **Caste hierarchies among non-Hindus:** Dalit Christians (church and burial-ground segregation), Dalit Sikhs (Mazhabis, Ramdasias, and Ravidassia Dera assertions vs. dominant Jat Sikhs), and Neo-Buddhists
- Constitutional friction over Paragraph 3 of the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950 (restricting SC status to Hindus, Sikhs, and Buddhists) and the Justice K.G. Balakrishnan Commission (2022–2026)
- Empirical findings and policy trajectories of the Justice Rajindar Sachar Committee Report (2006) and Ranganath Misra Commission Report (2007)
- Parsi demographic decline (Jiyo Parsi)
- Sociological debate over Uniform Civil Codes vs. minority cultural autonomy.

### Part 4: Family, Kinship, and Marriage Systems: Structural Continuity and Contemporary Transformations
- **Traditional Indian joint family:** coparcenary property rights (Mitakshara vs. Dayabhaga and equal coparcenary rights for daughters in Vineeta Sharma 2020), patriarchal authority, filial piety, and ritual unity
- A.M. Shah’s The Household Dimension of the Family in India (distinguishing the commensal household from the property/ritual family and refuting simplistic linear nuclearization myths) and I.P. Desai’s functional jointness
- **Iravati Karve’s four-zone comparative taxonomy of kinship organization in India:** Northern (patrilineal, patrilocal, village exogamy, four-gotra rule, hypergamous asymmetry between bride-givers and bride-takers), Southern (isogamous, preferential cross-cousin and uncle-niece marriages, territorial endogamy), Central, and Eastern zones, alongside transformations in matrilineal kinship (Khasi, Garo, and Kerala Nair Marumakkathayam / Tharavad) and Leela Dube’s Seed and Earth metaphor
- **Marriage institutions:** caste endogamy, Gotra/Sapinda exogamy, Anuloma (hypergamy) vs. Pratiloma (hypogamy), dowry inflation despite the Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, October 2024 Supreme Court Child Marriage Prevention guidelines (SEVA case), single-parent/dual-earner/DINK households, "silver divorce," Assisted Reproductive Technology and Surrogacy Acts (2021) reshaping biological kinship, and non-heteronormative partnerships (Supriyo 2023).

### Part 5: Gender Regimes, Brahmanical Patriarchy, Care Economy, and Women’s and Queer Mobilizations
- **Architecture of subcontinental patriarchy:** Sylvia Walby’s private vs. public patriarchy, Uma Chakravarti’s "Brahmanical Patriarchy" (controlling female sexuality to preserve caste purity and endogamy), and Sharmila Rege’s Dalit Feminist Standpoint intersectionality
- **Demographic manifestations of son preference:** Amartya Sen’s "Missing Women," skewed child sex ratios, sex-selective abortions under the PCPNDT Act (1994), and cross-regional "bride trafficking"
- **The care economy and labor market paradox:** NSO Time-Use Survey (2024) metrics on unremunerated domestic/care work, Claudia Goldin’s U-shaped feminization curve, low urban formal FLFPR vs. rural unpaid family labor, "pink-collarization," and the glass ceiling/cliff
- Political agency vs. proxy representation (73rd Amendment PRIs, Sarpanch Pati syndrome, and the 106th Constitutional Amendment Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam)
- **Gendered violence and 2024–2026 Supreme Court jurisprudence:** domestic abuse, workplace safety (National Task Force on Healthcare Professionals post-R.G. Kar case), marital rape exception debates (Section 63 BNS), Right to Menstrual Health under Article 21 (Jaya Thakur 2026), equal maternity benefits for adoptive mothers (Hamsaanandini Nanduri 2026), and pro-rata appraisal during maternity leave
- Trajectory of women’s movements in India (19th-century reform, nationalist era, 1970s–80s autonomous movements, digital feminism)
- Queer and Transgender sociology (Hijra/Kinnar traditions, NALSA 2014, Navtej Johar 2018, Jane Kaushik 2026 horizontal employment equality) and functional disability assessment (Anmol 2025).

### Part 6: Agrarian Social Structure, Rural Class Stratification, and Peasant Mobilizations
- **Sociological transformation of the Indian village:** breakdown of William Wiser’s traditional Jajmani system of hereditary patron-client (Kamin-Jajman) grain-for-service exchanges under monetization and market integration
- **Agrarian class taxonomy:** Daniel Thorner’s Malik (absentee/supervisory landlords), Kisan (working peasant proprietors), and Mazdoor (landless agricultural laborers, predominantly Dalits and Adivasis)
- The 1970s Rudra–Bardhan–Utsa Patnaik "Mode of Production in Indian Agriculture" debate (semi-feudalism vs. emerging agrarian capitalism)
- Post-Green Revolution enrichment of the "Bullock Capitalists" (Rudolph and Rudolph) and intermediate Dominant Peasant Castes (Jats, Marathas, Patidars, Kapus, Kammas, Reddys, Vokkaligas, Lingayats, Yadavs)
- **Contemporary agrarian stagnation and the reservation paradox:** land fragmentation, rising input costs, groundwater exhaustion, and dominant landed castes agitating for backward-class (OBC/EWS) reservation quotas due to inability to transition into urban formal employment
- Jan Breman’s "footloose labour," neo-bondage, and circular distress migration
- Feminization of agricultural labor without female legal land titles
- Sociology of farmer suicides (Durkheimian anomic/fatalistic currents)
- Dipankar Gupta’s "Whither the Indian Village?", rural non-farm employment, and Rurbanization.

### Part 7: Tribal Societies (Adivasis): Continuum Debates, Development-Induced Displacement, and Indigenous Assertions
- **Demographic and geographical cartography of India’s 705+ Scheduled Tribes:** Central Indian heartland (Bhils, Gonds, Santhals, Oraons, Mundas) vs. North-Eastern frontier polities (Nagas, Mizos, Khasis, Garos, Apatanis) and Island tribes
- **Structural properties of tribal social organization:** kinship-based reciprocity, customary communal resource ownership, youth dormitories (Ghotul, Dhumkuria, Morung), and animistic/totemic traditions
- Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (75 PVTGs) and Denotified, Nomadic, and Semi-Nomadic Tribes (DNTs: colonial Criminal Tribes Act stigma, Idate Commission, SEED scheme)
- **Classical sociological debates on tribal policy:** Verrier Elwin’s isolationist "National Park" approach vs. G.S. Ghurye’s assimilationist "Backward Hindus" thesis vs. Jawaharlal Nehru’s integrationist Tribal Panchsheel
- N.K. Bose’s "Hindu Method of Tribal Absorption" and Surajit Sinha’s "Tribe-Caste and Tribe-Peasant Continuum"
- Virginius Xaxa Committee Report (2014) and emerging internal class stratification/elite capture within tribes
- **The Development-Displacement Nexus:** mega-dams, open-cast coal/bauxite/iron mining, industrial corridors, and wildlife sanctuaries dispossessing Adivasis without rehabilitation
- Customary land alienation, Forest Rights Act (2006) bottlenecks, Sarna religious code demands, and Pathalgadi / Fifth and Sixth Schedule autonomy assertions.

### Part 8: Urbanization, Spatial Segregation, the Informal Proletariat, and Digital-Age Urban Sociology
- **Sociological theories of urbanism:** Ferdinand Tönnies (Gemeinschaft community vs. Gesellschaft association), Georg Simmel (Blasé metropolitan mental life), Louis Wirth (Urbanism as a Way of Life: size, density, heterogeneity), Manuel Castells (collective consumption), and David Harvey (Right to the City)
- **Subcontinental urbanization dynamics:** rural distress push vs. urban pull, "subaltern urbanization" (Denis and Zérah) in small and Census Towns, and unplanned peri-urban interfaces
- **Urban spatial segregation and housing exclusion:** persistence of caste-based residential clustering and religious ghettoization in Indian metropolises (exclusionary rental/housing society practices and culinary policing)
- Slums, informal squatter settlements, and precarious struggles over urban commons (water cartels, sanitation, street vending spaces)
- **Sociology of the urban informal proletariat:** migrant construction workers, feminized paid domestic workers, street vendors, waste-pickers, and algorithmically managed platform gig workers
- Gated residential enclaves, gentrification, and Amita Baviskar’s "Bourgeois Environmentalism" (framing the urban poor as ecological polluters to justify slum demolitions)
- Psychological atomization, youth coaching-hub alienation (Kota syndrome), adolescent digital/smartphone and online-gaming addiction (Economic Survey 2024–26 warnings), and urban mental health crises.

### Part 9: Secularism, Communalism, Regionalism, Globalization, and Inter-Generational Metamorphosis
- **Sociology of Indian Secularism:** Western negative secularism (strict Church-State wall and French Laïcité) vs. Indian positive secularism (Sarva Dharma Sambhava) and Rajeev Bhargava’s model of contextual "Principled Distance"
- Sociological critiques of secularism by T.N. Madan and Ashis Nandy vs. B.R. Ambedkar’s Constitutional Morality (and the 2026 9-Judge Supreme Court hearings on Constitutional vs. Social/Popular Morality)
- **Communalism as a modern political-ideological construct (Bipan Chandra):** colonial divide-and-rule, Partition memory, majoritarian assertiveness vs. minority fundamentalism, Paul Brass’s "Institutionalized Riot Systems," and algorithmic hate-speech echo chambers
- **Regionalism and sub-nationalism:** Myron Weiner’s "Sons of the Soil" nativist doctrine, private-sector domicile reservation demands, inter-state riparian and boundary conflicts, linguistic sub-nationalism, and "internal colonialism" developmental grievances
- **Globalization and cultural metamorphosis:** Yogendra Singh’s Modernization of Indian Tradition, rise of the post-1991 "New Middle Class" (Leela Fernandes), conspicuous consumerism, George Ritzer’s "McDonaldization" vs. Roland Robertson’s "Glocalization" (hybridization of food, cinema, festivals, and matrimonial apps), and revival of identity/religiosity under globalization
- Inter-generational friction, geriatric isolation, feminization of aging, the "Silver Economy" (NITI Aayog senior-care reforms), and the North-South demographic transition divide.
