> [!abstract]+ PART A: The "Zero-Jargon" Big-Picture Story
> In 1789, France was the richest and most populous power in continental Europe, yet its monarchy went bankrupt. Why? Because the French king had borrowed billions to fight global wars against Britain, but France's medieval legal system forbade him from taxing the wealthiest landowners—the Catholic Church and the nobility. When a catastrophic hailstorm and freezing winter doubled bread prices in 1788–1789, the aristocratic courts blocked tax reform and forced the king to summon a medieval parliament for the first time in 175 years. That fiscal deadlock triggered a chain reaction: middle-class lawyers seized parliamentary sovereignty, starving Parisian workers stormed the royal fortress, and rural peasants burned the legal registers recording their feudal dues.
> 
> Once the old order shattered, the revolution radicalized across land and sea. Invaded by European monarchs, radical republicans in Paris established a wartime dictatorship that drafted an 800,000-man citizen army and imposed strict price controls, while in France's richest Caribbean sugar colony, half a million enslaved Africans overthrew both their plantation masters and three invading European empires to birth the independent Black republic of Haiti. Exhausted by terror and instability at home, the French propertied classes eventually turned to military general Napoleon Bonaparte, who locked in the Revolution's legal equality and property rights inside an authoritarian bureaucratic empire. When Napoleon overreached by trying to blockade British trade across the entire continent—bleeding his armies dry in Spanish guerrilla warfare and the freezing Russian steppe—the victorious powers met at Vienna in 1814–1815 to ring-fence France with buffer states and manage European peace through collective summitry, even as the unstoppable forces of nationalism and British industrialization brewed beneath their feet.

## PART B: Section-by-Section Story Threads & Inline Decoder

### I. The Fiscal-Agrarian Implosion of the Bourbon Ancien Régime and the Constitutional Rupture (1789–1791)

* **1. Structural Bankruptcy of the Bourbon Fiscal-Military State and the Estates-General (May 1789):** After financing the Seven Years' War and the American Revolution through high-interest loans, the Bourbon ***Ancien Régime*** (pre-1789 feudal-absolutist order) accumulated a 4.5-billion-***livre*** (French silver currency) debt that consumed over 50% of state revenue in interest, yet the Crown could not tap France's wealth because society was split into three corporate ***états*** (legal orders). The **First Estate** (clergy, paying only a token voluntary ***don gratuit*** [periodic gift] while collecting the ***dîme*** [10% church tithe]) and **Second Estate** (nobility, shielded by regional ***pays d'états*** [provincial tax privileges]) were exempt from the ***taille*** (direct land tax) and evaded the ***vingtième*** and ***capitation*** (royal income and head levies), dumping the entire burden onto the unprivileged **Third Estate** (peasants, urban workers, and the ***bourgeoisie*** [propertied middle class]), who paid the *taille*, the ***gabelle*** (salt tax monopoly collected by the ***Ferme générale*** [private tax-farming syndicate]), ***traites*** (internal customs), ***corvée royale*** (forced unpaid road labor), and feudal seigneurial dues like ***champart*** (harvest-share rent), ***banalités*** (compulsory fees to use the lord's mill, oven, and winepress), and ***lods et ventes*** (land-transfer fees). When royal ministers proposed a ***subvention territoriale*** (universal land tax), aristocratic judges in the thirteen regional ***Parlements*** (sovereign high courts staffed by the ***noblesse de robe*** [judicial nobility]) staged the ***Révolte nobiliaire*** (1787–1788 Aristocratic Revolt) to demand the **Estates-General**, which fatally coincided with the 1788–1789 **Labrousse Subsistence-Industrial Spiral** (a hailstorm and winter freeze that pushed bread to 88% of worker wages, collapsing demand for manufactured goods already battered by cheap British imports under the **1786 Eden Agreement**). Armed with local ***cahiers de doléances*** (grievance lists) and **Abbé Sieyès'** pamphlet redefining the productive Third Estate as the entire sovereign nation, commoner deputies rejected **voting by order** (one collective vote per estate, guaranteeing a 2-to-1 privileged veto) and demanded **voting by head** (a single unified chamber where individual votes counted equally).

* **2. Popular Insurrection, the Abolition of Feudalism, and the Bourgeois Constitutional Order (June 1789 – 1791):** Joined by sympathetic parish ***curés*** (lower clergy), the Third Estate proclaimed itself the **National Assembly** and, when locked out of their hall before a ***séance royale*** (royal session), swore the **Tennis Court Oath** at the ***Jeu de Paume*** (indoor royal tennis court) on June 20, 1789 to write a constitution. When Louis XVI massed troops around Paris, insurgents burned the ***barrières*** (royal customs gates) and stormed the **Bastille** on July 14 for gunpowder, while peasants during ***La Grande Peur*** (The Great Fear rural panic) attacked noble châteaux to burn the ***terriers*** (manorial legal registers recording feudal obligations), forcing the Assembly to pass the **August Decrees** (which abolished personal servitudes like ***mainmorte*** [serf-like inheritance limits] immediately, while making property dues like ***cens*** [feudal quit-rents] redeemable only by cash buyout). The Assembly then issued the **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen** enshrining natural rights, sacred private property, and Rousseau's **General Will (*volonté générale*)** (the doctrine that legitimate law expresses the collective civic interest of the sovereign nation), though the **Constitution of 1791** restricted voting to **Active Citizens** (taxpaying men over 25) while disenfranchising **Passive Citizens** (the poor and all women) and outlawing worker unions via the **Le Chapelier Law (1791)**. After 7,000 working-class Parisian ***poissardes*** (market women) marched on Versailles in October 1789 to haul the king to the Tuileries Palace in Paris, the Assembly sought to pay off the national debt by confiscating Church estates as ***biens nationaux*** (national lands) to back paper ***assignats*** (land-backed bond currency) and passing the **Civil Constitution of the Clergy (1790)**—which turned priests into elected state employees and split France between **juring (constitutional) clergy** (who swore the civic oath) and **refractory (non-juring) clergy** (who obeyed the Pope's condemnation and fueled rural counter-revolution).

### II. Total War, Jacobin Radicalization, the Thermidorian Reaction, and the Black Jacobins of Saint-Domingue (1791–1804)

* **3. Factional Radicalization, the Reign of Terror, and the Thermidorian Reaction (1792–1799):** After Louis XVI destroyed his legitimacy in the **Flight to Varennes** (June 1791 failed border escape) and Austria and Prussia threatened intervention, the republican club split between the **Girondins / Brissotins** (provincial commercial bourgeoisie favoring *laissez-faire* free markets, federalism, and ideological war) and the **Montagnards / Jacobins** ("the Mountain": radical centralizers led by Robespierre who allied with the Parisian working class). As invading armies crossed the frontier in August 1792, Parisian ***Sans-Culottes*** ("without knee-breeches": working-class artisans and shopkeepers organized in 48 neighborhood ***sections*** [wards]) and provincial ***fédérés*** (citizen-militia volunteers) stormed the Tuileries, overthrew the monarchy, and elected the **National Convention**, which executed Louis XVI in January 1793 after finding treasonous letters in his ***armoire de fer*** (secret iron safe). Facing the First Coalition invasion alongside the Catholic-royalist **Vendée Rebellion** and Girondin **Federalist Revolts**, the Montagnards concentrated wartime dictatorship in the twelve-man **Committee of Public Safety (*Comité de salut public*)**, enforcing the **Reign of Terror (1793–1794)** through the **Law of Suspects**, the **Law of 22 Prairial** (stripping defendants of lawyers and mandating only acquittal or death), and provincial ***représentants en mission*** (parliamentary commissars who carried out brutal reprisals like the Nantes ***noyades*** [mass drownings]). To win the war and appease the *Sans-Culottes* demanding death to ***accapareurs*** (grain hoarders), the Jacobins imposed the **Law of the General Maximum** (wartime price and wage ceilings enforced by paramilitary ***armées révolutionnaires*** [grain-requisitioning squads]) and decreed the ***Levée en masse*** (universal national mobilization for total war), through which Lazare Carnot raised 800,000 troops via the ***amalgame*** (fusing revolutionary volunteers with professional line regiments); once victory at Fleurus (June 1794) ended the invasion threat, the propertied bourgeoisie overthrew Robespierre in the **Thermidorian Reaction (9 Thermidor / July 1794)**, unleashed ***Muscadins*** (bourgeois youth gangs) in the anti-Jacobin **White Terror**, abolished the *Maximum*, and installed the corrupt five-man executive **Directory (1795–1799)**.

* **4. The Haitian Revolution: Plantation Capitalism, Racial Caste Rupture, and Universal Emancipation (1791–1804):** France's maritime wealth depended on **Saint-Domingue** (modern Haiti), which produced 40% of the world's sugar and half its coffee under the brutal ***Code Noir*** (1685 royal slave code) and mercantilist ***Exclusif*** (French colonial trade monopoly). When the 1789 Revolution fractured the colony's ruling strata—pitting wealthy white planters (***Grands Blancs***) and poor white overseers (***Petits Blancs***) against free, property-owning mixed-race people (***Gens de couleur libres*** or ***Affranchis***)—the **500,000 enslaved Africans** (two-thirds of whom were African-born ***Bossales*** with Kongo military experience and traditions of ***marronage*** [escaped fugitive slave communities]) launched a massive insurrection at **Bois Caïman** in August 1791. Under the brilliant leadership of **Toussaint Louverture**, the black insurgent army outmaneuvered invading Spanish and British forces, compelling French Jacobin commissioners and the National Convention in Paris on **16 Pluviôse Year II (February 4, 1794)** to abolish slavery across the entire French Empire. When Napoleon subsequently sent 40,000 troops under General Leclerc in 1802 to capture Louverture and re-establish chattel slavery, **Jean-Jacques Dessalines** united the black and *Affranchis* forces to annihilate the French army at the **Battle of Vertières (November 1803)** and proclaim the independent Black republic of **Haiti** on January 1, 1804—only for restored Bourbon King Charles X to send warships in **1825** imposing a punitive **150-million-franc Indemnity** (to compensate former French slaveholders) and predatory French bank loans (**the "Double Debt"**) that bled Haiti's treasury until 1947.

### III. The Napoleonic Imperium: Administrative Rationalization, Continental Hegemony, and Imperial Overstretch (1799–1815)

* **5. The Brumairean State: Authoritarian Modernization and Institutional Consolidation (1799–1804):** Seeking a strong executive to protect their ***biens nationaux*** property titles from both Jacobin workers and royalist counter-revolutionaries, bourgeois conspirators backed **Napoleon Bonaparte** in the **18 Brumaire Coup (November 9, 1799)**, establishing a regime of **plebiscitary Caesarism** (military autocracy legitimized by staged popular referendums) as First Consul and later Emperor (1804). Napoleon constructed a centralized bureaucratic state by appointing departmental **Prefects (*préfets*)** (all-powerful governors answering directly to Paris), stabilizing public finance through restored ***droits réunis*** (indirect consumption taxes on salt, tobacco, and alcohol), the **Bank of France (1800)**, and the silver-backed **Franc de germinal (1803)**, training meritocratic elites in state ***Lycées*** (secondary schools), and healing the Catholic schism via the **Concordat of 1801** (in which the Pope accepted the loss of Church lands while the state paid clerical salaries). His crowning export, the **Napoleonic Code (Civil Code of 1804)**, permanently abolished feudalism, enshrined bourgeois private property, and mandated ***partage*** (equal partible inheritance among heirs), while rolling back social radicalism by reducing married women to legal minors (***incapables***) under patriarchal authority and subordinating workers via the mandatory ***livret ouvrier*** (worker passbook)—forging a consolidated ruling elite of ***notabilités*** ("Notables" blending wealthy bourgeois landowners and surviving nobility).

* **6. Geopolitical Hegemony, the Continental System, and the Collapse of the Grand Empire (1800–1815):** Operating self-contained **Corps d'Armée (*Grande Armée*)** formations that marched dispersed for speed and united on the battlefield, Napoleon crushed Austria, Prussia, and Russia at Austerlitz (1805), Jena (1806), and Friedland (1807), consolidating petty German states (following the 1803 ***Reichsdeputationshauptschluss*** territorial reorganization) into the French-controlled **Confederation of the Rhine (*Rheinbund*)** and causing the **dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire (August 1806)**. However, because Admiral Nelson's victory at **Trafalgar (1805)** secured British naval mastery—and Britain had already thwarted Bonaparte's **1798 Egypt Expedition** and eliminated his Indian Jacobin ally **Tipu Sultan of Mysore (1799)** while turning the Indian Ocean into a British lake—Napoleon resorted to economic warfare via the **Continental System (1806–1807)** (the Berlin and Milan Decrees barring all European ports from British trade, countered by Britain's naval **Orders in Council**). Enforcing a 100% coastal seal dragged Napoleon into fatal two-front overstretch: first, his 1808 dynastic takeover of Spain ignited the **Peninsular War ("Spanish Ulcer")**, where asymmetric Spanish peasant-clerical ***guerrilla*** ("little war") insurgents and Wellington's Anglo-Portuguese army pinned down 300,000 French troops; second, when Tsar Alexander I exited the blockade, Napoleon invaded Russia in June 1812 with 600,000 men, only to be destroyed by the Russian army's **scorched-earth retreat**, the burning of Moscow, and the freezing winter, leading to his defeat by a revitalized Sixth Coalition (bolstered by Prussia's new ***Landwehr*** [citizen-reserve militia]) at **Leipzig (1813)** and his final downfall at **Waterloo (June 18, 1815)**.

### IV. The Vienna Settlement and the Conservative Restoration Architecture (1814–1815)

* **7. The Congress of Vienna: Geopolitical Equilibrium and the Concert of Europe (1814–1815):** Convening from 1814 to 1815, Austria's **Prince Metternich**, Britain's **Viscount Castlereagh**, Russia's **Tsar Alexander I**, Prussia's **Prince Hardenberg**, and France's **Talleyrand** reconstructed Europe around three pillars: the **Principle of Legitimacy** (restoring pre-1789 hereditary dynasties like the French Bourbons under Louis XVIII's moderate constitutional ***Charte*** [1814 royal charter] that preserved the Napoleonic Code), the **Principle of Compensation** (trading territories without nationalist consent so Austria gained Lombardy-Venetia, Russia gained the Congress Kingdom of Poland, Britain kept global naval chokepoints like Malta, Ceylon, Mauritius, and the Cape Colony, and Prussia acquired the coal-and-iron-rich **Rhineland and Westphalia**), and the **Principle of Balance of Power (*Cordon Sanitaire*)** (encircling France with strengthened buffer states including the Kingdom of the United Netherlands, the Prussian Rhineland, neutral Switzerland, enlarged Piedmont-Sardinia, and the 39-state **German Confederation [*Deutscher Bund*]** under Austrian presidency). To maintain peace through periodic multilateral summitry known as the **Concert of Europe (Congress System)**, the powers signed both Alexander I's mystical Christian **Holy Alliance (1815)** (co-opted by Metternich into an autocratic police league against liberalism) and Castlereagh's hard-security **Quadruple Alliance (1815)** (expanded to the **Quintuple Alliance** with France in 1818)—a dual architecture that soon split between Metternich's **Troppau Protocol (1820)** (claiming the right of Eastern autocracies to militarily crush internal liberal revolutions in Naples and Spain) and **Castlereagh's State Paper of May 5, 1820** (asserting British parliamentary non-intervention in other nations' domestic politics and shielding newly independent Latin America behind the Royal Navy).
