> [!abstract]+ PART A: The "Zero-Jargon" Big-Picture Story
> When World War II pulverized the traditional powers of Europe and East Asia in 1945, it left two continental superpowers staring at each other across the rubble with incompatible survival blueprints: an industrial-surplus United States needing open global capitalist markets, and a war-ravaged Soviet Union determined to ring its western border with loyal socialist buffer states. Within a decade, this clash partitioned Europe behind physical fortifications and rival economic circuits, while paradoxically inspiring Western European enemies like France and West Germany to pool their heavy industries into a common market that laid the foundations of the modern European Union.
> 
> Once both superpowers developed hydrogen bombs and ocean-hidden missile submarines, a direct war between Washington and Moscow meant guaranteed mutual suicide. Locked in a nuclear stalemate at the top—which peaked during the terrifying thirteen days of the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis—the two blocs displaced their rivalry into bloody proxy wars, guerrilla insurgencies, and covert coups across Korea, Vietnam, and Latin America. Yet by the late 1960s, a bitter ideological and border split between Communist China and the Soviet Union shattered the illusion of a monolithic socialist bloc. The United States exploited this fracture through triangular diplomacy to usher in a decade of arms-control treaties and European relaxation—until disputes over Third World interventions, new European missiles, and the 1979 Soviet invasion of Afghanistan plunged the world into a fierce "Second Cold War."

## PART B: Section-by-Section Story Threads & Inline Decoder

### I. Geopolitical Architecture of Bipolarity and the Partition of Europe (1945–1961)

* **1. Epistemology of Containment and the Ideological Rupture (1946–1947):** The 1945 destruction of the German **Wehrmacht** (Nazi Germany's unified armed forces) and Imperial Japan erased Eurasia's traditional buffer states, pitting an American economy commanding 50% of global manufacturing—which needed multilateral dollar trade to avoid 1930s-style **autarkic blocs** (self-sufficient, closed economic empires)—against a devastated USSR that lost 27 million people and sought to turn the **Red Army**'s Eastern European occupation zone into a defensive **glacis** (protective buffer zone). In his February 1946 **Long Telegram** (published in 1947 as the **"X Article"**), US diplomat **George Kennan** diagnosed Moscow as driven by Tsarist insecurity and Marxist dogma, proposing **Containment** (blocking Soviet expansion by patiently applying counter-force at shifting geographic points rather than waging direct war). After Winston Churchill warned in March 1946 that an **Iron Curtain** (an ideological and physical barrier dividing Soviet-controlled Eastern Europe from the West) had fallen from Stettin to Trieste, a bankrupted Britain—crushed by $14 billion in wartime **Sterling Balances** (debts owed to colonial creditors like India and Egypt)—notified Washington in February 1947 that it could no longer defend royalist Greece or Turkey. President Harry S. Truman responded with the **Truman Doctrine (March 1947)** (a sweeping US commitment to financially and militarily aid "free peoples" resisting armed Communist minorities or outside pressure), permanently ending American isolationism across the Eurasian **Rimland** (the strategic maritime periphery of Eurasia).
* **2. Competing Economic Blocs: The Marshall Plan versus Cominform and COMECON (1947–1949):** By 1947, bombed-out Western Europe suffered a crippling **Dollar Gap** (an acute shortage of US dollars needed to buy American food, fuel, and machinery), sparking US fears that voters in France and Italy would elect the powerful **French Communist Party (PCF)** and **Italian Communist Party (PCI)**. Washington launched the **Marshall Plan / European Recovery Program (1948–1952)** (a $13.3 billion US grant-and-loan package administered through the **OEEC** [Organization for European Economic Cooperation]), which mandated that recipient states deposit matching local currency into **Counterpart Funds** (jointly supervised accounts used to retire debt and rebuild power grids and railways) while exporting the American **Fordist** model (high-productivity assembly-line mass manufacturing paired with labor-management wage bargains). Recognizing that currency convertibility and open books would pull Eastern Europe into the Western orbit, Joseph Stalin barred his satellites from participating and founded **Cominform (September 1947)** (the Communist Information Bureau enforcing ideological orthodoxy across Eastern European **"People's Democracies"**). Under Andrei Zhdanov's **"Two Camps" Doctrine** (***Zhdanovshchina***—the thesis that the world was irreconcilably split between US-led imperialism and Soviet-led socialism), Moscow engineered the **February 1948 Prague Coup**, expelled **Josip Broz Tito**'s Yugoslavia in June 1948 for pursuing autonomous "national communism," and established the **Molotov Plan** and **COMECON (January 1949)** (the Council for Mutual Economic Assistance, locking Eastern Bloc economies into bilateral, non-convertible ruble barter quotas and **Gosplan** five-year state command planning).
* **3. The German Fulcrum and Institutionalized Military Alliances (1948–1961):** After four-power unification talks collapsed, the Western Allies merged their German occupation zones into **Bizonia (1947)** and **Trizonia (1948)** and introduced the **Deutsche Mark (June 1948)** (a stable new currency to wipe out black-market inflation), prompting Stalin to sever land access to West Berlin in the **Berlin Blockade (1948–1949)**—which the Allies defeated via the 324-day **Berlin Airlift ("Operation Vittles")** delivering 2.32 million tons of supplies by air. This rupture hardened into two rival states in 1949: the **Federal Republic of Germany (FRG / West Germany)** in Bonn under the **Basic Law** (***Grundgesetz***, a democratic constitution) and Chancellor Konrad Adenauer's policy of ***Westbindung*** (anchoring West Germany to the West) and the **Hallstein Doctrine** (refusing diplomatic ties with any state that recognized East Germany) vs. the **German Democratic Republic (GDR / East Germany)** in East Berlin, a Marxist-Leninist state ruled by Walter Ulbricht's **SED** (Socialist Unity Party). To deter 175 Soviet divisions, twelve Western nations signed the **North Atlantic Treaty (April 1949)** creating **NATO** around **Article 5** (the collective defense pledge that an attack on one member is an attack on all); when the 1954 Paris Agreements admitted a rearmed West German **Bundeswehr** (armed forces) into NATO in May 1955, Moscow immediately countered with the **Warsaw Pact (May 1955)** (a unified Soviet-commanded military alliance of Eastern Bloc states). Finally, after roughly 3 million skilled East Germans fled westward through Berlin, Nikita Khrushchev authorized Ulbricht in **August 1961** to erect the **Berlin Wall** (a 155 km concrete barrier encircling West Berlin with a mined ***Todesstreifen*** ["death strip"]), halting the GDR's demographic collapse and freezing European borders until 1989.

### II. Post-War Western European Recovery and Supranational Integration (1946–1973)

* **4. National Reconstruction Models: The French Monnet Plan and the West German Wirtschaftswunder:** Shielded by NATO and Marshall Plan liquidity, Western Europe entered ***Les Trente Glorieuses*** (France's "Thirty Glorious Years" of 4.5%–6% annual GDP growth from 1945 to 1975) via two pioneering mixed-economy models. In France, **Jean Monnet** designed the **Monnet Plan (1946–1952)** around **Indicative Planning** (***Dirigisme***—state-guided investment targets channeling nationalized bank credit and Marshall Plan Counterpart Funds into six bottleneck heavy industries like coal, steel, and rail without Soviet-style coercion). In West Germany, Economics Minister **Ludwig Erhard** drew on Freiburg School **Ordoliberalism** (free-market price competition within a strong state regulatory and welfare framework) to build the **Social Market Economy** (***Soziale Marktwirtschaft***) and ignite the ***Wirtschaftswunder*** ("Economic Miracle," quadrupling 1950s industrial output). West Germany absorbed 12 million ***Heimatvertriebene*** (ethnic German expellees from Eastern Europe), captured global capital-goods export markets during the Korean War, maintained labor peace via ***Mitbestimmung*** (**1951–1952 Co-Determination Laws** granting unions up to 50% seats on coal and steel corporate supervisory boards in exchange for wage restraint), imported ***Gastarbeiter*** ("Guest Workers" from Italy and Turkey) to prevent wage inflation, and benefited from the **1953 London Debt Agreement** (which slashed German external debts by 50%).
* **5. From the Schuman Declaration to the European Coal and Steel Community (1950–1951):** As the US pressed for West German industrial recovery, France feared a revived military-industrial complex in the **Ruhr** (Germany's coal and steel heartland) yet needed Ruhr coking coal for the Monnet Plan. On **May 9, 1950**, French Foreign Minister **Robert Schuman** and **Jean Monnet** issued the **Schuman Declaration**, proposing to place Franco-German coal and steel production under a shared **High Authority** (a supranational executive body independent of national governments) so that war between France and Germany would become *"not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible."* Rejecting grand federalist constitutions in favor of **Neo-Functionalism** (the integration method where uniting one technical economic sector creates cross-border **"spillover"** pressures that compel states to integrate adjacent sectors like transport and customs), the **"Inner Six"**—France, West Germany, Italy, and **Benelux** (Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg)—signed the **Treaty of Paris (1951)** to establish the **European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC)**, abolishing internal coal and steel tariffs and cartels while Britain stood aloof.
* **6. The Treaty of Rome (1957) and the 1973 Enlargement:** After the French Parliament voted down the **European Defence Community (EDC)** (a 1952–1954 plan for a pan-European army) and the 1956 Suez Crisis exposed European vulnerability, the Inner Six signed the **Treaties of Rome (March 1957)**, establishing **EURATOM** (the European Atomic Energy Community for joint civil nuclear power) and the **European Economic Community (EEC Common Market)**. The EEC created a customs union with a **Common External Tariff (CET)** against non-members, enshrined the **"Four Freedoms"** (free movement of goods, services, capital, and labor), and forged the **Common Agricultural Policy (CAP, 1962)** (a Franco-German bargain trading duty-free German industrial exports for subsidized minimum prices for French farmers). French President **Charles de Gaulle** championed intergovernmental ***Europe des Patries*** ("a Europe of sovereign Fatherlands") against Brussels **Supranationalism** (rule by the European Commission and **Qualified Majority Voting [QMV]**), staging the 1965–1966 **"Empty Chair Crisis"** to secure the **Luxembourg Compromise (1966)** (preserving a national veto over vital interests) and twice vetoing British entry (1963, 1967). Only after de Gaulle resigned did the **United Kingdom, Ireland, and Denmark join the EEC in January 1973** (expanding the Six to the Nine after Britain's rival **EFTA** ["Outer Seven" free-trade bloc] lagged behind); Britain's accession terminated the **1932 Ottawa System of Commonwealth Preferences**, compelling **India** to pivot its textile, tea, and jute trade toward continental Europe via the **December 1973 Indo-EEC Commercial Cooperation Agreement** and the **Generalized System of Preferences (GSP)**.

### III. Thermonuclear Deterrence, the Space Race, De-Stalinization, and the Cuban Brink (1949–1962)

* **7. The Thermonuclear Revolution, Sputnik, and the Architecture of MAD:** America's monopoly over **fission weapons** (atomic bombs splitting heavy nuclei) ended in **August 1949** when the USSR tested **RDS-1**, prompting Truman to authorize **NSC-68 (1950)** (a blueprint tripling the US defense budget) and ignite the race for **thermonuclear / fusion weapons** (multi-megaton **hydrogen bombs** fusing hydrogen isotopes, tested by the US in **Ivy Mike [1952]** and the USSR in **RDS-37 [1955]**). While President Dwight D. Eisenhower's **"New Look"** defense policy relied on **Massive Retaliation** (threatening all-out thermonuclear war against localized Communist aggression), Soviet designer Sergei Korolev's launch of **Sputnik 1 (October 4, 1957)** atop an **R-7 Semyorka ICBM** (Intercontinental Ballistic Missile) proved Moscow could strike American cities from space within 30 minutes. By the 1960s, Defense Secretary Robert McNamara shifted the US to **Flexible Response** (calibrating conventional and nuclear options) while both superpowers deployed a strategic **Nuclear Triad**: hardened underground **ICBMs**, strategic bombers, and ocean-hidden **SLBMs** (Submarine-Launched Ballistic Missiles aboard nuclear **SSBN** submarines like the US *Polaris*). This locked in **Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD)**—a game-theory equilibrium where neither side could launch a surprise **counterforce first strike** (targeting enemy missile silos) without suffering a guaranteed **second-strike countervalue retaliation** (surviving submarines obliterating 25%–30% of the attacker's population and 50%–70% of its industry)—which in turn produced the **Stability-Instability Paradox** (freezing direct superpower war at the nuclear apex while emboldening intense Third World proxy wars).
* **8. Khrushchev’s 1956 De-Stalinization Rupture and the Hungarian Intervention:** Following Stalin's death in March 1953, **Nikita Khrushchev** initiated the **"Thaw"**—releasing millions of **GULAG** forced-labor prisoners and reconciling with Tito—before stunning the **20th Party Congress (February 25, 1956)** with his **"Secret Speech" (De-Stalinization)**, which denounced Stalin's cult of personality and 1930s purges while proclaiming **"Peaceful Coexistence"** (the thesis that nuclear-age socialism would defeat capitalism through peaceful economic competition rather than inevitable war). Leaked abroad, the speech fostered **polycentrism** (multiple autonomous centers of Communist authority) and sparked upheaval across Eastern Europe: in the **Polish October (1956)**, rehabilitated leader **Władysław Gomułka** averted Soviet invasion by pledging loyalty to the Warsaw Pact and one-party rule alongside domestic de-collectivization. Conversely, during the **Hungarian Revolution (October–November 1956)**, reformist Prime Minister **Imre Nagy** abolished the one-party state and announced Hungary's unilateral withdrawal from the **Warsaw Pact**. Exploiting Western distraction during the simultaneous Anglo-French-Israeli **Suez Crisis**—and knowing Eisenhower's rhetoric of **"Rollback"** (liberating captive nations) would not risk nuclear war—Khrushchev launched **Operation Whirlwind (November 4, 1956)**, sending 1,100 Soviet tanks into Budapest to crush the uprising, execute Nagy, and install **János Kádár**'s regime of **"Goulash Communism"** (strict Warsaw Pact loyalty paired with gradual consumer economic reforms).
* **9. The Cuban Missile Crisis (October 1962): Strategic Asymmetry and Brinkmanship:** In May 1962, facing a 17:1 American lead in strategic nuclear warheads, US **Jupiter IRBMs** (Intermediate-Range Ballistic Missiles) in Turkey, and US plots to overthrow Fidel Castro, Khrushchev launched **Operation Anadyr**—secretly shipping 42,000 Soviet troops, nuclear-armed **R-12 MRBMs** (Medium-Range Ballistic Missiles), **R-14 IRBMs**, and 100 tactical nukes to Cuba. When a US U-2 spy plane photographed the sites on October 14, President **John F. Kennedy** convened **EXCOMM** (the Executive Committee of the National Security Council), rejected the Joint Chiefs' call for an immediate air strike and invasion, and imposed a naval **"Quarantine"** (a selective blockade avoiding a formal act of war) as US Strategic Air Command moved to **DEFCON 2** (one step below nuclear war). On **"Black Saturday" (October 27, 1962)**—after a Soviet missile downed U-2 pilot Major Rudolf Anderson over Cuba and Soviet submarine officer **Vasili Arkhipov** vetoed firing a nuclear torpedo from depth-charged sub *B-59*—JFK executed the **"Trollope Ploy"** (publicly accepting Khrushchev's softer October 26 letter while ignoring a harsher October 27 demand): Moscow removed the missiles in exchange for a public **US non-invasion pledge of Cuba** and a secret backchannel guarantee (delivered by Robert F. Kennedy to Ambassador Anatoly Dobrynin) to dismantle the **US Jupiter missiles in Turkey**. Across the exact same October days, **Mao Zedong** exploited superpower distraction to launch the **1962 Sino-Indian Border War**, where Khrushchev briefly paused **MiG-21** deliveries to India to keep Beijing's backing before swinging back once the Cuban crisis ended, catalyzing the **1963 Moscow–Washington Hotline**.

### IV. Asymmetrical Proxy Warfare across East Asia, Southeast Asia, and Latin America (1950–1985)

* **10. The Korean War (1950–1953): Militarization of Containment along the 38th Parallel:** Partitioned along the **38th Parallel** in 1945 into **Kim Il-sung**'s Communist **DPRK (North Korea)** and **Syngman Rhee**'s anti-communist **ROK (South Korea)**, Korea erupted after US Secretary of State Dean Acheson's **January 1950 "Defensive Perimeter" Speech** omitted South Korea from America's Pacific defense line, convincing Stalin (now armed with the atom bomb and allied with Mao's China) to approve Kim's invasion on **June 25, 1950**. After North Korean (**KPA**) tanks trapped UN forces in the southeastern **Pusan Perimeter**, General **Douglas MacArthur** turned the tide with his amphibious **Inchon Landing (September 15, 1950)**; however, Truman and MacArthur then shifted from **Containment** to **Rollback** (crossing the 38th Parallel to unify Korea), ignoring Premier Zhou Enlai's warnings—relayed via **Indian Ambassador K.M. Panikkar**—and triggering the **Chinese People's Volunteer Army (PVA)** intervention (300,000 troops under General **Peng Dehuai** crossing the Yalu River in October–November 1950). To keep the conflict a **Limited War** rather than a nuclear war with China, Truman fired MacArthur in April 1951. The war ground into trench attrition until the **Panmunjom Armistice (July 27, 1953)** established the **Demilitarized Zone (DMZ)** after an Indian resolution created the **Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission (NNRC)**—chaired by India's General K.S. Thimayya with a 6,000-strong Indian Custodian Force—to resolve the deadlock over **voluntary POW repatriation**.
* **11. The Vietnam War (1955–1975): Revolutionary Guerrilla Attrition against Industrial Firepower:** After the **1954 Geneva Accords** temporarily divided Vietnam at the **17th Parallel** pending 1956 reunification elections supervised by an India-chaired International Control Commission, the US—operating under Eisenhower's **Domino Theory** (the belief that if Vietnam fell to **Ho Chi Minh**, all of Southeast Asia would topple to Communism)—backed South Vietnamese President **Ngo Dinh Diem** in cancelling the elections. Southern insurgents formed the **Viet Cong / National Liberation Front (NLF, 1960)**, supplied by North Vietnam's **PAVN** (People's Army of Vietnam) down the **Ho Chi Minh Trail** (a 16,000 km jungle logistics grid through Laos and Cambodia). Following Diem's 1963 assassination, President Lyndon B. Johnson used a disputed naval clash to pass the **Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (August 1964)** (a congressional blank check for war), deploying **543,400 US troops** by 1968 under General William Westmoreland's **attrition ("Body Count")** strategy while dropping 7.5 million tons of bombs (**Operation Rolling Thunder**) and **Agent Orange** (toxic defoliants). Although the surprise **January 1968 Tet Offensive** cost the Viet Cong 40,000 dead, televised urban combat opened a domestic **"Credibility Gap"** that forced Johnson from office; Richard Nixon and Henry Kissinger then pursued **Vietnamization** (withdrawing US troops while expanding South Vietnam's **ARVN** army and bombing Cambodia and Laos), signed the **January 1973 Paris Peace Accords**, and watched North Vietnamese tanks complete the **Fall of Saigon (April 30, 1975)**, leaving America with the **"Vietnam Syndrome"** (deep public aversion to overseas military interventions).
* **12. The Latin American Crucible: Hegemonic Intervention, Insurgency, and Counter-Revolution (1954–1985):** Treating Latin America through the lens of the **1947 Rio Treaty** and **1948 OAS**, Washington equated economic nationalism with Soviet subversion. In the **1954 Guatemala Coup (Operation PBSuccess)**, the CIA (directed by Allen Dulles alongside Secretary of State John Foster Dulles, both tied to the **United Fruit Company [UFC]**) used psychological warfare and a small exile force under Carlos Castillo Armas to topple elected President **Jacobo Árbenz** after his **Decree 900** land reform expropriated uncultivated UFC estates—convincing eyewitness **Ernesto "Che" Guevara** of the need for armed guerrilla revolution. In the **1959 Cuban Revolution**, **Fidel Castro** and Guevara applied **Foco Theory** (***Foquismo***—the doctrine that a small rural guerrilla vanguard can actively spark revolution without waiting for urban industrial conditions) to topple dictator Fulgencio Batista; after Castro nationalized $1 billion in US sugar and oil assets and crushed the CIA-backed **Bay of Pigs Invasion (April 1961)** by **Brigade 2506** at Playa Girón, he declared Cuba a Marxist-Leninist state. When Marxist **Salvador Allende** (*Unidad Popular* coalition) won Chile's presidency in 1970 and nationalized US copper mines, Nixon ordered a CIA **"Invisible Blockade"** (credit cutoffs and strike funding) culminating in General **Augusto Pinochet**'s **September 11, 1973 Chilean Coup**, where Pinochet empowered the **"Chicago Boys"** (Milton Friedman-trained economists pioneering **neoliberal shock therapy** via privatization and deregulation) and founded **Operation Condor (1975)** (a trans-border South American military junta intelligence network that kidnapped and assassinated dissidents worldwide). Finally, after Nicaragua's left-wing **Sandinistas (FSLN)** under Daniel Ortega overthrew the Somoza dynasty in 1979, Ronald Reagan applied the **Reagan Doctrine** (arming anti-communist insurgents globally) by funding 15,000 **Nicaraguan Contras**—and when Congress barred funding via the **Boland Amendments**, White House aides secretly diverted proceeds from illicit Iran arms sales to the Contras in the **1986 Iran-Contra Affair**.

### V. The Sino-Soviet Split, Triangular Diplomacy, Détente, and the Second Cold War (1960–1985)

* **13. The Sino-Soviet Split (1960–1969) and the Brezhnev Doctrine:** Beneath the 1950 Sino-Soviet alliance, **Mao Zedong** resented Tsarist border annexations and denounced Khrushchev's 1956 de-Stalinization and Peaceful Coexistence as **"Revisionism"** (betraying violent Marxist world revolution to appease US imperialism), while Khrushchev was alarmed by Mao's 1958 Great Leap Forward and shelling of Taiwan's offshore islands, leading Moscow to cancel its prototype atom bomb pledge (1959) and abruptly withdraw all **1,390 Soviet technical experts from China in 1960** (**The Sino-Soviet Split**). When Czechoslovak reformer **Alexander Dubček** launched the **1968 Prague Spring** to build **"Socialism with a Human Face"** (abolishing censorship and opening political pluralism), Soviet leader **Leonid Brezhnev** sent 500,000 Warsaw Pact troops to crush Prague in August 1968 and proclaimed the **Brezhnev Doctrine** (the **Doctrine of Limited Sovereignty**, asserting the right of the socialist bloc to militarily intervene whenever any socialist state tilted toward pluralism or capitalism). Branding Moscow a **"Social-Imperialist"** regime (socialist in words, imperialist in deeds) that might invade China next, Mao clashed militarily with Soviet forces in the **March–August 1969 Ussuri River Border War** at **Zhenbao (Damansky) Island**, prompting Soviet hints at a preventive strike on China's Lop Nor nuclear site.
* **14. Triangular Diplomacy, Ostpolitik, and the Architecture of Détente (1963–1975):** The 1969 Ussuri crisis enabled **Richard Nixon** and **Henry Kissinger**—confronted with Vietnam overstretch and Soviet nuclear parity—to play the **"China Card"** via **Triangular Diplomacy** (moving closer to both Beijing and Moscow than either Communist giant was to the other) and inaugurate **Détente (1969–1979)** (a structural relaxation of superpower tensions). Brokered secretly through Pakistan's Yahya Khan in July 1971, Nixon's February 1972 Beijing summit with Mao and **Zhou Enlai** produced the **1972 Shanghai Communiqué**, codifying the **"One China" formula** (acknowledging Taiwan as part of China) and a joint **Anti-Hegemony Clause** aimed against the USSR—while directly prompting **Indira Gandhi**'s India to neutralize the US-Pakistan-China axis by signing the **August 9, 1971 Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation**, which deterred US **Task Force 74** and China during the December 1971 liberation of **Bangladesh**. In Europe, West German Chancellor **Willy Brandt** pursued ***Ostpolitik*** ("Eastern Policy") via ***Wandel durch Annäherung*** ("Change through Rapprochement"), signing the **1970 Moscow and Warsaw Treaties** (recognizing Poland's post-1945 **Oder-Neisse border**) and the **1972 Basic Treaty** (***Grundlagenvertrag***, establishing *de facto* mutual recognition between West and East Germany). Simultaneously, the superpowers built an arms-control regime: the **1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty (PTBT)** (banning atmospheric, space, and underwater nuclear tests), the **1968 Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT)** (freezing recognized nuclear states at five—rejected by **India** as **"Nuclear Apartheid"** before its **May 1974 Pokhran-I** test prompted Western creation of the **Nuclear Suppliers Group [NSG]**), and the **May 1972 SALT I & ABM Treaty** (limiting **Anti-Ballistic Missile** defense shields to two sites to legally lock in MAD mutual vulnerability while freezing launcher totals, though leaving open the **MIRV** [Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicle] multiple-warhead loophole). Détente peaked at the 35-nation **1975 Helsinki Accords (CSCE)**, which traded **Basket I** (inviolability of post-1945 European borders) and **Basket II** (economic/tech trade) for **Basket III** (human rights pledges that legitimized internal Eastern Bloc dissident movements like the **Moscow Helsinki Group** and Václav Havel's **Charter 77**).
* **15. The Unraveling and 1979 Collapse of Détente:** Détente unraveled over incompatible definitions: while Kissinger insisted on **"Linkage"** (tying trade and arms control to Soviet geopolitical restraint in the Third World), Moscow believed the global **"Correlation of Forces"** allowed it to airlift thousands of Cuban troops and Soviet weapons to secure Marxist victories in **Angola (1975–1976)** and Ethiopia's **Ogaden War (1977–1978)**. In Europe, Soviet deployment of mobile, triple-warhead **SS-20 IRBMs** (1977) threatened to "decouple" the US nuclear umbrella from Western Europe, prompting NATO's **December 1979 Dual-Track Decision** (offering arms talks while committing to deploy fast-striking US **Pershing II** ballistic missiles and **Ground-Launched Cruise Missiles [GLCMs]** in 1983). Finally, when 85,000 Soviet troops executed the **December 1979 Invasion of Afghanistan** under the Brezhnev Doctrine to prop up Kabul's Communist regime against the **Mujahideen** insurgency, **Détente collapsed** into the **"Second Cold War" (1979–1985)**: President **Jimmy Carter** withdrew the **June 1979 SALT II Treaty** from the Senate, boycotted the 1980 Moscow Olympics, funded Afghan insurgents, and proclaimed the **Carter Doctrine (January 1980)** (pledging military force to defend the Persian Gulf), before **Ronald Reagan** escalated pressure with a $1.5 trillion military buildup and the **1983 Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)** space-based missile shield program.
