> [!abstract]+ PART A: The "Zero-Jargon" Big-Picture Story
> Every sovereign state faces a fundamental balancing act: how do you raise enough money to build world-class highways, power grids, and factories without taxing citizens into the underground shadow economy or drowning the country in inflationary debt? For decades, India's public finances suffered from three chronic leaks: borrowing household savings just to pay daily government bills like salaries and subsidies, enforcing a labyrinth of 1960s income-tax laws and multi-slab border duties that penalized domestic manufacturers, and hiding debt off the official budget books.
> 
> This chapter tracks how India re-engineered its entire fiscal engine between 2020 and 2027 to close those leaks. First, the government pivoted from low-return consumption handouts toward high-multiplier infrastructure building, backed by a completely rewritten **Income-Tax Act, 2025** and a simplified **Two-Tier Next-Gen GST (5% and 18%)** that reward formalization and domestic manufacturing. Finally, having brought its post-pandemic annual borrowing down to 4.3% of GDP by 2026–27, India shifted its long-term fiscal anchor to shrinking total Central Government debt to **50% of GDP by 2031**—funding new infrastructure without fresh debt by leasing out existing roads, power lines, and public-sector office parks through innovative asset-recycling trusts.

## PART B: Section-by-Section Story Threads & Inline Decoder

### I. Constitutional Fiscal Architecture, Budgetary Accounting, and the Deficit Multiplier Matrix

* **1. The Constitutional Budgetary Tri-Fund Architecture and Revenue vs. Capital Accounting:** Under **Article 112**, the Union Budget is presented as the **Annual Financial Statement (AFS)** (estimated receipts and spending from April 1 to March 31) across three accounts: the **Consolidated Fund of India [Art. 266(1)]** (main sovereign account for all revenues and loans, requiring a parliamentary **Finance Bill [Art. 110]** to tax under **Art. 265** and an **Appropriation Bill [Art. 114]** to spend), the **Contingency Fund [Art. 267]** (₹30,000 crore presidential emergency imprest), and the **Public Account [Art. 266(2)]** (provident and small savings funds where the state acts merely as a fiduciary banker). Inside the Consolidated Fund, transactions are split into the **Revenue Account** (current income and consumption flows that do *not* alter the state's asset-liability balance sheet) vs. the **Capital Account** (flows that directly alter sovereign physical or financial assets and liabilities). On the Revenue side, **Revenue Receipts (RR)** combine **Net Tax Revenue** (**Gross Tax Revenue [GTR]** minus the 41% **Article 270** Finance Commission devolution to States) and **Non-Tax Revenue (NTR)** (spectrum fees, PSU dividends, and RBI surplus transfers under the **Bimal Jalan Committee Economic Capital Framework**), whereas **Revenue Expenditure (RE)** covers non-asset-creating operational costs dominated by rigid **Committed Expenditure** (interest payments eating ~37% of net revenue, pensions, and salaries), major subsidies, and grants. On the Capital side, **Capital Receipts (CR)** split into **Non-Debt Capital Receipts (NDCR)** (loan recoveries and **DIPAM** disinvestment proceeds that liquidate existing assets without adding debt) vs. **Debt Capital Receipts** (borrowing via **Dated G-Secs**, **Treasury Bills**, and the **National Small Savings Fund [NSSF]** that finances the Fiscal Deficit), while **Capital Expenditure (CE)** builds durable physical/financial assets—Anchored by ₹1.5 lakh crore in **50-year interest-free Capex loans to States**.

* **2. Expenditure Quality and the Fiscal Multiplier Divergence (Capex vs. Revex):** The **Fiscal Multiplier (k = ΔY / ΔG)** (how many rupees of national income ΔY are generated by one rupee of government spending ΔG) reveals a sharp divergence between consumption and investment outlays. The **Revenue Expenditure Multiplier (k_RE ≈ 0.45–0.98)** (cash transfers and subsidies) gives a brief consumption bump near 0.98 in quarter one, but because it adds zero supply capacity, it triggers **Demand-Pull Inflation** (too much money chasing limited goods), import leakages, and **Crowding-Out** (absorbing financial savings away from private firms), decaying to ~0.45 over four years. Conversely, the **Capital Expenditure Multiplier (k_CE ≈ 2.45–3.25)** (highways, rail freight corridors, ports) stimulates steel and cement demand immediately while cutting economy-wide logistics bottlenecks, which raises the **Marginal Product of Private Capital (MPK_p)** (profitability of private machines/factories) and triggers **Crowding-In** (pulling in private corporate investment)—driving the Union's pivot to ₹12.2 lakh crore in Capex for FY 2026–27.

* **3. The Taxonomy of Budget Deficits and Intertemporal Fiscal Signaling:** To track whether borrowing violates the **Golden Rule of Public Finance** (governments should borrow strictly to fund new investments, never daily consumption), economists monitor five nested deficits. The **Revenue Deficit (RD = RE − RR)** (government dissaving where borrowed money pays for daily consumption; 1.5% of GDP in FY27) is refined by the Rangarajan Committee's **Effective Revenue Deficit (ERD = RD − Grants for Creation of Capital Assets)** (strips out Union grants used by States to build physical assets like rural roads/houses, isolating pure consumption dissaving at ~0.3%–0.5% of GDP). The Chakravarty Committee's **Gross Fiscal Deficit (FD = Total Expenditure − [RR + NDCR])** (the government's total net borrowing requirement from all sources; 4.3% of GDP in FY27 BE) is stripped of legacy debt-servicing costs to yield the **Primary Deficit (PD = FD − Interest Payments)** (gauges the current government's discretionary fiscal discipline; ~0.8% of GDP), while the **Monetized Deficit** (portion of FD funded by RBI printing high-powered **Reserve Money [M₀]**, which is barred in primary auctions under **FRBM Sec. 5** after 1997 abolished automatic **ad-hoc T-Bills**, occurring today only via secondary-market **Open Market Operations [OMOs]** or temporary cash-flow bridges).

### II. Direct Taxation Overhaul: The Income-Tax Act 2025, Corporate Tax Architecture, and Capital Market Levies

* **4. The Income-Tax Act, 2025: Statutory Simplification and the Unified "Tax Year" Paradigm:** Effective April 1, 2026, the **Income-Tax Act, 2025** replaces the amendment-clogged 1961 Act by compressing 819 sections into **536 sections** across 23 Chapters and 16 Schedules, slashing word count by half and replacing dense *"Provided that"* narrative exceptions with clean mathematical formulas and tabular schedules. Crucially, **Section 3** abolishes the confusing split between the **Previous Year (PY)** (year income was earned) vs. **Assessment Year (AY)** (following year when income was taxed), establishing a single unified **Tax Year (TY)** (the April 1–March 31 financial year used simultaneously for earning, TDS, and return references). To combat cloud-era tax evasion, the Act grants search-and-seizure jurisdiction over the **Virtual Digital Space** (remote cloud servers, email systems, and encrypted crypto/trading wallets), expands the **Dispute Resolution Panel (DRP)**, and preserves legacy loss/credit carry-forwards under **Section 536** transitional saving clauses.

* **5. Personal Income Taxation: The Default New Regime vs. Old Regime and Household Savings Dynamics:** Personal taxation balances the **Old Tax Regime** (high slab rates paired with 70+ tax-expenditure shelters like **Sec. 80C/80D/HRA** that forced middle-class contractual savings but narrowed the tax base) against the **Default New Personal Income Tax Regime [Section 202 of the 2025 Act / erstwhile Sec. 115BAC]** (lower rates with pruned exemptions to boost disposable consumption). The New Regime features seven uniform ₹4-lakh brackets (0% up to ₹4L, 5% up to ₹8L, 10% up to ₹12L, 15% up to ₹16L, 20% up to ₹20L, 25% up to ₹24L, and 30% above ₹24L) and caps the highest surcharge at **25%** (39% peak effective rate vs. 42.74% in the Old Regime). Under **Section 156 [erstwhile Sec. 87A]**, resident individuals earning up to ₹12 lakh receive a 100% tax rebate up to **₹60,000**—meaning salaried taxpayers combining this with the **₹75,000 Standard Deduction** pay **zero income tax up to ₹12.75 lakh**, buffered by **Marginal Relief** (ensuring extra tax never exceeds extra income earned just above ₹12 lakh).

* **6. Corporate Taxation, the Budget 2026–27 MAT Final-Tax Overhaul, and OECD Pillar Two (15% Global Minimum Tax):** To rival ASEAN investment hubs, India offers domestic firms a concessional **22% base corporate tax rate** (25.17% effective, provided they surrender exemptions), allowed the 15% new manufacturing rate to sunset in March 2024 in favor of targeted **Production Linked Incentive (PLI)** grants, and trimmed foreign corporate tax from 40% to **35%**. To push holdout companies out of the old 30% exemption regime, **Union Budget 2026–27** overhauled **Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT)** (a floor tax on accounting **Book Profits** designed to stop "Zero-Tax Companies"): effective April 1, 2026, the MAT rate drops from 15% to **14%**, but MAT becomes a **final tax in the old regime with zero new MAT Credit carry-forward**, and accumulated pre-2026 MAT credit can only be set off (capped at 25% of annual tax liability) if a domestic firm shifts into the new 22% regime. Internationally, under **OECD/G20 BEPS Pillar Two (GloBE Rules)**, multinational enterprises (MNEs with global revenue ≥ €750M) must pay a **15% Minimum Effective Tax Rate (ETR)** on profits exceeding a **Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE)** (carve-out for real local payroll and physical assets); if local tax holidays push an MNE's Indian ETR below 15%, India must collect the difference via a **Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (QDMTT)**, or else the MNE's foreign home treasury will seize that revenue via the **Income Inclusion Rule (IIR)** or **Undertaxed Profits Rule (UTPR)**.

* **7. Capital Gains Rationalization, Share Buyback Reclassification, STT Derivative Curbs, and Digital/Foreign Tax Regimes:** Capital gains now follow two holding thresholds (**12 months** for all listed securities vs. **24 months** for unlisted shares, gold, and property) with a unified **12.5% Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)** rate without indexation (above a ₹1.25L annual equity exemption) and **20% Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)** on listed equities. In **Union Budget 2026–27**, **Share Buybacks** were reclassified from deemed dividends back to **Capital Gains** (restoring acquisition-cost deductions for retail investors at 12.5% LTCG / 20% STCG, while charging **Promoters** an extra levy for an effective **22% corporate / ~30–33% non-corporate** rate to block dividend tax arbitrage), alongside a sharp Pigouvian hike in **Securities Transaction Tax (STT)** on **Futures & Options (F&O)** (Futures sale up from 0.02% to **0.05%**; Options sale up from 0.10% to **0.15%**) to curb retail derivative losses. Simultaneously, the state abolished **Angel Tax** (erstwhile Sec. 56(2)(viib) levy on startup share premiums above **Fair Market Value [FMV]**) and fully repealed the **Equalization Levy ("Google Tax")** (ending the 2% e-commerce levy in Aug 2024 and 6% ad levy in April 2025 to transition to **Significant Economic Presence [SEP]** rules), while maintaining the punitive **30% flat tax + 1% TDS on Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs)** (cryptos/NFTs, with zero expense deductions or loss set-offs) and launching **FAST-DS 2026** (a one-time 60% tax-plus-penalty compliance window granting immunity under the **Black Money Act, 2015** for returning NRIs and tech ESOP holders with undisclosed foreign assets ≤ ₹1 crore).

* **8. Macroeconomic Tax Diagnostics: Tax Buoyancy, Tax Elasticity, and the Laffer Curve:** Analysts separate **Tax Buoyancy (B = %ΔActual Tax Revenue / %ΔNominal GDP)** (total revenue responsiveness including discretionary rate hikes and digital audit drives) from **Tax Elasticity (E = %ΔOrganic Tax Revenue / %ΔNominal GDP)** (pure automatic responsiveness holding rates and slabs constant via the **Prest Method**, where progressive slabs create **Fiscal Drag / Bracket Creep** that acts as an **Automatic Macroeconomic Stabilizer**). Both operate within the **Laffer Curve** (an inverted-U relationship between tax rates *t* and revenue *T*): raising rates beyond the revenue-maximizing peak **t*** (where tax-base elasticity **ε_B,t = −1**) pushes the economy into the **Prohibitive Zone**, where evasion and capital flight shrink total collections—proving why rate cuts (like the 22% corporate tax and Next-Gen GST) expand formal compliance.

### III. Indirect Taxation: Customs Tariffs, Inverted Duty Pathologies, and the Next-Gen GST Ecosystem

* **9. Customs Tariff Architecture, Effective Rate of Protection (ERP), and Inverted Duty Structure Corrections:** Imports face a stacked border cascade: **Basic Customs Duty (BCD)**, a 10% **Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS)** on BCD, the **Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC)**, trade-remedy levies (**Anti-Dumping Duty [ADD]**, **Countervailing Duty [CVD]**, and **Safeguard Duties**), and finally **Integrated GST (IGST)** on the total landed value. Tariff design hinges on the **Nominal Rate of Protection (NRP)** (headline tariff on the final good) vs. the Corden-Balassa **Effective Rate of Protection (ERP)** (net protection given to domestic value addition after accounting for input tariffs). When raw materials face higher tariffs than finished imports—an **Inverted Duty Structure (IDS)** ($t_{\text{input}} > t_{\text{final}}$)—the ERP turns negative, penalizing domestic factories and encouraging finished imports; consequently, Budgets 2025–27 pruned seven tariff slabs and exempted 25+ critical minerals, battery, solar, and semiconductor inputs from BCD, while enforcing **CAROTAR Rules, 2020 [Sec. 28DA]** (strict origin value-add verification to block third-country dumping via FTA partners).

* **10. Goods and Services Tax (GST) Mechanics: Destination-Based VAT, the ITC Chain, and Digital Enforcement:** Enacted via the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**, GST replaced cascading origin-based taxes (**"Tax on Tax"**) with a destination-based value-added tax governed by **Article 246A** (concurrent intra-state **CGST + SGST**), **Article 269A** (inter-state **IGST** settled via clearinghouse to the destination state), and **Article 279A** (the **GST Council**, where the Centre holds 1/3 vote weight, States hold 2/3, and decisions require a 75% majority). Under the **Input Tax Credit (ITC) Hierarchy [Sec. 49 / Rule 88A]**, businesses must exhaust **IGST credit first** (against IGST, then CGST/SGST in any proportion), after which CGST and SGST credits are used—with a strict firewall that **CGST and SGST credits can never offset each other**. To crush fake-invoice syndicates, the **GSTN** enforces a three-layer digital grid: FASTag-linked **e-Way Bills** (for consignments > ₹50,000), real-time B2B **e-Invoicing** (mandatory for turnover ≥ ₹5 crore, generating a cryptographic **Invoice Reference Number [IRN]** on the **IRP**), and automated **GSTR-1 to GSTR-2B** matching under **Section 16(2)(aa)** (barring buyers from claiming ITC unless their supplier reports the invoice and remits tax in **GSTR-3B**).

* **11. Next-Gen GST Rate Rationalization (2025–26) and the Operationalization of GST Appellate Tribunals (GSTAT):** To fix rate erosion below the Subramanian Committee's ~15.5% **Revenue Neutral Rate (RNR)** and cure domestic inverted duties before the March 2026 sunset of the **GST Compensation Cess**, the 56th GST Council enacted the **Next-Gen GST Rate Rationalization (effective September 22, 2025)**, collapsing the old four-slab matrix (5%, 12%, 18%, 28%) into a **Two-Tier Core Structure**: a **5% Merit Rate** (essentials, medicines, farm and solar equipment) and an **18% Standard Rate** (most goods, services, consumer durables, and cement, cut from 28%), alongside insurance premium exemptions and a **40% Demerit Rate** (tobacco, pan masala, sugary drinks, and luxury autos/yachts, absorbing the old 28% + Cess). Furthermore, **Union Budget 2026–27** omitted **Section 13(8)(b) of the IGST Act** for **Intermediary Services** (shifting place of supply from supplier location to recipient location so Indian export intermediaries enjoy zero-rated export status), while the government operationalized the **GST Appellate Tribunals (GSTAT)** under the 2025 Rules—splitting adjudication between the **Principal Bench in New Delhi** (which holds exclusive jurisdiction under **Sec. 109(5)** over inter-state **Place of Supply** disputes with direct appeal to the Supreme Court) and **31 State Benches** (hearing regular valuation and ITC disputes).

### IV. Public Debt Sustainability, Post-FY26 FRBM Consolidation, Budgeting Innovations, and Public Asset Recycling

* **12. The FRBM Act, N.K. Singh Committee, Off-Budget Borrowings, and the Post-FY26 Debt-to-GDP Anchor (50 ± 1% by 2031):** The **FRBM Act, 2003** and the **N.K. Singh Committee (2017)** established a 60% General Government Debt ceiling (40% Centre + 20% States) paired with a statutory **Escape Clause [Sec. 4(2)]** (permitting a 0.5% of GDP deficit deviation only for war, national calamity, farm collapse, or structural reforms) vs. a symmetrical **Buoyancy Clause**. After cleaning up **Off-Budget Borrowings / Extra-Budgetary Resources (EBRs)** in FY21–FY22 (bringing FCI **NSSF** loans and CPSE government-serviced bonds directly onto the budget) and meeting the post-pandemic glide path by bringing the Fiscal Deficit down to **4.4% in FY26 RE** and **4.3% in FY27 BE**, the Union transitioned for **FY 2026–27 to FY 2030–31** from rigid annual deficit limits to a formal **Debt-to-GDP Anchor targeting Central Debt of 50 ± 1% of GDP by March 31, 2031**. Under the **Domar-Blanchard Debt Sustainability Identity [Δd_t = ((r − g)/(1 + g))d_{t-1} + pd_t]**, because India's nominal GDP growth (**g ≈ 10%**) exceeds the effective interest rate on its predominantly domestic, fixed-rate debt (**r ≈ 7%**), this negative interest-growth differential (**r − g < 0**, the favorable **Snowball Effect**) naturally erodes the debt ratio by ~1.5% a year even while running a ~0.8% Primary Deficit (**pd_t**)—absorbing the mechanical denominator shift from **56.1% to ~58.1%** caused by MoSPI's February 2026 **FY 2022–23 Base-Year GDP revision**.

* **13. Institutional Budgeting Reforms, Ways and Means Advances (WMA), and Sovereign Green Bonds:** Expenditure quality is policed through **Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)** (requiring every scheme to justify its entire allocation from a zero baseline annually, aided by **DMEO** sunset evaluations), **Outcome Budgeting (OOMF)** (shifting tracking from financial **Outlays** and physical **Outputs** to measurable developmental **Outcomes**), and **Gender Budgeting [Statement 13]** (~8.8% of total Union spending, disaggregated into **Part A [100% women]**, **Part B [30%–99% women]**, and **Part C [<30% women]**). For short-term cash management—*not* deficit financing—**Ways and Means Advances (WMA) [Sec. 17(5) of the RBI Act]** bridge intra-year tax-and-spending timing mismatches across three tiers: collateralized **Special Drawing Facility (SDF)** (at Repo − 1%), **Normal WMA** (at Repo Rate), and **Overdraft (OD)** (at Repo + 2%, strictly capped at 10 consecutive working days for the Centre). Meanwhile, **Sovereign Green Bonds (SGrBs)** (dated G-Secs eligible for bank **SLR** and unrestricted foreign investment under the **Fully Accessible Route [FAR]**) ring-fence proceeds inside a Consolidated Fund Green Account overseen by the **GFWC** to fund clean public Capex while capturing an ESG **Greenium** (lower yield accepted by green institutional investors).

* **14. Public Sector Capital Recycling: New PSE Policy (2021), DIPAM, NMP 2.0, and Dedicated CPSE REITs (Budget 2026–27):** Under the **New PSE Policy, 2021**, the state retains only a bare-minimum CPSE footprint across **Four Strategic Sectors** (defence/space/atomic, transport/telecom, power/minerals, and banking/finance) while exiting all non-strategic sectors, guided by **DIPAM's** shift toward holistic value creation (consistent dividends, buybacks, and strategic privatization like Air India/NINL over distressed minority stake dumping). To fund new **Greenfield Capex** without borrowing, **NITI Aayog's National Monetisation Pipeline 2.0 (NMP 2.0 [FY 2026–2030])** targets **₹10 lakh crore** (out of ₹16.72 lakh crore potential across 12 ministries) by leasing out revenue and operation rights of de-risked **Brownfield** assets via **Toll-Operate-Transfer (TOT)** models and **Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs)** with **zero transfer of underlying sovereign land/asset ownership**. Completing this recycling loop, **Union Budget 2026–27** launched **Dedicated CPSE Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)**—pooling dormant commercial buildings and urban land across PSUs into SEBI-regulated trusts that pay out ≥ 90% of cash flows to institutional and retail investors while unlocking upfront non-debt capital for CPSE reinvestment.
