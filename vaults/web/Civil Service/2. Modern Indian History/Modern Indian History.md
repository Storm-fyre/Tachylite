# 🧭 Subject Hub: Modern Indian History `#subject-hub`

> [!abstract] Hub Design Principle
> **Political Economy + Counter-Colonial Culture + Clean Post-1947 Boundaries:** Traces the structural arc from Mughal fragmentation to contemporary India (1707–Present) with colonial/modern art and architecture embedded chronologically. Pre-1947 chapters carry full Prelims + Mains firepower; Post-Independence India (Chs 16–18) focuses strictly on GS1's political-institutional consolidation while bridging economic/constitutional minutiae cleanly to `Economy`, `Agrarian`, `Polity`, and `Governance`.

### Chapter 1: 18th-Century Fragmentation, Regional Polities & European Mercantilist Penetration (1707–1765)
> **Depth Rubric:** Dual-Core Causal + Prelims Precision
> **Gated Obsidian Elements:** `Three Tiers of Regional States Table` · `Carnatic Wars & Plassey–Buxar Timeline` · `Prelims Traps` · `Atlas Hook`

- **Post-Aurangzeb Mughal decline & historiographical debate:** wars of succession, Irani/Turani/Afghani/Hindustani factions (Sayyid Brothers), Nadir Shah (Karnal 1739), Ahmad Shah Abdali; "Dark Age" decline vs. Revisionist regional-dynamism debate
- **Three tiers of regional states:** Successor States (Bengal: Murshid Quli, Alivardi, Jagat Seths; Awadh: Saadat Khan, Safdar Jung; Hyderabad: Asaf Jah), Warrior/Rebel States (Marathas, Sikhs under Banda Bahadur & 12 *Misls*, Jats under Suraj Mal, Rohillas/Bangash), and Modernizing Southern Polities (Mysore: Hyder Ali & Tipu Sultan’s commercial monopolies, French-trained infantry, iron-cased rockets; Travancore: Marthanda Varma’s victory over the Dutch VOC at **Battle of Colachel, 1741**)
- **European mercantilist rivalry:** Portuguese *Estado da Índia* (*Cartaz–Armada–Cafila*), Dutch VOC, Danish, French *Compagnie* (Dupleix), and English EIC (1600 Charter, Roe, 1717 Farrukhsiyar *Farman* & *Dastak* abuse)
- **Military-fiscal breakthrough:** Three Carnatic Wars (1746–63: St. Thome, Arcot, Wandiwash 1760), Battles of **Plassey (1757)** and **Buxar (1764)**, and Clive’s **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** securing Bengal–Bihar–Orissa *Diwani*
- **Embedded European enclave architecture:** Portuguese Iberian Baroque/Manueline in Goa & Diu (Bom Jesus, Se Cathedral, 1556 press), French Cartesian Pondicherry, and British Factory-Forts (Fort St. George, Fort William)

### Chapter 2: Company Statehood — Diwani Extraction, Statutory Charter Acts, Conquests & Early Colonial Aesthetics (1765–1849)
> **Depth Rubric:** Dual-Core Institutional + Prelims Precision
> **Gated Obsidian Elements:** `Charter Acts (1773–1853) Evolution Matrix` · `Anglo-Regional Wars & Treaties Table` · `Prelims Traps`
> **Provisional Context Bridge:** Constitutional lineage into 1950 Constitution → `Polity`

- Clive’s Dual Government (1765–72), "Plassey Plunder", Great Bengal Famine (1770), and Warren Hastings’ *Izaredari* farming
- **British parliamentary oversight:** Regulating Act 1773 (Calcutta Supreme Court 1774, Elijah Impey), Pitt’s India Act 1784 (Board of Control vs. Court of Directors), Charter Acts of 1793, 1813 (ending trade monopoly ex-tea/China, missionaries, ₹1 lakh education), 1833 (Governor-General of India, Macaulay First Law Commission), and 1853 (open ICS competition)
- **Territorial expansion doctrines (Ring-Fence & Wellesley’s Subsidiary Alliance):** Four Anglo-Mysore Wars (Madras, Mangalore, Seringapatam), Three Anglo-Maratha Wars (Salbai, Bassein, Poona/Mandsaur), Sindh (1843), Two Anglo-Sikh Wars (Treaty of Amritsar 1809, dismantling Ranjit Singh’s *Fauj-i-Ain*), and Buffer Wars (Nepal/Sagauli 1816, Burma/Yandabo 1826, First Afghan War 1839–42, Masterly Inactivity vs. Forward Policy)
- **Colonial administrative apparatus:** Cornwallis Code (1793), Covenanted Civil Services (Fort William & Haileybury), *Daroga* police, and paternalist administrators (Munro, Elphinstone, Metcalfe, Malcolm)
- **Embedded early colonial aesthetics:** Neo-Classical/Palladian Calcutta & Madras (Government House, Town Hall), Picturesque landscape art (Daniells, Hodges, Zoffany), and the **Company School of Painting** (Patna Kalam, Murshidabad, Thanjavur, Delhi)

### Chapter 3: Colonial Agrarian Settlements, Commercialization, De-Industrialization & the Drain of Wealth (1793–1914)
> **Depth Rubric:** Dual-Core Economic History *(High Prelims + Mains GS1 + GS3 Roots)*
> **Gated Obsidian Elements:** `Zamindari vs. Ryotwari vs. Mahalwari Matrix` · `Colonial Currency Commissions Table` · `Prelims Traps` · `Whole-UPSC Spillover`

- R.P. Dutt’s Three Stages of Colonialism (Mercantile Plunder, Industrial Free-Trade, Finance Imperialism)
- **Three colonial land-revenue regimes:** Permanent Settlement / *Zamindari* (1793: Cornwallis & John Shore; Sunset Law, *Patni* sub-infeudation, absentee landlordism), *Ryotwari* (1820: Read, Munro, Elphinstone; Ricardian rent theory, direct periodic upward revisions), and *Mahalwari* (1822–33: Holt Mackenzie, R.M. Bird, Thomason; village *Mahal* / *Lambardars*)
- **Forced commercialization & indentured labor:** rural debt, Indigo (*Nij/Ryoti/Tinkathia*), Bihar–Banaras opium monopoly, cotton/jute, Assam Tea Plantations, and overseas *Girmitiya* indenture (Workmen’s Breach of Contract Act 1859, Inland Emigration Acts)
- **De-industrialization & railways:** one-way tariff destruction of urban handlooms and indigenous iron/saltpetre, ruralization of labor, and the Railway Old & New Guarantee Systems (5% guaranteed private return funded by Indian taxes)
- **Economic nationalism & monetary extraction:** Dadabhai Naoroji (*Poverty and Un-British Rule*), R.C. Dutt (*Economic History of India*), M.G. Ranade, G.V. Joshi on Home Charges; colonial exchange manipulation — Herschell (1893), Fowler (1898), Babington Smith (1919), **Hilton Young Commission (1926, informed by Ambedkar’s *The Problem of the Rupee*)**, 1s 6d vs. 1s 4d ratio battle, and Acworth Committee (1921)

### Chapter 5: Dalhousie’s Annexations, the 1857 Watershed & Imperial Reorganization (1848–1858)

> **Gated Obsidian Elements:** `1857 Centers, Leaders & Suppressors Table` · `Historiographical Debate Matrix` · `Prelims Traps`

- **Spatial arc & social coalitions of 1857:** Meerut → Delhi (Bahadur Shah Zafar & **Bakht Khan’s Court of Administration**), Kanpur (Nana Saheb, Tantia Tope, Azimullah Khan), Lucknow/Awadh (Begum Hazrat Mahal, Birjis Qadr, Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah), Jhansi–Gwalior (Rani Lakshmibai), Arrah/Bihar (Kunwar & Amar Singh), Bareilly (Khan Bahadur Khan), Baghpat (**Shah Mal’s *Chaurasee Des* Jats**), Singhbhum (**Gonoo’s Kols**); the **Azamgarh Proclamation (Aug 1857)**
- **Historiography & structural defeat:** Savarkar, S.N. Sen, R.C. Majumdar, Eric Stokes, Rudrangshu Mukherjee; princely loyalty ("breakwaters in the storm"), middle-class aloofness, rail/telegraph superiority
- **1858 imperial pivot:** GOI Act 1858 (Secretary of State & 15-member Council, Viceroy), Queen Victoria’s Proclamation (Nov 1, 1858: conservative alliance with princes & *Taluqdars*), and **Peel Commission (1859)** (European artillery monopoly, raised European ratios, **"Martial Races"** ideology)
