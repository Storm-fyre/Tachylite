# The Revolutionary Underground, Transnational Radicalism & Socialist Evolution (1897–1935)

Armed anti-colonialism in India was neither an irrational fringe aberration nor a static cult of violence. It evolved across nearly four decades through two structurally distinct domestic waves linked by an audacious wartime global network—progressing from clandestine, religiously inflected individual assassinations in 1897–1915 to a rigorously secular, Marxist-inflected, gender-inclusive socialist movement by 1928–1934.

Both domestic surges arose from identical political mechanics: **strategic vacuum after a sudden retreat of open mass politics**. The first wave erupted when colonial repression and the 1907 Surat Split crushed open Swadeshi agitation; the second crystallized after Mahatma Gandhi abruptly suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement following Chauri Chaura in February 1922. While colonial archives dismissed both streams as "anarchical terrorism", nationalist and Marxist historiography (Bipan Chandra, Sumit Sarkar) recognizes them as an evolving ideological laboratory that shattered British psychological invincibility, internationalized Indian sovereignty, and forced agrarian-labor socialism onto the mainstream Congress agenda.

## Phase I Domestic Underground — Secret Societies and Individual Heroic Action (1897–1915)

Early Indian revolutionaries faced a stark operational asymmetry: a disarmed population confronted a heavily militarized imperial state. Unable to spark an immediate nationwide insurrection, Phase I cadres borrowed tactical templates from **Russian Nihilists**, **Irish Fenians**, and **Giuseppe Mazzini’s *Young Italy***—deploying targeted assassinations of notorious officials, *swadeshi dacoities* (to finance arms), and secret military cells designed to jolt youth out of colonial paralysis.

### Maharashtra — The Chapekars, Savarkar and Abhinav Bharat

Western India produced modern India's first political assassination of British officials, rooted in famine distress and intrusive colonial plague enforcement:

* **The Chapekar Brothers (June 22, 1897):** Outraged by humiliating house-to-house inspections conducted by British troops during the Poona bubonic plague epidemic, **Damodar Hari Chapekar** and **Balkrishna Hari Chapekar** (alongside younger brother Vasudeo Hari, affiliated with their physical-culture *Hindu Dharma Sanrakshini Sabha*) shot dead Poona Plague Commissioner **W.C. Rand** and his military escort **Lt. Charles Ayerst** on the night of Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee. All three brothers were eventually hanged, and colonial authorities used the uproar to prosecute Bal Gangadhar Tilak in his first sedition trial (1897) for his *Kesari* editorials invoking Shivaji's killing of Afzal Khan.
* **Savarkar Brothers — *Mitra Mela* (1899) & *Abhinav Bharat* (1904):** In Nasik, **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar** and his elder brother **Ganesh Damodar Savarkar** founded the secret youth society ***Mitra Mela*** in **1899**, reorganizing it in **1904** into ***Abhinav Bharat Society*** (*Young India Society*), modeled directly on Mazzini's revolutionary blueprint (whose biography V.D. Savarkar translated into Marathi). Departing for London in 1906, V.D. Savarkar authored the banned nationalist classic ***The Indian War of Independence of 1857*** (1909) and smuggled bomb-making manuals and Browning pistols back to Maharashtra.
* **The Nasik Conspiracy Case (1909):** After Nasik District Magistrate **A.M.T. Jackson** sentenced Ganesh Savarkar to transportation for life over nationalist verses, 18-year-old *Abhinav Bharat* cadre **Anant Laxman Kanhere** shot Jackson dead at a theatre farewell on **December 21, 1909**, using a Belgian Browning pistol sent from London by V.D. Savarkar. Kanhere, Karve, and Deshpande were hanged in April 1910. V.D. Savarkar was arrested in London (March 1910), executed a dramatic mid-voyage escape leap from the steamer *S.S. Morea* into Marseilles harbour (July 8, 1910—triggering an international arbitration dispute at The Hague), and was sentenced to **two consecutive life terms (50 years)** in the Andaman Cellular Jail.

### Bengal — *Anushilan Samiti*, *Jugantar*, Muzaffarpur and Alipore

Partitioned Bengal formed the intellectual and logistical epicenter of Phase I militancy, initially disguised as neighborhood gymnasiums (*akharas*) promoting *lathi* play, swordsmanship, and Gita-inspired selfless detachment:

* ***Anushilan Samiti* (1902):** Founded on **March 24, 1902** in Calcutta by **Satish Chandra Basu** under the presidency and patronage of barrister **Pramathanath Mitra** (drawing its name from Bankim Chandra's *Anushilan-Tattva*, "theory of discipline", and early encouragement from Aurobindo Ghose, Jatindranath Banerjee, and Sister Nivedita). In **1906**, **Pulin Behari Das** organized the fiercely disciplined **Dacca *Anushilan Samiti*** across Eastern Bengal, building ~500 branches bound by solemn religious oaths before Kali and staging high-profile funding actions like the **Barrah Dacoity (1908)**.
* ***Jugantar* Group (1906):** Disillusioned with slow physical-culture preparation, an impatient inner circle within Calcutta *Anushilan*—led by **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** (Aurobindo's younger brother) and **Bhupendranath Datta** (Swami Vivekananda's younger brother)—launched the fiery Bengali weekly ***Jugantar*** (March–April 1906; alongside Brahmabandhab Upadhyay's *Sandhya* and Aurobindo's 1905 pamphlet *Bhawani Mandir*). *Jugantar* bluntly declared after the police beating of Barisal protesters: *"Force must be stopped by force"* and urged 30 crore Indians to raise 60 crore hands against colonial tyranny.
* **Hemchandra Kanungo (*Biplabi Dronacharya*):** Recognizing that amateur explosives kept failing, **Hemchandra Kanungo** became the first Indian revolutionary to travel abroad (Paris, 1906–07) to obtain formal military and chemical training from Russian Socialist-Revolutionary exile **Nicolas Safranski**. Returning to Calcutta in January 1908, Kanungo established a covert picric-acid and dynamite bomb factory inside the **32 Muraripukur Garden House (Maniktala)**, supplying explosives to *Jugantar* cells.
* **Muzaffarpur Bombing (April 30, 1908):** After failed attempts on Bengal Lieutenant-Governor Andrew Fraser's train (Dec 1907), *Jugantar* dispatched **Khudiram Bose** (18) and **Prafulla Chaki** to Muzaffarpur (Bihar) to assassinate **Douglas Kingsford**, a hated former Calcutta Presidency Magistrate who had ordered public flogging of Swadeshi youths. Their Kanungo-built bomb struck the wrong carriage, accidentally killing Mrs. Pringle Kennedy and her daughter. Cornered at Mokama Ghat on May 2, 1908, **Prafulla Chaki shot himself**; **Khudiram Bose** was hanged on **August 11, 1908**, entering Bengali folk balladry as an icon of fearless youth.
* **The Alipore Conspiracy Case (Maniktala Bomb Case, 1908–09):** Within 48 hours of Muzaffarpur, police raided Muraripukur Garden (May 2, 1908), arresting 34+ revolutionaries including **Aurobindo Ghose**, Barindra Ghosh, Ullaskar Dutta, and Hemchandra Kanungo. When co-accused **Naren Goswami** turned approver, fellow prisoners **Kanailal Dutta** and **Satyendranath Bose** smuggled revolvers into Alipore Jail hospital and shot him dead. Brilliant legal defense by **Chittaranjan Das** secured **Aurobindo's acquittal (May 1909)**—after which Aurobindo withdrew to French Pondicherry (1910) for spiritual work—while Barindra, Ullaskar, and Kanungo had their death sentences commuted to transportation for life in the Andamans.

### Madras, Punjab and Wartime Domestic Escalation (1907–1915)

Phase I underground resistance was never restricted to Bengal and Maharashtra; agrarian distress in Punjab, Swadeshi repression in Madras, and wartime imperial vulnerability forged pan-Indian conspiratorial bridges:

* **Punjab — Sardar Ajit Singh & *Pagri Sambhal Jatta* (1907):** Triggered by the **Punjab Land Colonisation Bill (1906)**—which undermined proprietary inheritance rights in the Canal Colonies (Lyallpur)—and steep **Bari Doab Canal water-rate hikes**, **Sardar Ajit Singh** (uncle of Bhagat Singh), **Sufi Amba Prasad**, and Syed Haider Raza established the ***Anjuman-i-Mohisban-i-Watan* (Bharat Mata Society)** in Lahore, publishing *Bharat Mata* and Ajit Singh's Urdu journal *Peshwa*. Mobilizing Hindu, Muslim, and Sikh peasants alongside Lala Lajpat Rai around **Banke Dayal’s** stirring anthem ***Pagri Sambhal Jatta*** (editor of *Jhang Syal*), the agitation threatened to infect Punjabi regiments in the British Indian Army. Although Viceroy Minto deported Ajit Singh and Lajpat Rai without trial to Mandalay in May 1907, he was forced to **veto the Colonisation Bill**; upon release, Ajit Singh escaped abroad (1909), spending 38 years in anti-colonial exile across Iran, Europe, and Brazil.
* **Madras Presidency — Vanchi Iyer (June 17, 1911):** Infuriated by Tirunelveli District Collector **Robert Ashe’s** brutal suppression of V.O. Chidambaram Pillai's Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company and the 1908 Tuticorin strike, **Vanchinathan (Vanchi Iyer)**—a Travancore forest guard belonging to Nilakanta Brahmachari's secret ***Bharata Matha Association*** and trained in marksmanship by **V.V.S. Aiyar** in Pondicherry—boarded Ashe's first-class compartment at **Maniyachi Junction** on **June 17, 1911** and shot him dead with a Belgian Browning pistol linked to the Paris network, before shooting himself in the station lavatory. South India's first political assassination triggered the **Tirunelveli Conspiracy Case**.
* **Delhi Conspiracy / Hardinge Bomb Case (December 23, 1912):** To shatter colonial triumphalism during the imperial capital shift from Calcutta to Delhi, **Rash Behari Bose** (a forest research clerk at Dehradun acting as strategic bridge between Bengal and North India) and **Sachindranath Sanyal** (founder of the Banaras *Anushilan* wing) engineered an audacious bombing during **Viceroy Lord Hardinge II’s** ceremonial elephant procession through Chandni Chowk. **Basanta Kumar Biswas** hurled the bomb, wounding Hardinge and killing his attendant. Four patriots—**Master Amir Chand, Avadh Behari, Bal Mukund, and Basanta Kumar Biswas**—were hanged in May 1915, while Rash Behari vanished underground to organize the 1915 pan-Indian mutiny before escaping to Japan (1915).
* **Bagha Jatin & the Indo-German Zimmerman Plan (1914–1915):** World War I transformed individual terrorism into plans for coordinated insurrection. *Jugantar* supreme leader **Jatindranath Mukherjee (*Bagha Jatin*)**—having already forged cross-cell unity across Bengal—linked with the German Foreign Office's **Zimmerman Plan** via **Narendranath Bhattacharya** (later Communist pioneer **M.N. Roy**) in Batavia (Java) to land German rifles aboard the chartered steamer ***S.S. Maverick*** (supplied by *Annie Larsen*) along the Sundarbans and Odisha coast to sever Bengal's railways and seize Fort William. Tipped off by intelligence intercepts, British forces surrounded Jatin and four comrades on the banks of the **Buribalam River near Balasore (Odisha)** on **September 9, 1915**. Fighting a fierce trench gun-battle until ammunition ran out, Bagha Jatin succumbed to wounds on **September 10, 1915**—an act of battlefield defiance that commanded grudging respect even from Bengal Police Intelligence chief Charles Tegart.

## Transnational Radicalism and Wartime Networks (1905–1918)

Tightening domestic censorship and surveillance pushed Indian radicals across three continents—London, Paris, Geneva, Berlin, San Francisco, Vancouver, Singapore, and Kabul. Operating outside British Indian penal jurisdiction, these diasporic centers internationalized India's claim to sovereignty and attempted to exploit Britain's World War I entanglement into an empire-wide military revolt.

### London and Paris — *India House*, Dhingra and Madam Cama

* **Shyamji Krishna Varma’s *India House* (London, 1905):** A Cambridge-educated Sanskrit scholar and lawyer disillusioned with colonial service, **Shyamji Krishna Varma** launched the **Indian Home Rule Society** (Feb 1905), the monthly radical journal ***The Indian Sociologist***, and the Highgate student hostel ***India House*** (July 1905), funding scholarships that drew **V.D. Savarkar, Lala Har Dayal, V.V.S. Aiyar, and Madan Lal Dhingra**. After Varma relocated to Paris in 1907 to escape British prosecution, Savarkar transformed *India House* into a militant hub distributing bomb manuals and pistols.
* **Madan Lal Dhingra (July 1, 1909):** An engineering student radicalized at *India House*, Dhingra assassinated **Sir William Hutt Curzon Wyllie** (political ADC to the Secretary of State for India and spy-master monitoring Indian students) at the Imperial Institute in London. Refusing legal counsel, Dhingra declared at trial: *"The only lesson required in India at present is to learn how to die, and the only way to teach it is by dying ourselves."* Hanged on August 17, 1909, his act prompted British police to dismantle *India House*, dispersing its leadership to Paris.
* **Madam Bhikaji Cama (Paris & Stuttgart, 1905–1909):** Known as the *"Mother of the Indian Revolution"*, Parsi revolutionary **Bhikaji Rustom Cama** co-founded the **Paris Indian Society (1905)** alongside **S.R. Rana** and **Munchershah Burjorji Godrej**. On **August 22, 1907**, addressing over a thousand global delegates at the **Second International Socialist Congress in Stuttgart (Germany)**, Cama became the **first person to hoist an Indian independence flag on foreign soil**—a green, saffron, and red tricolour inscribed *Vande Mataram* in Devanagari, bearing eight half-open lotuses (eight provinces of British India) plus a sun and crescent (adapted from the 1906 Calcutta Flag alongside Savarkar, Kanungo, and Varma circles). From Paris and Geneva she financed ***Bande Mataram*** (edited with Lala Har Dayal) and backed ***Madan’s Talwar / The Talvar*** (Berlin, honoring Dhingra, edited with Virendranath Chattopadhyaya).

### North America — The Ghadar Party (1913) and *Komagata Maru* (1914)

The most socially democratic and fiercely secular revolutionary movement of the pre-1920 era emerged not among urban Bengali intellectuals, but among **Punjabi peasant-migrants and retired soldiers** toiling in lumber mills, railways, and farms along the US and Canadian Pacific Coast. Subjected to brutal white-supremacist immigration exclusions and realizing that colonial subjugation at home caused their humiliation abroad, diasporic pioneers—**Taraknath Das** (*Free Hindustan*), **G.D. Kumar** (*Swadesh Sevak Home*, Vancouver), and **Sohan Singh Bhakna** (*United India House*, Seattle)—laid the organizational foundation.

* **Formation of the Ghadar Party (1913):** Established initially as the **Pacific Coast Hindustan Association** (Astoria, Oregon, mid-1913) with **Sohan Singh Bhakna** as **President** and **Lala Har Dayal** as **General Secretary**—flanked by **Maulavi Barkatullah**, **Kartar Singh Sarabha**, **Kanshi Ram**, **Santokh Singh**, **Bhai Parmanand**, and **Ram Chandra**—the party headquartered at ***Yugantar Ashram* in San Francisco**. On **November 1, 1913**, it launched its fiery weekly ***The Ghadar*** ("Rebellion", published in Urdu and Gurmukhi, later Hindi, Gujarati, and Pashto) carrying the banner *"Angrezi Raj Ka Dushman"* and serialized the militant poetry anthology *Ghadar-di-Gunj*. Unlike Phase I Bengal or Maharashtra secret cells, Ghadar ideology was **uncompromisingly secular, anti-caste, egalitarian, and internationalist**—declaring *"We are not Sikhs or Punjabis; our religion is patriotism"* and envisioning a democratic Indian Republic established via coordinated military mutiny.
* **The *Komagata Maru* Incident (May–September 1914):** To challenge Canada's discriminatory **Continuous Journey Regulation (1908)**—which effectively banned Indian immigrants by demanding uninterrupted passage from India despite zero direct steamships—Hong Kong contractor **Baba Gurdit Singh** chartered the Japanese steamer ***Komagata Maru*** (rechristened *Guru Nanak Jahaz*). Sailing from Hong Kong (April 4, 1914) with **376 Punjabi passengers (340 Sikhs, 24 Muslims, 12 Hindus)**, the vessel reached Vancouver on **May 23, 1914**. Only 24 prior Canadian residents were admitted; despite fierce legal defense by Vancouver's **Shore Committee** (Hussain Rahim, Sohan Lal Pathak, Balwant Singh) and US rallies led by Barkatullah and Bhakna, Canadian authorities starved the vessel of supplies and forced it back under naval escort on July 23. Docking at **Budge Budge (near Calcutta)** on **September 27–29, 1914**, passengers refusing forced rail internment to Punjab clashed with British police on **September 29, 1914**; gunfire killed **20 passengers** (18–22 across records) and jailed over 200. The massacre electrified Punjab and propelled Ghadar into immediate wartime action.

### 1915 Mutinies, Berlin Committee, Kabul Government and Silk Letters (1915–1916)

With World War I erupting in August 1914, Britain stripped India of European regiments, creating what Ghadarites viewed as a once-in-a-century opening:

* **The 1915 Ghadar Uprising & First Lahore Conspiracy Case:** Around 8,000 Ghadarites sailed home to Punjab. Linking up with **Rash Behari Bose** and **Sachindranath Sanyal** (who arrived in Amritsar/Lahore to coordinate Bengal–Punjab–UP links), they infiltrated Indian army regiments at Ferozepur, Lahore, Rawalpindi, Meerut, and Banaras, fixing **February 21, 1915** for a simultaneous mutiny. Betrayed by police informer **Kirpal Singh**, leaders preponed the rising to **February 19**, only to find British commanders disarming native garrisons beforehand. Armed with the **Ingress into India Ordinance (1914)** and the draconian **Defence of India Act (1915)** (authorizing special tribunals without appeal), the Raj crushed the network. In the **First Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915)**, 42 revolutionaries received death sentences—including 19-year-old Ghadar prodigy **Kartar Singh Sarabha** (later Bhagat Singh's personal idol) and Maharashtra recruit **Vishnu Ganesh Pingle** (hanged November 16, 1915)—while **Sohan Singh Bhakna’s** death sentence was commuted to life imprisonment.
* **The Singapore Mutiny (February 15, 1915):** Five days prior to Punjab's foiled rising, ~850 Punjabi Muslim sepoys of the **5th Light Infantry** stationed in Singapore—influenced by Ghadar emissaries (such as merchant Kassim Mansoor) and Pan-Islamic resentment against fighting Ottoman Turkey—mutinied under **Jamadar Chisti Khan, Jamadar Abdul Gani, and Subedar Dunde Khan**. Holding sections of Singapore for nearly a week, they were suppressed by British, French, Russian, and Japanese allied troops; 47 sepoys were publicly executed by firing squad.
* **Berlin Committee for Indian Independence (1915):** Organized in Berlin under the German Foreign Office's **Zimmerman Plan** by **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya** (elder brother of Sarojini Naidu), **Bhupendranath Datta**, **Lala Har Dayal**, **Champakaraman Pillai**, and **Abhinash Bhattacharya**. The Committee sought to ship German arms to Bengal (Bagha Jatin's plot), mobilize Indian prisoners of war in Europe, and dispatch diplomatic missions to Baghdad, Persia, and Afghanistan.
* **Kabul Provisional Government of India (December 1, 1915):** Arriving in Kabul with the Indo-German-Turkish (**Niedermayer–Hentig**) expedition to urge Afghan Amir Habibullah to declare war on British India, exiled revolutionaries proclaimed the **First Provisional Government of India in Exile** on **December 1, 1915**: **Raja Mahendra Pratap** (aristocrat-revolutionary from Hathras) as **President**, **Maulavi Barkatullah** (Ghadar co-founder) as **Prime Minister**, and Deobandi revolutionary **Maulana Ubaidullah Sindhi** as **Home Minister**. Though Afghanistan remained neutral, the coalition exemplified cross-communal anti-imperialist diplomacy.
* **The Silk Letter Conspiracy (*Tehreek-e-Reshmi Rumal*, 1916):** Masterminded by **Maulana Mahmud Hasan** (*Shaykh al-Hind*, Principal of Darul Uloom Deoband), who travelled to Hejaz (1915) to secure Ottoman-Turkish military backing (*Ghalib Nama*), working in tandem with **Maulana Ubaidullah Sindhi** in Kabul to build an anti-British liberation army (*Junood-e-Rabbaniyah* / *Hizbullah*) uniting Afghan tribes, Ghadarites, and the Kabul Provisional Government. In **August 1916**, Punjab CID intercepted secret letters written by Sindhi on yellow silk cloth sewn inside a courier's coat lining addressed to Mahmud Hasan in Mecca. The leak aborted the plot; Mahmud Hasan was arrested by the Sharif of Mecca and interned by the British in **Malta (1917–20)**.

Operationally, wartime plots failed because they overestimated German-Turkish strategic commitment, lacked secure domestic communications, underestimated British intelligence networks, and possessed no organized peasant-worker mass machinery at home. Yet they forced the Raj onto the defensive—prompting the 1918 **Rowlatt Sedition Committee** report—and bequeathed to the next generation a profoundly **secular, internationalist ethos**.

## Phase II — Ideological Pivot to Secular Socialism (1924–1935)

The 1920s witnessed a qualitative intellectual revolution inside the underground. Young activists who had enthusiastically suspended studies to join Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) were shattered by its sudden withdrawal after **Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922)**. Unwilling to return to Moderate constitutionalism or Swarajist council wrangling, yet deeply transformed by the **1917 Russian Bolshevik Revolution**, the rise of Indian trade unions, and early socialist literature (*Atmasakti*, *Sarathi*, *Bijoli*), North India's underground abandoned religious mysticism for **scientific socialism and secular mass emancipation**.

### Hindustan Republican Association (HRA, 1924) and the Kakori Train Action (1925)

* **Foundation and Manifesto (1924–25):** In **October 1924** at **Kanpur**, veteran Bengal–UP underground organizers **Ram Prasad Bismil**, **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee**, and **Sachindranath Sanyal** (whose prison memoir ***Bandi Jeevan*** served as the recruiting manual of Phase II youth) founded the **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)** (also referred to as Army). Sanyal's January 1, 1925 manifesto, ***The Revolutionary***, bridged old and new eras: it demanded an organized armed revolution to establish a **"Federal Republic of the United States of India"** founded on **universal adult suffrage**, abolition of all systems permitting *"exploitation of man by man"*, and **nationalization of railways, heavy industries, mines, and shipping**.
* **Kakori Train Action (August 9, 1925):** *(Historically dubbed the Kakori Conspiracy Case by British courts; officially commemorated as the **Kakori Train Action**.)* Rejecting private *swadeshi dacoities* that alienated ordinary citizens, Bismil and **Ashfaqulla Khan** planned a raid exclusively targeting British colonial treasury revenues. On **August 9, 1925**, ten HRA revolutionaries—including **Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendranath Lahiri, Chandrashekhar Azad**, Sachindra Bakshi, Manmathnath Gupta, and Mukundi Lal—pulled the emergency chain of the **No. 8 Down Saharanpur–Lucknow Express** near **Kakori** and seized official cash bags (~₹4,600–₹8,000 across accounts; an accidental misfire tragically killed passenger Ahmad Ali).
* **Trial, Martyrdom and Communal Brotherhood (1927):** British police launched a sweeping manhunt arresting ~40 suspects; only **Chandrashekhar Azad** eluded capture. Despite vigorous legal defense led by **Govind Ballabh Pant** and Mohan Lal Saxena and prisoner hunger strikes demanding political-prisoner status, four HRA leaders were sent to the gallows across separate UP jails:
  * **Rajendranath Lahiri** — hanged on **December 17, 1927** at **Gonda District Jail** (preponed by two days fearing a jailbreak).
  * **Ram Prasad Bismil** (Gorakhpur Jail, immortalizing Bismil Azimabadi's *Sarfaroshi Ki Tamanna*), **Ashfaqulla Khan** (Faizabad Jail — the first Muslim revolutionary executed in the modern post-1857 armed underground, whose bond with Arya Samajist Bismil stood as an enduring emblem of Hindu–Muslim unity), and **Thakur Roshan Singh** (Naini / Allahabad Jail) — hanged on **December 19, 1927**.
  * Sachindranath Sanyal and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee received transportation for life.

### Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA, 1928) and Bhagat Singh's Vanguard

Decimated by Kakori, North India's surviving cells—led by Punjab's **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev Thapar, and Bhagwati Charan Vohra**, UP's **Shiv Verma, Vijay Kumar Sinha, and Jaidev Kapur**, and commander-in-chief **Chandrashekhar Azad**—met secretly at the ruins of **Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi** on **September 8–9, 1928**. Renaming the body the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**, they adopted collective democratic leadership and proclaimed **Socialism** as their explicit foundational goal.

**Bhagat Singh** represented the intellectual apex of Indian revolutionary thought, evolving rapidly from romantic heroism to mature Marxist humanism:

* ***Naujawan Bharat Sabha* (March 1926, Lahore):** Two years before HSRA, Bhagat Singh (with Chhabil Das and Yashpal) created this open secular mass front to mobilize youth, workers, and peasants, requiring strict pledges placing nation above religion, boycotting communal bodies, and organizing inter-communal dining alongside magic-lantern lectures on Russian/Irish revolutions and Kartar Singh Sarabha.
* **Saunders Assassination (December 17, 1928):** Although HSRA had ideologically resolved to move away from individual assassinations, the fatal lathi-charge ordered by Lahore Superintendent of Police **James A. Scott** against anti-Simon Commission protesters (Oct 30, 1928)—leading to **Lala Lajpat Rai’s death** on November 17, 1928—demanded a psychological reply to restore national self-respect. On **December 17, 1928** (exact first anniversary of Rajendra Lahiri's hanging), **Bhagat Singh, Shivaram Rajguru, and Chandrashekhar Azad** (planned with Sukhdev) shot Assistant Superintendent **John P. Saunders** outside Lahore police headquarters (mistaking him for Scott). Escaping Lahore in British officer disguise escorted by **Durga Devi Vohra (*Durga Bhabhi*)**, they pasted notices proclaiming **"Inquilab Zindabad"** *(coined by Maulana Hasrat Mohani in 1921, made a pan-Indian war-cry by Bhagat Singh)*—explicitly expressing regret at taking a human life while justifying striking an inhuman imperial system.
* **Central Legislative Assembly Bombing (April 8, 1929):** Executing HSRA's decisive pivot toward **propaganda by deed for mass awakening**, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** tossed two low-intensity, non-lethal bombs onto empty benches of the Central Legislative Assembly in New Delhi right as the government moved to enact the repressive **Public Safety Bill** (targeting socialist activists) and **Trade Disputes Bill** (outlawing general strikes). Showering leaflets opening with French anarchist Auguste Vaillant's phrase **"It takes a loud voice to make the deaf hear"**, both deliberately stayed behind to court arrest, turning the colonial courtroom into a national classroom explaining that *"Revolution is not the cult of the bomb and pistol; by revolution we mean that the present order of things, which is based on manifest injustice, must change."*
* **Jail Hunger Strike & Jatin Das’s Martyrdom (September 13, 1929):** Police raids on Lahore and Saharanpur bomb factories linked the Assembly bombers to the Saunders killing, launching the **Second Lahore Conspiracy Case**. Inside Lahore jail, Bhagat Singh and comrades initiated a historic **hunger strike** demanding **political prisoner status**—access to books, newspapers, humane food, and freedom from forced manual labor. Refusing compromise despite brutal nasal force-feeding, Bengali revolutionary **Jatindra Nath Das (Jatin Das)** died on **September 13, 1929** after an extraordinary **63-day fast**, drawing a 600,000-strong mourning crowd in Calcutta and compelling nationwide solidarity across Congress ranks.
* ***The Philosophy of the Bomb* (January 1930):** On December 23, 1929, Chandrashekhar Azad and Yashpal attempted to blow up Viceroy Lord Irwin's special train near Delhi. Mahatma Gandhi condemned the act in *Young India* (January 2, 1930) in an essay titled ***The Cult of the Bomb***. At Azad's request and in full consultation with him (with Yashpal assisting), HSRA propaganda secretary **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** drafted HSRA's definitive theoretical counter-manifesto, ***The Philosophy of the Bomb*** (released **January 26, 1930**). Directly debating Gandhi's philosophy of non-violence, the document argued that terrorism was merely a transitional phase triggered by colonial state terror, that revolutionary force differed fundamentally from criminal violence because its purpose was liberation, and that true revolution meant complete political, economic, and social freedom for workers and peasants. *(Vohra himself died tragically on May 28, 1930 along the Ravi river bank while testing a bomb meant to rescue Bhagat Singh from jail.)*
* ***Why I Am an Atheist* (October 1930) & Ideological Maturity:** Reading voraciously inside Lahore Central Jail (Marx, Lenin, Trotsky, Bakunin, Russell, Upton Sinclair), Bhagat Singh authored ***Why I Am an Atheist*** (written October 5–6, 1930; published posthumously in Lala Lajpat Rai's *The People* on September 27, 1931)—a fearless rationalist manifesto written under shadow of the gallows rejecting divine fatalism as an impediment to human agency and diagnosing **communalism** as an enemy equally lethal to colonialism. In his final treatise, ***To Young Political Workers*** (February 2, 1931), he explicitly repudiated individual heroic terrorism as outdated, urging youth to organize peasants, factory laborers, and students into a mass revolutionary socialist movement so that independence did not merely replace *"Lord Reading with Sir Purshottamdas Thakurdas"*.
* **Final Martyrdoms (February–March 1931):** Surrounded by police at **Alfred Park, Allahabad** on **February 27, 1931**, **Chandrashekhar Azad** held off officers until his final bullet and shot himself—keeping his vow never to be taken alive (*"Azad"*). Less than a month later, amidst raging national controversy over whether Gandhi could have made clemency a precondition of the March 5 Gandhi–Irwin Pact, British authorities secretly hanged **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev Thapar, and Shivaram Rajguru** a day ahead of schedule on **March 23, 1931** inside **Lahore Central Jail**, cremating their bodies covertly along the Sutlej river at Hussainiwala. Their composure turned them into the defining secular-socialist icons of modern India.

## Bengal Resurgence and the Rise of Women Revolutionaries (1924–1935)

Parallel to HSRA in North India, Bengal's underground underwent an equally transformative evolution after 1922. Initially weakened by factional splits inside the Bengal Provincial Congress after C.R. Das's death (1925)—with *Jugantar* backing Subhas Chandra Bose and *Anushilan* aligning with J.M. Sengupta—and lone strikes like **Gopinath Saha’s** January 1924 attempt on Calcutta Police Commissioner Charles Tegart (accidentally killing Ernest Day), younger Bengali cadres grew restless with older faction bosses and launched **collective, secular, and gender-inclusive revolt groups**.

### Surya Sen (*Masterda*) and the Chittagong Armoury Raid (April 18, 1930)

* **The Organizational Vision:** **Surya Sen**—revered as ***Masterda***, a quiet schoolteacher who had actively participated in Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement and served as Secretary of the Chittagong District Congress Committee—organized the **Indian Republican Army (IRA), Chittagong Branch** alongside **Ananta Singh, Ganesh Ghosh, Lokenath Bal, Ambika Chakrabarty, and Nirmal Sen**. Explicitly inspired by the **1916 Irish Easter Rising**, Sen rejected isolated individual assassinations in favor of an **audacious collective armed uprising** aimed at capturing an entire British district to prove the Empire could be militarily defeated.
* **The Raid (April 18, 1930):** On Good Friday night, 65 uniformed revolutionaries split into four squads simultaneously stormed and captured the **Chittagong Police Armoury** and the **Auxiliary Force Armoury**, severed telegraph and telephone links, and derailed the railway line isolating Chittagong from the rest of Bengal. Although they captured rifles and revolvers, the crucial ammunition store remained locked elsewhere—preventing sustained urban defense. Outside the Police Armoury, Surya Sen hoisted the national flag, took a formal military salute from his cadres chanting *Inquilab Zindabad* and *Vande Mataram*, and proclaimed a **Provisional Revolutionary Government**.
* **Jalalabad Hills & Three-Year Guerrilla War:** Retreating to the **Jalalabad Hills**, the outnumbered young rebels fought a fierce pitched battle against British regiments on **April 22, 1930** (losing 12 cadres, including teenage hero Harigopal Bal 'Tegra') before breaking into tiny guerrilla bands across rural Chittagong. Crucially, the Chittagong revolt shattered two entrenched Phase I weaknesses: it **abandoned Hindu religious oath-taking**, drawing Muslim cadres (**Satar, Mir Ahmed, Fakir Ahmed Mian, Tunu Mian**) into active support, and sustained three full years of underground survival primarily because **local Muslim peasants sheltered Surya Sen** despite crushing collective fines and huge bounties. Betrayed by a kinsman (Netra Sen), Surya Sen was arrested on **February 16, 1933**, subjected to savage torture, and hanged alongside **Tarakeswar Dastidar** on **January 12, 1934**.
* **Writers' Building Raid (December 8, 1930):** Mirroring Chittagong's shift to disciplined collective action, three young cadres of the **Bengal Volunteers**—**Benoy Basu, Badal Gupta, and Dinesh Gupta**—stormed the administrative heart of Calcutta (**Writers' Building**) in European suits, shooting dead notoriously cruel Inspector-General of Prisons **Colonel N.S. Simpson** in the legendary "Verandah Battle" (Badal took cyanide, Benoy died of self-inflicted wounds, Dinesh was hanged in July 1931).

### Women Revolutionaries — From Couriers to Frontline Combatants

Nowhere was the ideological leap between Phase I and Phase II more unmistakable than in gender politics. While Phase I societies (*Anushilan*, *Abhinav Bharat*) barred women from combat roles, Phase II Bengal placed educated young women at the very center of armed planning, bomb-making, and direct strikes:

* **Pritilata Waddedar (September 24, 1932):** A brilliant Bethune College philosophy graduate and school headmistress recruited by Surya Sen, Pritilata was entrusted with leading a 15-member armed raid on the racially exclusive **Pahartali European Club** in Chittagong (notorious for its gate placard *"Dogs and Indians Not Allowed"*). Dressed in male *dhoti-kurta*, she executed the assault cleanly; struck by a bullet during withdrawal, she swallowed potassium cyanide to prevent capture—becoming **Bengal’s first woman revolutionary martyr** and leaving a powerful testament asserting women's equal right to fight and die for national liberation.
* **Kalpana Dutt (later Joshi):** A Chittagong science student who joined Surya Sen's IRA, prepared explosives, conducted reconnaissance disguised in male clothes, and fought alongside Surya Sen during the 1933 Gairala village encounter. Arrested in May 1933 and tried alongside Masterda in the Chittagong Supplementary Trial, she was spared the gallows due to her youth and sentenced to **transportation for life** (released in 1939 following public appeals by Rabindranath Tagore and Mahatma Gandhi; later joined the Communist Party of India and authored the primary memoir *Chittagong Armoury Raiders: Reminiscences*).
* **Santi Ghosh & Suniti Chaudhuri (December 14, 1931):** Two teenage Comilla schoolgirls (aged 15 and 14, affiliated with *Jugantar*) walked calmly into the official bungalow of oppressive Tippera/Comilla District Magistrate **C.G.B. Stevens** on the pretext of presenting a petition for a girls' swimming competition, drew revolvers, and shot him dead at point-blank range. Both received life imprisonment due to their minority.
* **Bina Das (February 6, 1932):** A 21-year-old student at Diocesan College, Calcutta (member of the radical women's network *Chhatri Sangha*, daughter of Subhas Bose's mentor Beni Madhab Das), Bina concealed a revolver inside her academic gown while stepping forward to receive her BA degree at the **Calcutta University Convocation** and fired five shots at point-blank range at Bengal Governor **Sir Stanley Jackson** (who dodged unharmed). Sentenced to nine years' rigorous imprisonment, her courtroom statement indicted colonial rule for driving even sensitive young women to desperation.

By 1934–35, savage colonial counter-insurgency (Bengal Criminal Law Amendment ordinances, mass detentions without trial, Hijli Detention Camp firings) broke the armed underground. Yet its survivors did not vanish: across jails in the Andamans and Bengal, hundreds of incarcerated *Jugantar*, *Anushilan*, and HSRA cadres studied Marxism systematically—joining the **Communist Party of India (CPI)**, **Congress Socialist Party (CSP)**, **Revolutionary Socialist Party (RSP)**, or **Forward Bloc** upon release in the late 1930s, permanently radicalizing Indian mass politics.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> * **Ghadar Party Leadership & Membership (UPSC Prelims 2014 & 2022 Traps):**
>   * **Sohan Singh Bhakna** was the founding **President** of the Ghadar Party (1913, initially *Pacific Coast Hindustan Association*); **Lala Har Dayal** was the **General Secretary**, NOT President.
>   * Headquarters was ***Yugantar Ashram*, San Francisco** (NOT Berlin, Singapore, or Tashkent).
>   * **Rash Behari Bose** was actively linked with the 1915 Ghadar mutiny plan in India; **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** (*Jugantar* / Alipore Case, jailed in Andamans 1909–20) and **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee** (Phase II *Anushilan*/HRA) were **NOT** associated with the Ghadar Party (UPSC Prelims 2022).
> * ***The Philosophy of the Bomb* vs. *Why I Am an Atheist* vs. *Bandi Jeevan*:**
>   * ***The Philosophy of the Bomb*** (Jan 1930, countering Gandhi's *The Cult of the Bomb*) was drafted by **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** in consultation with **Chandrashekhar Azad** (NOT Bhagat Singh, who was already in Lahore Jail).
>   * ***Why I Am an Atheist*** (written Oct 1930) was authored by **Bhagat Singh**.
>   * ***Bandi Jeevan*** and HRA's 1925 manifesto ***The Revolutionary*** were authored by **Sachindranath Sanyal**.
> * **Kakori Execution Dates & Jails Precision:** All four martyrs were NOT hanged on the same day—**Rajendranath Lahiri** was hanged two days early on **December 17, 1927** (Gonda Jail), whereas **Ram Prasad Bismil** (Gorakhpur), **Ashfaqulla Khan** (Faizabad), and **Thakur Roshan Singh** (Naini/Allahabad) were hanged on **December 19, 1927**.
> * **First vs. Second Lahore Conspiracy Cases:**
>   * **First Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915):** Ghadar mutiny trial (**Kartar Singh Sarabha, Vishnu Ganesh Pingle, Sohan Singh Bhakna**).
>   * **Second Lahore Conspiracy Case (1929–31):** HSRA trial for Saunders murder & bomb factories (**Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru** — hanged March 23, 1931; **Batukeshwar Dutt** was tried for the **Assembly Bomb Case** and transported for life, NOT hanged!).
> * **Two Different *Jatin*s:** **Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee)** led *Jugantar* and died in the 1915 Balasore gunfight (Indo-German plot); **Jatin Das (Jatindra Nath Das)** was the HSRA revolutionary who died after a **63-day hunger strike** in Lahore Jail (Sept 13, 1929).
> * **Two Bills Protested by the April 8, 1929 Assembly Bomb:** **Public Safety Bill** and **Trade Disputes Bill** (NOT the Rowlatt Bill of 1919 or Simon Commission directly).
> * **Pritilata Waddedar vs. Kalpana Dutt:** **Pritilata Waddedar** led the 1932 Pahartali European Club raid and died taking cyanide; **Kalpana Dutt** was arrested, tried alongside Surya Sen, and given a **life sentence** (later released in 1939).
> * **Origin of Iconic Slogans:** ***Pagri Sambhal Jatta*** (1907) was composed by **Banke Dayal** (editor of *Jhang Syal*), mobilized by **Ajit Singh**; ***Inquilab Zindabad*** was coined by **Maulana Hasrat Mohani (1921)** and popularized by **Bhagat Singh**; ***Sarfaroshi Ki Tamanna*** was composed by **Bismil Azimabadi (1921)** and immortalized by **Ram Prasad Bismil**.

> [!tip] Whole-UPSC Spillover
> * **GS2 (Polity — Preventive Detention & Prisoners' Rights):** The colonial state's legal war against the revolutionary underground—**Defence of India Act (1915)**, **Rowlatt Act (1919)**, and **Bengal Criminal Law Amendment Acts**—forged India's statutory architecture of executive preventive detention without trial, forming the exact historical backdrop against which Constituent Assembly framers debated Article 22 safeguards. Simultaneously, the Kakori and **Jatin Das (1929)** jail hunger strikes laid the normative foundation for distinguishing political dissenters from common criminals in Indian prison jurisprudence.
> * **GS4 (Ethics — Means vs. Ends & Moral Courage):** The historic 1930 textual debate between **Mahatma Gandhi (*The Cult of the Bomb*)** and **HSRA (*The Philosophy of the Bomb*)**—paired with Bhagat Singh's ***Why I Am an Atheist***—provides a premier ethical case study contrasting deontological *Ahimsa* (purity of means) against consequentialist liberation ethics, rationalist moral courage under death sentence, and selfless altruism stripped of supernatural reward.
> * **Essay & GS1 Society (Secularism & Women's Agency):** Tracing the underground's own self-correction—from Phase I's upper-caste Hindu oaths (*Anushilan*) to Phase II's radical secularism (*Ghadar*, Bismil–Ashfaqulla, Bhagat Singh's anti-communal tracts, Muslim peasant shelter for Surya Sen) and women's leap from domestic symbols to frontline fighters (Pritilata, Kalpana, Bina Das)—offers rich original fodder for essays on youth idealism, dissent in nation-building, and substantive gender equality.

### Prelims Trap Signature

* **Phase-Cross-Contamination:** UPSC deliberately mixes Phase I domestic figures (*Barindra Ghosh, Hemchandra Kanungo*), wartime transnational figures (*Rash Behari Bose, Lala Har Dayal, Barkatullah*), and Phase II cadres (*Jogesh Chatterjee, Sachin Sanyal, Bhagat Singh*) inside a single "associated with X organization" item.
