# Chapter 2 - Agro-Ecological Zones, 2025–2026 Diversification Missions & Irrigation Hydro-Agronomy

Indian agriculture operates across a violent disconnect between **ecological endowment** and **economic incentives**. Nature endowed the subcontinent with twenty distinct agro-ecological regions spanning alpine valleys, humid deltas, basalt plateaus, and arid dunes; yet six decades of cereal-centric procurement, unmetered farm power, and distorted input subsidies have compressed farm choices into water-guzzling monocultures planted in precisely the wrong hydrological basins.

Understanding modern Indian agronomy therefore demands connecting **soil-water physics** directly to **fiscal architecture**. Between 2024 and 2026, Union policy has executed an assertive strategic pivot—launching targeted self-reliance missions in pulses, oilseeds, cotton, and lagging districts while wrestling with the deep hydro-agronomic crisis of groundwater mining, canal inefficiency, and massive virtual water exports.

## Spatial Agronomy - Agro-Climatic Zones, Agro-Ecological Regions and Monoculture Traps

### India's Three Spatial Classification Frameworks

Precision crop planning requires matching cultivar genetics to regional thermal, soil, and moisture envelopes. India utilizes three nested spatial frameworks that Prelims routinely conflates:

1. **15 Agro-Climatic Zones (Erstwhile Planning Commission, 1988 / 7th Plan):** Delineated for macro-level resource development using climate (temperature, rainfall), soil groups, topography, and water availability. These group cleanly into five geographic clusters: two **Himalayan zones** (Western, Eastern); four **Gangetic Plains** (Lower, Middle, Upper, Trans-Gangetic); four **Plateaus and Hills** (Eastern, Central, Western, Southern); two **Coastal Plains and Hills** (East Coast, West Coast); plus the **Gujarat Plains and Hills**, the **Western Dry Region** (Rajasthan), and the **Island Region** (Andaman–Nicobar and Lakshadweep).
2. **20 Agro-Ecological Regions (AERs) and 60 Sub-Regions (AESRs) (ICAR–NBSS&LUP):** Built upon the FAO methodology by the Nagpur-based National Bureau of Soil Survey and Land Use Planning. Unlike purely climatic zones, AERs superimpose **bioclimate**, **soil-scape/physiography**, and **Length of Growing Period (LGP)**—defined as the continuous days in a year when rainfall plus stored soil moisture meet crop evapotranspiration demand. LGP ranges from under 90 days in hot/cold deserts (Ladakh, Thar) to over 210 days in per-humid North-East and Western Ghats tracts. Subdividing these into **60 AESRs** incorporates narrower LGP classes, soil depth, mineralogy, and Available Water Capacity (AWC) for precision technology transfer.
3. **127 Agro-Climatic Zones under NARP (ICAR):** Established under the National Agricultural Research Project to anchor Zonal Agricultural Research Stations (ZARS) within State Agricultural Universities (SAUs), enabling micro-regional varietal breeding tailored to local pest and rainfall cycles.

### Cropping Seasons, Intensity and Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)

India's monsoon rhythm structures cultivation into three overlapping calendars, generating a national **cropping intensity of 155.9%** (Gross Cropped Area of **219.36 million ha** over ~140.7 million ha Net Sown Area; *Land Use Statistics 2022–23*):

- **Kharif (June–October):** Synchronized with the South-West monsoon; favors warm, humid, photo-sensitive crops—rice, maize, jowar, bajra, ragi, tur (arhar), urad, moong, groundnut, soybean, cotton, and jute.
- **Rabi (October–March):** Anchored to cool winters, residual soil moisture, irrigation, and north-western Western Disturbances—wheat, barley, gram (chana), masoor (lentil), rapeseed-mustard, and linseed.
- **Zaid (March–June):** Short-duration summer crops grown under assured irrigation or receding riverbeds—watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder, and summer moong. Several pulses (*urad* and *moong*) span both Kharif and Rabi seasons, while gram (*chana*) dominates Rabi output.

For the **86%+ Small and Marginal Farmers** unable to scale acreage, **Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)** offer a closed-loop risk-management solution. By integrating field crops with dairy livestock, backyard poultry/duckery, inland aquaculture, apiculture, mushroom cultivation, and agroforestry on a single holding, IFS converts the waste of one enterprise into the input of another—crop straw feeds cattle, dung fuels biogas and replenishes soil organic carbon, and fish graze paddy weeds (e.g., Rice–Fish–Duck systems in Assam and West Bengal). Beyond ecological circularity, IFS smooths seasonal cash flows and insulates households against single-crop price or weather crashes. **Prelims 2026 Anchor:** Under the **National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA)**, the **Rainfed Area Development (RAD)** component specifically promotes cluster-based, location-specific **Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)** to enhance productivity and minimise climatic risks in rainfed zones (explicitly rejecting monoculture in rainfed areas or irrigated rice expansion).

### The Two Great Monoculture Traps

#### 1. The Rice–Wheat Rotation in North-West India

Engineered during the 1966 Green Revolution across **Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh** (Trans-Gangetic and Upper Gangetic zones), the intensive Rice–Wheat sequence paired Norman Borlaug's semi-dwarf varieties with tubewell irrigation, subsidized urea, free farm power, and open-ended FCI procurement at Minimum Support Price (MSP). It lifted India from humiliating PL-480 wheat imports to chronic grain surpluses—yet transformed a semi-arid ecological zone (450–650 mm annual rainfall) into an ecological time bomb:

- **Hydrological Insolvency:** Transplanted flooded paddy consumes **3,500–5,000 liters of water per kilogram of rice**. Pumping aquifers to sustain non-native Kharif paddy has saddled Punjab with an estimated **9 BCM annual groundwater deficit** (ICRIER–NABARD).
- **Soil Degradation and Yield Plateaus:** Repeated puddling creates an impermeable subsoil **hardpan** restricting wheat root penetration, strips **Soil Organic Carbon below 0.5%**, triggers severe micronutrient lockups (zinc, iron, manganese), and locks in herbicide-resistant *Phalaris minor* weeds.
- **The Unintended Policy–Pollution Nexus:** To arrest summer evapotranspiration losses from May paddy nurseries, Punjab and Haryana enacted the **Preservation of Subsoil Water Act (2009)**, barring nursery sowing before May 10 and transplanting before June 10. While the law slowed water-table decline by shifting growth onto monsoon rains, it pushed paddy harvests into late October and early November. Left with barely **10–15 days** to sow winter wheat before yield penalties hit, combine-harvesting farmers turned to burning ~19.5 million tonnes of silica-rich paddy straw right as winter thermal inversions settle over Delhi-NCR. Though subsidized residue machinery (>3.24 lakh machines) and biomass buyers cut Punjab and Haryana farm fires by over **90% between 2021 and 2025** (CAQM/PIB data), lasting resolution requires breaking the paddy monoculture itself toward maize, pulses, oilseeds, and cotton.

#### 2. Water-Guzzling Sugarcane in Drought-Prone Maharashtra

Sugarcane is a 12-to-18-month tropical grass demanding **1,800–2,200 mm** of water annually (**~2,000+ liters per kg of sugar**). Yet political economy—anchored by entrenched cooperative sugar mills, assured **Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP)**, and expanding **E20 ethanol diversion**—has concentrated nearly a third of India's cane output in semi-arid **Western Maharashtra and drought-prone Marathwada** (<700 mm annual rainfall).

Sitting atop hard-rock Deccan basalt with poor aquifer storage, sugarcane occupies barely **~4% of Maharashtra's net cropped area** while guzzling **60%–70% of the state's irrigation water**—starving adjacent rainfed millets, pulses, and drinking-water wells. Conversely, the water-abundant **sub-tropical Ganga plains (Uttar Pradesh and Bihar)** enjoy far superior hydro-ecological suitability for sugarcane, though shorter frost-free growing seasons depress sucrose recovery compared to tropical Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu.

## Flagship 2025–2026 Diversification and Productivity Missions

Recognizing that cereal self-sufficiency has matured into an ecological liability while India spends billions annually importing edible oils, pulses, and extra-long staple cotton, the Union Government rolled out four targeted, value-chain diversification missions across 2024–2026 (complementing the FY25 renaming of the National Food Security Mission into the **National Food Security and Nutrition Mission — NFSNM**).

### 1. PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (PMDDKY, 2025–31)

First announced in Union Budget 2025–26, approved by the Union Cabinet on **July 16, 2025**, and formally launched on **October 11, 2025**, PMDDKY is a **six-year (2025–26 to 2030–31)** territorial transformation programme carrying an annual outlay of **₹24,000 crore** and targeting **1.7 crore farmers** directly.

- **Aspirational Agriculture Districts Selection:** Modeled explicitly on NITI Aayog's 2018 Aspirational Districts Programme—but dedicated exclusively to agriculture and allied sectors—PMDDKY targets **100 lagging districts** selected on **three objective indicators**: (i) **low agricultural productivity**, (ii) **low cropping intensity**, and (iii) **below-average agricultural credit disbursement**. District allocation weights each state's share of net cropped area and operational holdings while guaranteeing **at least one district per State**.
- **Saturation Convergence Architecture:** Rather than spawning parallel bureaucracy, PMDDKY mandates **saturation-based convergence of 36 existing Central schemes across 11 Ministries and Departments**, layered with State schemes, FPOs, and CPSE CSR adoption (enabled for FY 2026–28).
- **Governance and Core Objectives:** Executed via a **three-tier structure**—a **District Dhan-Dhaanya Samiti** chaired by the District Collector/DM (drafting agro-climatic District Action Plans with SAUs and KVKs), a State Steering Committee, and dual Union Ministerial/Secretarial oversight committees. It tracks five core goals via real-time monthly digital rankings: (1) enhancing productivity, (2) promoting crop diversification and sustainable farming, (3) expanding panchayat/block-level post-harvest storage, (4) strengthening irrigation, and (5) widening short- and long-term credit. By August 2026, average composite scores across the 100 districts rose from 18.85 (April 2026) to **46.89**.

### 2. Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (*Dalhan Aatmanirbharta Mission*, 2025–31)

India is simultaneously the world's largest producer, consumer, and importer of pulses. Because nearly **74% of India's pulse acreage remains rainfed** (Economic Survey 2025–26 reports ~26% irrigation cover), erratic monsoons routinely ignite protein inflation and force heavy imports of Tur, Urad, Masoor, and yellow peas.

Approved as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme on **October 1, 2025** and launched on **October 11, 2025** at IARI, New Delhi, the **Dalhan Aatmanirbharta Mission** runs for **six years (2025–26 to 2030–31)** with an outlay of **₹11,440 crore** across **489 focus districts**, targeting **~2 crore farmers**:

- **Production and Rice-Fallow Expansion Targets:** Aims to raise domestic pulse output from **242 lakh tonnes to 350 lakh tonnes by 2030–31** and expand cultivated area by **35 lakh hectares**—comprising **24.5 lakh ha** in traditional belts and **10.5 lakh ha** in non-traditional zones, crucially targeting **Eastern India's vast post-Kharif rice fallows** (~11–12 million ha sitting idle after paddy despite residual soil moisture).
- **Seed and Processing Backbone:** Distributes **87.5–88 lakh free seed kits** of pest-resistant, climate-resilient HYVs tracked through the **SATHI** seed-traceability portal and **Krishi Mapper**, while subsidizing **1,000 new pulse processing and packaging units** (at 33% project cost, capped at ₹25 lakh) to curb dal-milling post-harvest losses.
- **The Structural Breakthrough — 100% Assured MSP Procurement:** Historically, farmers shunned pulses because private mandi prices collapsed below MSP during bumper harvests. The mission breaks this risk trap by guaranteeing **100% assured procurement of Tur (Arhar), Urad, and Masoor** from registered farmers via **NAFED and NCCF** (for four years under PM-AASHA Price Support Scheme norms), wired to **e-Samriddhi** and **AgriStack** for transparent direct transfers.

### 3. National Mission on Edible Oils — Oilseeds (NMEO–OS, 2024–31) and Oil Palm (NMEO–OP)

Edible oil represents India's single largest agricultural import drain—though **Economic Survey 2025–26** records import dependence moderating from **63.2% (2015–16)** to **56.25% (2023–24)** as domestic availability reached 121.75 lakh tonnes. India addresses this structural deficit through a dual-track mission architecture:

- **NMEO–Oilseeds (2024–25 to 2030–31):** Approved on **October 3, 2024** with a seven-year outlay of **₹10,103 crore**, focusing on five **primary oilseeds** (Rapeseed-Mustard, Groundnut, Soybean, Sunflower, Sesamum) and three **secondary sources** (Cottonseed, Rice Bran—where India leads the world—and Tree-Borne Oils). It targets raising primary oilseed output from **39 million tonnes (2022–23)** to **69.7 million tonnes by 2030–31** (reaching **43.06 MT** in 2025–26 3rd Advance Estimates) and, alongside NMEO–OP, lifting domestic edible oil production to **25.45 million tonnes (~72% of projected demand)**. Execution relies on **600+ Value Chain Clusters** managed by FPOs and cooperatives, free HYV seed distribution, **60–65 Seed Hubs** plus 50 specialized seed storage units, an online 5-year rolling seed plan on **SATHI**, advanced breeding including **genome editing**, extraction-efficiency upgrades for oil mills, and MSP procurement under **PM-AASHA**.
- **NMEO–Oil Palm (2021–22 onwards):** Launched as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme with **₹11,040 crore** outlay (GoI share ₹8,844 crore; continued into FY 2026–27 via ₹1,143.9 crore Annual Action Plans) across **15 states**, strategically prioritizing the **North-East and Andaman & Nicobar Islands**. Because oil palm yields **~5 times more vegetable oil per hectare** than traditional oilseeds, the mission targeted adding 6.5 lakh ha by 2025–26 (achieving **2.73 lakh ha** new area during 2021–26, lifting total coverage past 6.2 lakh ha, while Crude Palm Oil doubled from 1.91 lakh tonnes in 2014–15 to **3.80 lakh tonnes in 2024–25**, targeting **28 lakh tonnes by 2029–30**). Currently, **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana still generate ~98%** of India's CPO. Crucially, NMEO–OP protects farmers against global palm-oil volatility via a **Viability Gap Payment (VGP)** formula guaranteeing a floor **Viability Price** for Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFBs). *Ecological caution:* Because oil palm is water-intensive (~200–300 L/tree/day) and monocultural, expansion in the biodiversity-rich North-East and Andaman islands must remain strictly restricted to degraded or fallow non-forest lands to prevent deforestation and biodiversity loss.

### 4. Mission for Cotton Productivity (*Kapas Kranti*, 2026–31)

India is the **only country globally cultivating all four recognized *Gossypium* cotton species**, ranking **first in global acreage** and **second in production and consumption**. Yet cotton output slid from 352.48 lakh bales (2020–21) to **290.91 lakh bales (2025–26 provisional)**—falling below domestic textile consumption of **328 lakh bales**—owing to yields stagnating at **428–451 kg/ha** (versus >1,800 kg/ha in China and Brazil), Pink Bollworm resistance to Bt-II hybrids, whitefly/leaf-curl outbreaks, and rainfed moisture stress.

Announced in Union Budget 2025–26 as a five-year mission and formally approved by the Union Cabinet on **May 5, 2026** for **2026–27 to 2030–31**, the **Mission for Cotton Productivity**—officially parenthesized as ***KAPAS KANTI*** in DA&FW Operational Guidelines and Ministry of Textiles releases (popularly also dubbed *Kapas Kranti*)—carries an outlay of **₹5,659.22 crore** aligned with India's **5F Vision (Farm → Fibre → Factory → Fashion → Foreign)**, aiming to boost production to **498 lakh bales** (170 kg lint each) and raise **lint productivity from 440 kg/ha to 755 kg/ha by 2030–31** (benefiting ~32 lakh farmers):

- **Component-I (*Kapas Kranti* — Productivity & Sustainability):** Formally subdivided into **Component-I(A) (DARE/ICAR: ₹555.05 crore)** and **Component-I(B) (DA&FW: ₹3,804.17 crore)** across **140 potential districts in 14 major cotton states**. Scales ICAR–CICR Nagpur breakthroughs—notably **High Density Planting System (HDPS)** (~40% yield gain in trials) and **Closer Spacing (CS)** (>32% gain)—alongside pest-resistant/climate-resilient HYVs and domestic **Extra Long Staple (ELS)** cotton to cut import dependence for premium apparel.
- **Component-II (*Kapas Kanti* — Quality & Traceability):** Implemented by the **Cotton Corporation of India (CCI, Ministry of Textiles; ₹1,000 crore)** to modernize **~2,000 ginning and pressing factories**, slash **trash contamination below 2%**, expand accredited testing labs, digitize cotton mandis via CCI's ***Kapas Kisan*** digital slot-booking and Aadhaar-payment portal, and scale global traceability branding under **Kasturi Cotton Bharat**.
- **Component-III (*Navya Fibre* — Allied Natural Fibres):** Implemented by the **National Jute Board (₹300 crore)** to promote allied natural fibres and circular cotton-waste recycling.

## Irrigation Hydro-Agronomy - Infrastructure Gaps, Aquifer Mining and Governance

### Macro Baseline and Structural Asymmetries

Access to controlled water is the sharpest divider between subsistence and commercial farming. According to **Economic Survey 2025–26** and *Land Use Statistics (2022–23)*, India's **Gross Irrigated Area (GIA)** reached **122.29 million hectares**, covering **55.8% of Gross Cropped Area** (up from 41.7% in 2001–02 and 49.3% in FY16), while **Net Irrigated Area (NIA)** stood at **79.31 million hectares (56.4% of Net Sown Area)**.

Yet two severe asymmetries distort this aggregate progress:

1. **Inter-Crop Irrigation Bias:** While wheat and sugarcane enjoy >90% irrigation coverage and **rice reaches ~67%**, irrigation covers barely **~26% of pulses** and **under 15% of millets**—locking nutritional crops into monsoon volatility (*Economic Survey 2025–26*).
2. **The Groundwater Hegemony:** Whereas pre-1970 irrigation relied primarily on surface canals and village tanks, private pump revolutions have inverted the hydro-portfolio. Today, **groundwater accounts for ~62.8% of NIA** (**tubewells 49.34% + other wells 13.46%**), dwarfing **canals (22.85%)** and **tanks (2.82%)** (*Agriculture Annual Report 2024–25*). India now operates the **world's largest groundwater-irrigated area**, extracting nearly a quarter of global groundwater—more than China and the United States combined.

### The IPC–IPU Gap and Canal Command Failures

India's **Ultimate Irrigation Potential (UIP)** is assessed at **~139.9 million hectares** (58.46 M ha major/medium surface; 17.38 M ha minor surface; 64.05 M ha minor groundwater). Although cumulative **Irrigation Potential Created (IPC)** has crossed 80% of UIP (~113+ M ha), **Irrigation Potential Utilized (IPU)** trails persistently behind by an estimated **~20% gap** (MoJS *Water Vision@2047*).

Five institutional failures drive this canal command breakdown:

- **Last-Mile Missing Links:** State budgets disproportionately fund headline dams and main canals while delaying lined distributaries, field channels (*guls*), and on-farm land leveling under Command Area Development (CAD).
- **Head-End Capture vs. Tail-End Starvation:** Upstream farmers routinely breach outlets to flood water-intensive paddy and sugarcane, leaving tail-end commands dry and forcing downstream cultivators onto private borewells.
- **Abysmal Water Use Efficiency (WUE):** Unlined conveyance seepage, evaporation, and flood-irrigation techniques depress Indian canal WUE to **~35%–40%**, compared to 55%–60% in peer economies and 80%–90% in pressurized micro-irrigation.
- **Fiscal Starvation of O&M:** Politically frozen canal irrigation fees recover less than 15% of Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenditure, leaving reservoirs silted and distributaries choked.
- **Weak Participatory Irrigation Management (PIM):** Statutory **Water Users Associations (WUAs)** remain underfunded and captured by rural elites rather than empowered to enforce equitable *warabandi* (rotational) water delivery.

### The Energy–Groundwater Nexus and CGWB 2025 Assessment

The primary catalyst behind India's aquifer collapse is **zero-marginal-cost farm electricity**. Populist regimes offering free or flat-rate unmetered power (prominently in Punjab, Haryana, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and Karnataka) abolish any economic price on groundwater extraction. Farmers keep automated submersible motors running unattended, chasing falling water tables with deeper borewells—creating a triple doom loop: **DISCOM fiscal insolvency**, rising energy intensity per cubic meter pumped, and mobilization of **geogenic contaminants** (fluoride, arsenic, uranium, and coastal seawater intrusion) from deep fossil strata.

The **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) Dynamic Ground Water Resource Assessment, 2025** (released December 2025) captures both national stabilization gains and acute regional distress:

- **National Water Balance (2025):** Total Annual Groundwater Recharge rose to **448.52 BCM** (up from 432 BCM in 2017 and 446.90 BCM in 2024); Annual Extractable Resources stand at **407.75 BCM**; Annual Extraction totals **247.22 BCM** (~87%–89% for irrigation), yielding a national **Stage of Groundwater Extraction (SoE) of 60.63%**.
- **Block-Level Categorization (6,762 Assessment Units):** **730 units (10.80%) are Over-Exploited** (SoE >100%, declining from 17.2% in 2017), **201 (2.97%) Critical** (90%–100%), **758 (11.21%) Semi-Critical** (70%–90%), **4,946 (73.14%) Safe** (<70%, up from 62.6% in 2017), and **127 (1.88%) Saline**.
- **The Tyranny of the National Average:** The seemingly moderate 60.63% aggregate masks extreme hydrological polarization. **Punjab (~156%–164% SoE), Rajasthan (~148%), Haryana (~135%), and Delhi** relentlessly mine non-renewable aquifers, whereas Eastern Ganga–Brahmaputra states (**Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh**) sit below 45% extraction—suffering *economic water scarcity* from poor rural electrification and fragmentation despite abundant aquifers.

Institutionally, scientific mapping (including **NAQUIM 1.0** across 25 lakh sq km and **NAQUIM 2.0** heliborne high-resolution aquifer mapping) is conducted by **CGWB**, whereas statutory regulation rests with the **Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA)**—constituted under **Section 3(3) of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** and managing industrial/infrastructure NOCs via the **Bhu-Neer** portal (agricultural extraction remains largely exempt from statutory metering).

### Core Hydro-Agronomy Policy Frameworks

#### 1. Atal Bhujal Yojana (*Atal Jal*) and Community Water Governance

Launched in December 2019 (operational **April 1, 2020 to October 15, 2025**) with an outlay of **₹6,000 crore** (50:50 GoI–World Bank financing: ₹1,400 crore institutional strengthening + ₹4,600 crore performance-linked incentives to states), **Atal Bhujal Yojana** pioneered **community-led, demand-side groundwater governance**. Implemented as a pilot across **8,203 water-stressed Gram Panchayats in 229 blocks of 80 districts across seven states**—**Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh** (notably excluding Punjab)—the scheme institutionalized village-level **Water Security Plans**, annual **Gram Panchayat Water Budgets** (completed across all 8,203 GPs), Digital Water Level Recorders (DWLRs), and crop-water convergence.

Independent assessment (Quality Council of India) and 2023–25 monitoring confirmed **groundwater-level improvements in 180 of the 229 blocks**. Following the pilot's October 2025 completion, its participatory blueprint feeds into state replication and the converged **Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari: Catch the Rain (JSJB: CTR)** campaign (launched June 1, 2026), which has mobilized over **1.5 crore geo-tagged low-cost recharge structures** alongside India's First Water Bodies Census (24.2 lakh+ enumerated water bodies).

#### 2. PM-KUSUM Solar Pumps and the Solar–Aquifer Paradox

Implemented by the **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)** and extended (KUSUM 1.0) until **March 31, 2027**, **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan (PM-KUSUM)** targets **34.8 GW** of farm solar capacity with **₹34,422 crore** Central Financial Assistance to de-dieselise irrigation, trim DISCOM agricultural subsidy bills, and deliver reliable daytime power:

- **Component A:** 10,000 MW of decentralized ground/stilt-mounted solar power plants (up to 2 MW) on barren, fallow, pasture, marshy, or cultivable farmer land, supported by a 40 paise/kWh Procurement-Based Incentive to DISCOMs.
- **Component B:** Installation of **14 lakh standalone off-grid solar pumps** (~11.91 lakh installed by September 30, 2026; 30% Central + 30% State subsidy, raised to 50% CFA in NE/hilly/island states).
- **Component C:** Solarization of **35 lakh existing grid-connected agricultural pumps** via **Individual Pump Solarisation (IPS)** and **Feeder-Level Solarisation (FLS)**—reaching **13,111.87 MW** cumulative PM-KUSUM capacity in FY 2025–26 and saving >734 million litres of diesel and 12.59 MT of CO₂.

**The Hydro-Economic Catch:** Off-grid standalone solar pumps (Component B) provide free daytime electricity with zero marginal pumping cost—creating a severe risk of **accelerated groundwater depletion** in over-exploited blocks. The elegant institutional fix lies in **grid-connected net-metering with guaranteed DISCOM buyback (Component C / Gujarat's SKY model)**: when farmers earn cash per kWh for feeding surplus solar energy into the grid, pumping unnecessary water carries a direct financial **opportunity cost**, turning farmers from groundwater miners into solar energy harvesters.

#### 3. PMKSY and Micro-Irrigation (*Per Drop More Crop*)

Launched on **July 1, 2015**, **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)** unified fragmented surface, command, watershed, and micro-irrigation interventions under *Har Khet Ko Pani* and *More Crop Per Drop* (receiving **₹6,587 crore** in Union Budget 2026–27; over ₹64,407 crore cumulative central assistance creating/restoring 24.61 M ha irrigation potential over a decade). Its four pillars span **three distinct Union Ministries**:

1. **Accelerated Irrigation Benefits Programme (AIBP) — Ministry of Jal Shakti (DoWR, RD&GR):** Fast-tracks completion of languishing major and medium irrigation dams/canals (**30.66 lakh ha** potential created since April 2016).
2. **Har Khet Ko Pani (HKKP) — Ministry of Jal Shakti:** Bridges last-mile gaps through **CAD&WM** (23.42 lakh ha command developed), **Surface Minor Irrigation (SMI)**, **Repair, Renovation & Restoration (RRR)** of traditional tanks/water bodies, and targeted groundwater development.
3. **Watershed Development Component (WDC–PMKSY 2.0) — Department of Land Resources (DoLR), Ministry of Rural Development:** Executes ridge-to-valley rainfed soil-moisture conservation, check dams, and degraded-land restoration across 9,434 projects.
4. **Per Drop More Crop (PDMC) — Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (MoA&FW):** Operated inside PMKSY from 2015–16 to 2021–22, and **implemented operationally under RKVY / restructured PM-RKVY since 2022–23**. Offers **55% financial assistance to Small & Marginal Farmers** and **45% to other farmers** for drip and sprinkler installations, supplemented by the **Micro Irrigation Fund (MIF)** with **NABARD** (2% interest subvention to States). By July 2026, PDMC brought **over 115 lakh hectares** under micro-irrigation—still barely **~16%** of India's estimated **70 million ha** micro-irrigation potential.

**Micro-Irrigation Promise vs. Jevons Paradox:** Drip irrigation achieves **80%–90% WUE** (saving 40%–60% water over flood irrigation), enables precision **fertigation** (reducing urea leaching), suppresses weeds, and lifts yields by 20%–40%. Yet micro-irrigation alone cannot solve aquifer collapse: high capital outlay, hard-water emitter clogging, and fragmented landholdings constrain adoption, while the **Jevons Paradox** (rebound effect) frequently sees farmers deploy "saved" water to expand irrigated acreage or switch into water-thirsty sugarcane and bananas—leaving total basin-level groundwater extraction unchanged unless paired with volumetric water-use caps or feeder-level power budgeting.

### The Virtual Water Export Paradox

Coined by hydro-geographer **Tony Allan**, **virtual water** measures the total freshwater embedded in producing a traded agricultural commodity. Despite supporting **~18% of humanity on just ~4% of global freshwater**, India has emerged as the **world's largest net exporter of virtual water**, shipping out an estimated **~95.4 BCM/year**—nearly **four times** India's entire annual domestic and industrial water consumption (*Water Footprint Network / ICRIER–NABARD*).

This hydrological inversion operates on two tiers:

- **International Trade Asymmetry:** India exports vast volumes of water-intensive commodities—**rice (world's largest exporter), raw sugar, cotton, and buffalo meat** (each embedding **2,000–5,000 L/kg**, heavily sourced from scarce *blue* groundwater)—while importing comparatively less blue-water-intensive **pulses and oilseeds** (grown mostly on *green* rainwater abroad).
- **Domestic Inter-State Misallocation:** FCI procurement systematically buys paddy and wheat from water-bankrupt **Punjab and Haryana** (high *Land Productivity*, abysmal *Irrigation Water Productivity*) and rails them into water-abundant **Bihar, West Bengal, and Assam** via PDS—exporting fossil groundwater out of arid basins into wet deltas.

**Policy Re-Orientation (Gulati–ICRIER–NABARD Framework):** Indian agricultural policy must pivot from maximizing **Land Productivity (kg/ha)** to optimizing **Irrigation Water Productivity (kg/m³)**. Shifting paddy procurement incentives eastward toward the water-surplus Eastern Indo-Gangetic Plains ("Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India") while deploying direct per-hectare diversification cash transfers (*Pani Bachao, Paise Kamao* / maize-pulses procurement parity) in North-West India aligns ecological physics with farm profitability.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Spatial Classification Triplet:** Planning Commission delineated **15 Agro-Climatic Zones**; **ICAR–NBSS&LUP** delineated **20 Agro-Ecological Regions (60 Sub-Regions)** adding **soil-scape & Length of Growing Period (LGP)**; **ICAR–NARP** delineated **127 zones** for Zonal Research Stations. Never swap these numbers or nodal agencies.
> - **Rainfed Area Development (RAD) under NMSA (Prelims 2026 Trap):** RAD focuses on **enhancing productivity and minimising climatic risks through cluster-based, location-specific Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)** in rainfed regions—it does **NOT** promote rainfed monoculture or irrigated rice expansion.
> - **PMKSY Ministry Split & PDMC Relocation Trap:** PMKSY is NOT run by one ministry—**AIBP & HKKP** sit with **Ministry of Jal Shakti**, **WDC 2.0** sits with **DoLR (Ministry of Rural Development)**, and **Per Drop More Crop (PDMC)** sits with **MoA&FW**—and critically, **since 2022–23 PDMC is implemented operationally under RKVY / PM-RKVY**, not under the PMKSY budget line.
> - **Atal Bhujal Yojana 7-State Trap:** Covered **Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and UP**. **Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and Chhattisgarh were NOT covered!** Its pilot phase concluded on **October 15, 2025**.
> - **CGWA vs. CGWB (Prelims 2020 Echo):** **Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA)** is the **statutory regulator** constituted under **Section 3(3) of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; **CGWB** is the non-statutory scientific/technical assessment body. Under **CGWB 2025**, **10.80%** of assessment units (730/6,762) are over-exploited and national SoE is **60.63%**—while India holds the **largest area under groundwater irrigation in the world**.
> - **Oil Palm Origins & Price Support Trap (Prelims 2021 Echo):** Oil palm (*Elaeis guineensis*) is native to **West Africa** (not South-East Asia); its oil feeds both cosmetics and first-generation biodiesel. Domestically, **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana produce ~98% of CPO**, and NMEO–OP provides a **Viability Gap Payment (VGP)** for Fresh Fruit Bunches—**NOT** statutory MSP!
> - **Pulses Seasonality & Procurement Precision (Prelims 2020 Echo):** **Gram (Chana)**—not green gram (moong)—accounts for the largest share (~45%–50%) of India's pulse output; **Black gram (Urad)** and **Green gram (Moong)** are cultivated in **both Kharif and Rabi**. Under the **Dalhan Aatmanirbharta Mission**, **100% assured MSP procurement** via NAFED/NCCF applies specifically to **Tur, Urad, and Masoor**.
> - **Cotton Global Uniqueness & Mission Convergence:** India is the **only nation growing all four cultivated *Gossypium* species**. The **Mission for Cotton Productivity (*KAPAS KANTI* / *Kapas Kranti*, 2026–31)** is NOT solely a Textiles scheme—its largest component (**Component-I(B)**, ₹3,804.17 Cr) is run by **DA&FW**, paired with **DARE/ICAR [Component-I(A)]** and **Ministry of Textiles (CCI & National Jute Board)**, targeting **755 kg lint/ha** (498 lakh bales) and modernization of **~2,000 ginning units** by 2030–31.
> - **PMDDKY District Criteria:** The 100 districts are picked on **three specific criteria**—low productivity, low cropping intensity, and low agricultural credit—with **at least one district from every State**, converging **36 existing Central schemes across 11 Ministries**.

> [!map] Atlas & Visual Hook
> - **North-West Aquifer Red Belt (Zone VI Trans-Gangetic & Zone XIV Western Dry):** **Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Western UP, and Western–Northern Rajasthan**—semi-arid alluvial plains with CGWB Stage of Extraction exceeding **100%–160%**, coinciding precisely with intensive tubewell-fed Rice–Wheat monoculture and the post-2009 paddy stubble-burning plume blowing south-east toward NCR.
> - **Deccan Basalt Sugarcane Rain-Shadow Trap (Zone IX Western Plateau & Hills):** **Marathwada** (Beed, Latur, Dharashiv/Osmanabad) and **Western Maharashtra** (Solapur, Sangli, Satara, Pune) lying leeward of the Western Ghats—fractured hard-rock basalt aquifers with low recharge carrying >60% of state irrigation water diverted into sugarcane.
> - **Eastern Rice-Fallow Diversification Frontier (Zones III, IV & VII):** **Eastern UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, West Bengal, and Assam**—humid/sub-humid Gangetic–Mahanadi–Brahmaputra plains with groundwater extraction under 45% and **~11–12 million ha of post-Kharif rice fallows**, designated as the primary expansion theater for **Dalhan Aatmanirbharta** and **NMEO–Oilseeds**.
> - **Oil Palm Dual Geography (Zones XI, II & XV):** Existing production core locked in the irrigated **Krishna–Godavari delta** (Andhra Pradesh & Telangana, ~98% CPO), expanding into humid **North-Eastern foothills** (Assam, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Tripura, Manipur) and **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**.
> - **India's Cotton Productivity Arc:** **Central Black Regur Belt** (Gujarat's Saurashtra/Kutch, Maharashtra's Vidarbha/Marathwada, Madhya Pradesh's Malwa/Nimar—predominantly rainfed), **Southern Belt** (Telangana, Karnataka's Raichur/Dharwad, Andhra Pradesh), and **Northern Irrigated Belt** (Punjab, Haryana, Sri Ganganagar–Hanumangarh in Rajasthan).
> - **Hydro-Agronomic Inversion Visual (NCERT / ICRIER Contrast):** Plot **Land Productivity (kg/ha)** peaking in semi-arid **Punjab/Haryana** against **Irrigation Water Productivity (kg/m³)** peaking in humid **West Bengal, Bihar, and Southern Deltas**—overlay arrows showing India's paradoxical **Virtual Water Export Flow** draining fossil groundwater out of North-West aquifers into both water-abundant Eastern PDS depots and international rice/sugar markets.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **Statutory vs. Technical Groundwater Body Swap:** Attributing CGWB's aquifer assessments to CGWA, or claiming CGWA was created under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 rather than the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
- **Scheme Umbrella & Geographic Exclusion Swaps:** Placing **Per Drop More Crop (PDMC)** exclusively under Ministry of Jal Shakti (ignoring its MoA&FW ownership and post-2022–23 relocation under **PM-RKVY**), or assuming **Punjab** was included in the 7-state **Atal Bhujal Yojana** pilot.
- **Price Assurance Mechanism Inversion:** Equating **NMEO–Oil Palm's Viability Gap Payment (VGP)** with statutory MSP, or claiming the **Dalhan Aatmanirbharta Mission** guarantees 100% open-ended procurement for *all* pulses rather than **Tur, Urad, and Masoor** specifically.
- **Absolute Agronomic Extremes:** Options claiming micro-irrigation eliminates groundwater depletion entirely (ignoring Jevons Paradox) or that off-grid **PM-KUSUM Component B** solar pumps inherently conserve groundwater (ignoring zero-marginal-cost over-pumping without grid buyback).

### 3. Cross-Hub AIR-1 Provocations

1. **GS3 Energy Transition × Hydro-Agronomy:** *"De-dieselizing Indian agriculture through off-grid solar pumps (PM-KUSUM Component B) risks trading carbon emissions for irreversible fossil aquifer collapse."* Evaluate how feeder-level solarization and guaranteed DISCOM power buyback can realign India's energy–water–food nexus.
2. **GS1 Spatial Geography × GS3 Fiscal Federalism:** Despite India launching four major crop-diversification missions across 2024–2026, inter-state virtual water flows still drain semi-arid North-West aquifers to feed the water-abundant East. Analyze why ecological crop zoning fails without restructuring State power subsidies and FCI's regional procurement footprint.
