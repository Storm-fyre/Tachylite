# Chapter 9 - Agrarian Risk Mitigation (PMFBY), Livestock Economics & High-Value Allied Sectors

## The Structural Case - Risk De-Coupling & Allied Growth Engines

India’s agrarian transformation confronts an elementary mathematical constraint: average operational holdings have shrunk below 1.08 hectares, rendering pure cereal monoculture incapable of delivering middle-income household earnings. Simultaneously, intensifying climate volatility—erratic monsoon breaks, terminal heatwaves, and coastal cyclones—exposes uninsured rainfed smallholders to catastrophic balance-sheet ruin.

Resolving this twin bind requires two interlocking levers. The first is **actuarial risk transfer** through modernized crop insurance (PMFBY 2.0 and RWBCIS), preventing weather shocks from triggering debt spirals and distressed land sales. The second is **structural diversification into allied and high-value sectors**—livestock, fisheries, and horticulture—which exhibit dramatically lower land elasticity, steadier year-round cash flows, and superior value addition per unit of water and soil.

Macroeconomic accounts confirm this structural shift. While crop-only growth oscillates sharply with monsoon variance, **livestock (30.87% of Agriculture & Allied GVA in 2023–24)**, **horticulture (~33% of Agri GVA)**, and **fisheries (7.43% of Agri GVA, growing fastest at 7.87%–8.74% annually)** now drive the overwhelming majority of real agrarian income expansion. Together, they convert Indian farming from a seasonal calorie-production system into a resilient, protein- and micronutrient-led rural enterprise economy.

## Agrarian Risk Mitigation - PMFBY 2.0 & RWBCIS

### 1. Core Architecture - Premium Caps, Coverage Stages & Parametric Contrast

Launched on **February 18, 2016** (effective Kharif 2016) to replace the fragmented NAIS and MNAIS regimes, the **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)** established a unified, **area-yield-index** crop insurance architecture. Insurers quote competitive **actuarial premium rates (APR)** through state-level tenders, while farmer liability is strictly capped at uniform, ultra-low slabs:

- **Kharif Crops (foodgrains & oilseeds):** Maximum **2.0%** of Sum Insured.
- **Rabi Crops (foodgrains & oilseeds):** Maximum **1.5%** of Sum Insured.
- **Annual Commercial & Horticultural Crops:** Maximum **5.0%** of Sum Insured.

The balance between the actuarial tender rate and the farmer’s capped share is subsidized **50:50 between the Centre and States**—upgraded to **90:10** for **North-Eastern States** (effective Kharif 2020) and **Himalayan States** (effective Kharif 2023). Unlike legacy schemes, PMFBY removed arbitrary caps on sum insured, insuring farmers up to the full district **Scale of Finance** across four lifecycle stages:

1. **Prevented / Failed Sowing:** Up to 25% of sum insured if adverse weather prevents sowing across >75% of the notified Insurance Unit (IU).
2. **Standing Crop (Comprehensive Yield Loss):** Area-based protection against non-preventable risks—drought, dry spells, floods, widespread pests/diseases, and natural fires.
3. **Mid-Season Adversity:** Immediate on-account relief up to 25% of likely claims when expected yield drops below 50% of normal mid-season.
4. **Localized Calamities & Post-Harvest Losses:** Individual farm-level assessment for hailstorms, landslides, cloudbursts, and inundation—plus **post-harvest cover up to 14 days** for crops lying in *"cut and spread"* condition in the field damaged by cyclones, cyclonic rains, or unseasonal rains.

**PMFBY vs. RWBCIS (Restructured Weather Based Crop Insurance Scheme):** Both schemes share identical farmer premium caps (2% / 1.5% / 5%) and Central continuation outlays (**₹69,515.71 crore cleared by the Union Cabinet in January 2025 through FY 2025–26**; **₹12,200 crore allocated in Union Budget 2026–27**). Their actuarial mechanics, however, diverge sharply:
- **PMFBY** settles main claims via **actual yield shortfall** (comparing current season’s yield against a 7-year moving average *Threshold Yield* derived from Crop Cutting Experiments [CCEs] and remote sensing). This minimizes mismatch with field reality but introduces administrative settlement lags.
- **RWBCIS** is a **parametric weather-index product** that pays out automatically whenever rainfall, temperature, wind speed, or humidity breaches pre-defined thresholds at a Reference Weather Station. It delivers rapid, transparent payouts (especially suited to perennial horticultural crops where CCEs are difficult), but exposes farmers to **basis risk**—where station readings diverge from micro-level farm damage.

Across its first decade (Kharif 2016 through Rabi 2025–26, PIB August 2026), PMFBY insured **92.46+ crore farmer applications** and disbursed **over ₹2.06 lakh crore in claims to 26.33+ crore farmer applications**—returning roughly **₹500 in claims for every ₹100 paid by farmers** in premium.

### 2. PMFBY 2.0 Revamp - Solving Moral Hazard, State Exits & Assessment Lags

Early PMFBY implementation triggered fiscal and operational backlash. Several large states (Bihar, West Bengal, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand, Telangana) exited between 2018 and 2020, citing steep actuarial premiums, insurer windfall profits during normal monsoons, mandatory deductions from KCC loanee accounts without consent, and 6–12 month delays in CCE-based claim payouts.

Beginning **Kharif 2020** and accelerating through **2024–2026**, MoA&FW re-engineered the architecture into **PMFBY 2.0**—triggering a steady rejoining wave (25+ States/UTs participating in 2026, with **West Bengal rejoining from Kharif 2026** and **Bihar onboarding from Rabi 2026–27** in place of its state assistance scheme):

- **Voluntary Enrollment & Subsidy Delinking:** Mandatory bundling for loanee/KCC farmers was abolished from Kharif 2020; PMFBY is now strictly **voluntary for both States and farmers**. Equally critical, the Centre **delinked Central premium subsidy releases from State releases**, ensuring farmers receive proportionate Central-share claims even if a State delays its matching grant. From **Kharif 2024**, the National Crop Insurance Portal (NCIP) auto-levies a **12% penal interest** on insurers failing to clear claims within statutory deadlines.
- **Beed Model (80–110 Cup & Cap) & Alternate Risk Transfer (ART):** To eliminate insurer windfall profits in benign weather years, PMFBY 2.0 allows States to opt out of standard actuarial contracts into three ART frameworks: **Cup & Cap (80–110)** (pioneered in Maharashtra’s Beed district), **Cup & Cap (60–130)**, and **Profit & Loss Sharing**. Under the **Beed 80–110 formula**:
  - Insurer liability is capped at **110% of Gross Premium**; catastrophic claims exceeding 110% are borne by the State Government.
  - Conversely, if total claims fall below **80% of Gross Premium**, the insurer retains a maximum **20% management/handling margin** and **refunds the surplus premium back to the State treasury**.
  - *Trade-off:* Beed protects state exchequers during normal monsoons, but transfers catastrophic tail risk (>110%) back onto fiscal budgets during multi-district mega-droughts.
- **The FIAT Technology Stack (₹824.77 Crore Corpus, Jan 2025):** Replacing manual, fraud-prone village CCEs is the centerpiece of PMFBY 2.0, anchored by the **Fund for Innovation and Technology (FIAT)**:
  - **YES-TECH (Yield Estimation System based on Technology):** Rolled out from **Kharif 2023** (paddy, wheat) and **Kharif 2024** (soybean), YES-TECH assigns a **mandatory minimum 30% weightage** to satellite remote-sensing, biophysical crop modeling, and AI-derived yield estimates alongside CCEs. Deployed across **12 States and 344 districts in 2025–26**, several states escalated adoption far beyond the floor—**Madhya Pradesh shifted to 100% tech-based yield estimation**, Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh adopted **50%**, and Odisha and Karnataka adopted **40%**.
  - **WINDS (Weather Information Network & Data System):** Builds a national hyperlocal grid of **Automatic Weather Stations (AWS)** at the block level and **Automatic Rain Gauges (ARGs)** at the Gram Panchayat level (7 States/UTs onboarded with 16,843 sites approved by July 2026) to feed YES-TECH, lower RWBCIS basis risk, and generate block-level agro-advisories.
  - **CROPIC (Collection of Real-Time Observations and Photos of Crops):** An AI-powered mobile imaging platform integrated with **AgriStack Farmer IDs (*Kisan Pahchaan Patra*)** and YES-TECH. Field officials and farmers capture geotagged, timestamped crop photographs at five phenological stages to validate standing crop health and automate localized damage claims.
  - **DigiClaim Module:** Operationalized from Kharif 2022, DigiClaim directly wires **NCIP** into the **Public Financial Management System (PFMS)** and insurer accounting ledgers, automating direct Aadhaar-seeded claim transfers with real-time farmer tracking.
- **Kharif 2026 Landmark Peril Expansion (PIB Nov 2025 / Feb & Aug 2026):** Addressing two chronic farmer grievances across forest-fringe, Himalayan, North-Eastern, and coastal belts, MoA&FW operationalized two major upgrades from **Kharif 2026**:
  1. **Wild Animal Attack Cover:** Recognized as the **fifth add-on cover under Localized Risks**. States notify vulnerable districts and species (elephants, wild boar, nilgai, deer, monkeys); farmers report attacks within **72 hours** using geotagged photos on the Crop Insurance App *(with add-on cost and SOP execution anchored by State Governments)*.
  2. **Reintroduction of Paddy Inundation:** Restored as a localized calamity cover after being excluded in 2018 due to moral hazard concerns surrounding hydrophilic crops (paddy, jute, mesta, sugarcane)—extending vital flood resilience to coastal and deltaic rice growers.

## Economics of Animal-Rearing (>30% of Agri GVA)

### 1. Macroeconomic Weight & BAHS 2025 Baseline

Animal husbandry functions as rural India’s living savings bank and premier gender-inclusive employment engine. According to the **Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying (DAHD)** and **Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics (BAHS) 2025** (released November 26, 2025 based on the Integrated Sample Survey 2024–25):

- **GVA & Livelihood Anchor:** Livestock’s share in total Agriculture & Allied GVA climbed steadily from **24.38% (2014–15) to 30.87% (2023–24, current prices)**—accounting for **5.49% of India’s national GVA** (~₹15.06 lakh crore in 2024–25). The sub-sector expanded at a **12.77% nominal CAGR** over the decade, anchoring livelihoods for **~8 crore rural households**, with **women comprising ~70% of the dairy workforce**.
- **Livestock Wealth:** India maintains **535.78 million livestock** (including **302.79 million bovines**) and **851.8 million poultry** (*20th Livestock Census, 2019*; digital enumeration for the **21st Livestock Census** commenced October 2024).
- **Production & Global Standing (BAHS 2025 — FY 2024–25):**
  - **Milk (#1 globally since 1998; ~25% of world output):** Reached **247.87 million tonnes** (+3.58% YoY; up 69% from 146.31 MT in 2014–15), comfortably exceeding domestic demand (~243 MT). Per capita availability rose from 319 g/day (2014–15) to **485 g/day**—far above the global average of 328 g/day. **Buffaloes (~45%)** supply the largest share, followed by exotic/crossbred cattle (~33%), indigenous cattle (~21%), and goats (~3%). Top five states—**Uttar Pradesh (15.66%), Rajasthan (14.82%), Madhya Pradesh (9.12%), Gujarat (7.78%), and Maharashtra (6.71%)**—produce **54.09%** of India’s milk.
  - **Eggs (#2 globally after China):** **149.11 billion eggs** (106 eggs/capita/year; **84.5% commercial poultry**, 15.5% backyard); led by **Andhra Pradesh (18.37%)**, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, West Bengal, and Karnataka.
  - **Meat (#4 globally):** **10.50 million tonnes**, half contributed by **poultry meat (~5.18 MT)**; led by **West Bengal (12.46%)**, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana.
  - **Wool:** **34.57 million kg**; dominated by **Rajasthan (47.85%)** and **Jammu & Kashmir (22.88%)**.

### 2. Operation Flood / AMUL to White Revolution 2.0

India’s dairy model represents a historic triumph of **"production by the masses" over "mass production."** Unlike industrial mega-dairies in the US or New Zealand, India’s global lead emerged from **Operation Flood (1970–1996)**—architectured by **Dr. Verghese Kurien** and the **National Dairy Development Board (NDDB, 1965)** using European dairy food-aid monetization to scale Gujarat’s three-tier **Anand/AMUL Pattern**: Village Dairy Cooperative Societies (DCSs) → District Milk Unions → State Marketing Federations.

Yet a glaring organizational gap persists: **organized dairy cooperatives still procure barely ~20%–22% of India’s marketable milk surplus**, leaving nearly two-thirds of surplus milk in informal, unchilled, middleman-controlled markets prone to seasonal price crashes and adulteration.

To close this gap, the **Ministry of Cooperation** and **MoFAHD/DAHD** jointly launched **White Revolution 2.0** (concept unveiled **September 19, 2024**; SOP operationalized **December 25, 2024**) across a five-year horizon (**FY 2024–25 to FY 2028–29**):
- **Quantitative Targets:** Raise dairy cooperative milk procurement by **50%**—reaching **1,007 lakh kg per day by 2028–29**—by forming **75,000 new Multi-purpose Dairy Cooperative Societies (M-DCSs)** in uncovered Gram Panchayats and strengthening **46,422 existing DCSs** (by July 2026: **24,097 new DCSs formed** and **31,394 strengthened**).
- **Funding & Digital Integrity:** Financed primarily through the **Revised NPDD**, equipping village societies with Bulk Milk Coolers, Automatic Milk Collection Units (AMCUs), mandatory **digital fat/protein milk quality testing**, and Rupay KCC accounts (animal husbandry KCC coverage crossed **50.42 lakh farmers in FY 2025–26**).

### 3. Genetic Upgradation - Revised Rashtriya Gokul Mission (RGM)

Despite leading total output, Indian dairy economics suffers from a severe **yield deficit**: average indigenous cow productivity sits far below global peers (though overall bovine yield jumped **36.63%** from 1,648 kg/year in 2013–14 to **2,251 kg/year in 2024–25**, and indigenous cattle yield jumped **44.89%** to 1,343 kg/year—the fastest yield growth rate globally).

Launched in December 2014 as a **Central Sector Scheme**, the **Rashtriya Gokul Mission (RGM)** received a **March 19, 2025 Union Cabinet revision** adding ₹1,000 crore to reach **₹3,400 crore** for the 15th Finance Commission cycle (2021–26). Its core scientific arsenal combines conservation of climate-resilient indigenous breeds (Gir, Sahiwal, Red Sindhi, Tharparkar, Murrah) with frontier reproductive genomics:

- **Genomic Chips (*Gau Chip* & *Mahish Chip*):** Indigenously engineered by **NDDB and ICAR–National Bureau of Animal Genetic Resources (NBAGR)** (alongside *IndiGau*), these unified single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) chips enable early genomic selection of high-yielding indigenous cattle (*Gau Chip*) and buffaloes (*Mahish Chip*).
- **Sex-Sorted Semen (*Gau Sort*) & IVF:** NDDB’s indigenous **Gau Sort** technology sorts female-bearing spermatozoa with >90% accuracy—simultaneously accelerating dairy herd productivity and resolving India’s growing **stray male cattle crisis** caused by tractor mechanization. Complemented by **22+ IVF laboratories** producing High Genetic Merit (HGM) calves and the **Nationwide Artificial Insemination Programme (NAIP)** providing free doorstep AI across 605 low-coverage districts via trained rural **MAITRIs** (8.39+ crore animals covered).
- **2025 Revised RGM Additions:** Introduced a **35% one-time capital subsidy** for establishing **Heifer Rearing Centres** (30 units / 15,000 HGM heifers) and a **3% interest subvention** on farmer loans taken to purchase HGM IVF heifers.

### 4. Infrastructure, Fodder Deficit & Biosecurity Architecture

Genetic potential collapses without cold-chain processing, balanced nutrition, and disease immunity—addressed through four complementary DAHD schemes:

- **Revised National Programme for Dairy Development (NPDD):** Approved by Cabinet on **March 19, 2025** as a **Central Sector Scheme** with a revised outlay of **₹2,790 crore** (2021–26). **Component A** finances village milk chilling, testing labs, 10,000 new DCSs, and 2 Milk Producer Companies (MPCs) in the North-East; **Component B (*Dairying Through Cooperatives*)** runs across 9 states with **JICA ODA loan** financing. By June 2026, NPDD created **168 lakh litres/day (LLPD)** of chilling capacity.
- **Realigned Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund (AHIDF):** Extended through **FY 2025–26** with its corpus nearly doubled from ₹15,000 crore to **₹29,610 crore** (February 2024 Cabinet), **subsuming the erstwhile Dairy Infrastructure Development Fund (DIDF)**. Open to private firms, MSMEs, FPOs, and now **Dairy Cooperatives**, AHIDF provides **3% interest subvention** (raised from DIDF’s 2.5%) and **25% Credit Guarantee cover** (via NABARD) for dairy/meat processing, feed plants, and vaccine manufacturing—approving **418 LLPD** of processing capacity by August 2026. In **Union Budget 2026–27**, GoI further announced a **loan-linked capital subsidy scheme** to set up **private-sector veterinary/para-vet colleges, veterinary hospitals, diagnostic labs, and breeding centres**.
- **National Livestock Mission (NLM) & The Chronic Fodder Deficit:** According to **ICAR–Indian Grassland and Fodder Research Institute (IGFRI), Jhansi** (PIB April/July 2025), barely **8.5 million hectares** (~4% of cropped area) is dedicated to cultivated fodder, producing structural shortfalls of **11.24%–32% in green fodder**, **~23% in dry fodder**, and **>40% in concentrate feeds**. Nutritional deficit alone explains nearly half of India’s unrealized milk yield gap. The Centrally Sponsored **NLM** (realigned July 2021 and **March 2024** to add entrepreneurship for **horses, donkeys, mules, and camels**) tackles this via **50% capital subsidy (up to ₹50 lakh)** for silage, hay, Total Mixed Ration (TMR), and fodder-block units, certified fodder-seed incentives (₹100–₹250/kg), and **100 fodder-centric FPOs** across 19 states.
- **Disease Control — Revised LHDCP, NADCP & Lumpy Skin Disease:** Infectious outbreaks depress milk yields by 20%–50% and trigger sanitary trade bans (SPS barriers). On **March 5, 2025**, Cabinet approved the **Revised Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme (LHDCP)** (₹3,880 crore for 2024–26) uniting three wings:
  1. **NADCP (launched 2019, 100% Central funding):** Targets **100% vaccination** to control and eradicate **Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD)** by 2030 and control **Brucellosis**. By August 2026, **147.16 crore FMD vaccine doses** cut national outbreaks from 132 (2019) to 40 (2025), slashed NSP antibody seroprevalence from 20.8% to 8.1%, and achieved **three consecutive years without Serotype Asia-1 outbreaks**.
  2. **LH&DC:** Encompasses **CADCP** (Peste des Petits Ruminants [PPR] and Classical Swine Fever), **ASCAD**, and **4,019 Mobile Veterinary Units (ESVHD-MVU)** operating via **toll-free 1962** (delivering doorstep care to 276 lakh animals).
  3. **Pashu Aushadhi (New 2025 Component, ₹75 crore):** Retails affordable generic veterinary medicines through PMKSKs and Cooperative Societies.
  - **Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD) & Digital Traceability:** Caused by the vector-borne **Capripox virus** (first detected in Odisha/WB in 2019, with major 2022–23 losses and localized July–September 2026 recurrences in UP/Rajasthan), LSD is countered using India’s indigenous homologous live-attenuated vaccine **Lumpi-ProVacInd** (developed jointly by **ICAR-NRCE Hisar and ICAR-IVRI Izatnagar**). Concurrently, **39+ crore livestock** have been uniquely ear-tagged on the **Bharat Pashudhan (NDLM)** national traceability database. *(Constitutional nuance: Animal Husbandry falls under **Entry 15, State List** under Article 246(3); Central missions strictly supplement state legislative authority.)*

## Fisheries & High-Value Horticulture - The Next Agrarian Frontier

### 1. Fisheries - Inland Aquaculture Boom, Shrimp Trade & Budget 2026–27

Fisheries is India’s **fastest-growing primary sub-sector** (decadal annual average growth of **7.87%–8.74%**), generating **7.43% of Agricultural GVA** and sustaining **~3 crore fishers and fish farmers**. India ranks **#2 globally in total fish production (~8% world share)**, **#2 in aquaculture**, and **#1 in shrimp production and exports**.

**The Inland Inversion:** Total fish output more than doubled (+106%) from **95.79 lakh tonnes (2013–14) to 197.75 lakh tonnes (2024–25)**. Structurally, Indian fisheries has inverted from a marine capture economy into an **inland aquaculture powerhouse**: **inland fisheries now deliver ~76.7% (151.60 lakh tonnes, +147% in a decade)**, while marine capture contributes ~23.3% (46.15 lakh tonnes). High-density, water-thrifty technologies—**Recirculatory Aquaculture Systems (RAS)** (closed-loop water filtration; 12,081 units approved by March 2026) and **Bio-floc systems** (microbial waste-to-protein conversion)—now enable intensive production even in semi-arid interiors like Punjab and Telangana.

- **PMMSY (Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, Sept 2020; ₹20,050 crore, extended through 2025–26; ₹2,500 crore in Budget 2026–27):** The flagship value-chain scheme funding modern fishing harbours, broodbanks, cage culture, cold chains, **2,195 Fish FPOs**, vessel transponders, and group accident insurance (3.3 million beneficiaries).
- **PM-MKSSY (Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana, Feb 2024; ₹6,000 crore, FY 2023–24 to FY 2026–27):** A dedicated PMMSY sub-scheme (50% public finance including World Bank/AFD + 50% private leverage) designed to **formalize the unorganized fisheries economy**. Its digital backbone, the **National Fisheries Digital Platform (NFDP, launched Sept 2024)**, has issued work-based digital IDs to **30+ lakh stakeholders**, unlocking aquaculture crop insurance incentives, performance grants, and export traceability.
- **FIDF (Fisheries & Aquaculture Infrastructure Development Fund, 2018–19; extended April 2023–March 2026):** Provides concessional infrastructure credit via NABARD, NCDC, and SCBs with up to **3% interest subvention** and **credit guarantee cover up to ₹12.50 crore** (225 projects worth ₹6,685.78 crore approved by Jan 2026).
- **Shrimp Exports & Vulnerability:** Indian seafood exports doubled from ₹30,213 crore (2013–14) to **₹62,408 crore in FY 2024–25**, overwhelmingly anchored by **frozen shrimp** (*Litopenaeus vannamei*, 11.84 lakh tonnes output; Andhra Pradesh cluster; US and China primary markets). Heavy market and single-species concentration creates acute vulnerability to **US anti-dumping/countervailing tariffs**, EU/Japanese **antibiotic-residue SPS rejections**, and pond diseases (WSSV/EHP). Policy counters include indigenous **Andaman shrimp broodstock centres**, Basic Customs Duty cuts on **Surimi and shrimp-feed hydrolysates (down to 5%)**, and strict NFDP traceability.
- **Union Budget 2026–27 Landmark Blue-Economy Push (Record ₹2,761.80 Crore Outlay):**
  1. **Integrated Development of 500 Reservoirs & Amrit Sarovars:** Harnesses India’s **31.5 lakh hectares of inland reservoirs** and **68,827 Amrit Sarovars** to build coastal and inland fisheries value chains integrating **~200 fisheries startups**, women-led collectives, and Fish FPOs.
  2. **Deep-Sea & EEZ Fiscal Unlock:** Makes **fish catch by Indian vessels in the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) and High Seas duty-free**, treats landings at foreign ports as **exports**, and raises the duty-free import limit for seafood-processing inputs from **1% to 3%**—complementing the **2025 EEZ/High Seas Sustainable Harnessing Rules** and the fully digital **Marine Fisheries Census 2025** (*VyAS* apps).

### 2. High-Value Horticulture, Clean Plants & Niche Commodity Missions

Horticulture represents India’s quiet "Golden Revolution." From just ~15% of gross cropped area (**297.21 lakh ha**), horticulture generates **~33% of Agricultural GVA**, achieving an all-time record output of **386.92 million tonnes in FY 2025–26 (Third Advance Estimates, Nov 2026)**—surpassing India’s total foodgrain tonnage (~376.56 MT) on average yields nearly five times higher. India ranks **#2 globally in fruits and vegetables** (after China) and **#1 globally in mango, banana, papaya, spices, ginger, okra, dry onion, coconut, and makhana**.

- **Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture (MIDH):** Launched in 2014–15 as a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme** under *Krishonnati Yojana* (**60:40** Centre–State; **90:10** NE & Himalayan states; **100% Central** for NHB, CDB, and CIH). MIDH consolidates **NHM** (non-NE/Himalayan), **HMNEH** (NE & Himalayas), **National Horticulture Board (NHB, Gurugram)**, **Coconut Development Board (CDB, Kochi)**, **Central Institute for Horticulture (CIH, Medziphema, Nagaland)**, and **National Bamboo Mission (NBM)**. Its **2025 Restructured Operational Guidelines** expanded coverage to all districts, ultra-high-density orchards, trellises, soilless cultivation, exotic/medicinal crops, and solar post-harvest assets—alongside the **55-cluster Horticulture Cluster Development Programme (HCDP)**.
- **Clean Plant Programme (CPP — Cabinet Approved August 9, 2024; ₹1,765.67 Crore):** Perennial fruit orchards stay productive for 15–30 years; planting virus-infected saplings locks farmers into permanent low yields and export rejections. Approved as a **Central Sector Scheme under MIDH** (backed by a **$98 million Asian Development Bank loan**) and executed by **NHB with ICAR** (in technical partnership with **Israel and the Netherlands**), CPP rests on three pillars:
  1. **9 World-Class Clean Plant Centres (CPCs):** Including three in Maharashtra—**Pune (grapes)**, **Nagpur (citrus)**, **Solapur (pomegranates)**—and **CITH Srinagar & Mukteshwar** for temperate fruits (apple, almond, walnut), equipped with High-Throughput Sequencing (HTS) diagnostics, tissue culture, and cryotherapy to maintain virus-free **G1 mother blocks**.
  2. **Accredited Nursery Scaling:** Capital grants (**₹3 crore for large** and **₹1.5 crore for medium** nurseries) targeting **8 crore certified disease-free saplings annually**.
  3. **Certification & Traceability:** Voluntary regulatory certification framework auditing nursery propagation from G1 stock to orchard gate.
- **Sericulture (*Silk Samagra-2*, 2021–22 to 2025–26):** Implemented by the **Central Silk Board** (**Ministry of Textiles**—not MoA&FW!), India is the **world’s #2 silk producer** (**41,121 MT**) and the **only country commercially producing all five varieties of silk**—**Mulberry, Tropical Tasar, Oak Tasar, Eri, and Muga** (Assam’s golden GI-tagged silk). *Silk Samagra-2* focuses on *Atmanirbharta* in international-grade **Bivoltine raw silk** to curb Chinese imports while empowering smallholders, women, SCs, and tribal forest-based tasar rearers.
- **Apiculture (*Sweet Revolution* & National Beekeeping & Honey Mission — NBHM):** Launched in 2020 under Atmanirbhar Bharat (**₹500 crore**; extended through **2025–26** with ₹370 crore balance) and implemented by the **National Bee Board (NBB)**. NBHM runs three Mini-Missions (pollination productivity, post-harvest processing, R&D), backed by the **Madhukranti Portal** for digital source traceability and NMR labs combating C3/C4 sugar-syrup adulteration. Beyond honey/beeswax income for landless youth, managed bee colonies deliver a **20%–50% cross-pollination yield multiplier** for mustard, sunflower, pulses, and apple orchards.
- **National Makhana Board (Bihar — Constituted Sept 14, 2025):** Announced in Budget 2025–26 and formally launched in Bihar under **DA&FW (MoA&FW)** alongside a **₹476.03 crore Central Sector Scheme for Development of Makhana (2025–26 to 2030–31)**. Makhana (*Euryale ferox*, aquatic fox nut) is concentrated in Bihar’s Kosi–Mithila–Seemanchal wetlands (Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnia, Katihar, Saharsa, Supaul; **ICAR–NRC on Makhana in Darbhanga**). Bihar supplies **~75% of India’s output** (60,000 MT of India’s 80,590 MT in 2025–26 2nd AE) and **80%–85% of global supply**. The Board targets mechanized harvesting/popping (eliminating hazardous manual pond diving), quality seed production, FPO collectivization for marginalized fisher/landless communities and women, and global superfood branding.
- **Union Budget 2026–27 High-Value Plantations & Temperate Nut Suite:** Marking a decisive shift from staple cereals toward high-margin export commodities, Budget 2026–27 unveiled four targeted initiatives (anchored by an initial **₹350 crore** allocation for coconut, cashew, and cocoa alongside dedicated hill and forestry programmes):
  1. **Coconut Promotion Scheme:** India leads global coconut output (**30.37% world share**; ~20,301–21,374 million nuts; **Kerala** largest area, **Tamil Nadu** top producer, **Andhra Pradesh** highest productivity; 30 million livelihoods). The scheme focuses on **replanting senile, low-yielding palms** with elite disease-resistant hybrids and scaling value-added copra/virgin-oil clusters.
  2. **Dedicated Programme for Indian Cashew & Cocoa:** Aims to end India’s heavy import dependence on raw cashew nuts (from West Africa) and cocoa beans, expand domestic processing, and establish **Indian Cashew and Indian Cocoa as premium global brands by 2030**.
  3. **Temperate Nut Programme (Walnuts, Almonds & Pine Nuts):** Rejuvenates senile Himalayan orchards and expands high-density cultivation of **walnuts** (J&K hub), **almonds**, and **pine nuts (*chilgoza*)** across J&K, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand with youth-led value addition.
  4. **Sandalwood Cultivation & Post-Harvest Ecosystem:** Partners with States to restore India’s heritage sandalwood ecosystem through legal cultivation incentives and value-added post-harvest processing.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **PMFBY Premium & Scope Traps:** Farmer premiums are **NOT** a flat 2% across all seasons—they are **2% Kharif, 1.5% Rabi, and 5% annual commercial/horticultural**. Enrollment is now **voluntary for both States and all farmers** (mandatory loanee bundling ended in Kharif 2020). Post-harvest cover applies for **14 days** *only* for crops kept in *"cut and spread"* condition in the field, and **Wild Animal Attack** (5th localized add-on) + **Paddy Inundation** entered from **Kharif 2026**.
> - **PMFBY vs. RWBCIS Actuarial Distinction:** PMFBY is an **area-yield-index** scheme (subject to CCE/satellite estimation); RWBCIS is a **parametric weather-index** scheme (faster payout, higher **basis risk**).
> - **Ministry Mismatch Trap (*Silk Samagra-2* vs. *NBHM/Makhana*):** **Sericulture (*Silk Samagra-2* & Central Silk Board)** is administered by the **Ministry of Textiles**, **NOT** the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA&FW). Conversely, **NBHM (Sweet Revolution)**, **MIDH**, **Clean Plant Programme**, and the **National Makhana Board** fall under **MoA&FW**, while **White Revolution 2.0** is co-led by the **Ministry of Cooperation** and **MoFAHD**.
> - **Inland vs. Marine Fisheries Inversion:** **Inland fisheries & aquaculture (~76.7%)**—not marine capture (~23.3%)—account for over three-quarters of India’s 197.75 lakh tonnes fish output. Yet **frozen shrimp** (predominantly brackishwater coastal aquaculture) dominates India’s ₹62,408 crore seafood export earnings.
> - **Livestock Rankings & State Leaders (BAHS 2025):** India is **#1 in Milk** (**UP** #1, Rajasthan #2), **#2 in Eggs** (**Andhra Pradesh** #1), and **#4 in Meat** (**West Bengal** #1, UP #2)—while **buffaloes (~45%)** produce *more* of India’s total milk than crossbred cows (~33%) or indigenous cows (~21%).
> - **Constitutional Subject & Clean Plant Trap:** **Animal Husbandry** is a **State Subject** (Entry 15, List II). **Clean Plant Programme (2024)** targets **virus-free perennial fruit planting material** via 9 CPCs (implemented by **NHB + ICAR** with ADB loan)—*not* transgenic GM crops or cereal seeds.
> - **Coconut State Leaders (Area vs. Output vs. Yield):** **Kerala** holds the largest *area*, **Tamil Nadu** leads total *production*, and **Andhra Pradesh** records the highest *productivity*.

> [!info] Mains Arsenal
> - **Three-Pillar Analytical Frame for Allied Diversification (GS3):** Frame allied sectors as solving India’s **Trilemma of Smallholder Farming**—(1) *Land Constraint* (livestock, apiculture, RAS aquaculture, and high-density horticulture decouple income from acreage); (2) *Risk Concentration* (milk and eggs supply daily/weekly liquidity buffering seasonal crop failures); and (3) *Nutritional & Gender Equity* (70% women workforce in dairy; affordable protein via eggs, fish, and dairy combating hidden hunger).
> - **Data Anchors That Elevate GS3 Answers:**
>   - **PMFBY 2.0:** ₹2.06L+ cr claims paid (~₹5 returned per ₹1 farmer premium); MP at 100% YES-TECH; Kharif 2026 wild-animal + paddy-inundation upgrade.
>   - **Livestock:** 30.87% of Agri GVA (5.49% total GVA); 247.87 MT milk (485 g/day); 11%–32% green & 23% dry fodder deficit (IGFRI); 147.16 cr FMD vaccine doses (zero Asia-1 in 3 yrs).
>   - **Fisheries & Horticulture:** 197.75 LMT fish (+106%; 76.7% inland); ₹62,408 cr seafood exports; 386.92 MT horticulture (~33% Agri GVA, exceeding foodgrain tonnage); 9 Clean Plant Centres (₹1,765.67 cr); National Makhana Board (Bihar = 80%–85% global share).
> - **Balanced Policy Critiques (Avoiding Blind Cheerleading):**
>   - *PMFBY 2.0:* High basis risk in weather indices, delayed state premium shares, exclusion of non-notified crops/oral tenants, and Beed 80–110 leaving states exposed to >110% catastrophe tail risk.
>   - *Dairy & Livestock:* Chronic fodder/concentrate deficits, unorganized sector capturing ~75%+ of marketable milk, methane emissions (enteric fermentation) vs. smallholder livelihoods, and antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in commercial poultry/shrimp ponds.
>   - *Fisheries & Horticulture:* Over-dependence on *vannamei* shrimp + US market tariffs, post-harvest cold-chain deficits for perishables, and viral/climate vulnerability of Himalayan temperate orchards.

**Verified Prelims PYQ Focal Points:**
- **Prelims 2026:** Core objective of the **Rainfed Area Development (RAD)** initiative under NMSA—minimizing climatic risks via **Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)** combining crops with horticulture, livestock, fishery, and agroforestry.
- **Prelims 2016:** **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)**—crop/season-differentiated farmer premium caps (*2% Kharif / 1.5% Rabi / 5% commercial-horticultural* vs. uniform 2% trap) and 14-day post-harvest cover against cyclones and unseasonal rains.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **Uniform Premium & Mandatory Bundling Fallacy:** Framing PMFBY as charging a flat 2% premium across all seasons or remaining mandatory for KCC loanee farmers, or overlooking the Kharif 2026 inclusion of wild animal attacks and paddy inundation.
- **Ministry & Sub-Scheme Attribution Swaps:** Placing *Silk Samagra-2* / Central Silk Board under MoA&FW instead of the **Ministry of Textiles**, or attributing *White Revolution 2.0* exclusively to DAHD while omitting the **Ministry of Cooperation**.
- **Marine-vs-Inland & Cattle-vs-Buffalo Output Inversions:** Claiming marine fisheries still outweigh inland aquaculture in India’s total fish output, or asserting that crossbred/indigenous cows supply more national milk than **buffaloes**.
- **Scheme Scope Confusion (*Clean Plant* & *AHIDF*):** Mistaking the **Clean Plant Programme (2024)** for an organic/natural farming or GM-seed mission rather than virus-free perennial fruit nurseries under NHB/ICAR, or forgetting that **DIDF has been subsumed into AHIDF** (making dairy cooperatives eligible for 3% interest subvention).

### 3. Cross-Hub AIR-1 Provocations

1. **Climate Mitigation vs. Smallholder Protein Security (GS3 Agriculture + Environment):** India’s livestock herd drives >30% of Agricultural GVA and rural gender equity, yet enteric fermentation accounts for the bulk of India’s agricultural methane emissions. How can genomic selection (*Gau Chip*), *Gau Sort* sex-sorted semen, balanced fodder rations, and FMD eradication simultaneously raise milk productivity and bend India’s livestock methane intensity curve without penalizing smallholder livelihoods?
