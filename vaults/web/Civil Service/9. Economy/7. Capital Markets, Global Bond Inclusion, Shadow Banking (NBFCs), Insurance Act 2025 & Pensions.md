# Chapter 7 - Capital Markets, Global Bond Inclusion, Shadow Banking (NBFCs), Insurance Act 2025 & Pensions

Financial markets are the transmission grid matching surplus units (households, insurers, sovereign funds) with deficit units (corporations, infrastructure SPVs, governments) across risk and maturity horizons. Between 2024 and 2026, India's non-bank financial architecture vaulted into global institutional scale: Indian G-Secs completed staggered entry into **all three major global EM bond indices**, SEBI reined in hyper-speculative retail derivatives and expanded **optional T+0 settlement**, RBI hardened shadow banking through **Scale-Based Regulation (SBR)** and expanded **Co-Lending Arrangements**, Parliament enacted **100% Insurance FDI** via the ***Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha Act, 2025*** (notified February 5, 2026), and the **Unified Pension Scheme (UPS)** redefined public retirement liabilities.

## Money Market Architecture & Equity Market Ecosystem

### Money Market Instruments (Maturity ≤ 1 Year — RBI Domain)

The **Money Market** trades high-liquidity, short-term debt instruments with original maturities of **up to one year**, governed by the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** to smooth day-to-day cash-flow mismatches and anchor monetary transmission. Every Prelims question in this zone turns on *who can issue*, *whether collateral is required*, and *maximum maturity*:

- **Call, Notice & Term Money:** **Uncollateralized** inter-bank lending restricted strictly to Scheduled Commercial Banks (excluding Local Area Banks), eligible Cooperative Banks, and Primary Dealers (corporates, mutual funds, and NBFCs are excluded). Maturities are cleanly tiered: **Call Money** (**1 day / overnight**), **Notice Money** (**2 to 14 days**), and **Term Money** (**15 days to 1 year**). Banks lean on Call Money primarily to plug daily **CRR** deficits.
- **Treasury Bills (T-Bills):** Sovereign, **zero-coupon** (non-interest-bearing) money-market securities issued **exclusively by the Central Government**—**State Governments cannot issue T-Bills**—at a discount to face value and redeemed at par on maturity. Issued via RBI auctions in three tenors (**91-day, 182-day, and 364-day**; minimum ₹25,000), T-Bills carry zero sovereign default risk and qualify for bank SLR.
- **Cash Management Bills (CMBs):** Introduced jointly by the Union Government and RBI in 2010 to bridge temporary sovereign cash-flow mismatches. CMBs share every generic attribute of T-Bills (zero-coupon, issued at discount, SLR-eligible) except one defining legal boundary: **maturity strictly less than 91 days**.
- **Commercial Papers (CPs):** **Unsecured** short-term money-market **promissory notes** issued at a discount by creditworthy **corporates, Primary Dealers, and All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs)** (minimum credit rating **`A3`**) to fund working capital outside bank loans. Tenors run from **7 days to 1 year** in minimum denominations of ₹5 lakh; because CPs are unsecured, defaults (as during the 2018 IL&FS crisis) immediately freeze wholesale liquidity.
- **Certificates of Deposit (CDs):** Negotiable, dematerialized, **unsecured** short-term time-deposit instruments issued at a discount by **Scheduled Commercial Banks** (including Regional Rural Banks and Small Finance Banks, but **excluding Payments Banks** and LABs) for **7 days to 1 year**, and by **AIFIs** for **1 to 3 years** (minimum ₹5 lakh). Unlike fixed deposits, CDs are freely tradable in secondary markets; unlike T-Bills, they are *never* issued by RBI.
- **TREPS (Triparty Repo Dealing System):** Launched in **November 2018** by the **Clearing Corporation of India Ltd (CCIL)** to replace **CBLO (Collateralized Borrowing and Lending Obligations)**. TREPS is a **collateralized** short-term money-market borrowing/lending platform (1 day to 1 year, overwhelmingly overnight) backed by G-Secs and T-Bills, where **CCIL acts as the tri-party agent** managing collateral valuation, haircuts, and default settlement. Crucially, unlike inter-bank Call Money, TREPS opens sovereign-backed overnight funding to **mutual funds, insurers, NBFCs, and eligible corporates**, making it the single deepest segment of India's money market.

### Equity Markets, Settlement Modernization & SEBI Governance

The **Capital Market** channels long-term debt (>1 year) and equity (perpetual ownership) capital under the statutory oversight of the **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** (*SEBI Act, 1992*; appellate body: **Securities Appellate Tribunal — SAT**):

- **Primary vs. Secondary Markets & Issuance Routes:** The **Primary Market** creates fresh productive capital directly for issuers via four routes: **IPO** (maiden public listing), **FPO** (follow-on public offer by an already-listed firm), **Rights Issue** (pro-rata offer to existing shareholders at a discount), and **QIP (*Qualified Institutional Placement*)**—a streamlined private placement exclusively to registered Qualified Institutional Buyers (mutual funds, FPIs, insurers, banks) that lowers time and underwriting costs compared to an FPO. *(By contrast, a pure **Offer for Sale — OFS** transfers shares from existing promoters/investors to buyers without infusing new cash into the company.)* The **Secondary Market** (NSE, BSE) provides continuous price discovery and liquidity, supported by two electronic depositories—**NSDL** (1996) and **CDSL** (1999)—which hold securities in dematerialized form.
- **T+1 & T+0 Instant Settlement:** After completing a full market-wide transition to **T+1 settlement** (funds and shares settled one business day after trade) in January 2023—far ahead of the US and EU—India launched **optional T+0 (same-day) settlement** in March 2024. Effective **January 31, 2025**, SEBI scaled optional T+0 from 25 pilot stocks to the **top 500 listed stocks** by market capitalization, enabling qualified stock brokers, custodians, and FPIs/QIBs to participate alongside optional T+0 block-deal windows—cutting counterparty risk and unlocking trapped margin liquidity.
- **SEBI Market-Integrity Pillars & 2024–2026 Enforcement:**
  - **Insider Trading vs. Front-Running:** **Insider Trading** (governed by *SEBI PIT Regulations, 2015*) exploits **Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI)** (e.g., unannounced earnings or M&A) by corporate insiders or connected persons. **Front-Running** occurs when a broker, dealer, or asset-management insider trades in a personal account *ahead* of a known large pending institutional client order to profit from the subsequent price impact; SEBI mandated institutional surveillance systems across all Asset Management Companies (AMCs) in 2024 to curb AMC front-running.
  - **Related Party Transactions (RPTs) & LODR:** Under *SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements — LODR)* rules (tightened in 2024–2025), material transactions between a listed firm and its promoter-linked entities require prior Audit Committee approval (voted strictly by **Independent Directors**) and minority-shareholder clearance—blocking corporate tunnelling.
  - **Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) & FPIs:** Privately pooled investment vehicles for HNIs and institutions operate in three SEBI tiers: **Category I** (venture capital, angel, SME, social/infra funds receiving regulatory incentives), **Category II** (private equity, real-estate, and debt funds prohibited from operational leverage), and **Category III** (hedge funds employing complex/leveraged trading strategies). Following RBI–SEBI coordination, regulators banned banks/NBFCs from routing stressed loans through AIFs to disguise NPAs (**anti-evergreening curbs**). For **Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs)** (Cat I sovereign/regulated funds vs. Cat II unregulated funds), SEBI enforces granular **beneficial-ownership disclosures** on entities holding highly concentrated single-group equity stakes to block circumvention of Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS) and Press Note 3 norms.
  - **Retail F&O Derivatives Crackdown:** After SEBI empirical audits revealed that **>91%–93% of individual Futures & Options (F&O) traders suffered net losses** (burning ₹1.8+ lakh crore over FY22–FY24 while diverting household savings into hyper-speculative expiry-day bets), SEBI enforced a sweeping **six-step curb** across November 2024–April 2025: **(i)** restricting each exchange to **only one benchmark weekly expiry**, **(ii)** tripling minimum contract sizes (**₹15–20 lakh** notional), **(iii)** mandating **upfront option-premium collection** from buyers, **(iv)** withdrawing **calendar-spread margin offsets** on expiry day, **(v)** imposing an additional **+2% Extreme Loss Margin (ELM)** on short options on expiry day, and **(vi)** real-time intraday position-limit monitoring—subsequently reinforced by **Union Budget 2026–27's sharp hike in F&O STT**.

## Debt Markets, Global Bond Inclusion, GIFT City & Illicit Financial Channels

### The Corporate Bond Gap, CDMDF & RBI Retail Direct

India's financial system remains heavily bank-skewed: outstanding **corporate bonds hover around ~17%–18% of GDP** (compared to >80%–120% in the US and South Korea), concentrated overwhelmingly in top **`AAA`/`AA+`** public and conglomerate issuers (>80% of volume) with thin secondary-market trading. Because mutual funds and insurers shy away from lower-rated `A`/`BBB` papers, mid-sized manufacturing and infrastructure firms stay chained to shorter-tenure bank loans—exposing the economy to recurrent **Asset-Liability Mismatches**.

Two institutional innovations address bond-market depth and fragility:
- **Corporate Debt Market Development Fund (CDMDF):** Operationalized in **2023** as a SEBI-regulated close-ended backstop AIF (managed by **SBI Funds Management**) to prevent Franklin Templeton-style debt-mutual-fund redemption spirals. Funded by an initial **₹3,000 crore** corpus contributed by AMCs and specified open-ended debt mutual funds, CDMDF is backed by a **10x sovereign-guaranteed credit line of ₹30,000 crore** from the National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company (**NCGTC**), creating a **₹33,000 crore liquidity shield**. During periods of acute market dislocation triggered by SEBI, CDMDF purchases investment-grade corporate bonds from distressed debt schemes at fair value to break fire-sale contagion.
- **RBI Retail Direct (2021 & 2024–26 Digital Upgrades):** Enables retail individuals to open zero-fee **Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) accounts** directly with RBI to bid non-competitively in primary auctions of Central G-Secs, T-Bills, State Government Securities (SGS), and Sovereign Gold Bonds, and trade in the secondary **NDS-OM** market. Enhanced with a dedicated mobile application (2024) and **auto-bidding / T-Bill SIP** tools, Retail Direct diversifies sovereign debt ownership away from commercial banks' captive SLR holdings.

### Sovereign Global Bond Index Inclusion (2024–2026 Milestone)

In March 2020, RBI carved out the **Fully Accessible Route (FAR)**—removing all quantitative FPI investment caps on designated liquid benchmark G-Secs (expanded in June 2026 to cover all new Sovereign Green Bonds). That structural plumbing unlocked India's historic integration into **all three premier emerging-market sovereign bond indices**:

1. **J.P. Morgan GBI-EM Global Diversified Index:** Staggered entry across 10 months (**June 28, 2024 – March 31, 2025**) at `1%` per month, reaching the maximum **`10%` index cap** and catalyzing ~$20–25 billion+ in passive foreign debt inflows.
2. **Bloomberg Emerging Market Local Currency Government Index:** Phased inclusion over 10 months (**January 31, 2025 – October 2025**), also reaching its **`10%` weight ceiling**. *(Precision note: In January 2026, Bloomberg **deferred** India's induction into its much larger flagship **Bloomberg Global Aggregate Index**, keeping it under review over operational and clearing frictions such as domestic T+1 vs. global T+2 cycles and pre-funding rules.)*
3. **FTSE Russell Emerging Markets Government Bond Index (EMGBI):** Completed a six-month phased induction (**September 2025 – February 2026**), assigning India a **`~9.35%–9.48%` weight** (second largest behind China).

**Macroeconomic Balance Sheet:** Passive global inclusion lowers India's long-term sovereign borrowing cost, diversifies the investor base, bolsters the BoP Capital Account, and frees domestic bank deposits to finance private Capex. The countervailing risk is **imported rate volatility**: sticky foreign holdings make Indian G-Sec yields and the rupee more sensitive to US Federal Reserve policy swings, global risk-off shocks, and fiscal slippage—narrowing domestic policymakers' margin for fiscal indiscipline.

### GIFT City IFSCA, Black Economy, Tax Havens & P-Notes

- **GIFT City & IFSCA:** Located in Gandhinagar, *Gujarat International Finance Tec-City* houses India's maiden **International Financial Services Centre (IFSC)**—a specialized offshore zone legally treated as a **foreign jurisdiction under FEMA** so international capital can transact in freely convertible foreign currencies without onshore capital-control frictions. Governed by the **International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA)**—a unified statutory regulator under the ***IFSCA Act, 2019*** (operational 2020) consolidating the jurisdictions of **RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA** within IFSC—GIFT City anchors offshore International Banking Units (IBUs), aircraft and ship leasing, the **India International Bullion Exchange (IIBX)**, foreign university campuses, global reinsurance, and direct overseas equity listings. Union Budget 2026–27 sharpened its competitive edge by doubling GIFT City's **tax holiday window from 10 to 20 years** (out of 25), followed by a flat 15% tax rate.
- **Black Economy, Tax Havens & Participatory Notes (P-Notes / ODIs):** Unreported illicit cash flows (**black money**) historically exited India via **hawala** networks and **trade misinvoicing** (over-invoicing imports / under-invoicing exports), pooled in shell companies inside low-tax secrecy jurisdictions (**tax havens**), and returned disguised as tax-free foreign capital—a cycle called **round-tripping**. India dismantled the core loopholes via **GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rules)**, renegotiated bilateral **DTAAs** with Mauritius, Singapore, and Cyprus (2016 protocols ending capital-gains exemptions + OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test), **PMLA 2002**, and CRS/FATCA Automatic Exchange of Information.
- **Participatory Notes (P-Notes / Offshore Derivative Instruments — ODIs):** Financial instruments issued offshore by SEBI-registered FPIs to foreign investors seeking returns linked to Indian equities or debt **without registering directly with SEBI**. Once accounting for >50% of FPI inflows in 2007 and criticized for masking beneficial owners, P-Notes have been tightly cordoned off by mandatory KYC/AML reporting, ban on speculative leverage, and **SEBI's December 2024 circular** prohibiting FPIs from issuing ODIs with **derivatives as the underlying** (and requiring a separate, dedicated FPI registration strictly for ODI issuance)—shrinking P-Notes to **~1%–2% of total FPI assets under custody**.

## Shadow Banking (NBFCs) - ALM Crises, 4-Layer SBR, Co-Lending & SROs

### Functional Boundary & the 2018–19 ALM Contagion

**Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)** are companies registered under the *Companies Act* engaged in lending, leasing, hire-purchase, and investment—regulated predominantly by RBI under Chapter III-B of the *RBI Act, 1934* (including **Housing Finance Companies — HFCs**, whose regulation shifted from NHB to RBI in 2019; meanwhile, stock brokers, insurers, chit funds, and Nidhi companies remain outside RBI's NBFC remit). Three hard legal walls separate NBFCs from Scheduled Commercial Banks:
1. NBFCs **cannot accept demand deposits** (current/savings accounts);
2. NBFCs are **outside the payment and settlement system** and cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves;
3. Deposits placed with deposit-taking NBFCs (`NBFC-D`) carry **zero DICGC deposit insurance**, and **NBFCs have no direct access to RBI's Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) repo window** *(directly tested in UPSC Prelims 2024)*.

**Why Shadow Banking Triggered Systemic Stress (IL&FS 2018 & DHFL 2019):** Historically operating under light-touch arbitrage, major infrastructure and housing NBFCs financed **long-duration, illiquid 7–15 year projects** using **short-term wholesale Commercial Papers (30–90 days)** rolled over repeatedly by liquid debt mutual funds—a textbook **Asset-Liability Mismatch (ALM)**. When **IL&FS** defaulted in September 2018 (followed by **DHFL** in 2019), mutual funds halted CP rollovers across the sector overnight, freezing credit for real estate, automobiles, and MSMEs and proving that interconnected shadow banks transmit bank-scale systemic risk.

### RBI's 4-Layer Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) & Co-Lending Overhaul

Abandoning the blunt binary of "Systemically Important vs. Non-SI", RBI enforced **Scale-Based Regulation (SBR)** from October 2022, calibrating capital, governance, and liquidity intensity proportionally to size and systemic interconnectedness across **four tiers** (`Base`, `Middle`, `Upper`, `Top`—detailed in Table 2 below). Two milestones matured across 2025–2026:
- **90-Day NPA Harmonization:** Even the smallest **Base Layer (`NBFC-BL`)** entities completed their multi-year glide path from the legacy 180-day overdue norm to the unified **90-day NPA recognition norm** effective **March 31, 2026**, closing regulatory arbitrage against banks.
- **Upper Layer (`NBFC-UL`) Bank-Like Oversight:** RBI's FY 2025–26 Upper Layer roster comprises **15 systemically pivotal NBFCs** (including LIC Housing Finance, Bajaj Finance, Shriram Finance, Tata Sons, L&T Finance, Cholamandalam, Muthoot Finance, Piramal Finance, HDB Financial Services, and Tata Capital), bound by **CET-1 ≥ 9%**, Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) applicability, and **mandatory stock-exchange listing within three years** of identification.
- **Bank–NBFC Co-Lending — RBI's August 2025 CLA Framework (Effective January 1, 2026):** Originating in 2020 strictly to pair banks' low-cost CASA deposits with NBFCs' last-mile rural/MSME origination for Priority Sector Lending (PSL), RBI's **August 2025 Co-Lending Arrangements (CLA) Directions** revolutionized the model effective **January 1, 2026**. Co-lending now extends **beyond PSL to all lending segments** across Regulated Entities (banks, AIFIs, NBFCs/HFCs); each co-lender must retain a **minimum 10% share** of every loan on its own books (reduced from the earlier 20% floor for NBFCs); borrowers receive a single **blended interest rate**; **borrower-level NPA harmonization** applies (if one co-lender tags a borrower as NPA, the partner must immediately mirror it); and originating-partner **Default Loss Guarantee (DLG)** is capped at **5%** of the outstanding portfolio.

### Microfinance Credit Stress (2024–26) & RBI's SRO Architecture

In March 2022, RBI deregulated microfinance interest-rate margin caps across all lenders (banks, SFBs, NBFC-MFIs) and installed a unified **50% Household FOIR (*Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio*)** ceiling to protect low-income borrowers earning ≤₹3 lakh annually. Instead of disciplined underwriting, intense growth chasing fuelled **multi-lender over-indebtedness** (borrowers juggling 4–5+ unsecured loans simultaneously), compounded by 2024 heatwaves, agrarian income stress, and localized loan-waiver (*Karza-Mukti*) campaigns. As delinquencies surged across FY25, RBI intervened sharply—barring four non-compliant NBFCs/MFIs from fresh lending in October 2024 over usurious pricing, while softening NBFC-MFI qualifying-asset norms from **75% to 60%** of total assets in June 2025 to enable safer portfolio diversification.

Crucially, RBI anchored soft-law market discipline through **Self-Regulatory Organizations (SROs)** recognized under its **March 2024 Omnibus SRO Framework** (not-for-profit Section 8 bodies bridging regulator and industry):
- **Microfinance SROs (`MFIN` & `Sa-Dhan`):** Enforced binding **industry guardrails** effective April 2025 capping aggregate microfinance debt at **₹2 lakh per borrower**, limiting total microfinance lenders to **at most 3 per borrower**, and prohibiting fresh loans to borrowers >60 days past due.
- **Broader Sectoral SROs:** RBI recognized **FACE (*Fintech Association for Consumer Empowerment*)** as India's first FinTech SRO (August 2024) and **FIDC (*Finance Industry Development Council*)** as India's first NBFC-sector SRO (**October 2025**), institutionalizing peer-monitored conduct standards across shadow lending.

## Insurance Act 2025, Bima Sugam & the Pension Architecture (OPS vs. NPS vs. UPS)

### *Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025* & Bima Sugam

India's **insurance penetration** (total premiums as a % of GDP) plateaued near **~3.7%** in FY24–FY25 (against a ~7% global average), constrained by capital intensity and rural distribution costs. Advancing **IRDAI's** (*IRDAI Act, 1999*; HQ Hyderabad) vision of **"Insurance for All by 2047"**, Parliament passed the ***Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025*** (Presidential assent December 20, 2025; core statutory provisions and foreign-investment rules **notified February 5, 2026**), amending the *Insurance Act, 1938*, *LIC Act, 1956*, and *IRDAI Act, 1999*:

- **100% Insurance FDI (Automatic Route):** Raises the foreign equity ceiling in Indian insurance companies from **74% to 100%** (completing a 25-year liberalization arc: `26% [2000] → 49% [2015] → 74% [2021] → 100% [2025/26]`), paired with simplified governance requiring only **one** among the Chairperson, MD, or CEO to be a resident Indian citizen.
- **Statutory Policyholders' Education and Protection Fund (PEPF):** Upgrades the previously administrative fund into a ring-fenced **statutory fund** under the Insurance Act—capitalized by IRDAI monetary penalties, grants, and **unclaimed policyholder amounts >10 years** (which insurers must transfer)—dedicated exclusively to consumer literacy and protection.
- **Reinsurance, Intermediaries & IRDAI Powers:** Slashes the minimum **Net Owned Fund (NOF)** required for **foreign reinsurance branches** from **₹5,000 crore to ₹1,000 crore** to deepen domestic risk capacity; raises the equity-transfer threshold needing prior IRDAI approval from **1% to 5%**; introduces **one-time permanent registration** for insurance intermediaries (replacing three-year renewals); equips IRDAI with SEBI-style **disgorgement powers** (recovering wrongful gains plus penalties up to ₹10 crore); and grants LIC's Board operational autonomy to open domestic branches and overseas units without prior Central Government sign-off. *(Prelims precision check: Although earlier draft consultation papers proposed **composite licences** allowing a single insurer to sell life, health, and general policies simultaneously, the **enacted 2025 Act did NOT introduce composite licences**.)*
- **Bima Sugam & the Bima Trinity:** Complementing statutory capital liberalization, IRDAI is deploying the **Bima Trinity**—**Bima Vistaar** (affordable bundled life, health, property, and accident cover), **Bima Vahak** (women-centric gram-panchayat distribution corps), and **Bima Sugam** (a unified, not-for-profit **Digital Public Infrastructure** built by the insurer-owned Bima Sugam India Federation). Conceived as the *"UPI of Insurance"*, Bima Sugam (whose Phase-1 informational portal launched in September 2025 ahead of full transactional rollout across 2026) integrates insurers, agents, and consumers onto one protocol for frictionless policy comparison, demat e-policy vaults, and paperless claims settlement.

### Pensions - PFRDA & OPS vs. NPS vs. Unified Pension Scheme (UPS, 2025)

Administered by the statutory **Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA)** (*PFRDA Act, 2013*), India's public pension policy balances two competing imperatives: **fiscal sustainability** for taxpayers and **income security** for aging retirees.

- **Old Pension Scheme (OPS — Pre-2004):** An **unfunded, pay-as-you-go Defined Benefit (DB)** regime guaranteeing **50% of last drawn basic pay + Dearness Relief (DR)** with zero employee contribution. Because current tax revenues directly pay past retirees without a dedicated asset pool, OPS creates an exploding **inter-generational fiscal time bomb**—consuming 15%–25%+ of own tax revenues in states that reverted to OPS during 2022–23.
- **National Pension System (NPS — Effective January 1, 2004 for Central Govt; 2009 All Citizens):** A **funded Defined Contribution (DC)** system (**10% employee + 14% Central Government** invested in market-linked equity/debt funds via Pension Fund Managers). While NPS caps sovereign liabilities upfront, it shifts **investment and longevity risk entirely onto retirees** (historically permitting up to 60% tax-free lump-sum withdrawal at age 60 with mandatory 40% annuity—liberalized by PFRDA in late 2025/2026 to allow non-government subscribers up to 80% lump-sum flexibility, alongside **NPS Vatsalya** launched in September 2024 for minors).
- **Unified Pension Scheme (UPS — Notified January 24, 2025; Effective April 1, 2025):** Designed on the **T.V. Somanathan Committee** blueprint as a **funded, assured-payout hybrid option within the NPS framework** for Central Government employees (with an August 2025 amendment granting subscribers a **one-time, one-way switch option back to NPS** before retirement):
  1. **Assured Pension:** **50% of average basic pay of the last 12 months** prior to superannuation upon completing **≥25 years** of qualifying service (proportionately scaled for 10–25 years).
  2. **Assured Minimum & Family Payouts:** Guaranteed floor of **₹10,000/month** for ≥10 years of service, and an **Assured Family Payout of 60%** of the retiree's pension immediately upon death.
  3. **Inflation Protection & Lump-Sum Benefit:** **Dearness Relief (DR)** indexed to **AICPI-IW** (matching serving staff), plus a superannuation lump-sum of **1/10th of monthly emoluments (Pay + DA) for every completed 6 months** of service—over and above gratuity, without reducing the assured pension.
  4. **Why UPS Differs Fundamentally from Unfunded OPS:** Unlike pay-as-you-go OPS, UPS is **explicitly pre-funded**—employees contribute **10%** (to their Individual Corpus) while the Central Government raises its contribution from **14% to 18.5%** (`10%` to the Individual Corpus + **`8.5%` into a sovereign Pool Corpus**). That dedicated pool buffers market-return shortfalls and longevity risk today rather than deferring an unfunded liability onto future budgets.

Bajaj Finance, LIC HF, Shriram, Tata Sons, etc.)* |
| **4. Top Layer (`NBFC-TL`)** | **Ideally empty** — populated only if RBI judges a specific Upper Layer NBFC to pose extreme systemic risk | Surge supervisory tier: punitive additional capital charges & intensive direct RBI intervention | Dynamic regulatory deterrence buffer; zero permanent members |

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **NBFC Access to RBI LAF Window:** NBFCs do **NOT** have direct access to RBI's Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) repo/reverse-repo window (unlike SCBs and Standalone PDs), **cannot** accept demand deposits, and carry **no DICGC insurance** on deposits.
> - **CBLO / TREPS Market Identity:** Both legacy **CBLO** and its 2018 successor **TREPS** (operated by CCIL) are **Money Market instruments**—even though they use long-dated Government Securities as collateral! Never misclassify them as Bond Market instruments.
> - **Who Issues T-Bills vs. CDs vs. CPs:** **T-Bills & CMBs** are issued strictly by the **Central Government** (never States); **CDs** are issued by **SCBs (excluding Payments Banks) and AIFIs** (never RBI); **CPs** are **unsecured** promissory notes issued by corporates/PDs/AIFIs; **Zero-Coupon Bonds** pay *no* periodic coupon interest.
> - **Global Bond Index Precision:** India is fully included in **J.P. Morgan GBI-EM (10%)**, **Bloomberg EM Local Currency (10%)**, and **FTSE Russell EMGBI (~9.4% completed Feb 2026)** via RBI's **FAR** route—but Bloomberg **deferred** India's entry into its flagship **Global Aggregate Index** in January 2026.
> - **P-Notes / ODIs Legal Status:** Participatory Notes are **not banned**, but SEBI's **December 2024** rules prohibit issuing ODIs with **derivatives as the underlying** and require a dedicated FPI registration strictly for ODI issuance.
> - **GIFT City FEMA & Regulator Trap:** Under **FEMA**, GIFT City IFSC is legally deemed a **foreign jurisdiction**, and **IFSCA** (not SEBI or RBI separately) acts as the **unified statutory regulator** across banking, capital markets, insurance, and pensions inside IFSC.
> - **NBFC 4-Layer SBR Traps:** **All deposit-taking NBFCs (`NBFC-D`)** and **all HFCs** sit at least in the **Middle Layer (`NBFC-ML`)** regardless of asset size; the **Top Layer (`NBFC-TL`)** is designed to remain **empty**; and even **Base Layer (`NBFC-BL`)** entities follow the **90-day NPA norm** from March 31, 2026.
> - **Co-Lending 2026 Expansion:** Effective **January 1, 2026**, RBI's new Co-Lending Arrangements (CLA) framework applies to **all lending segments** (no longer restricted to Priority Sector Lending), requires a **minimum 10%** loan retention per co-lender, and caps **Default Loss Guarantee (DLG)** at **5%**.
> - **Insurance Act 2025 — The Composite Licence Trap:** The *Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha Act, 2025* (notified Feb 5, 2026) enacted **100% Insurance FDI**, cut foreign reinsurer NOF from **₹5,000 Cr to ₹1,000 Cr**, and made **PEPF statutory**—but it did **NOT** introduce **composite licences** (single entity selling life + general + health).
> - **UPS vs. OPS Funding Distinction:** Unlike unfunded pay-as-you-go OPS, the **Unified Pension Scheme (UPS, effective April 1, 2025)** is a **pre-funded** scheme (**10% employee + 18.5% Central Government**, including an 8.5% Pool Corpus) whose assured 50% payout uses the **average basic pay of the last 12 months** (not merely the last single month's basic pay) and requires **≥25 years** of service for the full 50% rate.

- **Verified Prelims Anchors (2014–2026):**
  - **Prelims 2024 (Q1):** Classifying **Collateralized Borrowing and Lending Obligations (CBLO)** accurately under the **Money Market** (rather than Bond, Forex, or Stock Market).
  - **Prelims 2024 (Q2):** Testing institutional boundaries—**NBFCs cannot directly access RBI's LAF window**, **FIIs/FPIs can hold Indian G-Secs**, and **Indian stock exchanges offer separate debt trading platforms**.
  - **Prelims 2024 (Q3):** Identifying **ETFs and Currency Swaps** as financial instruments (excluding physical capital like motor vehicles).
  - **Prelims 2023:** **Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs)**—taxability of distributed interest/dividend income vs. statutory recognition of InvITs as borrowers under the **SARFAESI Act, 2002**.
  - **Prelims 2020:** Quartet testing atomic definitions of **Commercial Paper** (short-term unsecured promissory note), **Call Money** (inter-bank short-term finance), **Certificate of Deposit** (falsely attributed to RBI as long-term), and **Zero-Coupon Bonds** (falsely labelled interest-bearing).
  - **Prelims 2018:** Sovereign debt mechanics—**RBI manages both Central and State Government securities**, **State Governments cannot issue Treasury Bills**, and **T-Bills are issued at a discount to par value**.

### Prelims Trap Signature

- **Issuer–Regulator Swaps:** Attributing CDs to RBI, T-Bills to State Governments, HFC regulation to NHB (shifted to RBI in 2019), or GIFT City IFSC regulation to SEBI/RBI individually instead of unified **IFSCA**.
- **Collateral vs. Market Conflation:** Misclassifying TREPS/CBLO as Bond Market instruments simply because G-Secs serve as underlying collateral, or calling Commercial Papers and Certificates of Deposit "collateralized" (both are **unsecured**).
- **2025–2026 Statutory & Regulatory Over-Extrapolation:** Assuming the *Insurance Amendment Act, 2025* enacted composite licences, assuming India entered the *Bloomberg Global Aggregate Index* (deferred Jan 2026), assuming Co-Lending remains confined strictly to Priority Sector Lending, or assuming UPS is an unfunded OPS replica.
- **Shadow-Bank Symmetry Fallacy:** Assuming NBFCs enjoy LAF access, DICGC deposit insurance, or cheque-clearing membership equivalent to Scheduled Commercial Banks.
