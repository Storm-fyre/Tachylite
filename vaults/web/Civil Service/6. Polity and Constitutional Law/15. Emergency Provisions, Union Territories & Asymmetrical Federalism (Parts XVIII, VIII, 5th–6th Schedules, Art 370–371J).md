# Emergency Provisions, Union Territories & Asymmetrical Federalism (Parts XVIII, VIII, 5th–6th Schedules, Art 370–371J)

Classical federations prize symmetry—every constituent unit holding identical powers under rigid peacetime rules. India's constitutional framers rejected that rigidity as lethal for a newly partitioned, demographically heterogeneous republic, embedding two countervailing escape valves inside the text: **centripetal crisis conversion** (Part XVIII Emergency Provisions and Part VIII Union Territories) and **centrifugal cultural accommodation** (Part XXI Special Provisions and the Fifth–Sixth Schedules).

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described India's emergency mechanism as a unique capacity to shift seamlessly from federal to unitary without formal constitutional amendment. Yet seventy-six years of constitutional practice—from the 1975–77 Emergency and *S.R. Bommai* (1994) to *In Re: Article 370* (2023), Manipur's 2025–2026 President's Rule, and Ladakh's Sixth Schedule impasse—prove that every escape valve demands strict judicial and democratic guardrails to prevent exceptionalism from devouring federalism.

## Emergency Provisions - Crisis Centralization and Post-1978 Guardrails (Part XVIII, Articles 352–360)

Borrowed structurally from the **Government of India Act, 1935** (with the suspension of Fundamental Rights modeled on the **Weimar Constitution of Germany**), **Part XVIII** provides for three distinct emergencies: **National Emergency (Art 352)**, **President's Rule / Constitutional Breakdown (Art 356 & Art 365)**, and **Financial Emergency (Art 360)**.

### National Emergency (Article 352) and the 44th Amendment Fortress

Under **Article 352(1)**, the President may proclaim a National Emergency if satisfied that a grave emergency exists threatening the security of India, or **any part of its territory** (territorial tailoring inserted by the **42nd CAA, 1976**), on three exhaustive grounds: **War**, **External Aggression**, or **Armed Rebellion**. Proclamations may even be issued *prior* to actual occurrence if there is imminent danger.

Stung by the abuse of the 1975–77 internal Emergency, the **44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978** erected six formidable constitutional firewalls around Article 352:

1. **Ground Tightened:** Replaced the vague phrase **"internal disturbance"** with **"armed rebellion"**, preventing ordinary political protests or law-and-order agitation from triggering Article 352.
2. **Written Cabinet Advice (Art 352(3)):** The President **cannot** issue a Proclamation unless the decision of the **Union Cabinet** (the Prime Minister and Ministers of Cabinet rank under Art 75) is communicated to him **in writing**—ending unilateral prime ministerial advice. *(This clause is the **sole place in the entire Constitution** where the word **"Cabinet"** appears.)*
3. **Compressed Approval & Elevated Majority (Art 352(4) & (6)):** Both Houses of Parliament must approve the Proclamation within **one month** (slashed from two months) by a **Special Majority**—a majority of total membership AND not less than two-thirds of members present and voting in each House. *(If Lok Sabha is dissolved during that month, Rajya Sabha approves within 1 month and the reconstituted Lok Sabha must approve within **30 days** of its first sitting.)*
4. **Periodic Parliamentary Renewal (Art 352(5)):** Once approved, the Emergency lasts **six months** at a time and may be extended **indefinitely** with fresh Special Majority approval every six months.
5. **Lok Sabha Disapproval & Special Sitting (Art 352(7)–(8)):** While the President may revoke a Proclamation unilaterally at any time, the President **must** revoke it if the **Lok Sabha alone** passes a resolution disapproving its continuance by a **Simple Majority**. Furthermore, if **one-tenth ($1/10\text{th}$)** of total Lok Sabha members submit written notice (to the Speaker when in session, or to the President when not), a **special sitting must be convened within 14 days**.
6. **Judicial Review Restored:** Deleted the 38th CAA's clause barring judicial scrutiny; in ***Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India* (1980)**, the Supreme Court held that a Proclamation under Art 352 can be challenged on grounds of *mala fides* or wholly extraneous/irrelevant satisfaction.

**Systemic Impact on Federalism and Legislatures:** During an Article 352 Emergency, State governments are **not dismissed**, nor are State Legislatures suspended—rather, they come under complete Union superintendence. The Centre may issue executive directions to States on *any* matter (**Art 353**), Parliament acquires concurrent legislative power over List II and Art 246A GST (**Art 250**—laws lapsing 6 months after Emergency ends), and the President may modify financial distribution under Articles 268–279 (**Art 354**, subject to parliamentary tabling, expiring at the end of the financial year in which Emergency ceases). Additionally, Parliament may extend the normal 5-year tenure of the **Lok Sabha (Art 83(2))** and **State Assemblies (Art 172(1))** by **one year at a time** (not beyond 6 months after the Emergency ends).

**Fundamental Rights Suspension — Article 358 vs. Article 359 (Surgical Distinction):**
- **Article 358 (Automatic Suspension of Article 19 ONLY):** Operates **automatically** upon proclamation, *but* following the 44th CAA applies **ONLY** when Emergency is declared on grounds of **War or External Aggression** (**NEVER** on **Armed Rebellion**!). It spans the entire duration of the Emergency, covers the whole country (or specified part), and immunizes **only those laws/executive actions containing an explicit recital** that they relate to the Emergency.
- **Article 359 (Presidential Order Suspending Enforcement of Specified FRs):** Does **not** suspend rights automatically; requires a specific **Presidential Order** suspending the *right to move any court* to enforce named Part III rights for a specified period and territory. Applies across **all three grounds** (including Armed Rebellion). Crucially, post-44th CAA, **Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended under any circumstance**—overruling the infamous ***ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla* (1976)** precedent (formally interred by the 9-Judge Bench in *K.S. Puttaswamy*, 2017).

India has proclaimed National Emergency **three times**: **October 1962** (Chinese aggression; lasted till Jan 1968), **December 1971** (Pakistan war), and **June 1975** ("internal disturbance"—overlapping the 1971 proclamation; both revoked March 1977).

### President's Rule (Articles 355, 356 & 365) and the *Bommai* Firewall

**Article 355** imposes a triple constitutional duty on the Union: to protect every State against (i) external aggression and (ii) internal disturbance, and (iii) to ensure that every State government is carried on in accordance with the Constitution. Operationalizing that third limb, **Article 356(1)** empowers the President—on receipt of a **Governor's report or *"otherwise"***—to proclaim President's Rule upon satisfaction that constitutional machinery in the State has broken down. Independently, **Article 365** triggers Article 356 whenever a State fails to comply with or give effect to any binding Union executive direction.

**Approval, Duration & the 44th CAA One-Year Wall:** A Proclamation under Article 356 must be approved by both Houses within **two months** by a **Simple Majority** (if Lok Sabha is dissolved, Rajya Sabha approves within 2 months and the new Lok Sabha within 30 days of first sitting). Once approved, it runs for **six months** and can be renewed up to **one year** normally. Extending President's Rule beyond one year (up to an absolute **three-year maximum**) requires **BOTH** stringent conditions under **Article 356(5)** (44th CAA) to be satisfied simultaneously:
1. A Proclamation of National Emergency (**Art 352**) is in operation in the whole of India, or in the whole or any part of that State; **AND**
2. The **Election Commission of India certifies** that continuation of President's Rule is necessary due to difficulties in holding general elections to that State's Legislative Assembly.

*(Anything beyond three years—as happened in Punjab during 1987–1992—requires an explicit **Article 368 Constitutional Amendment**.)*

**Effects & Limitations:** The President assumes State executive functions (run by the Governor aided by advisors) and declares that State legislative powers shall be exercisable by or under the authority of **Parliament** (which may delegate law-making via **Article 357**—and notably, laws enacted during President's Rule **do NOT automatically lapse** upon revocation; they survive until amended or repealed by the restored State Legislature under **Art 357(2)**). Crucially, under the **Proviso to Art 356(1)**, the President **cannot assume any powers vested in a High Court** or suspend constitutional provisions governing High Courts, nor does Article 356 affect citizens' **Fundamental Rights**.

**From "Dead Letter" to Judicial Containment (*Bommai* → *Rameshwar Prasad* → *Harish Rawat*):** Dr. Ambedkar famously hoped Article 356 would remain a *"dead letter"*, yet partisan Central regimes invoked it over **130 times** (first in PEPSU/Punjab in 1951, peaking across the 1970s–1980s). Its dramatic decline since the mid-1990s stems from coalition politics, conscientious Presidents (such as K.R. Narayanan returning Cabinet proposals on UP in 1997 and Bihar in 1998), and three landmark judicial firewalls:

- ***S.R. Bommai v. Union of India* (1994, 9-Judge Bench):** Made Presidential satisfaction strictly **justiciable** for *mala fides* or irrelevant/non-existent material (holding Art 74(2) bars inquiry into ministerial *advice*, NOT the underlying *material*); ruled that **Assembly dissolution cannot occur prior to Parliamentary approval**—only *suspended animation* is permitted in the interim; declared the **floor of the House** the sole legitimate test of majority; affirmed **secularism** as Basic Structure (validating 1992 dismissals of BJP regimes in MP, HP, Rajasthan post-Babri demolition while invalidating Karnataka, Meghalaya, and Nagaland proclamations); and held that courts possess power to **restore a dismissed government and revive a dissolved Assembly**.
- ***Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India* (2006, 5-Judge Bench):** Struck down the 2005 dissolution of a hung Bihar Legislative Assembly prior to its first sitting, condemning Governor Buta Singh's unverified *"horse-trading"* claims as a *mala fide* attempt to block a rival coalition from staking claim.
- ***Nabam Rebia* (2016 — Arunachal Pradesh) & *Harish Rawat* (2016 — Uttarakhand):** Translated *Bommai*'s restorative remedy into live reality—the Supreme Court and Uttarakhand High Court actually invalidated Article 356 proclamations and **reinstated ousted Chief Ministers** via court-monitored floor tests.

**Live 2025–2026 Constitutional Illustration — Manipur President's Rule:** Following Chief Minister N. Biren Singh's resignation on February 9, 2025 amid protracted ethnic conflict, President Droupadi Murmu imposed **President's Rule in Manipur on February 13, 2025**, carefully adhering to *Bommai* by keeping the 60-member Assembly (tenure till March 2027) in **suspended animation** rather than dissolving it. Renewed once for six months in August 2025, the Proclamation hit the **one-year Article 356(5) constitutional ceiling** in February 2026 (where neither an Art 352 Emergency nor an ECI election-impossibility certificate existed). Nine days before expiry, on **February 4, 2026**, the President revoked Article 356 under **Article 356(2)**, swearing in an NDA coalition headed by CM **Yumnam Khemchand Singh** alongside two Deputy CMs from the Kuki-Zo and Naga communities.

### Financial Emergency (Article 360)

Under **Article 360(1)**, if the President is satisfied that a situation has arisen threatening the **financial stability or credit of India** or any part thereof, a Financial Emergency may be proclaimed (subject to judicial review post-44th CAA). It requires **Simple Majority** approval by both Houses within **two months**—and carries a unique structural quirk: **once approved, it continues indefinitely without requiring periodic parliamentary renewal** until revoked by the President.

Its centralizing powers are sweeping: the Union may direct States to observe canons of financial propriety, require **reservation of ALL State Money Bills and Financial Bills** for Presidential consideration (**Art 360(4)(a)(ii)**), and direct the **reduction of salaries and allowances** of any class of persons serving a State or the Union—expressly **including Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts** (**Art 360(4)(b)**). India has **never proclaimed a Financial Emergency** (even during the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis).

## Union Territories and the NCT of Delhi Constitutional Deadlock (Part VIII, Articles 239–241)

### Constitutional Architecture of India's Eight Union Territories

Carved out for political-administrative delicacy (Delhi, Chandigarh), strategic frontier location (Ladakh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep), or distinct colonial/cultural heritage (Puducherry, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu), India today comprises **8 Union Territories**:
- **3 UTs with Legislative Assemblies:** **NCT of Delhi** (Art 239AA), **Puducherry** (Art 239A + GoUT Act, 1963), and **Jammu & Kashmir** (J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019, modeled via Section 13 on Art 239A).
- **5 UTs without Legislative Assemblies:** **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**, **Chandigarh**, **Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu** (merged January 26, 2020), **Lakshadweep**, and **Ladakh**.

Part VIII lays down four foundational mechanisms distinguishing UTs sharply from federal States:
- **Article 239 (Direct Presidential Agency):** Every UT is administered by the President through an **Administrator** (designated Lieutenant Governor in Delhi, Puducherry, J&K, Ladakh, and A&N Islands; Administrator in Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, and DNH&DD). Unlike a State Governor—who is a constitutional head—a UT Administrator is a **direct agent of the President**.
- **Article 239A & 239B (Statutory Assemblies & Ordinances):** Added by the **14th CAA, 1962**, Art 239A empowers Parliament by simple law to create a Legislature/CoM for Puducherry. Under **Art 239B** (27th CAA, 1971), the Administrator of Puducherry may promulgate Ordinances during recess only with **prior instructions from the President**.
- **Article 240 (Presidential Regulations with Parliamentary Force):** Empowers the President to make regulations for the peace, progress, and good government of **Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, and Puducherry** (*for Puducherry ONLY when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved*). Crucially, although **Ladakh** is not listed in Article 240's bare text, **Section 58(2)–(3) of the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** explicitly vested **Article 240 regulation-making power** in the President for **Ladakh**—power extensively invoked in June and November 2025. Article 240 regulations carry the **same force as an Act of Parliament** and may amend or repeal existing Parliamentary laws. Neither **NCT Delhi**, **J&K**, nor **Chandigarh** falls under Article 240.
- **Article 241 (High Courts for UTs):** Parliament may constitute a High Court for a UT or extend a neighbouring State High Court's jurisdiction. Only **NCT of Delhi** has its own exclusive High Court (1966), while **J&K and Ladakh** share a common High Court.

### NCT of Delhi - Article 239AA, the 2018 & 2023 Verdicts, and the NCCSA Override

Acting on the **S. Balakrishnan Committee (1987)** report, the **69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991** inserted **Articles 239AA and 239AB**, granting Delhi a *sui generis* asymmetric status—retaining Union Territory architecture while adding a 70-member elected Assembly and Council of Ministers. Key textual peculiarities include:
- **10% Ministerial Cap (Art 239AA(4)):** Delhi's Council of Ministers cannot exceed **10% of total Assembly strength** (maximum **7 ministers including the Chief Minister**)—sharply tighter than the **15% ceiling** for States (Art 164(1A)).
- **Three Excluded Entries (Art 239AA(3)(a)):** Delhi's Assembly may legislate on List II and List III matters *applicable to UTs*, **EXCEPT List II Entry 1 (Public Order), Entry 2 (Police), and Entry 18 (Land)** (and Entries 64, 65, 66 insofar as they relate to them). Parliament simultaneously retains overriding competence over *all* matters for Delhi (Art 239AA(3)(b)–(c)).
- **Difference-of-Opinion Proviso (Art 239AA(4)):** The LG acts on ministerial aid and advice except where required by law to act in discretion; in case of a *"difference of opinion on any matter"*, the LG refers the issue to the President and takes urgent interim action pending Presidential decision.
- **Article 239AB:** Provides a tailored President's Rule equivalent for constitutional breakdown in NCTD.

**The Three-Stage Constitutional Showdown over Bureaucratic Control:**
1. ***GNCTD v. Union of India* (July 4, 2018, 5-Judge Bench):** Resolving years of administrative paralysis, the Constitution Bench held that Delhi's LG is **not a concurrence-wielding viceroy**; he is bound by the aid and advice of the elected Council of Ministers across all non-excluded subjects. The Proviso's phrase *"any matter"* does **not** mean *"every matter"*—referrals to the President must remain rare, principled exceptions, anchored in *constitutional trust* and *collaborative federalism*.
2. ***GNCTD v. Union of India* (May 11, 2023, 5-Judge Bench — "Services Verdict"):** Unanimously settling a split 2019 verdict, CJI D.Y. Chandrachud's Bench ruled that **NCTD holds full legislative and executive control over List II Entry 41 ("Services")**—barring officers linked directly to Public Order, Police, and Land. Grounded in the **"triple chain of accountability"** (permanent civil servants accountable to ministers → ministers accountable to the Assembly → Assembly accountable to voters), the Court warned that stripping an elected government of control over transfers and discipline hollows out representative democracy.
3. **GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023 (NCCSA) & Pending 5-Judge Reference:** Eight days after the May 2023 verdict, the Union promulgated an Ordinance—subsequently enacted as the **GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023** amending the parent GNCTD Act, 1991—invoking **Article 239AA(7)(a)** ("supplemental, incidental and consequential" parliamentary law). Its structural overhaul:
   - Created the **National Capital Civil Services Authority (NCCSA)** to recommend Group A/DANICS postings, transfers, and vigilance matters, consisting of the **Chief Minister (Chairperson)**, **Chief Secretary (Member)**, and **Principal Home Secretary (Member Secretary)**—the latter two being **Union-appointed bureaucrats** who can outvote the elected CM via majority rule (quorum: 2).
   - Empowered the **LG to act in "sole discretion"** to approve, return, or override NCCSA recommendations, and mandated department Secretaries to report any matter likely to spark Union–NCTD controversy directly to the LG.

In **July 2023**, the Supreme Court referred the constitutional validity of this override to a **5-Judge Constitution Bench** on a profound textual question: *Can Parliament deploy ordinary statutory power under **Article 239AA(7)** to effectively alter the distribution of executive power inside Article 239AA without an Article 368 Constitutional Amendment?* As of **November 2026**, that 5-Judge reference remains **pending adjudication** *(note: Parliament's routine Repealing and Amending Act, 2025 repealed the 2023 amending statute for statute-book cleanup while leaving the incorporated NCCSA provisions fully operative inside the parent GNCTD Act, 1991)*.

## Asymmetrical Federalism - *In Re: Article 370* and Part XXI Special Provisions (Articles 370–371J)

Unlike *"coming-together"* federations like the United States, India exemplifies Alfred Stepan's **"holding-together" asymmetrical federalism**: constitutional design deliberately calibrates bespoke autonomy bargains across border regions, tribal homelands, and historically underdeveloped sub-regions to forge voluntary national integration.

### *In Re: Article 370* (December 11, 2023) and the J&K Post-Reorganisation Regime

Inserted in Part XXI (*"Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions"*) following Maharaja Hari Singh's Instrument of Accession (October 1947), **Article 370** originally confined Parliament's legislative competence in Jammu & Kashmir to Defence, External Affairs, and Communications (plus matters extended by Presidential Orders with State concurrence), allowed a separate **J&K Constitution** (adopted 1956; Constituent Assembly dissolved January 1957), and facilitated **C.O. 1954** (inserting **Article 35A** protecting permanent-resident land and job privileges).

On **August 5–6, 2019**, during President's Rule in J&K, the Union executed a three-step constitutional integration: (i) **C.O. 272** invoked Art 370(1)(d) to amend **Article 367**, redefining *"Constituent Assembly of the State"* in Art 370(3) as *"Legislative Assembly of the State"* (whose powers Parliament was then exercising under Art 356); (ii) following Parliamentary resolutions, **C.O. 273** issued a Presidential Declaration under **Art 370(3)** rendering Article 370 inoperative and applying the entire Constitution of India to J&K; and (iii) Parliament enacted the **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019**, bifurcating the State into two Union Territories—**J&K (with Legislature)** and **Ladakh (without Legislature)**.

On **December 11, 2023**, a unanimous **5-Judge Constitution Bench** (CJI D.Y. Chandrachud, Justices S.K. Kaul, Sanjiv Khanna, B.R. Gavai, and Surya Kant) in ***In Re: Article 370 of the Constitution* (`2023 INSC 1058`)** upheld the abrogation via five razor-sharp holdings:

1. **Zero Internal Sovereignty:** Upon executing the Instrument of Accession and Yuvraj Karan Singh's Proclamation of November 25, 1949 declaring the Indian Constitution supreme, **J&K retained no vestige of internal sovereignty**. Article 370 was a manifestation of **asymmetric federalism**, not dual sovereignty.
2. **Temporary and Transitional Nature:** Relying on its placement in Part XXI, wartime context, and drafting history, the Bench ruled Article 370 was an interim bridge designed for gradual constitutional integration; it **did NOT become permanent** merely because the J&K Constituent Assembly dissolved in 1957.
3. **Para 2 of C.O. 272 Struck Down — Yet C.O. 273 Independently Upheld:** Crucial Prelims precision! The Court held **Paragraph 2 of C.O. 272 unconstitutional**—Parliament/President cannot obliquely amend Article 370 by modifying an interpretation clause (Article 367). *Nevertheless*, the Bench unanimously **upheld C.O. 273**, ruling that once the J&K Constituent Assembly ceased to exist, its recommendation proviso under Art 370(3) became otiose, leaving the **President with unilateral power under Art 370(3)** to declare Article 370 inoperative (and rejecting the claim that *Bommai* bars irreversible constitutional steps during President's Rule).
4. **Reorganisation & Deliberate Avoidance on State-to-UT Downgrade:** The Bench upheld carving out the **UT of Ladakh** under **Article 3(a) read with Explanation I** (separating territory from a State to form a UT). However, explicitly relying on the **Solicitor General's formal assurance that J&K's statehood would be restored**, the Court **declined to decide** the grave federal question of whether Article 3 empowers Parliament to extinguish an entire State and downgrade it into a Union Territory—directing instead that ECI conduct Assembly elections by **September 30, 2024** and statehood be restored *"at the earliest and as soon as possible"*.
5. **Justice S.K. Kaul's Concurring Epilogue:** Proposed establishing an impartial **Truth and Reconciliation Commission** (modeled on post-apartheid South Africa) to investigate human-rights violations by both state and non-state actors since the 1980s.

**J&K UT Assembly Architecture & 2024–2026 Statehood Status:** Under the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** (as amended in 2023), the UT of J&K possesses a unicameral Assembly (Legislative Council abolished via Section 57) modeled on Puducherry (Art 239A): **90 elected seats** (post-2022 Delimitation: 47 Kashmir, 43 Jammu; 24 additional seats kept vacant for PoJK) plus **5 LG-nominated MLAs** (2 women, 2 Kashmiri migrants, 1 PoJK displaced person). Its legislative competence covers List II and List III **excluding List II Entry 1 (Public Order) and Entry 2 (Police)** (and July 2024 Transaction of Business Rules expanded LG authority over All-India Services, Anti-Corruption Bureau, and prosecution sanction). Following Assembly elections in September–October 2024 and formation of an elected government, **J&K remains a Union Territory as of November 2026**: on **September 28, 2026**, the J&K Legislative Assembly passed a fresh resolution demanding immediate restoration of full statehood, while time-bound statehood petitions before the Supreme Court (where the Centre cited security considerations post-Pahalgam) remain pending.

### Special Provisions for Twelve States - Articles 371 to 371J

Far from creating a uniform *"special status"*, **Articles 371 through 371J** (11 Articles covering **12 States**—six in the Northeast and six in Peninsular/Western India) deploy targeted constitutional tools ranging from regional development boards to Assembly vetoes over Parliamentary statutes:

- **Article 371 — Maharashtra & Gujarat (7th CAA, 1956):** Empowers the President to assign Governors special responsibility to establish separate **Development Boards** for **Vidarbha, Marathwada**, and rest of Maharashtra, and **Saurashtra, Kutch**, and rest of Gujarat—ensuring equitable fund allocation, technical education, and public employment.
- **Article 371A — Nagaland (13th CAA, 1962):** The strongest customary-law veto in Part XXI. **No Act of Parliament** applies to Nagaland concerning (i) Naga religious or social practices, (ii) Naga customary law and procedure, (iii) administration of civil and criminal justice involving Naga customary law, and (iv) **ownership and transfer of land and its resources**, **unless the Nagaland Legislative Assembly so decides by resolution**. Also confers special law-and-order responsibility on the Governor (acting in individual judgment after consulting CoM) and special arrangements for **Tuensang district**.
- **Article 371B — Assam (22nd CAA, 1969):** Empowers the President to constitute a **Committee of the Assam Legislative Assembly** comprising MLAs elected from the State's tribal areas (plus other MLAs specified by President).
- **Article 371C — Manipur (27th CAA, 1971):** Authorizes a Presidential Order establishing the **Hill Areas Committee (HAC)** of the Manipur Legislative Assembly composed of MLAs elected from the State's Hill Areas, requires the Governor to submit an **annual report** to the President on Hill Area administration, and empowers the Union to issue executive directions thereon. In constitutional practice, friction over whether Imphal's valley-dominated cabinet bypassed HAC consultative mandates and scheduled-land protections directly fueled Manipur's governance breakdown—necessitating the February 2025–February 2026 Article 356 intervention before restoration of a multi-ethnic coalition. For the conflict dynamics, security architecture, and statutory military-powers regime governing the crisis, see [[4. Northeast Insurgencies, Peace Accords, Manipur & AFSPA#Manipur Crisis and AFSPA 1958|Manipur 2023-26 & AFSPA 1958]].
- **Articles 371D & 371E — Andhra Pradesh & Telangana (32nd CAA, 1973; extended to Telangana via 2014 Act):** **Art 371D** empowers the President to guarantee equitable local-cadre opportunities in public employment and education across sub-regions and establish a State Administrative Tribunal (note: *P. Sambamurthy*, 1986 struck down clause 371D(5)'s executive power to annul Tribunal orders as violating Basic Structure); **Art 371E** empowers Parliament to establish a Central University in Andhra Pradesh.
- **Article 371F — Sikkim (36th CAA, 1975):** Codifies Sikkim's 1975 merger terms: Legislative Assembly of **not less than 30 members**; **1 Lok Sabha seat**; Parliament's power to protect distinct ethnic communities via seat reservation (including the **Sangha** monastic constituency—upheld in ***R.C. Poudyal v. Union of India*, 1993** as permissible asymmetric democracy); **continuance of all pre-merger Sikkim laws** (such as old revenue/land-protection rules) until amended or repealed; and the Governor's special responsibility for peace and equitable socio-economic advancement.
- **Article 371G — Mizoram (53rd CAA, 1986):** Mirrors Nagaland's Assembly-resolution veto over Parliamentary Acts concerning Mizo religious/social practices, customary law, civil/criminal justice, and **ownership and transfer of land**—with one surgical Prelims distinction: **Art 371G covers land only and omits Nagaland's *"and its resources"* phrase**. Sets Assembly floor at **not less than 40 members**.
- **Article 371H — Arunachal Pradesh (55th CAA, 1986):** Assigns the Governor special responsibility regarding **law and order** (exercised in individual judgment after consulting CoM) and fixes Assembly minimum at **30 members**.
- **Article 371I — Goa (56th CAA, 1987):** Fixes Goa's Legislative Assembly strength at **not less than 30 members**.
- **Article 371J — Karnataka / Kalyana-Karnataka (98th CAA, 2012):** Empowers the President to grant the Governor special responsibility to set up a **Separate Development Board for the Hyderabad-Karnataka (now Kalyana-Karnataka) region**, guarantee equitable fund allocation, and provide **local reservation in education and state government employment**.

## Tribal Constitutional Governance - Fifth Schedule, Sixth Schedule, and the Ladakh Question (Article 244)

Recognizing that uniform statutory administration historically enabled tribal land alienation, bonded labour, and cultural erasure, **Article 244** bifurcates tribal self-governance across two bespoke constitutional regimes: **Scheduled Areas in Mainland India (Fifth Schedule — Art 244(1))** and **Tribal Areas in Four Northeastern States (Sixth Schedule — Art 244(2))**.

### Fifth Schedule Architecture (Article 244(1)) and *Samatha* (1997)

Currently operating across **10 Mainland States**—**Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, and Telangana**—the Fifth Schedule vests executive primacy in the **President and the Governor** rather than autonomous elected councils:
- **Declaration & Reporting:** The **President alone** (after consulting the State Governor) declares, enlarges, shrinks, or rescinds Scheduled Areas (Para 6). The Governor must submit an annual (or President-requested) report on Scheduled Area administration, and Union executive power extends to issuing binding directions to the State (Para 3).
- **Tribes Advisory Council (TAC — Para 4):** Mandatory in every Fifth Schedule State (and optional by Presidential direction in any State having STs without Scheduled Areas—e.g., Tamil Nadu, West Bengal). Comprises **not more than 20 members**, of whom **as nearly as may be three-fourths ($3/4\text{ths}$)** must be **ST MLAs** in the State Legislative Assembly. Crucially, the TAC is **purely advisory**—it holds zero legislative, executive, or fiscal autonomy.
- **Governor's Extraordinary Para 5 Law-Making Power:** Acting as a constitutional filter, the Governor may by public notification direct that any **Parliamentary or State Act** shall **not apply** to a Scheduled Area, or apply subject to specified exceptions/modifications (Para 5(1)). Further, after consulting the TAC, the Governor may make **Regulations** (requiring **mandatory Presidential assent** under Para 5(4)) to: **(a)** prohibit or restrict **transfer of land by or among STs**, **(b)** regulate land allotment to STs, and **(c)** regulate **money-lending** to STs (Para 5(2)).
- ***Samatha v. State of Andhra Pradesh* (1997, 3-Judge Bench):** Interpreting Andhra Pradesh's Fifth Schedule Land Transfer Regulation, the Supreme Court held that the statutory word **"person"** barred from acquiring tribal land **includes the State Government itself**—prohibiting State governments from leasing tribal or government forest land inside Fifth Schedule areas to private non-tribal mining corporations (permitting mining solely by State instrumentalities or tribal cooperatives, and mandating 20% net-profit reinvestment in tribal welfare). *(Grassroots Gram Sabha powers under PESA 1996 are examined in Chapter 16.)*

### Sixth Schedule Architecture (Article 244(2)) and Autonomous District Councils

Designed by the **Gopinath Bardoloi Sub-Committee** of the Constituent Assembly to accommodate distinct hill communities that never assimilated into mainland plains administration, the **Sixth Schedule** creates constitutionally entrenched *"mini-states within States"* across **four Northeastern States** (**Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram — AMTM**) comprising **10 Autonomous District Councils (ADCs)**:
- **Assam (3):** Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC), Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council (KAAC), Dima Hasao Autonomous Council (DHAC).
- **Meghalaya (3):** Garo Hills ADC, Khasi Hills ADC, Jaintia Hills ADC.
- **Tripura (1):** Tripura Tribal Areas Autonomous District Council (TTAADC).
- **Mizoram (3):** Chakma ADC, Lai ADC, Mara ADC.

**Governance Mechanics:** Unlike the Fifth Schedule, the **Governor** (not the President) organizes, reorganizes, alters boundaries of, or unites Autonomous Districts and Autonomous Regions (Para 1). Each **ADC** consists of **up to 30 members** (4 nominated by the Governor holding office at pleasure + up to 26 elected by adult suffrage for a 5-year term; **exception: Bodoland Territorial Council allows up to 46 members** under the 2003/90th Amendment settlement). ADCs wield **tripartite autonomy**:
1. **Legislative Power (Para 3):** May enact laws (requiring **Governor's assent**) on land allotment, management of non-reserved forests, canal/water use for agriculture, *jhum* (shifting) cultivation, village/town administration, inheritance, marriage/divorce, and social customs.
2. **Judicial Power (Para 4–5):** May constitute **Village and District Council Courts** to try suits and cases exclusively between ST parties; the **High Court's jurisdiction** over such cases is strictly as specified by the Governor, and criminal trials involving death, life imprisonment, or $\ge 5$ years' imprisonment require express conferment of CPC/CrPC powers by the Governor.
3. **Financial & Tax Power (Para 8–9):** Empowered to assess/collect land revenue, levy taxes on buildings, professions, trades, animals, vehicles, boats, entry of goods into markets, and tolls—plus receive a constitutionally guaranteed **share of mineral extraction royalties** from the State (disputes adjudicated by the Governor).

**Statutory Reform Impasse:** The **Constitution (125th Amendment) Bill, 2019**—introduced in the Rajya Sabha to create directly elected Village and Municipal Councils, bring ADCs under State Finance Commissions, mandate State Election Commission polls, and reserve 1/3rd seats for women—remains **pending in the Rajya Sabha** as of November 2026.

### 5th vs. 6th Schedule Matrix

| Feature | Fifth Schedule (Article 244(1)) | Sixth Schedule (Article 244(2) & Article 275(1)) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Territorial Coverage** | **10 Mainland States** (AP, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, HP, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Telangana). | **4 Northeastern States (AMTM):** Assam (3), Meghalaya (3), Tripura (1), Mizoram (3) — **10 ADCs** total. |
| **Area Declaration Authority** | **President** (declares, alters, or rescinds Scheduled Areas after consulting Governor — Para 6). | **Governor** (organizes, alters boundaries, creates, or unites Autonomous Districts/Regions — Para 1). |
| **Core Institutional Body** | **Tribes Advisory Council (TAC):** $\le 20$ members ($3/4\text{ths}$ ST MLAs) — **purely advisory**, zero law-making/tax power. | **Autonomous District Councils (ADCs) & Regional Councils:** $\le 30$ members (26 elected + 4 nominated; **BTC $\le 46$**) — **legislative, executive, judicial & taxing autonomy**. |
| **Regulation & Assent Rule** | Governor makes **Para 5 Regulations** (land transfer, allotment, money-lending) after consulting TAC; **Presidential assent mandatory**. | ADCs enact **Para 3 laws** (land, non-reserved forest, customs, *jhum*, inheritance); **Governor's assent mandatory** (no Presidential assent needed). |
| **Applicability of Parliamentary & State Acts** | **Governor** directs whether *any* Parliamentary or State Act applies (or applies with modifications) across all 10 States (Para 5(1)). | **Asymmetric Split (Para 12–12B):** In **Assam**, **Governor** directs both Parliamentary & State Acts. In **Meghalaya, Tripura & Mizoram**, **President** directs Parliamentary Acts while **Governor** directs State Acts! |
| **Judicial Architecture** | Regular State Judiciary (District Courts & High Court) exercises full normal jurisdiction. | **Village & District Council Courts** try tribal disputes; High Court reach strictly as specified by Governor (Para 4–5). |
| **Amendment Procedure** | Para 7 expressly allows Parliament to amend the Fifth Schedule by **Simple Majority** (deemed **NOT** an Art 368 amendment). | Para 21 expressly allows Parliament to amend the Sixth Schedule by **Simple Majority** (deemed **NOT** an Art 368 amendment). *(Note: Adding a brand-new State like Ladakh to Art 244(2) still requires an **Art 368 CAA**!)* |

### Ladakh's Sixth Schedule Demand - Constitutional Impasse (2019–2026)

Carved out as a **Union Territory without a Legislature** in October 2019, **Ladakh**—where **~97% of the ~3 lakh population belongs to Scheduled Tribes**—lost its 4 MLAs in the erstwhile J&K Assembly alongside Article 35A's land-and-job shield, triggering an acute **"representation deficit"**. Recognizing this vulnerability, the **National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)** formally recommended bringing Ladakh under the **Sixth Schedule** in its 119th meeting.

Since 2021, two umbrella civil-society platforms—the **Leh Apex Body (LAB)** and the **Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA)**—have united behind a four-point constitutional charter: **(1) Statehood**, **(2) Sixth Schedule inclusion**, **(3) a dedicated Ladakh Public Service Commission**, and **(4) two Lok Sabha seats** (separate representation for Leh and Kargil).

New Delhi's response reflects classic tension between **strategic frontier security** (bordering both China and Pakistan along the LAC/LoC) and **democratic tribal autonomy**. Rather than extending the Sixth Schedule outside the Northeast (which would require an Art 368 amendment to Art 244(2)), the Union exercised **Article 240 read with Section 58(2) of the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** in **June 2025** to promulgate executive-led statutory **Regulations** establishing a **15-year domicile rule**, **85% job reservation** (raising ST quota from 45% to 84%), **one-third women's reservation** in the statutory **Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (LAHDCs — 1997 Act)**, and official-language status for **Bhoti and Purgi**.

Yet statutory regulations under Article 240 remain revocable executive instruments lacking the constitutional permanence of Part XXI or the Sixth Schedule—and leave non-resident land purchases largely unregulated. Following violent protests in Leh in **September 2025** (leaving 4 dead), talks resumed under the MHA's **High-Powered Committee (HPC)** chaired by MoS Home Nityanand Rai. Although May 2026 discussions floated a customized *sui generis* **Article 371-like safeguard** and the LG expanded Ladakh to **eight districts** (adding Sankoo in October 2026) with plans for expanded hill councils, **LAB and KDA jointly rejected the MHA's October 2026 32-page draft proposal** for omitting an elected UT Assembly and explicit constitutional land/cultural safeguards—leaving Ladakh's governance deadlock unresolved as of **November 2026**.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Sole Occurrence of "Cabinet" in the Constitution:** The word **"Cabinet"** appears **ONLY ONCE** in the entire bare Constitution—inside **Article 352(3)** (inserted by the 44th CAA, 1978, mandating written Cabinet communication for National Emergency). It does **NOT** appear in Article 74, Article 75, or Article 356!
> - **Art 358 vs. Art 359 Double Trap:** **Article 358** (automatic suspension of Art 19) is triggered **ONLY** by *War or External Aggression*—**NEVER** by *Armed Rebellion*! **Article 359** works on all three grounds via Presidential Order, **NEVER** suspends *rights themselves* (only court enforcement), and **NEVER** touches **Articles 20 and 21**.
> - **Art 356 Dissolution & Lapsing Traps (UPSC Prelims 2018):** Invoking Article 356 does **NOT** automatically dissolve the State Assembly (*Bommai* bars dissolution until BOTH Houses approve!), does **NOT** suspend Article 19, and does **NOT** touch High Court jurisdiction. Extending Art 356 beyond **1 year** requires **BOTH** Art 352 Emergency **AND** ECI certification (**Art 356(5)**)—either alone is insufficient!
> - **Financial Emergency Indefinite Duration Trap (Art 360):** Unlike Art 352 and Art 356 (which mandate 6-month parliamentary renewals), **Article 360 requires ZERO periodic renewal** once approved within 2 months—it continues indefinitely until Presidential revocation.
> - **Article 240 UT List Trap (Live 2025–2026 Hot Zone):** Bare text of **Art 240** covers **A&N Islands, Lakshadweep, DNH&DD, and Puducherry** (*only when Assembly is suspended/dissolved*), **PLUS Ladakh** via **Section 58(2) of the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**. It does **NOT** apply to **NCT Delhi, UT of J&K, or Chandigarh**!
> - **NCT Delhi 10% Ministerial Cap & Three Exclusions (Art 239AA):** Delhi's Council of Ministers cap is **10%** (max 7 ministers), NOT the 15% applicable to States; its Assembly is barred from **Public Order (Entry 1), Police (Entry 2), and Land (Entry 18)**—whereas the **UT of J&K Assembly** is barred from **Public Order and Police ONLY** (it retains **Land**!).
> - ***In Re: Article 370* (Dec 2023) Precision Trap:** The 5-Judge Bench **STRUCK DOWN Paragraph 2 of C.O. 272** (amending Art 370 indirectly via Art 367), **UPHELD C.O. 273** (unilateral Presidential power under Art 370(3)), **UPHELD UT of Ladakh**, and **LEFT OPEN** whether Art 3 permits converting a full State into a UT.
> - **Art 371A (Nagaland) vs. Art 371G (Mizoram) Atomic Trap:** Both shield customary law and land transfer from Parliamentary Acts absent an Assembly resolution—**but ONLY Art 371A (Nagaland)** covers *"land **and its resources**"* (oil/minerals); **Art 371G (Mizoram)** covers land ownership/transfer *without* *"resources"*!
> - **Sixth Schedule Act-Extension Split (Para 12–12B):** In **Assam**, the **Governor** directs whether Parliamentary Acts apply to ADCs; in **Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram**, the **President** directs Parliamentary Acts while the **Governor** directs State Acts.
> - **Simple Majority vs. Art 368 Amendment Trap for 5th/6th Schedules:** Modifying internal rules of the Fifth Schedule (Para 7) or Sixth Schedule (Para 21) requires only a **Simple Majority** (outside Art 368)—**HOWEVER**, adding a new State/UT like **Ladakh** into **Article 244(2)** requires a **Special Majority Constitutional Amendment under Article 368**!

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **Northeast Schedule & Article Mix-Ups:** Examiners love slipping **Manipur, Nagaland, or Arunachal Pradesh** into Sixth Schedule options (none of them are in the Sixth Schedule—they rely on **Articles 371C, 371A, and 371H** respectively!), or swapping **Governor** (6th Schedule area declaration) with **President** (5th Schedule area declaration).
- **Emergency Majority, Timeline & Renewal Scrambles:** Classic inversion traps pair **Article 352** with *"2 months / Simple Majority"* (actually **1 month / Special Majority**), claim **Article 358** applies to *Armed Rebellion* (false—War & External Aggression only), or assert **Article 360** must be renewed every 6 months (false—indefinite once approved).
- **UT Legislative & Regulation Confusions:** Watch for false statements claiming that *Ladakh has no Article 240 regulation power* (false—conferred via Sec 58(2) of J&K Reorg Act, 2019), *Delhi's CoM ceiling is 15%* (false—**10%**), or *J&K UT Assembly cannot legislate on Land* (false—only Public Order and Police are excluded, unlike Delhi's three exclusions!).
- **Verdict Over-Generalization:** Any statement asserting *"In Re: Article 370 (2023) upheld both C.O. 272 and C.O. 273 in full and ruled that Parliament can permanently convert any State into a Union Territory under Article 3"* is an immediate elimination trigger (Para 2 of C.O. 272 was struck down; the State-to-UT question was deliberately left open).
