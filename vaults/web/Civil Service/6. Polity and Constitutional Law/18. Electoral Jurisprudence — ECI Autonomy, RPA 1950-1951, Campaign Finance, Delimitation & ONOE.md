# Chapter 18 - Electoral Jurisprudence — ECI Autonomy, RPA 1950/1951, Campaign Finance, Delimitation & ONOE

Democracy does not stand on rhetoric; it stands on its plumbing—who draws the constituencies, who controls the voter rolls, who referees the party factions, who funds the campaigns, and who appoints the referee. Part XV of the Constitution (Articles 324–329), backed by the twin Representation of the People Acts of 1950 and 1951, forms the load-bearing infrastructure of India's representative mandate.

Between 2023 and late 2026, almost every joint in that infrastructure came under intense constitutional stress. The **CEC & Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023** reshaped ECI appointments (triggering a September 2026 Supreme Court split on Constitution Bench reference); the **Electoral Bonds verdict (Feb 2024)** struck down anonymous corporate funding while triggering a shift to Electoral Trusts; the **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of voter rolls triggered the landmark **May 2026 Supreme Court ruling (`2026 INSC 564`)** and a direct **UPSC Mains 2026 GS2** question; **Census 2027** locked in the post-2026 North–South delimitation reckoning; and the **129th Amendment Bill, 2024** (One Nation, One Election) remained before a Joint Parliamentary Committee. Mastering this chapter means mastering both bare-article traps and the sharpest live debates in Indian constitutional law.

---

## Constitutional Framework and ECI Autonomy (Part XV - Articles 324–329)

Rejecting the colonial model where executive ministries ran polls, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar lifted election management completely out of executive control and vested it in a single permanent constitutional authority under Part XV.

### Bare-Article Architecture of Part XV

- **Article 324 - Plenary Superintendence:** Vests the *"superintendence, direction and control"* of preparing electoral rolls and conducting all elections to **Parliament, State Legislatures, the Office of the President, and the Office of the Vice-President** in the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**. Crucially, ECI has **zero jurisdiction over Panchayat and Municipal elections**—those belong exclusively to **State Election Commissions (SECs)** under Articles 243K and 243ZA.
  - ***Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner* (1978, 5-Judge Bench):** Construed Article 324 as a constitutional *"reservoir of power"* allowing ECI to act in areas left unoccupied by parliamentary legislation (e.g., cancelling a vitiated poll and ordering a repoll). Yet Article 324 is not boundless: ECI **cannot override valid parliamentary enactments** made under Article 327, and its orders remain bound by **natural justice and rule of law**.
- **Article 325 - Single General Electoral Roll:** Mandates one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency and bars excluding any person from a roll on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, sex, or any of them**—permanently burying colonial separate electorates.
- **Article 326 - Universal Adult Suffrage & Legal Status of the Right to Vote:** Declares that elections to the **House of the People (Lok Sabha) and State Legislative Assemblies** (not Rajya Sabha or Legislative Councils) shall be based on adult suffrage—every Indian citizen aged **18 years or above** *(lowered from 21 by the **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988**, effective March 28, 1989)* unless disqualified under law for non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, or corrupt/illegal practice.
  - **Is the Right to Vote a Fundamental Right? (Direct 2026 GS2 Focus):** **No.** Across *N.P. Ponnuswami* (1952), *Jyoti Basu v. Debi Ghosal* (1982), *Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India* (2006), and *ADR v. ECI* (`2026 INSC 564`), the Supreme Court settled that the **right to vote (and right to contest)** is a **constitutional/statutory right**, not a Part III Fundamental Right. However, the Supreme Court drew a crucial doctrinal nuance in *PUCL v. Union of India* (2003 & 2013): while the *right to vote* itself is statutory/constitutional, the **freedom of voting**—making an informed choice or rejecting all candidates via NOTA—is a facet of freedom of expression under **Article 19(1)(a)**. *(In Anoop Baranwal [2023], Justice Ajay Rastogi's separate concurrence argued the right to vote deserves constitutional-right status above ordinary statute, though the majority did not disturb the non-FR classification.)*
- **Articles 327 & 328 - Legislative Competence:** Article 327 grants **Parliament** plenary power to legislate on electoral rolls, delimitation, and all matters connected with Union and State Legislature elections (the constitutional parent of RPA 1950, RPA 1951, and Delimitation Acts). Article 328 allows State Legislatures to fill gaps for State polls only insofar as Parliament has not legislated.
- **Article 329 - Bar to Judicial Interference:**
  - **Article 329(a):** Immunizes the validity of any law relating to **delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats** under Articles 327/328 from being questioned in any court.
  - **Article 329(b):** Bars courts from questioning any election to Parliament or a State Legislature **except through an Election Petition** presented to an authority prescribed by law (the **High Court** under Section 80A of RPA 1951). In ***N.P. Ponnuswami v. Returning Officer* (1952)**, the Supreme Court read the word *"election"* expansively to cover the entire arc from notification to result declaration—prohibiting High Courts from entertaining Article 226 writ petitions that stall an ongoing poll, while confining judicial intervention to post-result election petitions (with statutory appeal to the Supreme Court under Section 116A of RPA 1951).

### Institutional Structure, *T.N. Seshan* (1995), and Asymmetric Removal Protection

From 1950 until October 1989, the ECI functioned as a **single-member** body headed solely by the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC). After a brief 3-month multi-member experiment in late 1989 (prompted by the 61st Amendment's voter surge), the President permanently converted the Commission into a **three-member body** (CEC + two Election Commissioners) in October 1993.

In ***T.N. Seshan v. Union of India* (1995, 5-Judge Bench)**, the Supreme Court rejected the CEC's challenge to multi-member parity and laid down the bedrock rule of internal governance:
- **Collective Parity:** The CEC is merely ***primus inter pares*** (first among equals) who chairs meetings, possessing **no veto or hierarchical superiority** over the other two Election Commissioners. All ECI business must be decided unanimously or by **majority vote**—a rule now codified in **Section 18 of the CEC & Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023** (and freshly invoked before the Supreme Court in October 2026 amid disputes over internal consensus during the nationwide SIR).

**The Structural Removal Asymmetry - Proviso to Article 324(5):**
Despite equal voting weight inside the Commission, the Constitution creates a stark **removal gap**:
1. **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC):** Enjoys **Supreme Court Judge-like security of tenure**—removable only by the President following a parliamentary special-majority impeachment motion on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity. Service conditions cannot be varied to the CEC's disadvantage after appointment.
2. **Other Election Commissioners (and Regional Commissioners):** Enjoy **no parliamentary impeachment shield**; they can be removed by the President **on the recommendation of the CEC**. *(Both the Goswami Committee [1990] and ECI's own 2016 reform proposals urged extending impeachment parity to all Election Commissioners to insulate them from executive or internal pressure.)*
3. **Constitutional Silences:** Article 324 does **NOT** prescribe qualifications, fix a numerical tenure (left to statute: 6 years or age 65), charge ECI's administrative secretariat directly on the Consolidated Fund of India, or constitutionally bar retiring Commissioners from post-retirement government appointments.

### *Anoop Baranwal* (2023) vs. The CEC & Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023 (2026 Status)

For 73 years, Parliament enacted no law under **Article 324(2)** governing *how* Election Commissioners are appointed—leaving selection entirely to the Union Cabinet's unchecked discretion.

In ***Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India* (March 2, 2023, 5-Judge Constitution Bench)**, the Supreme Court held that exclusive executive control over appointing the electoral referee violates free and fair elections (part of the Basic Structure). Invoking Article 142 to fill the legislative vacuum, the Bench directed that CEC and ECs be appointed by the President on the advice of a three-member committee comprising the **Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha (or leader of the largest opposition party), and the Chief Justice of India (CJI)**—expressly stipulating that this arrangement would hold **until Parliament enacted a law under Article 324(2)**.

In December 2023, Parliament occupied the field by enacting the **Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023** (repealing the 1991 Act):
- **Search Committee (Sec 6):** Chaired by the **Union Minister of Law and Justice**, plus two Union Government officers not below the rank of Secretary, tasked with preparing a **panel of five persons**.
- **Selection Committee (Sec 7):** Comprises the **Prime Minister (Chairperson), the Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha** (or leader of the single largest opposition party), and a **Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister**—replacing the CJI and restoring a **2:1 executive majority**. The Selection Committee may also consider any candidate **outside** the Search Committee's shortlist, and under **Section 8**, no appointment is invalidated merely due to a vacancy or defect in the Committee's constitution.
- **Qualifications, Tenure & Parity:** Appointees must be persons of integrity with knowledge/experience in election management who hold or have held a post equivalent to **Secretary to the Government of India**. Tenure is **6 years or age 65**, non-reappointable; salary and service conditions were restored to **Supreme Court Judge parity** via official amendment (after an initial draft drew criticism for downgrading them to Cabinet Secretary rank).
- **Section 16 Blanket Immunity:** Bars any court from entertaining **civil or criminal proceedings** against serving or former CECs and ECs for any act, thing, or word spoken or done while discharging official duties.

**Live Constitutional Status (November 2026):**
- **Sept 23, 2026 Split Verdict on Reference:** Challenging the 2023 Act as an unconstitutional legislative override of *Anoop Baranwal* that destroys ECI independence, petitions led by ADR reached a two-judge Bench (Justices Dipankar Datta and Satish Chandra Sharma). On **September 23, 2026**, the Bench delivered a **split verdict** on the preliminary question of reference—Justice Datta holding the division bench could decide the merits directly, Justice Sharma holding that fundamental constitutional questions required a larger bench. Both judges jointly placed the files before **CJI Surya Kant** to consider constituting a **5-Judge Constitution Bench** (pending as of November 2026, while appointments such as CEC Gyanesh Kumar proceeded under the 2023 Act).
- **October 2026 Challenge to Section 16 Immunity:** Separately, in **October 2026**, the Supreme Court issued notice to the Union Government on ADR's petition challenging **Section 16** as violating Article 14 equality before law and insulating constitutional functionaries from accountability.

## Representation of the People Acts (RPA 1950 & 1951), Electoral Rolls (SIR 2025–2026), Parties & Corrupt Practices

Parliament operationalized Article 327 through a clean two-statute split that Prelims routinely cross-wires:
- **Representation of the People Act, 1950 (*Pre-Election Architecture*):** Governs **allocation of seats** in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies, and Legislative Councils (Schedules 1–4), **delimitation** framework, **voter qualifications/disqualifications**, preparation and revision of **electoral rolls**, and administrative hierarchy (**Chief Electoral Officer [CEO]**, District Election Officer [DEO], **Electoral Registration Officer [ERO]**). Under **Section 30**, civil courts are barred from questioning ERO decisions on roll inclusion or deletion.
- **Representation of the People Act, 1951 (*Actual Conduct of Elections*):** Governs **conduct of elections**, **qualifications and disqualifications of candidates** (including Sections 8–11A), **registration of political parties** (Section 29A), notification and nomination procedures, campaign expenditure limits (Section 77), **corrupt practices** (Section 123), counting, and **election dispute adjudication** via High Court Election Petitions (Sections 80–116A).

### Electoral Roll Revision and the 2025–2026 Special Intensive Revision (SIR) Showdown

Maintaining accurate electoral rolls balances two competing constitutional imperatives: **electoral purity** (removing deceased, shifted, duplicate, and non-citizen entries) and **universal enfranchisement** (preventing arbitrary exclusion of genuine citizens).

**Statutory Mechanics (RPA, 1950):**
- **Section 16** disqualifies non-citizens, persons of unsound mind (declared by a competent court), and those disqualified for corrupt/illegal practices.
- **Section 19** requires ordinary residence in the constituency and attainment of 18 years on the qualifying date. The **Election Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021** expanded the single January 1 qualifying date to **four annual qualifying dates** (**January 1, April 1, July 1, and October 1**), made voter-roll wording gender-neutral for service voters' spouses, and inserted **Section 23(4)** authorizing EROs to request **voluntary Aadhaar seeding** solely to establish identity and authenticate roll entries.
- **Section 21(3)** grants ECI a sweeping *non-obstante* power to direct, **for reasons to be recorded**, a **"special revision"** of electoral rolls *"in such manner as it may think fit"*.

**The 2025–2026 SIR Controversy and *ADR v. ECI* (`2026 INSC 564`, May 27, 2026):**
On **June 24, 2025**, citing two decades of urbanization, internal migration, unreported deaths, and duplicate entries since the last intensive revision (2003), the ECI ordered a **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** across Bihar ahead of Assembly polls and subsequently scaled it into a phased **nationwide SIR** across 2025–2026. Under the SIR protocol, house-to-house Booth Level Officers (BLOs) required electors either to map themselves/parents/grandparents onto the ~2002–03 intensive rolls ("self-mapping" or "progeny-mapping") or submit fresh Enumeration Forms backed by a narrow list of eleven prescribed documents. Massive draft-roll deletions, compressed claim windows, initial omission of Aadhaar/ration cards, and fears of mass disenfranchisement of migrant workers, women, and minorities sparked nationwide litigation.

In its landmark judgment ***Association for Democratic Reforms v. Election Commission of India* (`2026 INSC 564`, May 27, 2026)**, a Bench of **CJI Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi** upheld the Bihar SIR while drawing strict constitutional guardrails directly tested in **UPSC Mains 2026 GS2**:
1. **Broad Procedural Competence Upheld:** Held that Section 21(3) of RPA, 1950 read with Article 324 confers wide procedural amplitude upon ECI to conduct intensive roll revisions upon recording germane reasons; the exercise satisfied the **proportionality test**.
2. **The Citizenship Red Line (Critical Distinction):** Held that because **citizenship is a constitutional condition precedent** for voting under Article 326 and Section 16 of RPA 1950, ECI/EROs may conduct a **limited administrative inquiry** into citizenship eligibility for enrolment. However, the Court drew a bright constitutional boundary: **formal adjudication or determination of Indian citizenship falls EXCLUSIVELY within the domain of the Competent Authority under the Citizenship Act, 1955**. ECI cannot declare an individual a foreigner; doubtful cases must be referred to the Competent Authority.
3. **Aadhaar's Evidentiary Ceiling:** Directing ECI to accept **Aadhaar** as an additional twelfth document under Section 23(4) of RPA 1950, the Court clarified unequivocally that **Aadhaar establishes identity ONLY—never citizenship or domicile**.
4. **Distinguishing *Lal Babu Hussein* (1995) & Late-2026 Follow-Up:** The Bench distinguished *Lal Babu Hussein* (1995) on factual context while reaffirming statutory remedies for aggrieved voters. Fresh petitions challenging the nationwide rollout across UP, West Bengal, Kerala, Chhattisgarh, and Delhi—as well as modifications to Form 6 and ECI internal deliberations—remain pending before the Supreme Court as of November 2026. On **November 3, 2026**, ECI clarified that previously enrolled voters dropped from SIR draft rolls may file **Form 6** afresh without facing false-declaration prosecution under **Section 31 of RPA, 1950**.

### Political Parties - Section 29A Registration, De-Registration Vacuum, and Symbol Disputes

**Section 29A of RPA, 1951** governs the registration of political parties with ECI, requiring every party's memorandum to affirm true faith and allegiance to the Constitution and to principles of **socialism, secularism, democracy, and sovereignty/unity/integrity of India**. Registration unlocks income-tax exemptions (Section 13A, Income Tax Act, 1961) and eligibility to seek recognized **National** or **State Party** status under the Election Symbols Order, 1968.

**The De-Registration Gap (*INC v. Institute of Social Welfare*, 2002):**
In ***Indian National Congress v. Institute of Social Welfare* (2002)**, the Supreme Court held that while Section 29A empowers ECI to *register* parties, **ECI possesses NO statutory power to de-register** an existing party—even if it violates constitutional pledges, holds no internal elections, or never contests a poll—except under **three razor-narrow exceptions**:
1. Registration was obtained by **fraud or forgery**;
2. The party itself notifies ECI that it has ceased to bear faith in the Constitution; or
3. The party is declared an **unlawful association** by the Central Government.

Because Parliament has never amended Section 29A despite repeated ECI requests, over 2,700 **Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPPs)** proliferated—many operating as paper conduits for money laundering and tax evasion. Unable to de-register them, ECI can only *delist* chronically inactive RUPPs from the active register, stripping their symbol concessions and forwarding financial anomalies to CBDT.

**Factional Splits and Symbol Disputes - *Sadiq Ali* (1971) & Para 15:**
When a recognized party fractures into rival factions, **Paragraph 15 of the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** vests ECI with **quasi-judicial authority** to award the party's reserved symbol (or freeze it pending decision). In ***Sadiq Ali v. Election Commission of India* (1971)**, the Supreme Court approved three sequential tests—(i) aims and objects, (ii) party constitution/internal democracy, and (iii) the **"Test of Majority"** across both the **organisational wing** (delegates/office-bearers) and the **legislative wing** (MPs/MLAs).

In recent high-stakes splits (**Shiv Sena, 2023** and **NCP, 2024**), where party constitutions lacked verifiable internal elections, ECI discarded the organisational test as unreliable and relied decisively on **legislative-wing majority**. Constitutional scholars flag an acute doctrinal clash here: under the Tenth Schedule, a 5-Judge Constitution Bench in *Subhash Desai v. Governor of Maharashtra* (May 2023) held that the **political party** (organisational leadership) is distinct from—and controls—the **legislature party**, yet ECI's Para 15 practice allows a defecting legislative majority to capture the parent political party's name and symbol!

### Corrupt Practices (Section 123), Election Petitions, and Digital Poll Oversight

**Section 123 of RPA, 1951** enumerates **corrupt practices** that render an election **void** under **Section 100** (adjudicated by the High Court via Election Petition filed within **45 days** of result declaration under Section 81) and trigger up to **6 years' disqualification** by the President upon ECI opinion (**Section 8A**):
- Core grounds include **bribery** (123(1)), **undue influence** (123(2)), **religious/caste/communal appeals** (123(3)), **promoting enmity** (123(3A)), **false personal statements** (123(4)), hiring vehicles for voters (123(5)), **exceeding statutory candidate expenditure limits** under Section 77 (123(6) — *note: political party expenditure carries ZERO statutory ceiling!*), obtaining **gazetted government officer assistance** (123(7)), and **booth capturing** (123(8)).
- **Secular Neutrality - *Abhiram Singh v. C.D. Commachen* (2017, 7-Judge Bench, 4:3):** Interpreting the phrase *"his religion, race, caste, community or language"* in **Section 123(3)**, the majority adopted a purposive reading barring appeals based on the identity not merely of the candidate or opponent, **but also of the voters/electorate**. *(The 3-judge dissent warned that an overbroad bar risks criminalizing legitimate mobilization of historically marginalized castes and minorities.)*
- **Disproportionate Assets as "Undue Influence" (*Lok Prahari*, 2018 & Mains 2025 GS2):** In ***Lok Prahari v. Union of India* (2018)**, the Supreme Court held that non-disclosure of sources of income and unexplained, disproportionate accretion of assets by legislators or their associates impairs voters' informed choice and constitutes **"undue influence" under Section 123(2)**—a direct corrupt practice.
- **Model Code of Conduct (MCC) & 2026 Digital Regulation:** Originating in 1960 Kerala assembly consensus guidelines and enforced nationwide from 1991, the **MCC is NOT a statute**—it operates from the date ECI announces the poll schedule until results are declared, backed by ECI's Article 324 moral suasion, reprimands/campaign bans, and underlying penal provisions (BNS/RPA). Adapting to digital warfare, ECI launched **ECINET** on **January 22, 2026** (integrating 40+ electoral apps across 22 Eighth Schedule languages plus English) and issued a **3-hour takedown mandate (April 2026)** targeting misleading AI-generated synthetic/deepfake election content.

## Disqualifications, Criminalisation of Politics, and Voting Technology

### Constitutional Disqualifications and Office of Profit (Articles 102, 103, 191, 192)

**Articles 102(1) (MPs) and 191(1) (MLAs/MLCs)** lay down five grounds of disqualification: (a) holding an **Office of Profit** under Union or State Government (other than a Minister or an office expressly exempted by law, such as under the *Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959*); (b) **unsoundness of mind** declared by a competent court; (c) **undischarged insolvent**; (d) non-citizen or voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship/allegiance; and (e) disqualification under **any law made by Parliament** (RPA 1951). **Articles 102(2) and 191(2)** incorporate Tenth Schedule defection disqualifications.

- **Binding ECI Opinion (Articles 103 & 192):** Any question regarding post-election constitutional/RPA disqualification (excluding Tenth Schedule defection, decided by the Presiding Officer) is decided by the **President (for MPs) or Governor (for State Legislators)**, who **must obtain and act strictly in accordance with the binding opinion of the ECI**.
- **Office of Profit Test (*Jaya Bachchan v. Union of India*, 2006):** To protect legislative independence from executive patronage, the Supreme Court held that what makes an office one "of profit" is whether the office is **capable of yielding pecuniary gain** (salary, honorarium, perks exceeding mere compensatory allowance)—it is completely irrelevant whether the legislator **actually drew** the remuneration or waived it.

### Statutory Criminal Disqualification - Section 8 RPA 1951 & *Lily Thomas* (2013)

**Section 8 of RPA, 1951** constructs a three-tier disqualification ladder upon **conviction** (not merely upon filing of an FIR or framing of charges):
- **Section 8(1):** Conviction for specified grave offences (bribery, rape, promoting enmity, untouchability/PCR Act, UAPA, narcotics, Prevention of Corruption Act, Sati, FERA) triggers disqualification for **6 years** from conviction (if fined only) or imprisonment term **plus 6 years after release**.
- **Section 8(2):** Conviction for hoarding, profiteering, food/drug adulteration, or Dowry Prohibition Act with imprisonment of **at least 6 months** triggers disqualification during jail **plus 6 years post-release**.
- **Section 8(3):** Conviction for **any other offence** carrying imprisonment of **not less than 2 years** triggers disqualification from conviction date **plus 6 years after release**. *(Note: Section 9 bars dismissed government servants found guilty of corruption/disloyalty for **5 years**; Section 10A bars failure to lodge election expense accounts for **3 years**; under **Section 11**, ECI may remove or reduce any RPA disqualification period for recorded reasons, except Section 8A corrupt-practice disqualifications decided by the President.)*

**The *Lily Thomas* Revolution (2013):**
Historically, **Section 8(4)** shielded *sitting* MPs and MLAs from disqualification for 3 months after conviction—and indefinitely thereafter if an appeal was filed—allowing convicted lawmakers to serve full terms while appeals languished. In ***Lily Thomas v. Union of India* (2013)**, the Supreme Court struck down **Section 8(4)** as ***ultra vires* Articles 102(1)(e) and 191(1)(e)**, holding Parliament lacked constitutional competence to create a softer disqualification regime for sitting legislators than for ordinary candidates.
- **Instant Disqualification Rule:** Membership now ceases **automatically on the moment of conviction**. Only an explicit appellate **stay of the conviction itself**—not merely bail or a stay of *sentence*—restores legislative membership (*Lok Prahari*, 2018; *Afjal Ansari*, 2023).

**Criminalisation Impasse and the Lifetime Ban Debate (2025–2026 Status):**
Despite dedicated MP/MLA Special Courts monitored by the Supreme Court, amicus reports in 2025 noted over **5,000 pending criminal cases** against legislators and over **42% of sitting Lok Sabha MPs** declaring criminal cases. Two judicial boundaries define the stalemate:
1. **No Judicial Disqualification at Charge-Framing (*Public Interest Foundation*, 2018, 5-Judge Bench):** Calling criminalization a *"termite to the citadel of democracy,"* the Court nevertheless held separation of powers bars judges from adding disqualification grounds—only Parliament can amend Section 8. Instead, the Court mandated disclosure (expanded in ***Rambabu Singh Thakur*, 2020** and ***Brajesh Singh*, 2021**, ordering political parties to publish candidate criminal histories **and reasons for selecting tainted candidates over clean alternatives** on websites, social media, and newspapers within **48 hours** of selection).
2. **Lifetime Ban Petition (*Ashwini Kumar Upadhyay v. UoI*, Pending 2025–2026):** In February 2025, a Supreme Court Bench questioned the stark conflict of interest in convicted lawbreakers returning as lawmakers after 6 years. In its **February 24, 2025 counter-affidavit**, the **Union Government opposed a lifetime ban** as "unduly harsh" and exclusively within parliamentary legislative policy domain. The 6-year post-release ceiling thus remains active law as of November 2026.

### Voter's Right to Know, NOTA, and the April 2024 EVM–VVPAT Judgment

**Candidate Affidavits & NOTA:**
- ***Union of India v. ADR* (2002) & *PUCL v. UoI* (2003):** Grounded mandatory **Form 26 affidavits** (disclosing criminal cases, assets/liabilities of candidate, spouse, and dependents, and educational qualifications) in the voter's **Article 19(1)(a)** right to informed political speech, striking down Section 33B of RPA 1951 that had sought to nullify the mandate.
- **NOTA (*PUCL v. Union of India*, 2013):** Directed ECI to include a **"None of the Above"** button on EVMs to safeguard the secrecy of negative voting. **Two atomic Prelims rules:** (i) NOTA confers **no "right to reject"**—even if NOTA polls the maximum votes, the candidate with the highest valid votes among contestants wins; and (ii) **NOTA does NOT apply to Rajya Sabha elections** (*Shailesh Manubhai Parmar v. ECI*, 2018), because Rajya Sabha polls use open-ballot proportional representation.

**Voting Technology - April 26, 2024 EVM–VVPAT Judgment (*ADR v. ECI*, `2024 INSC 341`):**
Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) paired with Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (**VVPAT**, mandated in *Subramanian Swamy*, 2013; illuminated for **7 seconds** behind glass before falling into a sealed box) undergo mandatory physical tallying of **5 randomly selected EVMs per Assembly Constituency / Assembly segment** (raised from 1 to 5 in *N. Chandrababu Naidu*, 2019).

In ***Association for Democratic Reforms v. ECI* (April 26, 2024)**, Justices **Sanjiv Khanna and Dipankar Datta** unanimously rejected pleas to revert to paper ballots or mandate **100% VVPAT slip counting**, noting standalone EVMs are network-isolated with one-time programmable burnt memory while paper ballots risk booth capturing and manual counting fraud. Balancing institutional trust with practicality, the Court imposed **two binding technical safeguards**:
1. **45-Day Sealing of Symbol Loading Units (SLUs):** Immediately after loading candidate symbols into VVPATs, SLUs must be sealed in containers signed by candidates/representatives and kept in strong rooms alongside EVMs for at least **45 days** post-result declaration (matching the statutory election-petition window).
2. **5% Burnt-Memory Verification on Request:** Candidates finishing **2nd or 3rd** may apply within **7 days** of result declaration for verification of the **burnt memory/microcontroller** in **5% of EVMs** (Control Unit, Ballot Unit, VVPAT) per Assembly Constituency/segment by manufacturer engineers (BEL/ECIL), at the candidate's expense—**refunded in full if any tampering is detected**. *(The Bench also urged ECI to examine barcode-assisted electronic counting machines for VVPAT slips.)*

## Political Finance - The Electoral Bonds Verdict (Feb 2024) and Post-Bonds Vacuum

Opaque political money is the primary distortion engine of Indian elections. Introduced through the **Finance Act, 2017** (controversially certified as a **Money Bill** under Article 110), the **Electoral Bond Scheme (notified Jan 2018)** allowed Indian citizens and companies to buy interest-free, bearer promissory notes exclusively from the State Bank of India (SBI) and donate them anonymously to political parties polling at least 1% of votes in the preceding Lok Sabha or Assembly election.

To erect this wall of secrecy, the Finance Act, 2017 simultaneously altered **four core statutes**:
1. **RBI Act, 1934 (Sec 31(3)):** Authorized the Centre to permit a scheduled bank (SBI) to issue bearer electoral bonds.
2. **RPA, 1951 (Sec 29C):** Exempted political parties from disclosing contributions received via Electoral Bonds in annual ECI contribution reports.
3. **Income Tax Act, 1961 (Sec 13A):** Exempted parties from maintaining donor records for bond receipts.
4. **Companies Act, 2013 (Sec 182):** **Abolished the 7.5% three-year average net-profit cap** on corporate donations and removed the mandate to disclose recipient party names in corporate profit-and-loss accounts—allowing even loss-making and freshly incorporated shell firms to donate unlimited sums. *(Separately, Finance Acts 2016 and 2018 retrospectively amended **FCRA 2010** to permit foreign-owned Indian subsidiaries to donate to parties.)*

### *ADR v. Union of India* (Feb 15, 2024, 5-Judge Constitution Bench)

In a unanimous **5:0 verdict** (`2024 INSC 113`, led by CJI D.Y. Chandrachud), the Supreme Court struck down the Electoral Bond Scheme and all linked statutory amendments as unconstitutional on **three foundational pillars**:
1. **Violation of Voters' Right to Information - Article 19(1)(a):** Holding that financial contributions give wealthy donors a *"seat at the table"* translating into policy capture and *quid pro quo*, the Court ruled that knowing who funds political parties is essential to casting an informed vote. **Curbing black money is NOT an enumerated restriction under Article 19(2)**. Balancing voters' right to know against donors' informational privacy (*Puttaswamy*, 2017), anonymous bonds **failed the "least restrictive means" prong of the proportionality test** because transparent banking instruments (cheques, UPI, and **Electoral Trusts**) already achieved clean money without secrecy.
2. **Manifest Arbitrariness on Unlimited Corporate Funding - Article 14:** Struck down the Finance Act 2017 amendment to **Section 182 of the Companies Act, 2013**. Equating deep-pocketed business corporations (whose donations are purely commercial transactions seeking regulatory favour) with ordinary citizens, and permitting **loss-making/shell companies** to make unlimited contributions, violated **political equality** and was **manifestly arbitrary**. The Court restored the **7.5% net-profit cap** and mandatory recipient disclosure.
3. **Selective Anonymity Fallacy:** Exposed that bond secrecy was strictly asymmetrical—opaque to citizens and opposition parties, yet fully traceable by the incumbent Union Government through state-owned **SBI** and investigative agencies.

The Court ordered SBI to immediately halt bond issuance and transmit full purchaser/redemption datasets (including hidden alphanumeric codes) to ECI for public publication—unmasking over ₹16,500 crore in bond flows (2018–2024). *(In August–September 2024, the Supreme Court dismissed review petitions and declined to constitute a court-monitored SIT probe into alleged quid pro quo, leaving criminal inquiries to ordinary law-enforcement remedies.)*

### The Post-Bonds Transparency Vacuum (2024–2026 Reality)

Scrapping Electoral Bonds restored donor visibility but left the deeper corporate-capture architecture untouched:
- **Massive Pivot to Electoral Trusts:** Corporations rapidly shifted to **Electoral Trusts** (non-profit Section 8/25 companies regulated by CBDT under the **Electoral Trusts Scheme, 2013**, required to distribute **≥95%** of annual receipts to registered parties and disclose both donor and recipient identities to ECI). Per **ADR's February 2026 analysis of FY 2024–25 reports**, ten active trusts routed **₹3,826.34 crore** to parties (over 95% from 228 corporate houses), yet funding concentration intensified—with the ruling party receiving **82.52% (₹3,157.65 crore)**, while five registered trusts failed to file timely disclosures.
- **Four Unresolved Loopholes:** (i) Anonymous **cash donations below ₹2,000** remain legal; (ii) **Section 77 of RPA, 1951** caps *candidate* expenditure but places **zero ceiling on political party expenditure**; (iii) national political parties still refuse to comply with the **2013 CIC order** declaring them public authorities under the **RTI Act, 2005**; and (iv) India lacks institutional **partial state funding of elections** (recommended by the **Indrajit Gupta Committee, 1998**) or an independent **National Electoral Fund** (proposed by the **Law Commission's 255th Report, 2015**).

## Delimitation, Census 2027, and One Nation, One Election (ONOE)

### Delimitation Architecture and the Census 2027 North–South Flashpoint

**Delimitation** fixes the number of seats allotted to each State and redraws territorial boundaries of Parliamentary and Assembly constituencies so every vote carries roughly equal demographic weight (**Article 81(2)**).

**Constitutional & Statutory Design (Articles 82, 170(3), 327, 329(a)):**
- **Mandate:** After each Census, Parliament enacts a Delimitation Act constituting an independent **Delimitation Commission** (chaired by a retired Supreme Court Judge, with the CEC or an EC nominee and the concerned State Election Commissioner as ex-officio members, assisted by non-voting associate MPs/MLAs).
- **Four National Delimitation Commissions Constituted So Far (Tested in Prelims 2024):** **1952** (1952 Act), **1963** (1962 Act), **1973** (1972 Act), and **2002** (2002 Act, chaired by Justice Kuldip Singh, which redrew internal boundaries and SC/ST seats using the **2001 Census** without altering inter-state seat totals). *(Region-specific exercises—such as the 2020–2022 J&K Delimitation Commission under Justice Ranjana Desai and ECI's 2023 Assam delimitation under Section 8A of RPA 1950—are distinct from the four nationwide Commissions.)*
- **Finality Lock:** Under **Article 329(a)** and Section 10 of the Delimitation Act, Commission orders carry the force of law, **cannot be challenged in any court**, and when laid before Lok Sabha or State Assemblies, **no modifications are permissible**.

**The Inter-State Freeze Chain (1976–2026):**
- **42nd CAA (1976):** Froze total Lok Sabha and Assembly seats per State at **1971 Census** levels until the first Census after 2000, preventing family-planning incentives from penalizing population-stabilizing States.
- **84th CAA (2001):** Extended the **1971 Census freeze on inter-state seat allocation** until the **first Census taken after the year 2026**.
- **87th CAA (2003):** Allowed intra-state constituency rationalization and SC/ST seat adjustment on **2001 Census** figures while retaining the 1971 inter-state cap.

**Census 2027 and the April 2026 Pivot:**
- **Census 2027 Timeline (Notified June 2025 – Oct 2026):** India's 16th Census (8th post-Independence) completed **Phase I (Houselisting & Housing Census)** between April and September 2026. On **October 30, 2026**, the Registrar General notified **Phase II (Population Enumeration)** for **January 25 – February 28, 2027** (reference date **00:00 hours, March 1, 2027**; snow-bound Ladakh/HP/J&K/Uttarakhand reference date **October 1, 2026**). It is India's **first fully digital Census**, introduces **self-enumeration**, and includes **caste enumeration**.
- **Defeat of the 131st Amendment Bill (April 16, 2026):** In April 2026, the Government introduced the **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026** attempting to use **2011 Census** data to accelerate delimitation and Women's Reservation; the Bill **failed to secure the Article 368 special two-thirds majority** in Lok Sabha. Consequently, **Census 2027** stands locked as the first post-2026 Census unlocking both national delimitation AND the **106th CAA (Women's Reservation)**!
- **The North–South Representation Crisis:** Between 1971 and 2027, Southern States (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka) achieved below-replacement fertility (TFR ~1.5–1.8) while Northern Hindi-belt States (UP, Bihar, MP, Rajasthan) grew rapidly. Applying strict one-person-one-vote proportionality post-2027 without federal adjustment would trigger a seismic power transfer: if Lok Sabha stays at 543, Southern States lose dozens of seats outright; even if Lok Sabha expands to ~848 seats (matching the new Parliament building's 888-seat chamber), Southern States' *percentage share* shrinks sharply despite contributing disproportionately to national GDP and tax revenue. Possible federal compromises include extending the inter-state freeze, adopting a **pro-rata expansion** keeping regional shares intact, or reforming the **Rajya Sabha** into an equal-state federal chamber like the US Senate.

### One Nation, One Election (ONOE) - Kovind Panel & 129th Amendment Bill, 2024

**Historical Precedent & Committee Consensus:**
India held simultaneous Lok Sabha and State Assembly polls in **1952, 1957, 1962, and 1967**, until premature Assembly dissolutions (Article 356) in 1968–69 and the 1970 Lok Sabha dissolution broke the cycle. Synchronization was subsequently examined by ECI (1983), the **Law Commission (170th Report, 1999 & 2018 Draft Report)**, the **Parliamentary Standing Committee (79th Report, 2015)**, and **NITI Aayog (2017)**.

**High-Level Committee (Ram Nath Kovind Panel - March 2024):**
Accepted by the Union Cabinet in September 2024, the Kovind Committee proposed a **two-step architecture**:
- **Step 1:** Synchronize **Lok Sabha and all State/UT Legislative Assemblies** via a constitutional amendment (designed to avoid requiring State ratification).
- **Step 2:** Synchronize **Panchayat and Municipal elections within 100 days** of the general polls, alongside a **Single Electoral Roll and Common EPIC** prepared by ECI in consultation with SECs (requiring **50% State ratification** under the Proviso to Article 368(2)).

**The Twin Bills (Introduced Dec 17, 2024; Under JPC Review as of Nov 2026):**
Translating **Step 1 only** (deferring local bodies and single electoral roll), the Government introduced two Bills on **December 17, 2024**, referred on **December 19, 2024** to a **Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC)** chaired by **P.P. Chaudhary**:
1. **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024:** Inserts **Article 82A** and amends **Articles 83, 172, and 327**:
   - **Appointed Date (Art 82A(1)):** Triggered by **Presidential notification on the date of the first sitting of Lok Sabha** after a general election (envisaged post-2029 for full simultaneous polls by **2034**).
   - **Automatic Curtailment of State Assemblies (Art 82A(2)):** Every State Legislative Assembly constituted in any general election *after* the Appointed Date and before the expiry of Lok Sabha's full term shall **automatically expire coterminously with that Lok Sabha**—regardless of how recently that Assembly was elected.
   - **Unexpired Term Formula (Amended Art 83 & Art 172):** If Lok Sabha or any State Assembly is prematurely dissolved due to a hung house or no-confidence vote, the fresh House elected in mid-term polls serves **only for the unexpired remainder** of the original 5-year cycle (expressly clarified as *not* a continuation of the old House).
   - **ECI Deferral Discretion (Art 82A(5)–(6)):** If ECI opines a State Assembly election cannot be held simultaneously, it may recommend a Presidential order deferring it—yet even a deferred Assembly's term terminates on the **exact same date as Lok Sabha**.
   - **Article 327 Amendment:** Adds *"conduct of simultaneous elections"* to Parliament's electoral law-making power.
2. **Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024:** Ordinary legislation aligning Legislative Assemblies of **NCT Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu & Kashmir** with the simultaneous cycle.

**Live Status (November 2026):** As of November 2026, both Bills remain **under active JPC review** (Lok Sabha extended the JPC's tenure into the **Winter Session 2026**). In **October 2026**, the JPC cancelled an informal consultation planned with Supreme Court judges after MPs and the Court's administration flagged **separation-of-powers** objections because any enacted amendment would likely face Basic Structure judicial review.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Permanent Disqualification Myth (Tested Prelims 2020):** Under **Section 8(3) of RPA, 1951**, conviction with ≥2 years' imprisonment triggers disqualification during jail **plus 6 years after release**—**NOT a permanent/lifetime ban**. (Dismissed corrupt government servants face a **5-year** ban under Section 9; expense-filing defaulters face **3 years** under Section 10A.)
> - **Right to Vote vs. Freedom of Voting (Tested Mains 2026):** The **right to vote** (Art 326) is a **constitutional/statutory right**, **NOT** a Fundamental Right; however, the **freedom of expression in voting** (candidate disclosure, NOTA) falls under **Article 19(1)(a)**.
> - **SIR Citizenship & Aadhaar Trap (May 2026 Verdict):** ECI can conduct a **limited administrative inquiry** into citizenship eligibility under Sec 16/21(3) RPA 1950, **NOT** a binding adjudication of citizenship (exclusive to Competent Authority under Citizenship Act, 1955). **Aadhaar** is accepted as **identity proof ONLY, NOT citizenship or domicile proof**.
> - **ECI Removal & Selection Traps:** Only the **CEC** has Supreme Court Judge impeachment parity; other **Election Commissioners** are removed by the President **on the CEC's recommendation** (Art 324(5)). Under the **2023 Act**, the Selection Panel is **PM + LoP (Lok Sabha) + a PM-nominated Union Cabinet Minister** (NOT the CJI), and the Search Committee is headed by the **Union Law Minister** (NOT Cabinet Secretary).
> - **RPA 1950 vs. RPA 1951 Swap:** **Electoral rolls, voter eligibility, and seat allocation/delimitation** belong to **RPA, 1950**; **candidate qualifications/disqualifications, party registration (Sec 29A), corrupt practices (Sec 123), and High Court Election Petitions** belong to **RPA, 1951**.
> - **Lok Sabha Contesting Eligibility (Tested Prelims 2017):** A candidate contesting a Lok Sabha seat need **NOT** reside in that specific constituency—any Indian citizen aged ≥25 whose name appears on the electoral roll of **any parliamentary constituency in India** (except certain reserved seats/Assam-Lakshadweep-Sikkim special conditions) can contest.
> - **Party Expenditure & NOTA Negatives:** Statutory campaign expenditure caps under **Section 77 of RPA, 1951** apply **only to individual candidates, NOT to political parties**. **NOTA** does **NOT** apply to **Rajya Sabha** elections and does **NOT** trigger a fresh election even if NOTA finishes first.
> - **Four Delimitation Commissions & Absolute Finality (Tested Prelims 2012 & 2024):** India has constituted exactly **four** nationwide Delimitation Commissions (**1952, 1963, 1973, 2002**). Their orders **cannot be challenged in any court** (Art 329(a)) and **cannot be modified** by Lok Sabha or State Assemblies.
> - **129th Amendment Bill (ONOE) Scope Trap:** Unlike the Kovind Committee's full two-step roadmap, the **129th Amendment Bill, 2024** covers **ONLY Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies** (plus 3 UT Assemblies via the companion UT Laws Bill)—it does **NOT** synchronize Panchayat/Municipal elections or create a single electoral roll yet.

> [!info] Mains Arsenal
> - **Core Dialectical Frame 1 - Referee Independence vs. Legislative Supremacy (ECI 2023 Act & SIR):** Electoral democracy fails if the umpire is picked by one captain. Replacing the CJI with a PM-nominated Cabinet Minister in the **2023 Act** gives the executive a built-in **2:1 majority**, while **Section 16** grants unprecedented civil/criminal immunity—creating an accountability paradox precisely when ECI wields high-stakes powers over party symbols (Para 15) and nationwide **SIR roll revisions** (`2026 INSC 564`). **Way Forward:** Restore a non-executive balance in the selection panel (per **Goswami Committee 1990** & *Anoop Baranwal* 2023), extend Art 324(5) impeachment parity to all Election Commissioners, charge ECI Secretariat expenses on the CFI, and codify statutory natural-justice checklists for SIR deletions.
> - **Core Dialectical Frame 2 - Transparency vs. Level Playing Field (Post-Electoral Bonds):** Striking down Electoral Bonds (*ADR*, Feb 2024) cured *informational opacity* under Art 19(1)(a) and restored the 7.5% corporate profit cap, yet **FY 2024–25 Electoral Trust data** (₹3,826 crore; >82% to one party) proves transparency alone does not cure **incumbency resource asymmetry**. **Way Forward:** Cap **political party expenditure** alongside candidate ceilings (Law Commission 255th Report), eliminate the ₹2,000 anonymous cash loophole, bring parties under **RTI**, empower ECI to **de-register shell RUPPs** (amending Sec 29A), and pilot **partial in-kind state funding** (Indrajit Gupta Committee) coupled with a **National Electoral Fund**.
> - **Core Dialectical Frame 3 - Governance Efficiency vs. Federal Democratic Rhythm (ONOE - 129th Bill):** Proponents cite fiscal discipline, uninterrupted CAPF/teacher availability, and ending recurring **Model Code of Conduct** policy freezes. Critics highlight four Basic Structure pressures: (i) arbitrarily **curtailing State Assembly terms** under proposed **Art 82A(2)** subordinates State mandates to Union timing; (ii) abolishing staggered off-cycle polls removes mid-term **electoral accountability checks** on the Centre; (iii) empirical studies suggest a ~77% voter coattail effect favouring national parties over regional voices; and (iv) granting ECI open-ended deferral discretion under **Art 82A(5)** risks indirect President's Rule. **Way Forward:** Explore **Constructive Vote of No-Confidence** (German Basic Law Art 67) to prevent mid-term collapses, test voluntary cluster-synchronization (two election cycles every 2.5 years per 2018 Law Commission discussion), and seek **50% State ratification** to build federal consensus rather than pushing a unilateral amendment.
> - **Core Dialectical Frame 4 - Demographic Equality vs. Federal Equity (Census 2027 Delimitation):** Post-131st Bill defeat (April 2026), **Census 2027** forces a clash between **one-person-one-vote** (Art 81(2)) and **federal solidarity**, risking penalizing Southern States for successful human-development and population stabilization. **Way Forward:** Pair any Lok Sabha expansion with either a **pro-rata inter-state preservation formula** or a **reformed federal Rajya Sabha** (equal state representation + stronger fiscal/federal vetoes) to guarantee no region is structurally marginalized.

### Prelims Trap Signature

1. **Statutory-Jurisdiction Cross-Wiring:** Swapping **RPA 1950 vs. RPA 1951** functions (e.g., attributing electoral rolls to RPA 1951 or candidate disqualifications to RPA 1950), or assigning **Panchayat/Municipal elections** to ECI instead of State Election Commissions.
2. **Pre-2023 / Pre-2024 Stale-Law Distractors:** Inserting obsolete provisions such as *"CJI is a member of the statutory ECI Selection Committee"* (replaced by Cabinet Minister in 2023 Act), *"Corporate donations carry no net-profit cap"* (7.5% cap restored by Feb 2024 Electoral Bonds verdict), or *"Sitting MPs get 3 months' protection upon conviction"* (Sec 8(4) struck down in *Lily Thomas*, 2013).
3. **Scope Over-Extension on New Reforms:** Falsely claiming the **129th Amendment Bill, 2024** covers local-body polls or creates a common voter roll; claiming **ECI can de-register inactive political parties** or **adjudicate citizenship** during SIR; or claiming **Aadhaar proves citizenship** during roll verification.
4. **Numeric & Threshold Precision:** Mixing up **6 years post-release** (Sec 8) vs. **5 years** (Sec 9) vs. **3 years** (Sec 10A); **45 days** (Election Petition & SLU sealing) vs. **7 days** (5% EVM burnt-memory verification window); and **4 qualifying dates** (2021 Amendment) vs. the legacy single January 1 date.

### Cross-Hub AIR-1 Provocations

1. **The Convergence of Delimitation (Census 2027), Women's Reservation (106th CAA), and ONOE (129th Bill):** With the April 2026 defeat of the 131st Amendment Bill locking **Census 2027** as the simultaneous trigger for inter-state seat reallocation and 33% parliamentary women's reservation—just as the **129th Bill** proposes synchronizing Assembly terms by 2034—could executing three structural electoral shocks inside a single decade fracture India's **North–South federal compact** unless paired with a parallel fiscal and Rajya Sabha constitutional renegotiation?
2. **Digital Verification vs. Substantive Enfranchisement:** Read together, the **May 2026 SIR judgment (`2026 INSC 564`)**, voluntary Aadhaar seeding under **Sec 23(4) RPA 1950**, and ECI's **ECINET / 3-hour AI takedown** architecture point toward a highly digitized electoral state—yet when documentary verification collides with informal labour migration and synthetic media warfare, where must constitutional law draw the line between **preventing phantom/foreign votes** and **preventing bureaucratic disenfranchisement of the marginalized**?
