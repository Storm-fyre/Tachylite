# Grassroots Decentralization — PRIs (73rd CAA), ULBs (74th CAA), PESA & Co-operatives (97th CAA)

India's original 1950 Constitution constructed a **two-tier federation**—Union and States—leaving village and municipal self-government entirely at the mercy of State legislatures. Behind that omission lay a sharp Constituent Assembly clash: Mahatma Gandhi's vision of *Gram Swaraj* (self-governing village republics) collided with Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's sober warning that Indian villages were *"a sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism"* where entrenched caste hierarchies would crush Dalits without higher-state legal protection.

The resulting compromise tucked village panchayats into a non-justiciable Directive Principle (**Article 40**). Over the next four decades, bureaucratic centralization exposed the limits of top-down planning, culminating in the **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1992)**—which transformed India into the world's largest **three-tier democratic federation** encompassing over **31 lakh elected local representatives**—followed by the **PESA Act (1996)** for tribal self-rule and the **97th Amendment Act (2011)** for co-operative democracy.

---

## Evolution of Democratic Decentralization - From CDP (1952) to Constitutional Status (1992)

Post-independence rural governance began with two bureaucratically driven experiments—the **Community Development Programme (CDP, 1952)** and the **National Extension Service (NES, 1953)**. Both faltered for lack of popular participation, triggering five landmark committees that progressively built the intellectual case for a constitutional third tier:

- **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957):** Recommended **"democratic decentralization"** through a **three-tier Panchayati Raj structure**—directly elected *Gram Panchayat* at village level, indirectly elected *Panchayat Samiti* at block level (as the primary executive body), and *Zilla Parishad* at district level (advisory/supervisory, chaired by the District Collector). Following NDC acceptance, **Rajasthan** became the first State to inaugurate Panchayati Raj (**Nagaur district, October 2, 1959**), followed swiftly by **Andhra Pradesh** (November 1959).
- **Ashok Mehta Committee (1977):** Appointed by the Janata Government to diagnose Panchayati Raj stagnation, it proposed replacing the three-tier setup with a **two-tier system**—**Zilla Parishad** (executive planning hub below the State) and **Mandal Panchayat** (grouping villages with 15,000–20,000 population)—while advocating **official political-party participation**, compulsory local taxation powers, a dedicated Minister for Panchayati Raj, and **constitutional recognition**.
- **G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985):** Appointed by the Planning Commission, it diagnosed rural administration as **"grass without roots"** due to bureaucratic capture via centrally driven development programmes. It recommended revitalizing the **Zilla Parishad** as the pivotal planning unit, creating a post of **District Development Commissioner (DDC)** as its CEO, and conducting regular local elections.
- **L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986):** Prepared the direct conceptual blueprint for the 73rd Amendment—calling for **explicit constitutional status and protection** for PRIs, designating the **Gram Sabha** as the embodiment of direct democracy, establishing **Nyaya Panchayats** for village dispute resolution, and creating independent judicial tribunals for local electoral disputes.
- **P.K. Thungon (1988) & V.N. Gadgil (1988) Committees:** Reinforced constitutionalization, fixed five-year terms, SC/ST/women reservations, State Finance Commissions, and State Election Commissions.

After Rajiv Gandhi's **64th and 65th Amendment Bills (1989)** cleared the Lok Sabha but failed in the Rajya Sabha over fears of bypassing State governments, the P.V. Narasimha Rao government enacted the **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** (effective **April 24, 1993**—commemorated as *National Panchayati Raj Day*) and the **74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** (effective **June 1, 1993**).

## The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 - Panchayati Raj Institutions (Part IX, Articles 243–243O)

Inserting **Part IX (Articles 243 to 243O)** and the **Eleventh Schedule (29 functional subjects)**, the 73rd Amendment balances national democratic floors (**Compulsory provisions** binding every State legislature) against federal flexibility (**Voluntary provisions** left to State discretion).

### Gram Sabha, Three-Tier Geometry, and Reservation Architecture (Articles 243A–243D)

- **Gram Sabha (Article 243A):** Defined in **Article 243(b)** strictly as a body consisting of **all persons registered in the electoral rolls** relating to a village within a Panchayat area (NOT all village residents!). While it is a mandatory constitutional institution, its exact powers and functions are **not fixed in Part IX**—they are left entirely to State legislation (a sharp contrast with PESA 1996).
- **Three-Tier Architecture & 20-Lakh Exemption (Articles 243B–243C):** Mandates Panchayats at **village, intermediate, and district levels**—EXCEPT under **Article 243B(2)**, States having a **population not exceeding 20 lakh** may dispense with the **intermediate tier** (e.g., Goa, Sikkim, Manipur, Mizoram where applicable). **All seats at all three levels** must be filled by **direct election** from territorial constituencies. Chairperson election splits neatly: **intermediate and district Chairpersons must be elected indirectly** by and from elected members, whereas the mode of electing the **village Chairperson** (direct vs. indirect) is left to the **State Legislature**.
- **SC, ST & Women Reservations (Article 243D):**
  - **SC/ST Quotas (Mandatory):** Seats reserved at every tier in strict proportion to their population in the Panchayat area, allotted by rotation; Chairperson offices similarly reserved in proportion to statewide SC/ST population. *(Exception: **83rd CAA, 2000** inserted **Art 243M(3A)** exempting **Arunachal Pradesh** from SC reservation since the State has no indigenous Scheduled Caste population.)*
  - **Women's Reservation (Mandatory 1/3rd Floor → 50% State Expansion):** **Not less than one-third ($33.3\%$)** of total seats (including seats reserved for SC/ST women) AND **not less than one-third** of Chairperson offices at every tier are reserved for women. Exceeding the constitutional floor, **21 States and 2 Union Territories (Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh)**—starting with Bihar in 2006—have legislated **50% women's reservation** in PRIs, pushing Elected Women Representatives (EWRs) to roughly **46.6%** (~14.5+ lakh women out of ~31 lakh total representatives).
- **OBC Reservations (Art 243D(6)) & the Supreme Court's Mandatory "Triple Test":** Unlike SC/ST/women quotas, OBC reservation is **voluntary** (left to State legislation). In ***Dr. K. Krishna Murthy v. Union of India* (2010, 5-Judge Bench)**, the Supreme Court held that *political backwardness* for local bodies differs from *social and educational backwardness* under Articles 15(4)/16(4), capping aggregate vertical local quotas at 50%. Crystallizing this rule in ***Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. State of Maharashtra* (2021, 3-Judge Bench)**, the Court struck down flat statutory OBC quotas and mandated a binding **Triple Test** prior to notifying OBC seats:
  1. Constitute a **dedicated Commission** to conduct a contemporaneous, rigorous **local-body-wise empirical inquiry** into the nature and implications of political backwardness;
  2. Specify the proportion of OBC reservation **strictly per that Commission's local data**;
  3. Ensure **total vertical reservation (SC + ST + OBC combined) NEVER exceeds 50%** of total seats (barring Scheduled Areas exemptions).
  *(Across 2025–2026, courts rigorously policed this ceiling—halting Telangana's 42% BC quota G.O. in October 2025, binding Maharashtra's local polls strictly to the 50% cap in November 2025, and dismissing Andhra Pradesh's plea for an unverified 34% BC quota in October 2026.)*

### Tenure, Disqualification Reconsideration (2026), SFC, and SEC (Articles 243E–243O)

- **Five-Year Term & Remainder Rule (Article 243E):** Every Panchayat serves a fixed **5-year duration** from its first meeting. Elections must be completed **before expiry** of its duration, or within **six months** of premature dissolution. A Panchayat reconstituted after premature dissolution serves **ONLY the remainder of the original term**—NOT a fresh five-year tenure (and if the remaining period is **less than six months**, holding a bye-election is not mandatory).
- **Age & Disqualification Jurisprudence (Article 243F) — The November 2026 Reference:** Minimum contesting age is **21 years** (**Art 243F(1)(a)**—NOT 25 or 30!). Beyond disqualifications applicable to State MLAs, State Legislatures may enact additional disqualifications—producing two fiercely debated verdicts:
  - ***Javed v. State of Haryana* (2003, 3-Judge Bench):** Upheld Haryana's two-child norm disqualifying parents with more than two living children from Panchayat posts as a reasonable classification furthering population control.
  - ***Rajbala v. State of Haryana* (2015/2016, 2-Judge Bench):** Relying directly on *Javed*, upheld Haryana's 2015 rules disqualifying candidates lacking matriculation (class 8/10), a functional household toilet, or clearance of cooperative/electricity dues—even while acknowledging the educational bar excluded roughly **68% of SC women and 41% of SC men**.
  - **Landmark November 4, 2026 Reference (`2026 INSC 1193`):** Hearing Maharashtra Sarpanch Mangala Bhimrao Ingle's appeal, a Division Bench (**Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe**) doubted *Javed* under the maxim *cessante ratione cessat ipsa lex* and **referred *Javed v. State of Haryana* (2003) to a larger Bench for reconsideration**. Observing that India's Total Fertility Rate has dropped from ~3.0 (2003) to **~1.9** (eliminating the overpopulation rationale), that the norm infringes **women's equality and Article 21 reproductive autonomy**, and that *Javed* spawned exclusionary barriers in *Rajbala* (earlier doubted by a 3-Judge Bench in *Sukanya Shantha v. UoI*, 2024), the Court signaled an impending constitutional reset of local candidature bars.
- **State Finance Commission (Article 243I & 243Y):** Constituted by the **Governor** (never the Chief Minister!) **every five years** to review financial positions of PRIs and ULBs and recommend tax sharing, assigned taxes, and grants-in-aid from the State Consolidated Fund. Reports plus an Explanatory Action Taken Memorandum must be laid before the State Legislature, and SFC recommendations directly inform the **Central Finance Commission** under **Articles 280(3)(bb) and (c)**.
- **State Election Commission (Article 243K & 243ZA):** Vests superintendence, direction, and control of Panchayat and Municipal electoral rolls and elections in an independent **State Election Commissioner** appointed by the **Governor**. Crucially, to insulate the SEC from State executive retaliation, the Commissioner **can be removed ONLY in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of a High Court**—meaning removal rests solely with the **President following a Special Majority address by Parliament**, NEVER the Governor! In ***Kishan Singh Tomar v. Municipal Corporation of Ahmedabad* (2006, 5-Judge Bench)**, the Supreme Court held that completing elections before the 5-year expiry is a mandatory constitutional imperative except during extraordinary natural calamities, placing SEC autonomy on par with the ECI. Reinforcing this firewall, ***State of Goa v. Fouziya Imtiaz Shaikh* (2021)** barred States from giving serving IAS/government bureaucrats *"additional charge"* as State Election Commissioner.
- **Exemptions & Judicial Bar (Articles 243M & 243O):** Part IX does **not** automatically apply to **Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram**, Fifth Schedule and Sixth Schedule areas, Hill Areas of Manipur (with District Councils), and the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council. **Article 243O** bars courts from questioning delimitation or seat-allotment laws and channels all election disputes exclusively through statutory election petitions.

## PESA Act, 1996 - Tribal Self-Rule in Fifth Schedule Areas

Because **Article 243M(1)** excluded Fifth Schedule areas from Part IX to protect tribal customary governance from mechanical uniform imposition, **Article 243M(4)(b)** empowered Parliament to extend Part IX to Scheduled Areas with tailored exceptions and modifications. Acting on the **Dilip Singh Bhuria Committee (1994–95)** report, Parliament enacted the **Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)** on **December 24, 1996**.

### Radical Gram Sabha Sovereignty and Natural-Resource Powers

Applying exclusively to **Fifth Schedule Areas across 10 States** (*Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, and Telangana*—**NOT** Sixth Schedule States!), PESA is celebrated as a **"Constitution within the Constitution"** because it flips representative hierarchy upside down—making the **Gram Sabha** (not the Gram Panchayat) the sovereign nucleus:

- **Organic Village & Mandatory ST Control (Section 4):** Defines a *"village"* around natural tribal habitations/hamlets managing affairs per customs rather than arbitrary revenue circles. Mandates **at least 50% seat reservation for STs** at every Panchayat tier (in proportion to population), reserves **ALL Chairperson offices at ALL three levels exclusively for STs**, permits State nomination of unrepresented STs up to 1/10th at intermediate/district tiers, and prohibits higher-tier Panchayats from assuming powers of lower tiers or the Gram Sabha.
- **Core Resource, Land & Regulatory Powers:**
  1. **Mandatory Consultation on Land Acquisition:** The Gram Sabha or Panchayats at the appropriate level **must be consulted** before making any acquisition of land in Scheduled Areas for development projects and before resettling/rehabilitating affected persons.
  2. **Ownership of Minor Forest Produce (MFP):** Gram Sabhas and Panchayats are expressly endowed with **ownership of MFP** (bamboo, tendu leaves, honey, lac, medicinal plants).
  3. **Minor Minerals (Prior Recommendation Mandate):** Prior **mandatory recommendation** of the Gram Sabha/Panchayat is compulsory before granting any prospecting license, mining lease, or auction concession for **minor minerals** (sand, stone, gravel—note: major minerals like coal/bauxite are not covered directly under this clause, though land acquisition/FRA triggers consultation).
  4. **Land Alienation, Markets & Social Control:** Empowered to **prevent alienation of tribal land** and restore unlawfully alienated land, enforce **prohibition/regulation of intoxicants**, manage **village markets**, regulate **money-lending** to STs, approve social-economic plans/beneficiaries, issue **utilization certificates** for funds spent by the Gram Panchayat, and control local social-sector institutions and Tribal Sub-Plan resources.

### *Niyamgiri* (2013) and 2026 Implementation Realities

In ***Orissa Mining Corporation Ltd. v. Ministry of Environment & Forests* (*Niyamgiri Verdict*, April 18, 2013, 3-Judge Bench)**, the Supreme Court fused PESA, the **Forest Rights Act (FRA, 2006)**, and **Articles 25–26** cultural-religious freedoms to recognize the **Dongria Kondh Gram Sabhas** as the decisive legal authority to adjudicate whether Vedanta's bauxite mining on the sacred Niyamgiri Hills infringed their customary rights. Conducting India's first grassroots environmental referendum, all **12 Gram Sabhas unanimously voted against mining**, forcing the MoEF to cancel forest clearance in 2014.

**Implementation Progress as of November 2026:** Because PESA is a central framework law operationalized through State Rules, bureaucratic foot-dragging delayed its rollout for decades. Breaking a 25-year logjam, **Jharkhand notified its PESA Rules (`PESA Niyamawali`) on January 2, 2026** (leading the Jharkhand High Court on January 13, 2026 to lift its stay on minor-mineral allocations). As of **November 2026**, **9 out of 10 Fifth Schedule States** have notified PESA Rules, leaving **Odisha as the SOLE remaining Fifth Schedule State** yet to finalize its rules (following its November 2023 draft notification). Even where rules exist, PESA remains blunted by conflicting State forest/mining laws, dilution of *"consent"* into pro-forma *"consultation"*, and weak Gram Sabha administrative capacity.

## The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 - Urban Local Bodies (Part IXA, Articles 243P–243ZG)

Inserting **Part IXA (Articles 243P to 243ZG)** and the **Twelfth Schedule (18 functional subjects)**, the 74th Amendment sought to equip India's rapidly expanding cities with democratic self-government and integrated spatial planning.

### Three ULB Tiers, Ward Committees, DPCs, and MPCs

- **Three Constitutional ULB Tiers (Article 243Q):**
  1. **Nagar Panchayat:** For a *"transitional area"* shifting from rural to urban character.
  2. **Municipal Council:** For a *"smaller urban area"*.
  3. **Municipal Corporation:** For a *"larger urban area"*.
  *(Proviso — **Industrial Township Exception**: The Governor may exempt constituting a municipality where an industrial establishment itself provides municipal services, e.g., Jamshedpur.)*
- **Ward Committees (Article 243S):** Mandatory constitution of Ward Committees (covering one or more wards) in every municipality with a population of **3 lakh or more**, bringing civic accountability closer to urban neighbourhoods.
- **District Planning Committee — DPC (Article 243ZD):** Mandatory in **every State** at the district level to consolidate plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities into a **Draft Development Plan** for the entire district. Constitutional democratic floor: **not less than four-fifths ($80\%$)** of DPC members must be **elected by and from elected members** of the District Panchayat and Municipalities in proportion to the district's rural–urban population ratio.
- **Metropolitan Planning Committee — MPC (Article 243ZE):** Mandatory in every **Metropolitan Area**—defined in **Article 243P(c)** as an area having a population of **10 lakh or more**, comprised in one or more districts and consisting of two or more Municipalities or Panchayats—to prepare a Draft Development Plan for the metropolitan region. Constitutional democratic floor: **not less than two-thirds ($66.7\%$)** of MPC members must be **elected by and from elected members** of Municipalities and Chairpersons of Panchayats in the metropolitan area.

### 73rd vs. 74th CAA Compulsory–Voluntary Matrix

| Constitutional Feature | 73rd CAA — PRIs (Part IX, Art 243–243O; 11th Schedule) | 74th CAA — ULBs (Part IXA, Art 243P–243ZG; 12th Schedule) | Compulsory vs. Voluntary Classification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Tiers & Grassroots Units** | **Gram Sabha (Art 243A)** + 3 Tiers (Village, Intermediate, District — **Art 243B**; intermediate optional if State pop $\le 20$ lakh). | 3 ULB types (**Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corp — Art 243Q**) + **Ward Committees** if pop $\ge 3$ lakh (**Art 243S**). | **Compulsory** (except Industrial Township exemption under Art 243Q Proviso & <20 lakh intermediate tier). |
| **Electoral Mandate & Tenure** | Direct election to all seats; **indirect** election of Intermediate & District Chairpersons; **5-yr term**, fresh poll within **6 months** of dissolution (**Art 243C, 243E**); min age **21 yrs (Art 243F)**. | Direct election to all municipal ward seats; **5-yr term**, fresh poll within **6 months** of dissolution (**Art 243R, 243U**); min age **21 yrs (Art 243V)**. | **Compulsory** — EXCEPT mode of electing **Village Panchayat Chairperson** and **Municipal Chairperson/Mayor** (direct vs. indirect), which is **Voluntary**. |
| **Reservations (SC/ST/Women vs. OBC)** | Proportional **SC/ST** seats & chairpersons + **$\ge 1/3\text{rd}$ Women** seats & chairpersons (**Art 243D**); **OBC** reservation (**Art 243D(6)**) subject to SC Triple Test. | Proportional **SC/ST** seats + **$\ge 1/3\text{rd}$ Women** seats (**Art 243T**); **SC/ST/Women Chairperson** & **OBC** quotas (**Art 243T(4),(6)**). | **SC/ST & 1/3rd Women seats = Compulsory**; **OBC quotas** (both) and **manner of Municipal Chairperson quotas (Art 243T(4))** = **Voluntary**. |
| **SFC, SEC & Constitutional Planning** | **State Finance Commission (Art 243I)** every 5 yrs + **State Election Commission (Art 243K)**. | **SFC (Art 243Y)** + **SEC (Art 243ZA)** + **DPC** ($\ge 4/5\text{ths}$ elected — **Art 243ZD**) + **MPC** ($\ge 2/3\text{rds}$ elected — **Art 243ZE**). | **Compulsory** (exact composition/chairpersonship of DPCs & MPCs above the elected floor is left to State law). |
| **Functional & Financial Devolution** | **Art 243G** (*"may, by law, endow"*) over **29 subjects (11th Schedule)**; **Art 243H** taxation, tolls, fees & grants. | **Art 243W** (*"may, by law, endow"*) over **18 subjects (12th Schedule)**; **Art 243X** municipal taxes, tolls & grants. | **Voluntary!** Permissive word *"may"* is the primary constitutional root of the **"Three Fs" deficit**. |
| **Representation of MPs/MLAs & Experts** | Voting/representation of MPs, MLAs & Chairpersons of lower tiers in higher Panchayats (**Art 243C(3)**). | Representation of MPs, MLAs & nominated persons with special municipal knowledge (without voting rights — **Art 243R(2)**). | **Voluntary** (left entirely to State legislative design). |

### Structural Pathologies - The "Three Fs" Deficit, Parastatal Bypass, and *Sarpanch Pati*

Thirty-three years after constitutionalization, India's third tier enjoys remarkable **electoral vitality** alongside crippling **functional anaemia**:

- **The "Three Fs" Deficit (Funds, Functions, Functionaries):** Because **Articles 243G and 243W** use the permissive word **"may"** rather than **"shall"**, State governments have transferred 11th and 12th Schedule subjects selectively on paper while retaining departmental budgets and staff. MoPR's *Devolution Index* highlights stark asymmetry—with Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Maharashtra leading while several northern States withhold core staff and taxation powers. Answering this chronic starvation, the **16th Finance Commission (February 2026)** raised third-tier Article 275 grants to **₹7.91 lakh crore** (₹4.35 lakh cr RLBs + ₹3.56 lakh cr ULBs), tying disbursements to three strict entry gates: regular elections, audited public accounts, and timely SFCs.
- **Urban Constitutional Hollowing & Parastatal Bypass:** In metropolitan India, the constitutional promise of **Articles 243W and 243ZE** is routinely short-circuited from above: State-appointed IAS **Municipal Commissioners** wield executive supremacy over weak, short-tenure ceremonial Mayors, while unelected State **parastatals** (Development Authorities, Water & Sewerage Boards, Smart City SPVs) siphon away urban planning, land-value capture, and capital budgets—leaving MPCs largely defunct. For the detailed municipal finance architecture, own-source revenue bottlenecks, municipal bond ecosystem, and parastatal governance mechanics, see [[7. Urban Governance, Municipal Finance & Parastatals#Municipal Financial Fragility and Parastatal Bypass|Municipal Finance & Parastatals]].
- ***Sarpanch Pati / Pradhan Pati* Proxy Syndrome:** Despite 14.5+ lakh Elected Women Representatives (~46.6%), patriarchal gatekeeping routinely converts reserved rural seats into proxy offices controlled by husbands or male relatives. Addressing this democratic distortion, the **Ministry of Panchayati Raj's Advisory Committee on "Pradhan Pati" (chaired by Sushil Kumar, February 2025 Report)** recommended deterrent administrative penalties against proxy attendance, mandatory **Mahila Sabhas**, dedicated women's helplines, and peer-leadership training—operationalized alongside the **Sashakt Panchayat-Netri Abhiyan** and **Model Women-Friendly Gram Panchayat** initiatives launched in March 2025.

## Co-operative Societies - The 97th Amendment Act (2011) and *Rajendra N. Shah* (2021)

Recognizing co-operatives as democratic economic engines spanning dairy (AMUL), credit, sugar, housing, and fertilizers, Parliament enacted the **97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011** (effective **February 15, 2012**), making three distinct constitutional insertions:

1. **Fundamental Right — Article 19(1)(c):** Added the words **"or co-operative societies"** alongside associations and unions, elevating the right to form co-operatives to Part III.
2. **Directive Principle — Article 43B (Part IV):** Directed the State to endeavour to promote **voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control, and professional management** of co-operative societies.
3. **Part IXB (Articles 243ZH to 243ZT):** Prescribed detailed structural rules for all co-operative societies—capping Boards at **maximum 21 directors** (with mandatory **1 SC/ST and 2 women** reserved seats), fixing a **5-year board term**, limiting executive supersession to **6 months** (and barring supersession altogether where there is no government shareholding, loan, or financial guarantee), mandating independent annual audits, and prescribing timely elections.

### *Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah* (July 20, 2021) - The Federal Ratification Guillotine

Under the Seventh Schedule, *"Co-operative Societies"* resides exclusively in **State List Entry 32**, whereas **Multi-State Co-operative Societies** (operating across more than one State) sit under **Union List Entry 44**. Crucially, Parliament passed the 97th Amendment using a **Special Majority alone**, completely **bypassing ratification by at least 50% of State Legislatures** required under the **Proviso to Article 368(2)** whenever an amendment alters any List in the Seventh Schedule!

Affirming the Gujarat High Court, a **3-Judge Bench** in ***Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah* (July 20, 2021, 2:1)** (**Justice R.F. Nariman** authored the majority opinion with Justice B.R. Gavai; **Justice K.M. Joseph** dissented strictly on severability) delivered a surgical federalist ruling:
- **Part IXB Struck Down for State Co-operatives:** Because Part IXB imposed mandatory structural restrictions fettering State legislative competence over **List II Entry 32**, failure to obtain **50% State ratification under Article 368(2) Proviso** rendered Part IXB **unconstitutional insofar as it applies to intra-State co-operative societies**.
- **Doctrine of Severability Saves Multi-State Co-operatives & UTs:** Applying severability, the 2:1 majority **upheld Part IXB** exclusively for **Multi-State Co-operative Societies (Art 243ZR)** and **Union Territories (Art 243ZS)**, where Parliament holds independent plenary competence under List I Entry 44 and Article 246(4). *(Justice K.M. Joseph dissented solely on severability, holding that Part IXB should have fallen in its entirety.)*
- **Article 19(1)(c) and Article 43B Remain Untouched:** Neither provision encroached on the Seventh Schedule; both remain fully valid and binding across all States.

**Post-Verdict Statutory Architecture:** Leveraging its constitutionally validated Entry 44 authority, Parliament enacted the **Multi-State Co-operative Societies (Amendment) Act, 2023**, establishing an independent **Co-operative Election Authority (CEA)**, a **Co-operative Ombudsman** for member grievance redressal, a **Co-operative Rehabilitation, Reconstruction and Development Fund** for sick societies, and strict anti-nepotism and board-diversity rules—aligning multi-state co-operatives with Part IXB's surviving governance standards while respecting State autonomy over intra-State societies.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **20-Lakh Exemption, Age 21 & Governor SFC Traps (UPSC Prelims 2025 Q91):** Intermediate-tier Panchayats are **NOT mandatory in all States**—States with population **$\le 20$ lakh** are exempt (**Art 243B(2)**). Minimum contesting age is **21 years** (NOT 25 or 30! — **Art 243F**). The **Governor** (NOT the Chief Minister!) constitutes the **State Finance Commission** every 5 years (**Art 243I**).
> - **SEC Removal Trap (Art 243K):** The State Election Commissioner is **appointed by the Governor**, *but* **CANNOT be removed by the Governor**! Removal follows the exact procedure and grounds of a **High Court Judge** (by the **President** after Parliamentary Special Majority address).
> - **Reconstituted Panchayat Remainder Trap (Art 243E):** A Panchayat elected after premature dissolution serves **ONLY the unexpired remainder** of the original 5-year term—**NOT** a fresh 5-year term! If the remaining period is **$< 6$ months**, no election is mandatory.
> - **PESA 1996 Scope & Chairperson Traps (UPSC Prelims 2013 & 2026):** PESA applies **ONLY to Fifth Schedule Areas (10 States)**—it does **NOT** create *"autonomous regions"* (that is the Sixth Schedule!). Under PESA, **ALL Chairperson posts at ALL Panchayat tiers must be reserved for STs**, and ST seat reservation cannot fall below **50%**. As of **November 2026**, **9 of 10** Fifth Schedule States have notified PESA Rules (after **Jharkhand notified on Jan 2, 2026**), leaving **Odisha alone** pending.
> - **DPC vs. MPC Elected-Member Floor Trap:** **DPC (Art 243ZD)** requires **$\ge 4/5\text{ths}$ ($80\%$)** elected members; **MPC (Art 243ZE)** requires **$\ge 2/3\text{rds}$ ($66.7\%$)** elected members. Ward Committees (**Art 243S**) are mandatory for municipalities with population **$\ge 3$ lakh**; Metropolitan Area threshold (**Art 243P(c)**) is **$\ge 10$ lakh**.
> - **OBC Local Quota "Triple Test" & 50% Ceiling:** OBC reservation under Art 243D(6)/243T(6) is **voluntary**, requires a **dedicated empirical commission** studying *political* backwardness local-body-wise (*Vikas Kishanrao Gawali*, 2021), and **total SC + ST + OBC reservation cannot exceed 50%** (except in Scheduled Areas).
> - **Two-Child Disqualification Live 2026 Nuance:** ***Javed v. State of Haryana* (2003)** upheld the two-child bar and ***Rajbala* (2015/16)** upheld educational/toilet disqualifications—**HOWEVER**, on **November 4, 2026 (`2026 INSC 1193`)**, the Supreme Court **referred *Javed* to a larger Bench for reconsideration** (it has **NOT yet overruled** *Javed* or struck down state laws!).
> - **97th CAA / *Rajendra N. Shah* (2021) Surgical Split:** The SC did **NOT** strike down the entire 97th CAA! **Article 19(1)(c)** and **Article 43B** remain **100% valid**, and **Part IXB remains fully valid** for **Multi-State Co-operative Societies and UTs**—ONLY its application to **State co-operatives (List II Entry 32)** was struck down for lacking Art 368(2) Proviso ratification.
> - **Gram Sabha Definition Trap:** The Gram Sabha (**Art 243(b)**) comprises **ONLY registered voters** on the village electoral roll—NOT all adult residents or all inhabitants.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **Compulsory vs. Voluntary Swaps:** UPSC routinely presents **OBC reservations (Art 243D(6))**, **direct election of Sarpanch/Mayor**, or **devolution of 11th/12th Schedule subjects (Art 243G/243W)** as *"mandatory constitutional requirements"*—all three are **voluntary**!
- **Authority & Threshold Inversions:** Classic elimination triggers swap **Governor** with **Chief Minister** (SFC constitution), claim **Governor removes the SEC** (false—only President via HC-Judge impeachment process), swap **21 years** with **25/30 years**, or swap **DPC's 4/5ths** elected threshold with **MPC's 2/3rds** threshold.
- **PESA vs. Sixth Schedule Conflation:** Any statement claiming PESA applies to *Assam/Meghalaya/Tripura/Mizoram* or *creates Autonomous District Councils* is immediately false—PESA operates strictly in the **10 Fifth Schedule States** through **Gram Sabhas**.
- **Partial vs. Total Invalidation Traps (*Rajendra N. Shah* & *Javed*):** Watch for false absolutes claiming the Supreme Court *"struck down the entire 97th Constitutional Amendment Act"* (false—Art 19(1)(c), Art 43B, and Part IXB for Multi-State Cooperatives survive!) or *"struck down the two-child disqualification norm in November 2026"* (false—it *referred* *Javed* to a larger Bench for reconsideration).
