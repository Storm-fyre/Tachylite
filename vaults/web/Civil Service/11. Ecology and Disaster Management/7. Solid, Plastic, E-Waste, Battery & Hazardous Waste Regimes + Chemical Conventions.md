# Chapter 7 - Solid, Plastic, E-Waste, Battery & Hazardous Waste Regimes + Chemical Conventions

India's waste architecture has pivoted decisively between 2022 and 2026—from municipal "collect-and-dump" housekeeping to legally enforceable **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** across **eight distinct waste streams** and a digitally traceable **Solid Waste Management (SWM) Rules, 2026** regime. Simultaneously, global chemical governance tightened sharply at the **2025 Geneva Triple COPs** (Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm) and **Minamata COP6** even as the **UN Global Plastics Treaty** stalled at Geneva's INC-5.2. Mastering this unit requires fusing circular-economy thermochemistry with surgical recall of statutory thresholds, exclusions, and treaty annexes.

## 1. Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, Thermal WtE & GOBARdhan

### Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 (Effective April 1, 2026)

Notified by MoEFCC under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** on **January 27, 2026**, and in full legal force from **April 1, 2026**, the **SWM Rules, 2026** supersede the decade-old SWM Rules, 2016. With urban India generating over **1.70–1.85 lakh tonnes of municipal solid waste (MSW) daily**—and roughly 65–70% historically remaining unsegregated—the 2026 overhaul replaces manual municipal self-reporting with digital traceability, Polluter Pays economics, and statutory generator accountability.

- **Mandatory Four-Stream Source Segregation ("Four-Bin Shift"):** Replaces the 2016 three-stream mandate by requiring every waste generator to segregate at source into four distinct streams:
  1. **Wet Waste:** Kitchen, food, garden, vegetable, meat, and flower organics—mandated for decentralized composting or biomethanation.
  2. **Dry Waste:** Recyclable and non-recyclable plastics, paper, metal, glass, and textiles—channelled to **Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs)**.
  3. **Sanitary Waste:** Used diapers, sanitary napkins, tampons, and condoms—wrapped securely for dedicated collection and safe disposal.
  4. **Special Care Waste:** Domestic hazardous items such as paint cans, CFL/LED bulbs, expired medicines, thermometers, and pesticide containers—kept strictly out of municipal landfills.
- **Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility (EBWGR):** Establishes clear quantitative thresholds classifying entities as **Bulk Waste Generators (BWGs)** if they generate **$>100\text{ kg/day}$ of waste**, consume **$>40,000\text{ litres/day}$ of water**, or occupy a floor/built-up area exceeding **$20,000\text{ m}^2$** (covering large campuses, hotels, hospitals, malls, and RWAs). BWGs must process wet waste on-site or procure verified **EBWGR certificates** from authorized off-site processors.
- **CPCB Centralised Online Portal & Governance Architecture:** Operationalizes a **Centralised Online Portal** developed by CPCB tracking the entire waste lifecycle—registration, collection, transport, MRF processing, landfill audits, and biomining returns. **Environmental Compensation** is levied on Polluter Pays grounds for unregistered operation, forged reporting, or non-compliance. State/UT implementation committees are chaired by the **Chief Secretary** (or UT Administrator), sanitary landfill compliance is overseen by the **District Collector / District Magistrate**, and **annual landfill audits** must be uploaded by SPCBs/PCCs or registered Environment Auditors. The rules also mandate formal identification, enumeration, and registration of informal **waste pickers**.
- **Graded Refuse-Derived Fuel (RDF) Mandates & Land Allocation:** High-calorific non-recyclable dry fractions (RDF) must replace fossil coal in energy-intensive industries (cement kilns, Waste-to-Energy plants), scaling the mandatory **RDF fuel substitution rate from 5% to 15% over a six-year period**. Graded siting criteria accelerate land allocation and buffer-zone demarcation for MRFs and processing plants.
- **Landfill Disincentives, Legacy Biomining & Hilly/Island Rules:** Sanitary landfills are restricted strictly to **non-recyclable, non-energy-recoverable inerts**. Critically, **landfill tipping fees for unsegregated waste must be fixed higher than the combined cost of segregation, transport, and processing**—inverting the perverse municipal incentive to dump mixed waste. Local bodies must map all legacy dumpsites, execute time-bound **biomining and bioremediation** with quarterly portal reporting, and cease fresh dumping on remediating sites (**Rule 15(7)**). For ecologically fragile **hilly areas and islands**, local bodies can levy **user fees on tourists**, regulate tourist inflow based on waste-processing carrying capacity, and require hotels/restaurants to process wet waste decentrally.
- *Implementation Reality — Indore vs. Delhi:* Indore achieved near-100% door-to-door multi-stream segregation, zero-landfill remediation of Devguradia, and profitable waste monetization (Asia's largest municipal Bio-CNG plant powering city buses), whereas Delhi's MCD continued dumping over **4,200 tonnes/day** of fresh mixed waste onto remediating legacy mountains (Bhalswa, Ghazipur, Okhla) in 2026 because planned WtE capacity lags behind daily generation.

### Thermal Waste-to-Energy (WtE) Technologies & GOBARdhan

Indian MSW averages **50–55% high-moisture organics** with a low calorific value ($800\text{–}1,200\text{ kcal/kg}$), meaning mass-burn thermal plants fail economically and environmentally unless fed pre-segregated dry RDF. Four thermochemical routes differ sharply in oxygen stoichiometry and emissions:

- **Mass-Burn Incineration:** Direct combustion in **excess oxygen** ($850\text{–}1,100^\circ\text{C}$) converting waste into flue gas, heat/steam, and bottom/fly ash. Burning unsegregated chlorinated plastics (PVC) below optimal residence times synthesizes carcinogenic **dioxins (PCDDs) and furans (PCDFs)** alongside toxic heavy-metal fly ash.
- **Pyrolysis:** Thermal decomposition of carbon-rich feedstocks (plastics, tyres, biomass) at **$400\text{–}800^\circ\text{C}$ in the complete absence of oxygen**. Rather than oxidizing waste to $\text{CO}_2$, it cracks polymers into three marketable outputs: solid **biochar**, liquid **pyrolysis oil (bio-oil)**, and combustible **syngas** ($\text{CO} + \text{H}_2 + \text{CH}_4$).
- **Gasification:** Partial oxidation at **$750\text{–}1,000^\circ\text{C}$** using a **controlled, sub-stoichiometric supply of oxygen, air, or steam**. It suppresses full combustion and maximizes clean **synthesis gas (syngas — $\text{CO} + \text{H}_2$)** usable in high-efficiency gas turbines, methanol synthesis, or hydrogen production.
- **Plasma Arc Gasification:** Exposes hazardous, biomedical, or refractory waste to an electrically ionized **plasma torch** at extreme temperatures (**$3,000\text{–}10,000^\circ\text{C}$**). Molecular bonds dissociate completely—destroying dioxin precursors—yielding high-purity syngas and an inert, leach-proof **vitrified glassy slag** suitable for road aggregates, though high parasitic electricity demand limits commercial scale in Indian ULBs.
- **GOBARdhan (*Galvanizing Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan*):** Launched in 2018 under **Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen)** by the **Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation (DDWS), Ministry of Jal Shakti**, GOBARdhan converts cattle dung, agricultural residue, and organic MSW via anaerobic digestion into **biogas, Compressed Biogas (CBG)**, and **Fermented Organic Manure (FOM / Liquid FOM)**. Operated as a whole-of-government umbrella through its **Unified Registration Portal** (covering **1,057+ community/cluster biogas plants** by December 2025 alongside ₹50 lakh/district SBM(G) Phase-II assistance and Budget 2023's 500 waste-to-wealth target), it was elevated by the **Union Cabinet in 2026** into the unified national **Bioenergy Scheme – GOBARdhan (₹23,731 crore outlay)** aimed at scaling domestic CBG production nearly tenfold and slashing LNG/fertilizer imports.

## 2. Plastic, E-Waste & Battery EPR Frameworks

### Plastic Waste Management Rules (2016–2026) & UN Plastics Treaty Deadlock

Notified under EPA 1986 (replacing the 2011 rules) and tightened iteratively in **2018, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, and March 31, 2026**, India's plastic regime blends upstream bans with market-based packaging circularity:

- **Single-Use Plastic (SUP) Ban & 120-Micron Norm:** The **August 12, 2021 Amendment** legally defined SUPs for the first time and prohibited manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale, and use of **19 identified low-utility, high-litter SUP items** from **July 1, 2022**—including plastic-stick earbuds/balloons/candy/ice-cream, decorative polystyrene (thermocol), disposable plates/cups/cutlery/straws/stirrers, wrapping films around sweet boxes/invitation cards/cigarette packs, and PVC banners $<100\text{ }\mu\text{m}$. Plastic carry-bag thickness was raised from $50 \to 75\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ (Sept 30, 2021) $\to$ **$120\text{ microns}$** (effective **December 31, 2022**) to increase ragpicker recovery value and multi-use durability (certified compostable and biodegradable carry bags are exempt from the thickness floor). Plastic sachets for gutkha, tobacco, and pan masala remain banned under Rule 4.
- **Tradable EPR Architecture & Packaging Categories (Dual-Standard Parity — 4 $\to$ 5 Categories):** Inserted as **Schedule II** via the **February 16, 2022 EPR Guidelines**, Producers, Importers, and Brand Owners (**PIBOs**) must register on CPCB's centralized EPR portal (operating in $>2$ States $\to$ CPCB; 1–2 States $\to$ SPCB) and meet binding recycling, reuse, and recycled-content targets by purchasing **tradable EPR certificates** from registered Plastic Waste Processors (**~232 lakh tonnes** processed by July 2026):
  - **Category I:** Rigid plastic packaging (bottles, jars, tubs).
  - **Category II:** Flexible plastic packaging of single layer or multilayer (different types of plastic sheets, carry bags, pouches).
  - **Category III:** Multilayered plastic packaging (**at least one layer of plastic and at least one layer of non-plastic** such as aluminium foil or paperboard).
  - **Category IV:** Plastic sheets/carry bags made of **compostable plastics** (must satisfy IS/ISO 17088 industrial-composting standards).
  - **Category V (Added March 14, 2024 Amendment):** **Biodegradable plastics**—defined strictly as plastics undergoing biological degradation in specific environments without leaving microplastics or toxic residues (note: legacy CPCB FAQs still reference 4 categories, whereas the amended Schedule II reflects 5).
- **2025–2026 Traceability & Recycled-Content Mandates:** Amended **Rule 11** (effective **July 1, 2025**) requires plastic packaging to carry producer name, EPR registration number, and a **barcode or QR code** for digital traceability. Mandatory **recycled plastic content** targets kicked in from FY 2025–26 onward across Categories I, II, and III—while the **March 31, 2026 Amendment** explicitly exempted packaging where statutory safety bodies like **FSSAI** prohibit recycled plastic contact (e.g., sensitive food/pharma applications).
- **UN Global Plastics Treaty (INC) Deadlock (2022–2026):** Authorized by **UNEA-5.2 (March 2022)** to deliver a legally binding global instrument on plastic pollution (including marine litter) by end-2024, negotiations stalled at **INC-5.1 (Busan, Dec 2024)** and collapsed without consensus at **INC-5.2 (Geneva, August 5–15, 2025)** as unresolved bracketed text ballooned from 379 to **1,351 brackets**. The structural fault line pits the **High Ambition Coalition** (EU, UK, Norway, Rwanda, Colombia, SIDS)—demanding legally binding **upstream caps on primary polymer production** and global phase-outs of hazardous plastic additives—against the **Like-Minded petrochemical/oil-producing bloc** (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iran, Kuwait), which insists on restricting the treaty strictly to **downstream waste management and recycling**. India advocates circular waste management, EPR, and curbing avoidable SUPs while resisting arbitrary upstream polymer caps that could constrain developing-country industrial growth. In **August 2026**, the INC Chair circulated an *"Aid to Negotiations"* attempting to break the consensus deadlock.

### E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 & Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022

India ranks **third globally** in e-waste generation (behind China and the US), yet over 70–80% historically flowed into hazardous informal backyard workshops (acid-bath gold leaching and open PVC-wire burning in hubs like Seelampur and Moradabad). Both 2022 rulebooks replace physical take-back bureaucracy with CPCB digital EPR certificate markets:

- **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 (Notified Nov 2, 2022; Effective April 1, 2023; Amended 2023 & 2024):**
  - **Expanded Scope & Solar PV Carve-In:** Covers **106 Electrical and Electronic Equipment (EEE)** categories (up from 21 under 2016 rules), bringing **Solar Photovoltaic (PV) modules, panels, and cells** under national e-waste law. Because utility-scale solar arrays have 20–25-year lifespans, manufacturers and producers of solar PV panels must register on the CPCB portal and **safely store waste generated up to FY 2034–2035** per CPCB guidelines while recycling capacity scales.
  - **Statutory Exclusions (Prelims Goldmine):** Explicitly excludes **(a) waste batteries** (Battery Rules 2022), **(b) packaging plastics** (PWM Rules 2016), **(c) micro-enterprises** under MSMED Act 2006, and **(d) radioactive wastes** under the Atomic Energy Act 1962.
  - **Digital EPR, Refurbishing & Urban Mining:** Eliminates the mandatory Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO) middleman; producers purchase **EPR certificates** directly from registered recyclers on CPCB's portal to meet escalating recycling targets (**60%** in 2023–25 $\to$ **70%** in 2025–27 $\to$ **80%** from 2027–28 onward). Introduces **Refurbishing Certificates** to promote repair and life extension—deferring producer recycling liability, with **only 75%** added back after extended life ends. Drives formal **urban mining** to recover gold, silver, palladium, copper, and rare earths (complemented by voluntary international recycler certifications such as SERI's **R2 Code of Practices**).
  - **RoHS Compliance (Rule 16):** Caps hazardous substances in new EEE homogeneous materials at **$\le 0.1\%$ by weight** for **Lead, Mercury, Hexavalent Chromium ($\text{Cr}^{6+}$), PBB, and PBDE**, and **$\le 0.01\%$ by weight** for **Cadmium** (exempting defence/strategic equipment).
- **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 (Notified Aug 22/24, 2022; Amended Oct 2023 & Mar 2024):**
  - Supersedes the narrow 2001 rules (which covered mainly lead-acid batteries) to govern **all four battery categories** across every chemistry (Li-ion, LFP, NMC, lead-acid, Ni-Cd): **Portable, Automotive, Industrial, and Electric Vehicle (EV)** batteries.
  - Strictly **prohibits landfilling and incineration** of waste batteries. Producers meet EPR via dry-weight EPR certificates generated by SPCB-registered recyclers/refurbishers on the CPCB portal.
  - **Dual Circular Mandates:** (1) **Minimum Material Recovery Targets** for recyclers—escalating for portable and EV batteries from **70% (FY 2024–25) $\to$ 80% (FY 2025–26) $\to$ 90% (FY 2026–27 onward)** by dry weight (55–60% for automotive/industrial)—securing domestic lithium, cobalt, and nickel streams; and (2) **Mandatory Domestic Recycled Content** in new batteries—35% rising to 40% for automotive/industrial batteries from FY 2024–25, and **5% from FY 2027–28 rising to 20% from FY 2030–31** for portable and EV batteries. Paying Environmental Compensation **does not absolve** the producer's carried-forward EPR shortfall.

## 3. Bio-Medical, C&D, Hazardous EPR, ELV Scrappage & Ecomark 2024

### Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (Four-Bin Colour Code)

Notified under EPA 1986 on **March 28, 2016** (amended 2018, 2019, and **April 17, 2026** to integrate AYUSH departments), these rules apply to every hospital, clinic, laboratory, blood bank, and vaccination camp. Occupiers must phase out **chlorinated plastic bags** (preventing dioxin formation upon burning), immunize staff against Hepatitis B and tetanus, implement **barcoding and GPS tracking** of waste consignments, never store untreated BMW beyond **48 hours**, and abstain from setting up on-site treatment if a **Common Bio-Medical Waste Treatment Facility (CBWTF)** operates within **75 km**. Segregation follows a strict **four-colour system** (abolishing the legacy Black bin):

- **Yellow (Non-Chlorinated Plastic Bag):** Human and animal anatomical waste, soiled blood-soaked dressings/cotton/plaster casts, expired or discarded cytotoxic medicines, chemical waste, blood-contaminated linen, and microbiology/biotechnology lab cultures $\to$ *Incineration, plasma pyrolysis, or deep burial (strictly in rural/remote areas without CBWTF access)*.
- **Red (Non-Chlorinated Plastic Bag):** **Contaminated recyclable plastics**—IV tubing and bottles, catheters, urine bags, **syringes without needles**, vacutainers, and gloves $\to$ *Autoclaving/microwaving + shredding $\to$ Authorized recycling (never incinerated or landfilled)*.
- **White — Translucent (Puncture-Proof, Leak-Proof, Tamper-Proof Container):** **Metallic waste sharps**—needles, scalpels, blades, syringes with fixed needles $\to$ *Autoclaving/dry-heat sterilization followed by shredding, mutilation, or concrete encapsulation*.
- **Blue (Cardboard Box with Blue Marking):** Broken or discarded **glassware** (medicine vials, ampoules—excluding cytotoxic glass) and **metallic body implants** $\to$ *Disinfection or autoclaving/microwaving $\to$ Authorized recycling*.

### C&D 2025, Hazardous/Specialized EPR, ELV Scrappage & Ecomark 2024

- **Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules, 2025:** Notified **April 2/4, 2025** (replacing the 2016 C&D rules; EPR portal phase-wise go-live April 2026), integrating C&D debris into India's EPR regime and prescribing binding targets for incorporating recycled C&D aggregates into building construction, reconstruction, and highway projects.
- **Hazardous & Other Wastes Rules, 2016 + Specialized EPR Expansions (2022–2025):** Governed under EPA 1986 (~18.51 million tonnes generated annually), these rules align domestically with the Basel Convention—banning import of hazardous waste for disposal and **prohibiting imports of solid plastic waste**. MoEFCC amended the 2016 Hazardous Rules to introduce tradable EPR certificates across **Waste Tyres (July 21, 2022)**, **Used Oil (September 18, 2023)**, and **Scrap of Non-Ferrous Metals — aluminium, copper, tin, zinc (July 1, 2025)**. Complementarily, MoEFCC notified the **Environment Protection (Management of Contaminated Sites) Rules, 2025** (July 2025) for statutory soil/groundwater remediation and the **Environment Audit Rules, 2025**, establishing certified third-party **Environment Auditors** accredited by the Environment Audit Designated Agency (EADA) to audit EPR, Ecomark, and Green Credit compliance.
- **Vehicle Scrappage Policy (2021) & End-of-Life Vehicles (ELV) Rules, 2025:** MoRTH's Voluntary Vehicle Fleet Modernization Program (2021) links de-registration to objective fitness checks at **Automated Testing Stations (ATS)** (after **15 years** for commercial vehicles / **20 years** for private vehicles) and scientific dismantling at **Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facilities (RVSFs)**. Adding an environmental EPR layer, MoEFCC notified the **Environment Protection (End-of-Life Vehicles) Rules, 2025** on **January 6, 2025** (in force **April 1, 2025**), binding all transport and non-transport vehicle OEMs (excluding agricultural tractors/trailers) to **recovered-steel EPR certificates** generated by RVSFs on CPCB's portal. Yet NITI Aayog's 2026 ELV audit exposes severe capacity drag: India held **~23 million ELVs in 2025** (projected to double to 50 million by 2030), against barely **156 operational ATS** (vs. 500 needed) and **129 RVSFs** (January 2026), leaving informal yards like Mayapuri to capture most scrap.
- **Ecomark Rules, 2024:** Notified on **September 26, 2024** under **EPA 1986** (replacing the largely dormant 1991 scheme), Ecomark translates **Mission LiFE** into consumer market signals while retaining the iconic **earthen pot** logo. Implemented by **CPCB in partnership with BIS** under a MoEFCC Steering Committee, this **voluntary** eco-label is granted to products holding BIS/Quality Control Order conformity that meet strict lifecycle benchmarks (lower pollution, energy efficiency, recyclability, reduced hazardous substances)—with tightened standards initiated in late 2025 across paints, batteries, paper, and wood substitutes.

## 4. Four International Chemical & Waste Conventions (2025 COP Updates)

Global chemical governance rests on four legally binding multilateral environmental agreements—the three **BRS Conventions** (Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm, which share a joint Secretariat and convened back-to-back in **Geneva from April 28 to May 9, 2025** under the theme *"Make visible the invisible"*) and the standalone **Minamata Convention on Mercury** (which held **COP6 in Geneva from November 3 to 7, 2025**).

### Physicochemical Logic of Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)

POPs share four dangerous traits (**PBLT**): extreme **Persistence** (decadal half-lives resisting photolytic, chemical, and microbial decay), **Bioaccumulation** (lipophilic magnification up to $70,000\times$ in apex predators), **Long-Range Environmental Transport**, and severe **Toxicity** (carcinogenic, neurodevelopmental, endocrine-disrupting). Driven by the **"Grasshopper / Global Distillation Effect"**, semi-volatile POPs evaporate in warm tropical latitudes, travel poleward via atmospheric circulation, and condense irreversibly in cold polar sinks—contaminating Arctic polar bears and Indigenous diets thousands of kilometres from any factory.

### The Four Treaty Pillars & Live 2025 Outcomes

- **1. Basel Convention (Transboundary Hazardous Wastes — Adopted 1989; In Force 1992; India Ratified 1992):**
  - *Core Mandate:* Minimizes hazardous waste generation, enforces **Environmentally Sound Management (ESM)** near the source, and governs cross-border shipments of hazardous and "other" wastes (household waste, incinerator ash, problematic plastics) via legally binding **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)**. Explicitly **excludes radioactive wastes** (IAEA domain) and wastes derived from the **normal operations of a ship** (MARPOL domain; distinct from shipbreaking under the Hong Kong Convention).
  - *Three Landmark Amendments & COP17 (2025):*
    - **Basel Ban Amendment** (adopted 1995; entered into force **December 5, 2019**): Prohibits *all* hazardous waste exports—including for recycling—from **Annex VII** developed nations (OECD, EU, Liechtenstein) to non-Annex VII developing countries. **Crucial India Trap:** **India has NOT ratified the Basel Ban Amendment** (nor have the US, Japan, or Canada; notably, the US has not ratified even the parent Basel Convention).
    - **Plastic Waste Amendments** (in force January 2021): Brought mixed, contaminated, and hard-to-recycle plastic wastes under binding PIC.
    - **E-Waste Amendments (In Force January 1, 2025):** Expanded mandatory PIC controls to **all transboundary e-waste, including non-hazardous e-waste**, closing loopholes that disguised dumping as "second-hand electronics".
    - **Basel COP17 (May 2025):** Adopted the **2025–2031 Strategic Framework**, updated POPs-waste technical guidelines, and formally launched international work on **transboundary movements of textile waste**.
- **2. Rotterdam Convention (Hazardous Chemicals & Pesticides Trade — Adopted 1998; In Force 2004; India Acceded 2005):**
  - *Core Mandate:* Jointly administered by **UNEP and FAO**, it establishes shared responsibility and a legally binding **PIC procedure** for international trade in banned or severely restricted pesticides and industrial chemicals listed in **Annex III**. Crucially, Rotterdam **does not ban** production or domestic use—it obliges exporting countries to respect importing nations' sovereign consent and share hazard profiles.
  - *COP12 (May 2025):* Added two hazardous pesticides—**Carbosulfan** and **Fenthion**—to Annex III, while consensus-rule objections from producer countries continued blocking listing of **chrysotile asbestos** and paraquat formulations.
- **3. Stockholm Convention on POPs (Adopted 2001; In Force May 2004; India Ratified January 2006):**
  - *Core Mandate:* Financed through the **Global Environment Facility (GEF)**, Stockholm categorizes POPs across three operative annexes: **Annex A (Elimination)** of production and use (with time-bound specific exemptions), **Annex B (Restriction)** (e.g., **DDT** strictly for malaria/kala-azar vector control; PFOS), and **Annex C (Unintentional Production)** minimization (dioxins, furans, HCB, PCBs).
  - *Evolution from "Dirty Dozen" to COP12 (May 2025):* Originated with **12 POPs ("Dirty Dozen")**—8 pesticides (*aldrin, chlordane, DDT, dieldrin, endrin, heptachlor, mirex, toxaphene*), 2 industrial chemicals (*PCBs, HCB*), and 2 unintentional byproducts (*dioxins, furans*). Landmark additions include **Endosulfan** (Annex A, 2011; banned across India by the Supreme Court in 2011), PFAS congeners (**PFOS, PFOA, PFHxS**), **COP11 (2023)** listings (**UV-328** [plastic UV stabilizer], **Dechlorane Plus**, **Methoxychlor**), and **COP12 (May 2025)** additions of **three major Annex A POPs**—neurotoxic pesticide **Chlorpyrifos**, industrial flame-retardant/plasticizer **Medium-Chain Chlorinated Paraffins (MCCPs)**, and **Long-Chain Perfluorocarboxylic Acids (LC-PFCAs, a major PFAS family)**. COP12 also operationalized the Convention's inaugural **Compliance Committee** work programme.
  - *India's Article 25(4) Opt-In Shield:* Upon ratification in January 2006, India invoked **Article 25(4)**, placing itself in a default **"opt-out" position**: new Annex A/B/C amendments do **not** legally bind India unless India explicitly deposits an instrument of ratification (as done for 7 POPs in 2020).
- **4. Minamata Convention on Mercury (Adopted 2013; In Force August 2017; India Ratified June 2018):**
  - *Core Mandate:* Administered by **UNEP** (with **GEF** financial support) and named after Japan's mid-20th-century industrial methylmercury tragedy, it regulates mercury across its entire lifecycle: banning new primary mercury mines, phasing out existing mines, controlling **coal-fired TPP emissions** and **Artisanal and Small-Scale Gold Mining (ASGM)**, phasing out mercury-added thermometers/sphygmomanometers/CFLs/batteries/cosmetics, and regulating mercury waste.
  - *COP6 (Geneva, November 3–7, 2025):* Adopted a historic global amendment mandating the **complete phase-out of dental amalgam by 2034** (~50% mercury content) alongside stricter enforcement against mercury-laced skin-lightening cosmetics. Notably, India voiced transition concerns regarding domestic dental infrastructure and retains an **Article 30(5) opt-in declaration**, meaning new annex amendments bind India only upon express domestic ratification.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **SWM 2016 vs. SWM 2026 Segregation Streams:** SWM Rules, 2016 mandated **3 streams** (Wet, Dry, Domestic Hazardous—UPSC 2019 trapped aspirants with "five categories"); **SWM Rules, 2026** mandate **4 streams** (**Wet, Dry, Sanitary, Special Care**) effective **April 1, 2026**.
> - **E-Waste Rules 2022 Exclusions & Solar PV Twist:** E-Waste Rules 2022 **exclude waste batteries** (Battery Rules 2022), **packaging plastics**, **micro-enterprises**, and **radioactive waste**, while **including Solar PV panels/cells**—subject to a special producer **storage mandate until 2034–2035** rather than immediate recycling quotas.
> - **Origin of EPR in Indian Law:** Extended Producer Responsibility was first introduced in India under the **E-Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 2011**—*not* the 1998 BMW Rules or 1999 Recycled Plastic Rules.
> - **Bio-Medical Waste 4-Bin Inversion Traps:** **Syringes *without* needles, gloves, IV tubes, and catheters** go into the **Red** bin (recyclable plastics), *not* Yellow or White; **sharps/needles** go into **White (translucent)**; **broken glassware & metallic implants** go into **Blue**; the legacy **Black bin was abolished** in 2016.
> - **Pyrolysis vs. Gasification Oxygen Distinction:** **Pyrolysis** occurs in the **complete absence of oxygen**; **gasification** uses a **controlled, limited supply of oxygen/steam**—both produce combustible **syngas ($\text{CO} + \text{H}_2$)**.
> - **Basel Ban Amendment & US Non-Ratification:** **India has NOT ratified the Basel Ban Amendment** (in force Dec 2019), and the **United States has not ratified even the parent Basel Convention**. Also, Basel excludes **radioactive waste** and **normal ship operations**.
> - **Rotterdam Does NOT Ban Chemicals:** Unlike Stockholm (which eliminates/restricts POPs), the **Rotterdam Convention** only enforces **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** in international trade for Annex III substances—never a production ban.
> - **Stockholm & Minamata Opt-In Shield:** Because India filed opt-in declarations under **Article 25(4) (Stockholm)** and **Article 30(5) (Minamata)**, 2025 COP listings (**Chlorpyrifos, MCCPs, LC-PFCAs, 2034 Dental Amalgam phase-out**) do *not* automatically bind India without explicit domestic ratification.
> - **Ecomark Rules, 2024 Parent Statute & Authority:** Notified under **EPA 1986** (replacing the 1991 scheme), **Ecomark** is a **voluntary** earthen-pot eco-label implemented jointly by **CPCB and BIS**—*not* a mandatory FSSAI or BEE label.
> - **Computer E-Waste Toxins Trap:** Discarded computers leach **Beryllium, Cadmium, Chromium, Mercury, and Lead**—*not* **Heptachlor** (a Stockholm organochlorine pesticide) or **Plutonium** (nuclear fissile material).

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **Cross-Convention Annex & Scope Swap:** Attributing **LC-PFCAs / Chlorpyrifos** (Stockholm Annex A) to Basel or Rotterdam, claiming Rotterdam bans pesticide production, or omitting Basel's exclusions of radioactive waste and normal ship operations.
- **EPR Stream Boundary Conflation:** Assuming **waste batteries** or **packaging plastics** fall inside E-Waste Rules, 2022, or missing that **agricultural tractors/trailers** are excluded from the 2025 ELV Rules.
- **Statutory Count & Timeline Shifts:** Testing 2016's 3-stream SWM segregation against **SWM Rules, 2026's 4 streams**, testing legacy 4-category plastic EPR against the 2024 **Category V (Biodegradable)** addition, or confusing **75 $\mu$m** (2021) with **120 $\mu$m** (Dec 2022) carry-bag floors.
- **Thermal & Medical Misclassification:** Reversing oxygen supply between **pyrolysis** (zero $\text{O}_2$) and **gasification** (limited $\text{O}_2$), or putting contaminated recyclable IV plastics into the Yellow rather than Red biomedical bin.

### 3. Cross-Hub AIR-1 Provocations
