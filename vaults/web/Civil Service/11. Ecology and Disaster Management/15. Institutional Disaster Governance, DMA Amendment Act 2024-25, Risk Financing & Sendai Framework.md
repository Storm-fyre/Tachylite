# Chapter 15 - Institutional Disaster Governance, DMA Amendment Act 2024/25, Risk Financing & Sendai Framework

Physical science can locate a fault line or forecast a cyclone landfall 72 hours ahead, but only institutional architecture converts scientific foresight into zero-casualty execution. Over the past two decades, India dismantled its colonial relief-centric famine codes and constructed a layered statutory, fiscal, and multilateral governance engine—culminating in the **Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025**, the **16th Finance Commission's ₹2.84 lakh crore risk-financing architecture (2026–31)**, and real-time integration with the **Sendai Framework (2015–2030)**.

---

## 1. Disaster Management Act, 2005 and the 2024/2025 Statutory Overhaul

### 1.1 The 2005 Paradigm Shift - Constitutional Basis and Three-Tier Spine
Prior to 2005, disaster governance operated through executive relief codes managed by the **Ministry of Agriculture**, focusing almost exclusively on post-calamity ex-gratia payouts. Catalyzed by the **1999 Odisha Super Cyclone**, the **J.C. Pant High Powered Committee (1999–2001)**, the **2001 Bhuj Earthquake**, and the **2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami**, Parliament shifted nodal responsibility to the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)** and enacted the **Disaster Management (DM) Act, 2005** (notified December 23, 2005).

A subtle constitutional quirk underlies the entire statute: **"Disaster Management" does not appear explicitly in any of the three Lists of the Seventh Schedule** of the Constitution. Parliament invoked **Entry 23 of the Concurrent List (*"Social security and social insurance; employment and unemployment"*)** to legislate a unified national framework shifting India from reactive relief to a **proactive, continuous Disaster Management Cycle** spanning Prevention, Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, Recovery, and Reconstruction.

The 2005 Act established a symmetrical **three-tier statutory hierarchy** paired with specialized training and response arms:

1. **National Tier — NDMA & NEC:**
   - **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA):** Apex policy-making body chaired ex-officio by the **Prime Minister**, comprising up to **9 other members** (one designated as Vice-Chairperson holding Cabinet Minister rank). Lays down national policies, approves the National Plan, and issues technical guidelines to ministries and states.
   - **National Executive Committee (NEC):** Chaired by the **Union Home Secretary**, comprising Secretaries of key line ministries (Finance, Agriculture, Defence, Health, Power, Water Resources, Space, Science & Tech, etc.) and Chief of Integrated Defence Staff. Acts as the executive coordinating arm of NDMA.
2. **State Tier — SDMA & SEC:**
   - **State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA):** Chaired by the **Chief Minister** (up to 9 members), setting state disaster policy and coordinating departmental mitigation budgets.
   - **State Executive Committee (SEC):** Chaired by the **Chief Secretary**, executing state guidelines and monitoring district readiness.
3. **District Tier — DDMA:**
   - **District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA):** Chaired by the **District Collector / District Magistrate / Deputy Commissioner**, with the **elected Chairperson of the Zila Parishad serving as Co-Chairperson** (in Sixth Schedule tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram, the **Chief Executive Member of the Autonomous District Council** serves as Co-Chairperson). Acts as the cutting-edge operational planning and relief authority on the ground.
4. **Capacity & Response Arms — NIDM & NDRF:**
   - **National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM — Sec 42):** Statutory apex institute for training, curriculum development, research, and post-disaster documentation (Union Home Minister = ex-officio President; Vice-Chairman NDMA = Vice-President).
   - **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF — Sec 44):** Constituted in 2006 as a dedicated, multidisciplinary specialist search-and-rescue and CBRN force operating under NDMA's general superintendence and MHA's administrative control. Currently comprises **16 battalions** (~1,149 personnel per battalion organized into 18 self-contained specialist teams) drawn on deputation from Central Armed Police Forces (**BSF, CISF, CRPF, ITBP, SSB, and Assam Rifles**), pre-positioned across regional response centres to minimize golden-hour deployment delays.

### 1.2 The Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025 (Act No. 10 of 2025)
Two decades of operational friction—urban megacity flooding, pandemic coordination clashes, duplicated planning between NDMA/NEC, and non-statutory ad-hoc committees—prompted the **Disaster Management (Amendment) Bill, 2024** (introduced in Lok Sabha on **August 1, 2024**; passed Lok Sabha December 12, 2024; passed Rajya Sabha March 25, 2025), which received Presidential Assent on **March 29, 2025** and came into force on **April 9, 2025** as the **Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025**. Notably, an **Explanation inserted under Section 2(d)** also clarified that **"man-made causes"** in the statutory definition of "disaster" **explicitly excludes any law-and-order-related matter or situation**.

Its **seven structural reforms** fundamentally rewire India's disaster governance:

1. **Statutory Urban Disaster Management Authorities (UDMAs — Sec 41A):** To plug India's acute urban governance gap (where DDMAs prioritized rural taluks while municipal corporations lacked statutory disaster mandates), the Act empowers State Governments to constitute a dedicated **UDMA** for every **state capital** and every **city having a Municipal Corporation**—explicitly **excluding Delhi and Chandigarh** (governed via separate UT mechanisms). Crucially, each UDMA is **chaired by the Municipal Commissioner**, with the **District Collector as Vice-Chairperson**, mandated to formulate and enforce a standalone **Urban Disaster Management Plan**.
2. **Direct National & State Planning by NDMA and SDMAs:** Eliminating bureaucratic duplication, the 2025 Act shifts the statutory duty of preparing the **National Plan** directly from the NEC to **NDMA**, and preparing **State Plans** directly from the SEC to **SDMAs**—re-focusing NEC and SECs on implementation, inter-departmental coordination, and compliance monitoring.
3. **Statutory Elevation of NCMC and HLC (Secs 8A & 8B):** Two powerful executive bodies that operated solely via administrative rules since pre-2005 now possess explicit parliamentary backing:
   - **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC — Sec 8A):** Chaired by the **Cabinet Secretary**, with Secretaries of concerned ministries and NDMA Member-Secretary, serving as the statutory nodal body managing major disasters with serious or national ramifications.
   - **High Level Committee (HLC — Sec 8B):** Chaired by the **Union Home Minister** (with Finance Minister, Agriculture Minister, and Vice-Chairman NITI Aayog as members), statutorily mandated to approve financial assistance from **NDRF** and mitigation projects under **NDMF**.
4. **Statutory National & State Disaster Databases (Secs 2(da), 6(2)(v) & 18(2)(p)):** Defines "disaster database" under **Section 2(da)** and obligates **NDMA (Sec 6(2)(v))** and **SDMAs (Sec 18(2)(p))** to create and maintain comprehensive **National and State Disaster Databases** recording hazard-risk assessments, fund allocations, real-time expenditure, mitigation registers, and loss inventories—directly operationalizing **Sendai Priority 1**.
5. **Statutory Status for State Disaster Response Forces (SDRFs — Sec 44A):** Empowers State Governments by statute under **Section 44A** (immediately following Section 44 NDRF) to raise, train, and deploy dedicated **State Disaster Response Forces** (distinct from the State Disaster Response *Fund*), standardizing first-line state rescue capability alongside NDRF.
6. **Institutional Autonomy, Day-to-Day Leadership & Emerging-Risk Mandate for NDMA (Secs 3(3A) & 5):** Inserts **Section 3(3A)** statutorily vesting NDMA's **day-to-day functions in the Vice-Chairperson** (or, in their absence, any Member designated by the Chairperson or Vice-Chairperson), broadens NDMA's mandate to periodically assess the full spectrum of disaster risks—explicitly including **emerging climate-change risks**—and empowers NDMA under substituted **Section 5** to **specify its own officers/employees and directly appoint domain experts and consultants** (with prior Central Government approval), reducing its chronic over-reliance on MHA generalist deputationists.
7. **Compliance Direction & Civil Penalty Ceiling (Sec 60A):** Empowers Central and State Governments via Official Gazette notification (valid for the specified period or **6 months**, whichever is earlier) to issue binding directives requiring any person to act or abstain from acting in relation to a hazard to reduce disaster impact, backed by a statutory civil penalty **which shall not exceed ₹10,000** (an upper ceiling of up to ₹10,000, not a mandatory flat fine) for refusal without reasonable cause.

### 1.3 Governance Friction — Federalism and Municipal Democracy Critiques
While widely welcomed for modernizing urban and financial structures, the 2025 Amendment triggered two sharp constitutional critiques during parliamentary scrutiny:
- **Centralization vs. Federal Autonomy:** Critics noted that expanding NDMA's uniform standard-setting and planning directives—under an Act resting on Concurrent Entry 23 rather than an explicit Seventh Schedule disaster entry—risks encroaching on State-List administrative domains (Land, Water, Police, Public Health) unless paired with cooperative Centre–State fiscal autonomy.
- **Bureaucratic Capture of UDMAs (74th Amendment Friction):** Unlike DDMAs (which include the elected Zila Parishad Chairperson as Co-Chairperson), **UDMAs are chaired by unelected IAS Municipal Commissioners with District Collectors as Vice-Chairpersons**, omitting the **elected Mayor** from statutory leadership—raising concerns that municipal disaster planning will remain bureaucratized and disconnected from ward-level democratic accountability.

## 2. Disaster Risk Financing - 15th and 16th Finance Commissions (2021–2031)

### 2.1 Breaking the Relief Trap - The 80:20 NDRMF / SDRMF Architecture
For 15 years after 2005, Sections 47 and 48 of the DM Act—mandating National and State Disaster Mitigation Funds—remained dead letters on paper because Finance Commissions funded only post-disaster relief (NDRF and SDRF). The **15th Finance Commission (FC-XV, 2021–26)** fundamentally restructured Indian disaster finance by establishing umbrella **National and State Disaster Risk Management Funds (NDRMF / SDRMF)** and operationalizing the **80:20 Response-to-Mitigation Split**:

- **80% Response & Recovery Window (NDRF / SDRF):** Further disaggregated internally into **40% Response & Relief**, **30% Recovery & Reconstruction**, and **10% Preparedness & Capacity Building**—ensuring states could fund post-disaster *Build Back Better* reconstruction (such as Joshimath's ₹1,658 cr plan) rather than just temporary tents and rations.
- **20% Ring-Fenced Mitigation Window (NDMF / SDMF):** Strictly locked for ex-ante risk-reduction investments—urban flood mitigation (UFRMP), GLOF monitoring (NGRMP), landslide stabilization, fire-service modernization, and early-warning telemetry. Funds **cannot** be diverted to routine relief payouts.
- **Centre–State Cost Sharing (SDRMF):** **75:25** for General Category States and **90:10** for **North-Eastern and Himalayan States** (while NDRMF is 100% Central, funded historically via the National Calamity Contingent Duty [NCCD] and budgetary grants).
- **10% Local Hazard Flexi-Window:** Within their annual SDRF allocation, states may spend up to **10%** on **state-specific local disasters** notified by the state itself (e.g., coastal erosion, human-wildlife conflict, drowning, snakebite) that fall outside the Union's central list.

### 2.2 Live 2026 Transition - 16th Finance Commission (2026–31) & the 12 $\rightarrow$ 14 Expansion
Tabled in Parliament on **February 1, 2026** and operative from **April 1, 2026**, the **16th Finance Commission (FC-XVI, 2026–31)** chaired by Dr. Arvind Panagariya preserved and expanded this proactive architecture while modernizing hazard coverage:

- **Enhanced ₹2.84 Lakh Crore Five-Year Pool:** Retained the **80:20 Response–Mitigation split**, **75:25 / 90:10** sharing ratios, and **10% state flexi-fund**, while lifting **SDRMF** from ₹1,60,153 crore (15th FC) to **₹2,04,401 crore** (Centre's share ₹1,55,916 crore) and **NDRMF** from ₹68,783 crore to **₹79,406 crore** across 2026–31.
- **Revamped Disaster Risk Index (DRI) Formula:** State-wise SDRMF distribution follows a four-factor formula: **Past Expenditure (30%)**, **Geographical Area (15%)**, **Population (15%)**, and a science-driven **Disaster Risk Index (40%)** incorporating multi-hazard exposure, vulnerability, and emerging climate extremes.
- **Historic Expansion of Centrally Notified Disasters (12 $\rightarrow$ 14 on June 30, 2026):** Under the 15th FC, only **12 disasters** qualified for full central SDRF/NDRF access (*cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, tsunami, hailstorm, landslide, avalanche, cloudburst, pest attack, and frost/cold wave*). Accepting the 16th FC's recommendation, **MHA issued revised SDRF/NDRF norms on June 30, 2026** (confirmed in Lok Sabha on **August 4, 2026**), formally adding **Heatwaves and Lightning**—expanding India's centrally notified roster from **12 to 14** and unlocking full mitigation and relief funding for India's two fastest-rising climate-mortality hazards.

## 3. Global Architecture, Early-Warning Integration and Community Resilience

### 3.1 Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) - The *Sachet* Integrated Alert Engine
Historically, warnings issued by IMD, CWC, INCOIS, FSI, and GSI resided in incompatible agency silos, reaching citizens too late or in unreadable English bulletins. To operationalize the ITU-T **X.1303 Common Alerting Protocol (CAP)** standard (aligned with the UN's *Early Warnings for All* initiative), **NDMA** partnered with the **Centre for Development of Telematics (C-DOT — Department of Telecommunications)** to deploy the **CAP Integrated Alert System (*Sachet*)**:

- **Multi-Agency Convergence:** Aggregates verified, standardized geo-referenced XML alerts from all Authorized Alerting Agencies (**IMD, CWC, INCOIS, FSI, GSI, DRDO–DGRE**) alongside all 36 State/UT Disaster Management Authorities on a single national command dashboard.
- **Multi-Channel Last-Mile Dissemination:** Simultaneously broadcasts hyper-local, polygon-targeted warnings in **regional vernacular languages** across **Location-Based SMS**, **Cell Broadcast** (instantaneous flash-screen alerts with piercing audio tones that bypass mobile tower congestion during active disasters), the ***Sachet* mobile app**, Doordarshan/AIR/FM radio, Indian Railways passenger displays, and coastal tower sirens—having delivered **billions of geo-targeted alerts** nationwide across every State and UT.

### 3.2 India's Global Resilience Diplomacy - CDRI and IRIS
Complementing domestic reforms, India has carved out leadership in global disaster-risk diplomacy—anchored by the **Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI)** and the **G20 Disaster Risk Reduction Working Group** (institutionalized under India's 2023 G20 Presidency):

- **Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI):** Launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi at the **UN Climate Action Summit (New York, September 23, 2019)** with its permanent **Secretariat in New Delhi** (accorded International Organization status by India under the UN Privileges and Immunities Act, 1947). As of late 2026, CDRI brings together **50+ sovereign member countries** and **8 international partner organizations** (including UNDRR, World Bank, ADB, EU, and UNDP). Rather than building roads or grids itself, CDRI serves as a global multi-stakeholder **standards, knowledge, and risk-financing accelerator** across power, telecom, transport, and urban health infrastructure—publishing its flagship biennial **Global Infrastructure Resilience (GIR)** report demonstrating that spending ~3–15% extra upfront on resilient design yields 4×–12× avoided disaster losses.
- **IRIS (*Infrastructure for Resilient Island States*):** CDRI's flagship operational facility launched at **COP26 Glasgow (November 2, 2021)** jointly by **India, the UK, Australia, Fiji, Jamaica, and Mauritius** (seeded initially with a $10 million Indian contribution). Targeting **58 Small Island Developing States (SIDS)** across the Caribbean, Pacific, and Indian Ocean/AIMS—where a single Category-5 cyclone can erase >100% of annual GDP—IRIS finances technical assistance, resilient port/power/telecom engineering, and project-preparation grants directly bridging climate vulnerability and infrastructure bankability.

### 3.3 Community First Responders - *Aapda Mitra*, *Yuva Aapda Mitra* and Build Back Better
Every major Indian calamity confirms a stark operational truth: **the local community—not external rescue battalions—conducts >80% of life-saving rescues during the critical first 6–12 hours**. To formalize grassroots resilience:

- ***Aapda Mitra* & *Yuva Aapda Mitra* Scheme (YAMS):** Launched by NDMA as a centrally sponsored scheme, *Aapda Mitra* trained **1 lakh community volunteers across 350 multi-hazard districts** in flood rescue, basic life support, collapsed-structure triage, and fire safety—equipping each with an Emergency Responder Kit and 3-year life/medical insurance. Scaling this reserve into youth networks, MHA/NDMA launched the **Yuva Aapda Mitra Scheme (YAMS)** (outlay **₹470.50 crore**, targeting **2.37 lakh youth volunteers** across **315 vulnerable districts** drawn from **NCC, NSS, NYKS, and Bharat Scouts & Guides**, supported by 1,300 master trainers)—forging a permanent community civil-defence backbone synced with DDMAs and UDMAs.
- **Build Back Better (BBB):** Codified under **Sendai Priority 4**, *Build Back Better* rejects returning disaster-ravaged zones to their pre-existing structural fragility. Instead, recovery investments systematically elevate structural codes, relocate vulnerable settlements outside active floodways, bury coastal power lines underground (as pioneered in Odisha post-*Phailin* and *Fani*), and upgrade municipal town planning (Gujarat's post-2001 Bhuj reconstruction under GSDMA)—now institutionally funded via NDRMF/SDRMF's dedicated **30% Recovery & Reconstruction** sub-window.

### 3.4 Sendai Framework (2015–2030) and the PM's 10-Point Agenda on DRR
Adopted at the **3rd UN World Conference on Disaster Risk Reduction in Sendai, Japan (March 18, 2015)**, the **Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (SFDRR, 2015–2030)** is a **voluntary, non-binding** 15-year global compact overseen by **UNDRR**. Succeeding the **Hyogo Framework for Action (HFA, 2005–2015)**, Sendai marked a decisive conceptual pivot: shifting global focus from **managing disasters** (reactive relief) to **managing disaster risk** (tackling underlying exposure, climate drivers, biological/technological hazards, and private-sector infrastructure investment).

Sendai rests on **4 Priorities for Action** and **7 Global Targets** (structured cleanly as **4 Reductions [A–D] + 3 Increases [E–G]**):

- **Four Priorities for Action:**
  1. **Understanding disaster risk** (in all dimensions: hazard, exposure, vulnerability, capacity, and environment).
  2. **Strengthening disaster risk governance** to manage disaster risk across national, local, and transboundary tiers.
  3. **Investing in disaster risk reduction for resilience** (public and private structural/non-structural investments).
  4. **Enhancing disaster preparedness for effective response** and to **"Build Back Better"** in recovery, rehabilitation, and reconstruction.
- **Seven Global Targets (Evaluated against 2005–2015 Baseline by 2030):**
  - **Target A (Reduce):** Substantially reduce global **disaster mortality** per 100,000 population.
  - **Target B (Reduce):** Substantially reduce the number of **affected people** globally per 100,000.
  - **Target C (Reduce):** Reduce direct **disaster economic loss** in relation to global GDP.
  - **Target D (Reduce):** Substantially reduce disaster damage to **critical infrastructure** and disruption of basic services (especially health and educational facilities).
  - **Target E (Increase):** Substantially increase the number of countries with **national and local DRR strategies** (initial target year: 2020).
  - **Target F (Increase):** Substantially enhance **international cooperation** to developing countries to complement national DRR actions.
  - **Target G (Increase):** Substantially increase availability of and access to **Multi-Hazard Early Warning Systems (MHEWS)** and disaster risk information.

To operationalize Sendai domestically and internationally, India unveiled the **Prime Minister's 10-Point Agenda on DRR** at the **Asian Ministerial Conference on DRR (AMCDRR, New Delhi, November 2016)**—which became the explicit blueprint behind India's 2016 National Disaster Management Plan (updated 2019), CDRI, *Sachet*, and the 2025 DM Amendment:
1. All development sectors must imbibe principles of disaster risk management.
2. Work toward **risk coverage for all**—from poor households and SMEs to multinational corporations and nation-states (parametric insurance & risk transfer).
3. Encourage greater **leadership and involvement of women** in disaster risk management.
4. Invest in **risk mapping globally** (for hazards from earthquakes to cyclones).
5. Leverage **technology** (GIS, remote sensing, CAP, AI) to enhance efficiency of DRR efforts.
6. Develop a **network of universities** to work on disaster issues (linking IITs/NIDM with local vulnerability research).
7. Utilize opportunities provided by **social media and mobile technologies** for disaster risk reduction.
8. Build on **local capacity and initiative** (*Aapda Mitra* / Panchayati Raj / SHGs).
9. Ensure that the opportunity to **learn from a disaster is not wasted** (mandatory post-disaster needs assessments & forensic studies).
10. Bring about **greater cohesion in international response** to disasters (CDRI, IRIS, SAREX, G20 DRR Working Group).

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Constitutional Entry Trap:** **"Disaster Management"** is **NOT** explicitly listed in the Union, State, or Concurrent Lists of the Seventh Schedule. Parliament enacted the **DM Act, 2005** using **Entry 23 of the Concurrent List** (*"Social security and social insurance"*).
> - **UDMA Chairperson & Territorial Exclusion (DM Amendment Act, 2025):** Under new **Section 41A**, **Urban Disaster Management Authorities (UDMAs)** are chaired by the **Municipal Commissioner** (NOT the Mayor!) with the **District Collector as Vice-Chairperson**—and explicitly **exclude Delhi and Chandigarh** (applicable to state capitals and cities with Municipal Corporations).
> - **Plan-Drafting Shift (2005 vs. 2025):** Under the 2005 Act, **NEC** prepared the National Plan and **SEC** prepared the State Plan. Under the **2025 Amendment**, **NDMA** directly prepares the National Plan and **SDMAs** directly prepare State Plans.
> - **NCMC vs. HLC Statutory Leadership (Secs 8A & 8B, 2025):** Both became statutory only in **March 2025**. **NCMC** is chaired by the **Cabinet Secretary** (crisis coordination); **HLC** is chaired by the **Union Home Minister** (NDRF/NDMF financial approval)—*not* the Prime Minister or Finance Minister.
> - **SDRF Double-Acronym & Bare-Act Section Traps (2025 Amendment):** Distinguish carefully between the **State Disaster Response Fund** (80% fiscal window of SDRMF) and the **State Disaster Response Force** (operational rescue force newly granted statutory backing under **Section 44A** of the 2025 Amendment). Also track three exact 2025 bare-Act insertions: **Section 2(d) Explanation** explicitly excludes **law-and-order matters** from "man-made causes" of disaster; **Section 60A** authorizes Gazette-notified preventive hazard directives (valid up to **6 months**) with a civil penalty **which shall not exceed ₹10,000** (an upper ceiling, not a flat mandatory fine); and **National/State Disaster Databases** sit under **Secs 2(da), 6(2)(v) & 18(2)(p)** (not Sec 60A).
> - **80:20 Split & Non-Divertibility:** Under both **15th FC** and **16th FC (2026–31)**, NDRMF/SDRMF follow an **80% Response (NDRF/SDRF) : 20% Mitigation (NDMF/SDMF)** split. Mitigation funds are strictly ring-fenced and **cannot** be reappropriated for routine post-disaster relief. Cost sharing for SDRMF is **75:25** (general states) and **90:10** (NE & Himalayan states).
> - **12 $\rightarrow$ 14 Notified Disasters (June 30, 2026):** Following the **16th Finance Commission**, MHA's **June 30, 2026** guidelines expanded centrally notified disasters from **12 to 14** by adding **Heatwaves and Lightning** (while retaining the 10% SDRF window for state-specific local disasters).
> - **CAP (*Sachet*) Developer & CDRI Status:** ***Sachet*** is built by **C-DOT (Department of Telecommunications)** for **NDMA** using the **ITU-T X.1303** standard—*not* by IMD or NIC. **CDRI** is **not** a UN specialized agency or lending bank; it is an India-initiated **multi-stakeholder international coalition** headquartered in **New Delhi** (recognized as an International Organization under India's 1947 Act), and **IRIS** (launched at COP26 Glasgow) is its flagship facility for **58 SIDS**.
> - **Sendai Legal Nature & Baseline:** The **Sendai Framework (2015–2030)** is **voluntary and non-binding** (not a legally binding treaty like Paris/BBNJ), succeeded the **Hyogo Framework (2005–2015)**, and comprises **4 Priorities & 7 Global Targets** (4 reductions + 3 increases).

April 9, 2025):** Statutory **UDMAs** (Sec 41A), direct NDMA/SDMA planning, statutory **NCMC & HLC** (Secs 8A–8B), **National/State Disaster Databases** (Secs 2(da), 6(2)(v) & 18(2)(p)), statutory **SDRF** (Sec 44A), NDMA day-to-day leadership by Vice-Chairperson (Sec 3(3A)), direct expert hiring (Sec 5), climate-risk assessments, and **Sec 60A** preventive directives (penalty up to ₹10,000).
>   - **16th Finance Commission (2026–31):** **₹2,04,401 cr SDRMF + ₹79,406 cr NDRMF = ~₹2.84 lakh cr**; retained **80:20** Response–Mitigation split & **40%-weighted Disaster Risk Index**; expanded centrally notified disasters **12 $\rightarrow$ 14** (Heatwaves & Lightning added **June 30, 2026**).
>   - **Resilience & Community Scale:** **16 NDRF battalions**; **1 lakh *Aapda Mitra*** trained (350 districts) + **₹470.50 cr *Yuva Aapda Mitra*** targeting **2.37 lakh youth** (315 districts); **CDRI** (50+ nations, 8 partners) & **IRIS** (58 SIDS); **C-DOT *Sachet* CAP** delivering billions of vernacular geo-targeted alerts across 36 States/UTs.
> - **High-Altitude Policy Critiques & Way Forward (GS3, GS2 & Essay):**
>   - **Democratizing Urban Disaster Governance (Fixing UDMAs):** To reconcile **Section 41A UDMAs** with the **74th Constitutional Amendment**, State Governments should notify **elected Mayors** as Co-Chairpersons (mirroring the Zila Parishad Co-Chair template in DDMAs) and integrate **Ward Disaster Management Committees** directly into municipal budgets—preventing urban plans from remaining top-down bureaucratic documents.
>   - **Moving Beyond Exchequer Dependence to Risk Transfer:** Even ₹2.84 lakh crore over five years cannot absorb compounding climate-driven GDP shocks (expected to rise sharply under $1.5^\circ\text{C}\text{–}2^\circ\text{C}$ warming). India must operationalize **PM's 10-Point Agenda Point #2 ("Risk Coverage for All")** via **sovereign catastrophe bonds, parametric heat/flood/cyclone micro-insurance** (scaling Nagaland/SEWA pilots), and mandatory catastrophe insurance pools for MSMEs and state public infrastructure.
>   - **Turning Community Participation from Episodic Tokenism to Permanent Infrastructure:** As highlighted by **UPSC Mains GS3 2026**, community volunteers frequently decay between disasters due to lack of refresher drills, insurance renewals, and ward-level authority. Institutionalize annual mock-drill calendars, tie **16th FC local-body grants** to functional Village/Ward DM Committees, guarantee **gender- and disability-inclusive leadership** (PM's Point #3 & NDMA 2019 Disability Guidelines), and legally integrate *Aapda Mitra* / *Yuva Aapda Mitra* rosters into DDMA/UDMA incident response systems.
>   - **Enforcing Statutory Mitigation Spending & Accountability:** States historically under-utilized the 20% SDMF mitigation window compared to easy ex-gratia SDRF disbursals. Use the new statutory **National & State Disaster Databases (Secs 2(da), 6(2)(v) & 18(2)(p))** to publish real-time public dashboards tracking **ex-ante mitigation outcomes** (lives saved, avoided infrastructure loss) alongside post-disaster **Build Back Better** audits.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature
- **UDMA Composition & Exclusion Ambush:** Claiming UDMAs under the DM (Amendment) Act, 2025 are chaired by the **Mayor** or apply mandatorily to **Delhi and Chandigarh** (wrong on both counts: chaired by the **Municipal Commissioner** with **District Collector as Vice-Chair**, and explicitly **excluding Delhi and Chandigarh**).
- **Pre- vs. Post-2025 Statutory Authority Swap:** Stating that **NCMC and HLC** are non-statutory bodies or that **NEC** still prepares the National Disaster Management Plan (the **March 2025 Amendment** made NCMC & HLC statutory and shifted plan drafting directly to **NDMA/SDMAs**).
- **Finance Commission Allocation & Divertibility Confusion:** Claiming States can divert the **20% SDMF mitigation fund** toward emergency relief payouts during severe calamities, or citing outdated 15th FC figures (12 notified disasters) after the **16th FC / June 30, 2026 MHA expansion to 14** (adding heatwaves and lightning).
- **International Body Misclassification:** Describing **CDRI** as a UN multilateral development bank extending sovereign infrastructure loans, or calling the **Sendai Framework** a legally binding UN treaty.

### 3. Cross-Hub AIR-1 Provocations
1. **GS2 × GS3 (Cooperative Federalism & 74th Amendment):** Evaluate the **Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025** through the dual lenses of *fiscal/administrative efficiency* and *democratic decentralization*: does vesting **UDMAs** in unelected Municipal Commissioners while expanding NDMA's national planning reach cure urban disaster paralysis, or does it hollow out elected municipal self-governance?
2. **GS3 × IR × Economy (Global Climate-Risk Architecture):** Contrast India's domestic shift toward ex-ante disaster financing (**16th FC ₹2.84 lakh crore NDRMF/SDRMF 80:20 model**) with its international leadership in **CDRI, IRIS, and the G20 DRR Working Group**—how can India leverage domestic parametric-insurance and CAP (*Sachet*) innovations as diplomatic public goods for the Global South?
