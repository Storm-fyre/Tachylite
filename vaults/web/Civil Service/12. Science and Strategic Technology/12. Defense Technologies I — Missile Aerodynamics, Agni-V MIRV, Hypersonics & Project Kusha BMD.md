# Chapter 12 - Defense Technologies I - Missile Aerodynamics, Agni-V MIRV, Hypersonics & Project Kusha BMD

Modern missile warfare is a contest between offensive penetration physics and defensive reaction time. Over four decades since the launch of the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), India has moved from legacy liquid-fuelled short-range rockets to a full-spectrum precision strike and air-defense architecture—highlighted across 2024–2026 by **Agni-V MIRV (*Mission Divyastra*)**, the **>1,500 km Long-Range Hypersonic Missile**, **BMD Phase-II AD-1**, and the **July 23, 2026 maiden flight test of *Project Kusha***.

---

## 1. Missile Flight Mechanics & the IGMDP Legacy

### Ballistic vs. Cruise vs. Quasi-Ballistic Flight Regimes

Missiles divide fundamentally along how they generate lift, sustain propulsion, and interact with Earth's atmosphere—distinctions UPSC routinely weaponizes through complete textbook inversions:
- **Ballistic Missiles:** Rocket-powered **only during the initial boost phase** (burning internal fuel and oxidizer). Once engines cut off, the warhead coasts along an unpowered high-altitude **Keplerian parabolic/elliptical trajectory**—exiting Earth's atmosphere (exo-atmospheric mid-course) before gravity pulls the Re-entry Vehicle (RV) back into the atmosphere at steep hypersonic terminal speeds (Mach 10–24 for ICBMs). They carry massive strategic payloads over vast distances (**SRBM** <1,000 km; **MRBM** 1,000–3,000 km; **IRBM** 3,000–5,500 km; **ICBM** >5,500 km), but their predictable free-fall arc and scorching boost-phase infrared plume expose them early to space-based IR sensors and long-range ballistic radars.
- **Cruise Missiles:** Effectively unmanned, expendable guided aircraft that stay **entirely within the atmosphere**, generating aerodynamic lift via stubby wings and running air-breathing **jet engines (turbofan, turbojet, ramjet, or scramjet)** continuously from launch to impact. Classified by speed into **subsonic** (<Mach 0.8; e.g., Nirbhay/ITCM, Tomahawk), **supersonic** (Mach 2–3; e.g., BrahMos), and **hypersonic** ($\ge\text{Mach }5$; scramjet HCMs). By hugging terrain or sea-skimming at 10–15 metres altitude, cruise missiles hide beneath ground radar horizons until seconds before impact, trading raw payload weight for pinpoint accuracy and stealth.
- **Quasi-Ballistic Missiles (The Hybrid Bridge):** Rocket-boosted onto a **depressed endo-atmospheric trajectory** (never climbing to classical ballistic apogees) and executing aerodynamic pull-ups or lateral S-turns during mid-course and terminal phases (**Pralay**, Russian *Iskander-M*). This hybrid profile denies adversary air defenses both early high-altitude radar warning and clean parabolic intercept solutions.

### The IGMDP Legacy (1983–2008) & Modernized Derivatives

Conceived in **1983** under Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam to circumvent Western MTCR technology denials, the **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)** laid India's propulsion, inertial guidance, and seeker foundation across five core missile families (formally concluded by DRDO in **2008**, with final Agni/Nag technology handovers completing through 2012):
1. **Prithvi (Tactical SRBM):** India's first indigenous liquid-fuelled ballistic series—*Prithvi-I* (Army, 150 km, phased out for Prahaar/Pralay), *Prithvi-II* (IAF, 350 km), *Prithvi-III* (solid-fuelled, 350–600 km), and ship-launched ***Dhanush*** (350 km).
2. **Agni (Strategic Deterrent):** Transitioned from a technology demonstrator (*Agni-TD*) into India's solid-fuelled land-based nuclear triad spine (*Agni-I* to *Agni-V* and *Agni-P*).
3. **Trishul (Short-Range SAM):** Closed in 2008 as a technology demonstrator whose beam-riding and radar-guidance subsystems fed directly into subsequent indigenous SAMs.
4. **Akash → Akash-NG (Medium-Range SAM):** Legacy *Akash* (25–30 km, ramjet-sustained, Rajendra PESA radar) proved combat-effective in India's layered air-defense network and was exported to Armenia. Its successor, **Akash-NG (New Generation)**—completing user evaluation trials in **December 2025**—switches to a **canisterized 360° vertical launch**, a **dual-pulse solid rocket motor**, and an indigenous **active RF/AESA seeker**, pushing engagement range to **70–80 km** with minimal radar signature and high-G maneuverability against low-RCS cruise missiles and fighters.
5. **Nag & the Indigenous ATGM Family:** Third-generation **"Fire-and-Forget"**, top-attack Anti-Tank Guided Missile using an **Imaging Infrared (IIR)** seeker (targeting thinner overhead turret armour) fired from the tracked *NAMICA* carrier. Modernized across four specialized derivatives:
   - **Nag Mk-2:** Upgraded 7–10 km land variant (field evaluation trials completed January 2025).
   - **HELINA (*Helicopter-Launched Nag*) / *Dhruvastra* (IAF variant):** 7 km all-weather, lock-on-before-launch ATGM deployed on HAL *Rudra* and LCH *Prachand*.
   - **MPATGM (*Man-Portable ATGM*):** Lightweight shoulder/tripod-fired infantry system (2.5–4 km; validated top-attack capability against moving targets in **January 2026**).
   - **SANT (*Stand-Off Anti-Tank*):** Upgraded 10–20 km helicopter-launched variant replacing IIR with a **Millimetre-Wave (MMW) active radar seeker** enabling **Lock-On After Launch (LOAL)** through battlefield dust, smoke, and rain.

## 2. Strategic Deterrent & Tactical Precision-Strike Arsenal

### Canisterized Agni-P & Agni-V MIRV (*Mission Divyastra*, March 2024)

Older Indian ballistic missiles required hours of exposed field assembly and pre-launch checks, creating acute vulnerability to adversary satellite ISR. **Canisterization** hermetically seals a solid-fuelled missile inside a climate-controlled maraging-steel tube atop a road or rail transporter-erector-launcher (TEL): the missile is cold-launched by a gas generator before its main solid motor ignites mid-air, shrinking launch-readiness time from hours to **minutes** and guaranteeing credible No-First-Use (NFU) second-strike survivability.

- **Agni-Prime (Agni-P):** A new-generation, **two-stage solid, canisterized MRBM (1,000–2,000 km)** constructed with lightweight carbon-composite casings—weighing roughly half as much as Agni-III while carrying a **Maneuverable Re-entry Vehicle (MaRV)** guided by dual-redundant Ring-Laser Gyro INS and optical/radar terminal correction. On **September 24–25, 2025**, DRDO and the Strategic Forces Command achieved a landmark survivability leap by launching Agni-P for the first time from a **Rail-Based Mobile Launcher System**, enabling silent cross-country dispersal across India's 68,000+ km railway grid.
- **Agni-V MIRV (*Mission Divyastra*, March 11, 2024):** Launched from Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Island (Odisha) under Project Director **Sheena Rani**, *Mission Divyastra* tested India's first indigenous **Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicle (MIRV)** architecture atop the three-stage, solid-fuelled, canisterized **Agni-V ICBM** (officially rated **>5,000 km**; strategic assessments cite 5,500–8,000 km depending on warhead mass).
  - **MIRV Physics & BMD Saturation:** After the third solid stage burns out in space, a liquid-propelled **Post-Boost Vehicle (PBV) "bus"** executes fine-attitude thruster burns to sequentially release **multiple miniaturized nuclear warheads (typically 3–10)** onto independent ballistic trajectories capable of striking cities or hardened silos separated by hundreds of kilometres—accompanied by **penetration aids (penaids)** such as radar-reflective chaff, inflatable Mylar decoys, and electronic jammers. Because an adversary's Ballistic Missile Defense (BMD) system must commit multiple expensive interceptors against each incoming real warhead *and* indistinguishable decoy, MIRVing multiplies deterrent lethality per missile—placing India alongside the **US, Russia, China, UK, and France** as the sixth nation to test MIRV technology.

### Conventional Tactical Strike, Maritime Stand-Off & BVR Air Dominance

To bridge the dangerous escalation gap between localized infantry clashes and nuclear retaliation, India has fielded a potent conventional **Rocket Force and Stand-Off Strike** inventory:
- **Pralay (Quasi-Ballistic SRBM):** Indigenous canisterized solid-fuel tactical missile (**150–500 km** range, 350–1,000 kg modular conventional warheads, **<10 m CEP**). Flying a depressed endo-atmospheric arc with terminal maneuverability to defeat enemy SAMs, Pralay targets hardened command bunkers, airfields, and radar grids without crossing the nuclear threshold. Validated across back-to-back salvo user trials (**July 28–29, 2025** and **December 31, 2025**) and a subsequent user evaluation flight test on **November 5, 2026**.
- **Pinaka Multi-Barrel Rocket Launcher (MBRL) Family:** Twelve-tube artillery saturation system delivering high-explosive/submunition salvos in 44 seconds—progressively extended from unguided **Pinaka Mk-1 (37.5 km)** and **Pinaka-ER (45 km)** to **Guided Pinaka (75 km)** and the newly flight-tested **Pinaka LRGR-120 (120 km Long-Range Guided Rocket, maiden trial December 29, 2025)**. Now a flagship Indian defense export (inducted by Armenia; evaluated by France).
- **BrahMos Supersonic Cruise Missile (450–800 km):** Developed via the India (DRDO) – Russia (NPO Mashinostroyenia) joint venture, BrahMos remains the world's fastest operational **supersonic cruise missile (Mach 2.8–3.0)**—generating ~9× the terminal kinetic energy of subsonic Tomahawk-class missiles. Its **two-stage propulsion** uses a **solid rocket booster** to accelerate to supersonic velocity before jettisoning and handing over to a **liquid-fuelled ramjet sustainer** for sea-skimming (10–15 m) terminal flight. Originally capped at 290 km under MTCR guidelines, India's **June 2016 accession to the MTCR** legally unlocked indigenous range upgrades to **450 km (operational Extended Range)** and **800 km** (undergoing extended-range validation across 2025–2026). Deployed across land, naval Vertical Launch Systems (VLS), submerged platforms, and Su-30MKI (**BrahMos-A**), with a compact **BrahMos-NG** under development for LCA Tejas and flagship export deliveries underway to the **Philippines ($375 million contract)**.
- **Rudram-I / II / III (Anti-Radiation Missiles — NGARM):** India's indigenous **SEAD/DEAD** (*Suppression/Destruction of Enemy Air Defences*) suite built to hunt hostile radar batteries. Equipped with a **Passive Homing Head (PHH)** scanning wide RF bands plus an **INS/GPS target-memory mode**—ensuring impact even if enemy radar operators switch off emitters upon detecting the launch. **Rudram-I** (100–250 km, Mach 2) handles tactical SAM suppression; **Rudram-II** (solid-propelled air-to-surface quasi-ballistic, ~300–350 km, Mach 5.5; flight-tested from Su-30MKI on **May 29, 2024**) strikes deep hardened radar/C2 nodes; **Rudram-III** (~550 km class) extends stand-off bunker-busting reach.
- **SMART (*Supersonic Missile-Assisted Release of Torpedo*):** A canisterized **~643–650 km** stand-off Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW) weapon (successfully flight-tested **May 1, 2024** from APJ Abdul Kalam Island). Conventional heavyweight/lightweight torpedoes move slowly (~40–50 knots) with effective ranges of just 20–40 km; SMART mounts an indigenous **Advanced Lightweight Torpedo (ALWT)** inside a supersonic rocket-boosted missile, rocketing across hundreds of kilometres in minutes before parachute-deploying the acoustic homing torpedo directly atop a hostile submarine's patrol box.
- **Astra BVRAAM Family (Beyond Visual Range Air-to-Air):**
  - **Astra Mk-1:** ~100–110 km, Mach 4.5, active radar seeker (operational on Su-30MKI and Tejas; indigenous RF seeker flight-validated July 2025).
  - **Astra Mk-2:** ~160–200+ km, powered by a **dual-pulse solid rocket motor** extending high-energy endgame maneuverability.
  - **Astra Mk-3 (*Gandiva*):** **300–340+ km** extreme-BVR missile powered by a **Solid Fuel Ducted Ramjet (SFDR)** air-breathing engine (throttlable solid fuel-rich gas generator mixing with rammed atmospheric oxygen). Unlike conventional solid-rocket air-to-air missiles that burn out early and coast slowly into the endgame, SFDR sustains powered supersonic thrust right up to impact—creating an enormous **"No-Escape Zone"** surpassing Europe's MBDA *Meteor* and neutralizing adversary long-range threats like China's PL-15/PL-17 (SFDR propulsion trials continued through February 2026).

## 3. Hypersonic Weapons ($\ge\text{Mach }5$) - Physics & India's 2024–2026 Breakthroughs

True **hypersonic weapons** are not merely fast (ICBMs already exceed Mach 20 during steep vacuum re-entry); their strategic disruption comes from sustaining **$\ge\text{Mach }5$ (~6,174 km/h) while maneuvering aerodynamically *inside* Earth's atmosphere**. Flying at low-to-medium altitudes keeps them hidden behind Earth's curvature from terrestrial radars until late terminal flight, while unpredictable lateral pull-ups shatter the predictive intercept math of existing BMD computers.

### HGV vs. HCM - Competing Aerodynamic Architectures

- **Hypersonic Glide Vehicles (HGVs):** Boosted to 40–100 km altitude by a conventional rocket motor, the unpowered wedge-shaped lifting body detaches and **glides/skips along the upper stratosphere** at Mach 5–20 (converting altitude into horizontal range and executing abrupt cross-range turns). Extreme aerothermal friction ($\sim 2,000\text{–}3,000^\circ\text{C}$) requires advanced **Ultra-High Temperature Ceramics (UHTCs)** and carbon–carbon composites, while surrounding air ionizes into a **radio-opaque plasma sheath** that complicates terminal guidance, GPS reception, and mid-course data links.
- **Hypersonic Cruise Missiles (HCMs):** Powered continuously across atmospheric altitudes (20–30 km) by an air-breathing **scramjet engine** at Mach 5–8. Smaller, cheaper, and launchable from fighters, ships, or mobile TELs without a giant ballistic boost flash, HCMs face the supreme propulsion hurdle of sustaining stable supersonic combustion and preventing thermal wall meltdown across 15–20+ minutes of flight.

### India's Dual Hypersonic Track - LR-AShM (Nov 2024) & Scramjet HCM (May 2026)

- **Long-Range Hypersonic Missile / LR-AShM (November 16, 2024 Flight Trial):** From Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Island, DRDO's Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Missile Complex (Hyderabad) successfully flight-tested India's maiden **>1,500 km range Hypersonic Missile** designed to deliver varied payloads across all three Armed Forces. Publicly unveiled at the **January 26, 2026 Republic Day Parade** as the **Long-Range Anti-Ship Missile (LR-AShM)**, this two-stage solid-boosted quasi-ballistic / hypersonic glide vehicle peaks near **Mach 10** (~Mach 5 average across atmospheric skip-glide maneuvers), carrying an indigenous RF seeker to neutralize adversary aircraft carrier battle groups and destroyers deep across the Indian Ocean—placing India within a tiny global club (US, Russia, China) with tested operational-range hypersonic strike.
- **HSTDV → Actively Cooled Scramjet HCM (*Project Vishnu* / ET-LDHCM):** Building on the **September 7, 2020 HSTDV** flight demonstration (20 seconds of indigenous Mach 6 scramjet cruise), DRDO's DRDL Hyderabad cleared the decisive endurance hurdle for an operational scramjet **Extended Trajectory Long Duration Hypersonic Cruise Missile (ET-LDHCM, ~1,500 km, Mach 8)**—achieving a **>1,200-second (>20-minute) full-scale ground burn of an Actively Cooled Scramjet Combustor on May 9, 2026** utilizing indigenous endothermic liquid hydrocarbon fuel and ceramic Thermal Barrier Coatings.

## 4. Multi-Layered Ballistic Missile Defense (BMD) & Integrated Air Defense

No single interceptor can defend simultaneously against a Mach 20 exo-atmospheric ICBM warhead, a sea-skimming supersonic cruise missile, and a ₹50,000 quadcopter drone. India is therefore weaving an overlapping **five-tier, sensor-fused National Air & Missile Defense Architecture**—crystallized conceptually under **Mission Sudarshan Chakra** (announced August 15, 2025, targeting comprehensive nationwide multi-layered protection by **2035**).

### Indigenous Two-Phase Ballistic Missile Defense (BMD)

- **BMD Phase-I (2,000 km MRBM Threat — Operationalized):** A two-tier hit-to-kill shield providing >99% cumulative intercept probability via sequential engagement:
  1. **Outer Tier — PAD (*Prithvi Air Defence* / *Pradyumna*):** **Exo-atmospheric** interceptor destroying incoming warheads at **50–80 km altitude** outside the dense atmosphere.
  2. **Inner Tier — AAD (*Advanced Air Defence* / *Ashwin*):** **Endo-atmospheric** single-stage solid interceptor engaging leakers at **15–30 km altitude** via direct kinetic collision.
  - Both tiers rely on the indigenous **Swordfish Long-Range Tracking Radar (LRTR)** (evolved from Israel's EL/M-2080 *Green Pine*, upgraded from 600 km to **~1,500 km tracking range** for tiny ballistic targets).
- **BMD Phase-II — AD-1 & AD-2 (5,000 km IRBM/ICBM Threat):** Engineered against longer-range, faster regional ballistic threats and high-speed aircraft. **AD-1** is a two-stage solid interceptor equipped with thrust-vectoring and side-jet divert thrusters capable of both **low exo-atmospheric and endo-atmospheric** intercepts, complemented by the dedicated exo-atmospheric **AD-2**. On **July 24, 2024**, DRDO successfully flight-tested **Phase-II BMD (AD-1)** from ITR Chandipur against a live ballistic target launched from LC-IV Dhamra, proving end-to-end automated detection, tracking, and kill-chain coordination across distributed land- and sea-based radars.

### Long-Range SAMs - S-400 Triumf & DRDO *Project Kusha* (July 2026)

- **S-400 Triumf (*Sudarshan Chakra*):** Acquired from Russia under a $5.43 billion (2018) deal for five squadrons (three deployed by 2024–2025; final two slated by 2026–2027), the S-400 derives its technical superiority from **four layered missile families** fired from a single mobile launcher—short (40 km), medium (120 km), long (250 km), and **ultra-long (400 km 40N6E)**—backed by multi-band phased-array radars (600 km detection, 36 simultaneous engagements, rapid 5-minute shoot-and-scoot deployment, and high anti-stealth/EW resilience). Its combat pedigree alongside indigenous SAMs was validated decisively during **May 2025 Operation Sindoor** air-defense engagements.
- **DRDO *Project Kusha* (Indigenous 3-Tier LRSAM / ER-SAM — Maiden Flight Test July 23, 2026):** Conceived to eliminate foreign supply-chain/CAATSA vulnerability and serve as India's sovereign long-range pillar alongside S-400, *Project Kusha* (led by DRDO with BEL as lead integrator and domestic private partners) achieved its **successful maiden flight test on July 23, 2026** at ITR Chandipur, Odisha. Firing the **M1 interceptor** against a high-speed aerial target, the trial validated its dual-pulse solid propulsion, thrust-vector agility, and indigenous active RF seeker. *Project Kusha* fields three tiered mobile interceptors:
  - **M1 (~150 km):** Fighters, precision-guided munitions, cruise missiles, and tactical UAVs.
  - **M2 (~250 km):** Stealth fighters, stand-off bombers, and tactical ballistic missiles.
  - **M3 (~350–400 km):** High-value force multipliers (AWACS, mid-air refuellers, ISR aircraft) and high-speed maneuvering threats—targeting $\ge 80\%$ single-shot ($\ge 90\%$ salvo) kill probability.

### Medium, Short, Point Defense & the IACCS–Akashteer Battle Network

- **Barak-8 / MRSAM & LRSAM (70–100 km):** Co-developed by **DRDO and Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI)**; 360° vertically launched, dual-pulse active-radar SAM arming Indian Navy destroyers/frigates (LRSAM), IAF airbases, and Army regiments (MRSAM).
- **QRSAM (*Quick Reaction SAM*, 25–30 km):** Indigenous canisterized mobile shield engineered specifically for the Army's mechanized strike corps—capable of **search-on-the-move and track-on-the-move** with short halts to fire against low-flying helicopters, attack jets, and drones.
- **VSHORADS (0.25–6 km):** Indigenous fourth-generation **Man-Portable Air Defence System (MANPADS)** combining a dual-thrust solid motor, miniaturized Reaction Control System (RCS) for sharp turns, and an uncooled **Imaging Infrared (IIR)** seeker (completing user validation trials across **February–March 2026** against low-RCS drones and low-altitude aerial targets).
- **IACCS – Akashteer Automated Kill-Chain:** Hardware interceptors fail without sub-second sensor fusion. The IAF's **IACCS (*Integrated Air Command and Control System*)** networks all military radars (*Rohini*, *Arudhra*, *Swordfish*), civilian AAI radars, airborne AWACS (*Phalcon* & indigenous *Netra*), fighters, and SAM squadrons into a single real-time Recognized Air Picture (RAP). Complementing it at the tactical Army Air Defence layer, **Akashteer**—developed indigenously by **BEL** and combat-proven during **May 2025 Operation Sindoor** (with 100+ mobile control units inducted)—digitizes low-level radar feeds and integrates seamlessly with IACCS to automate weapon-to-target pairing across Akash, MRSAM, QRSAM, and guns while eliminating friendly-fire fratricide.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Ballistic vs. Cruise Total Inversion Trap (Prelims 2023 Repeat Zone):** **Ballistic missiles** (Agni, Prithvi) are **rocket-powered only during initial boost** and follow a high unpowered parabolic arc; **cruise missiles** (BrahMos, Nirbhay) stay **inside the atmosphere and are jet/ramjet-powered throughout flight**. Never swap Agni-V (solid-fuelled ICBM) with BrahMos (supersonic cruise missile).
> - **Solid vs. Liquid Propellant Traps:** All operational **Agni** missiles (I to V and Agni-P), **Pralay**, and **Kusha** are **solid-fuelled** (NOT liquid-fuelled—UPSC Prelims 2014 specifically trapped candidates by calling Agni-IV "liquid-propelled"). **Prithvi-I/II** and **Dhanush** are liquid-fuelled; **BrahMos** is a two-stage hybrid (**solid booster + liquid ramjet**). Note that Agni-V's *Post-Boost Vehicle (PBV) bus* uses a small liquid maneuvering engine, while all three main boost stages are solid!
> - **HGV vs. HCM Propulsion Trap:** **Hypersonic Glide Vehicles (HGVs)** are **unpowered** after rocket boost separation and glide aerodynamically in the upper atmosphere; **Hypersonic Cruise Missiles (HCMs)** are **continuously powered by air-breathing scramjets** at lower altitudes (~20–30 km).
> - **PAD vs. AAD Altitude Inversion:** Within BMD Phase-I, **PAD (*Pradyumna*)** is the **exo-atmospheric** upper tier (50–80 km), whereas **AAD (*Ashwin*)** is the **endo-atmospheric** lower tier (15–30 km). In Phase-II, **AD-1** handles *both* low exo-atmospheric and endo-atmospheric intercepts against 5,000 km threats.
> - **Project Kusha Tiers & Lineage:** *Project Kusha* (maiden test July 23, 2026) is an **indigenous DRDO 3-tier LRSAM** (**M1: 150 km, M2: 250 km, M3: 350–400 km**)—NOT a joint Indo-Russian derivative of the S-400, though it performs a comparable strategic role within *Mission Sudarshan Chakra*.
> - **Akash vs. Akash-NG Propulsion Shift:** Legacy *Akash* (25–30 km) used an **integral ramjet**; **Akash-NG** (70–80 km) drops the ramjet in favour of a **dual-pulse solid rocket motor** and canisterized vertical launch!
> - **SMART Misclassification Trap:** **SMART** is NOT an anti-ship cruise missile or an underwater-launched ballistic missile—it is a **supersonic rocket missile that air-drops a lightweight ASW torpedo (ALWT)** by parachute up to ~643–650 km away.
> - **SANT vs. Nag Seeker Distinction:** **Nag, HELINA/*Dhruvastra*, and MPATGM** use **Imaging Infrared (IIR)** seekers, whereas **SANT** (10–20 km stand-off variant) upgrades to a **Millimetre-Wave (MMW) active radar seeker** for Lock-On After Launch in dust/rain.
> - **BrahMos MTCR Range Nuance:** India's **June 2016 accession to the MTCR** (35th member) removed the 300 km Category-I export/cooperation ceiling on joint Indo-Russian BrahMos variants, legally enabling the 450 km and 800 km versions.
> - **IACCS vs. Akashteer Ownership:** **IACCS** is the **Indian Air Force's** overarching nationwide command network; **Akashteer** (built by BEL) is the **Indian Army Air Defence's** tactical control-and-reporting system networked seamlessly *into* IACCS.
