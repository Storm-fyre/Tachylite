# Chapter 16 - Telecommunications, 5G and 6G, Direct-to-Device Satellite Broadband, Subsea Cables and Telecom Act 2023

Modern telecommunications operates as a three-layer planetary fabric: terrestrial cellular and optical-fiber grids delivering dense urban throughput, subsea glass arteries carrying intercontinental financial and AI traffic, and non-terrestrial satellite constellations closing topographical dead zones. Engineering choices across this stack are governed by unforgiving electromagnetic trade-offs between bandwidth and propagation distance, while strategic governance hinges on spectrum law, vendor sovereignty, and undersea resilience.

---

## Electromagnetic Refresher, Wireless Propagation Physics and Cellular Evolution

### Spectrum Mechanics and Cellular Generations (1G to 4G VoLTE)

`[BRIEF REFRESHER]` Electromagnetic waves propagate at $c = \lambda\nu$ with photon energy $E = h\nu$. All radio-frequency ($3\text{ kHz}$–$300\text{ GHz}$) and optical-wireless communications lie strictly in the **non-ionizing** spectrum ($E \ll 10\text{ eV}$), incapable of breaking covalent DNA bonds and regulated solely against tissue thermal heating via Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) limits. Cellular standards evolved from **1G** (analog voice) to **2G** (digital GSM/CDMA voice + SMS), **3G** (UMTS/HSPA mobile data), and **4G LTE** (Long-Term Evolution — an all-IP packet-switched broadband network); early LTE networks fell back to 2G/3G circuit-switched channels for calls until **VoLTE (Voice over LTE)** carried voice natively as prioritized IP packets over LTE's IP Multimedia Subsystem (IMS), allowing simultaneous HD voice and high-speed data.

### The Fundamental Wireless Trade-Off - Shannon Capacity vs. Friis Attenuation

Two coupled equations dictate every spectrum auction and network topology decision in 5G, 6G, and satellite broadband:

$$C = B \log_2(1 + \text{SNR}), \qquad \text{FSPL} = \left(\frac{4\pi d f}{c}\right)^2$$

The **Shannon–Hartley Theorem** shows that peak channel capacity $C$ scales linearly with available channel bandwidth $B$ and only logarithmically with signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). Because sub-1 GHz radio bands are narrow and heavily crowded, unlocking multi-gigabit speeds forces telecom systems upward into wider mid-band ($3.5\text{--}7\text{ GHz}$), millimetre-wave ($24\text{--}100\text{ GHz}$), and sub-Terahertz ($>100\text{ GHz}$) spectrum.

However, the **Friis Free-Space Path Loss (FSPL)** relation shows that received power drops with the square of carrier frequency $f^2$ (for fixed antenna aperture) while building, foliage, and rain absorption escalate sharply at shorter wavelengths. Consequently, wireless architecture faces an immutable inverse trade-off: **lower frequencies travel farther and penetrate concrete walls, whereas higher frequencies carry vastly more data over much shorter ranges**—mandating dense arrays of small cells, Massive MIMO beamforming, and deep fiber backhaul.

## 5G Architecture, Open RAN, Indigenous BSNL Stack and the Bharat 6G Frontier

### 5G Core Pillars, Spectrum Layers and NSA vs. SA Network Slicing

Standardized under **ITU-R IMT-2020**, fifth-generation wireless (**5G New Radio / NR**) was engineered around three distinct performance verticals rather than raw smartphone download speed alone:

- **eMBB (Enhanced Mobile Broadband):** Peak multi-gigabit speeds ($1\text{--}20\text{ Gbps}$) supporting 4K/8K streaming, Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) home broadband, and cloud XR.
- **uRLLC (Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communications):** Sub-millisecond ($<1\text{ ms}$) radio air-interface latency with $99.999\%$ packet reliability for autonomous V2X transport, telesurgery, and synchronized factory robotics.
- **mMTC (Massive Machine-Type Communications):** Ultra-dense low-power IoT connectivity scaling up to **$10^6$ devices per square kilometre** for smart grids, logistics trackers, and precision agriculture.

India's commercial 5G rollout (launched October 1, 2022) expanded to **99.9% of districts** (85% population coverage) with **5.08 lakh 5G Base Transceiver Stations (BTS)** deployed by December 2025, lifting India's median mobile download speed from 10.71 Mbps (2019) to **131.47 Mbps** and total internet connections past **100 crore**. Networks combine a **three-layer spectrum cake**: **Low-band** ($700\text{ MHz}$) for rural reach and indoor penetration; **Mid-band / C-band** ($3.3\text{--}3.67\text{ GHz}$, reinforced by upper $6\text{ GHz}$ under NFAP 2025) as the urban workhorse balancing capacity and radius; and **High-band mmWave** ($26\text{ GHz}$) for ultra-dense stadium, airport, and FWA hotspots.

Architecturally, two 5G deployment modes differ sharply in capability:
- **Non-Standalone (NSA):** Anchors 5G NR radio towers onto a legacy **4G Evolved Packet Core (EPC)** (initially used by Airtel for rapid rollout). While delivering fast eMBB speeds, NSA **cannot support true uRLLC or end-to-end Network Slicing**.
- **Standalone (SA):** Pairs 5G NR radios with a native cloud-based **5G Core (5GC)** (deployed nationwide by Jio True 5G and in BSNL's indigenous 5G FWA pilots). Only SA unlocks **Network Slicing**—dynamically partitioning one physical cellular network into multiple logically isolated virtual networks with guaranteed Service-Level Agreements (SLAs), ensuring an emergency disaster-response or industrial-robotics slice never degrades during civilian video congestion.

Two radio-layer physics breakthroughs sustain 5G capacity: **Massive MIMO** (deploying 64T64R or 128T128R antenna arrays at a single tower to serve dozens of devices simultaneously on the same frequency via spatial multiplexing) and **Active Beamforming** (electronically steering phase-shifted constructive RF lobes straight toward individual moving handsets, defeating high-frequency path loss while slashing inter-cell interference).

### Open RAN and BSNL's Indigenous Swadeshi 4G/5G Stack

Legacy cellular infrastructure locked operators into monolithic proprietary stacks dominated by a handful of foreign vendors (Ericsson, Nokia, Huawei, ZTE, Samsung), creating severe supply-chain and national-security risks. **Open RAN (O-RAN)** breaks this vertical lock-in by disaggregating base stations into modular Radio Units (RU), Distributed Units (DU), and Centralized Units (CU) running cloud-native software over standardized open interfaces—allowing operators to mix commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) servers with indigenous software.

India achieved a landmark strategic breakthrough on **September 27, 2025**, when Prime Minister Narendra Modi formally launched **BSNL's fully indigenous, cloud-native, 5G-ready 4G stack** alongside **~98,000 to 1 lakh Swadeshi telecom towers**:

- **Tripartite Indigenous Architecture:** The **Core Network** was developed by **C-DOT** (Centre for Development of Telematics), the **Radio Access Network (RAN)** hardware/software by **Tejas Networks**, and end-to-end system integration by **Tata Consultancy Services (TCS)**, deployed by **BSNL**.
- **Strategic Significance:** This milestone made India only the **fifth nation globally** (joining Sweden, Finland, South Korea, and China) capable of designing, manufacturing, and deploying a sovereign end-to-end commercial cellular mobility stack—upgradable seamlessly to 5G via software and targeted for export to Global South nations. Complementing this push, DoT's **Telecom PLI Scheme** (₹12,195 crore outlay) crossed ₹96,240 crore in cumulative equipment sales by late 2025.

### Bharat 6G Vision (2030), ITU IMT-2030 and Frontier 6G Physics

Unveiled on **March 23, 2023**, India's **Bharat 6G Vision** targets commercial 6G deployment and **10% of global 6G IPR/patents by 2030**, steered by the industry-academia **Bharat 6G Alliance (B6GA)** (launched July 2023; expanded to **90 member organisations** by July 2026) and supported by **100 academic 5G Use Case Labs**, the **Telecom Technology Development Fund (TTDF)** (funding a 6G THz Testbed and Advanced Optical Communications Testbed worth ₹240.51 crore), and DoT's **TDIP** standards-participation scheme.

Crucially, India's diplomatic engagement shaped the **International Telecommunication Union's (ITU-R) IMT-2030 (6G) framework**, successfully embedding **"Ubiquitous Connectivity"** (bridging rural/remote digital divides) as one of 6G's six official global usage scenarios alongside sustainability and interoperability. At the **3rd International Bharat 6G Symposium (October 2026)**, 3GPP shifted from conceptual studies into formal specification drafting ahead of WRC-27 and Release 21.

Three physical-layer innovations distinguish 6G from 5G:
- **Sub-THz / Terahertz ($100\text{ GHz}\text{--}3\text{ THz}$) Spectrum:** Unlocks gigahertz-wide contiguous channels for holographic communications and terabit wireless links, while confronting severe oxygen/water-vapour molecular absorption windows and strictly line-of-sight propagation.
- **ISAC (Integrated Sensing and Communication):** Uses the *same* 6G radio/optical waveforms and base-station hardware simultaneously for data transfer and centimetre-precision **ambient RF radar sensing**—mapping gesture, traffic obstacles, crowd density, drone intrusions, and digital twins without separate radar hardware or cameras.
- **RIS (Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces):** Ultra-thin, near-passive programmable **metamaterial** panels mounted on building walls or windows that dynamically adjust electromagnetic reflection phases to bounce obstructed mmWave/THz beams cleanly around corners into shadow zones at minimal power cost.

| Technical Dimension | 5G (ITU-R IMT-2020) | 6G (ITU-R IMT-2030 — Target 2030) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Spectrum Bands** | Low ($<1\text{ GHz}$), Mid ($3.3\text{--}7\text{ GHz}$), High mmWave ($24\text{--}52\text{ GHz}$) | Existing 5G bands + Upper Mid-band ($7\text{--}15\text{ GHz}$ FR3) + **Sub-THz / THz ($100\text{ GHz}\text{--}3\text{ THz}$)** & Optical wireless |
| **Peak Data Rate** | Up to $20\text{ Gbps}$ downlink | Up to **$1\text{ Tbps}$** ($50\times$–$100\times$ peak throughput) |
| **Air-Interface Latency** | $\sim 1\text{ ms}$ (uRLLC target) | **$0.1\text{ ms}$ ($100\,\mu\text{s}$)** deterministic tactile/holographic latency |
| **Core Architecture & Radio** | Cloud-native SA 5GC, Network Slicing, Massive MIMO, Beamforming | **AI-Native Air Interface**, Ultra-Massive MIMO, **RIS (Metamaterial Surfaces)**, zero-energy ambient IoT |
| **Network Scope & Sensing** | Terrestrial cellular primary; NTN added late (Release 17/18); communications only | **Native Terrestrial + Non-Terrestrial (Satellite/HAPS) 3D Integration** + **ISAC (Integrated Sensing & Communication)** |
| **Global / Indian Anchor** | 99.9% Indian districts covered (5.08 lakh BTS, Dec 2025); BSNL indigenous 4G/5G-ready stack | **Bharat 6G Vision (2030)**, **B6GA (90 members, Jul 2026)**, **10% global patent target**, ITU "Ubiquitous Connectivity" scenario |

## High-Capacity Optical Fiber, BharatNet Phase-III, Wi-Fi 7, Li-Fi and Free-Space Optics

### Optical Fiber Physics (DWDM) and BharatNet Phase-III (ABP)

Every wireless 5G/6G tower ultimately depends on terrestrial **optical fiber backhaul**—hair-thin ultra-pure silica strands guiding infrared laser pulses via **Total Internal Reflection** with negligible attenuation ($\sim 0.18\text{ dB/km}$ in the $1550\text{ nm}$ C-band) and complete immunity to electromagnetic interference. Modern long-haul trunks multiply capacity through **Dense Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (DWDM)**, injecting 80 to 160+ slightly different laser wavelengths simultaneously down a single fiber core and amplifying all channels purely optically via **Erbium-Doped Fiber Amplifiers (EDFAs)** without costly optical-electrical conversion—scaling single-fiber throughput into tens of terabits per second.

India's optical fiber footprint more than doubled from 19.35 lakh route km (2019) to **42.36 lakh route km** by December 2025, reinforced by the **National Broadband Mission (NBM) 2.0** (launched January 17, 2025) and the **Call Before u Dig (CBuD)** portal under PM Gati Shakti to prevent accidental excavation fiber cuts.

At the rural backbone sits the **Amended BharatNet Programme (ABP / Phase-III)**—approved in **August 2023** with an outlay of **₹1,39,579 crore (~₹1.39 lakh crore)**, funded as national open-access assets under **Digital Bharat Nidhi (DBN)** with **BSNL** as Project Management Agency (completion targeted by 2028):
- **Engineering Overhaul over Phases I & II:** Earlier phases suffered crippling downtime because linear spur cables collapsed whenever a single trench broke. Phase-III upgrades to a **resilient Ring Topology** backed by **IP-MPLS routers** (10 Gbps at Block level, 1 Gbps at Gram Panchayat level) under a **10-year Design, Build, Operate, and Maintain (DBOM)** contract with Remote Fibre Monitoring and $>98\%$ uptime mandates.
- **Scope & Last-Mile Economics:** Extends fiber to all **2.64–2.65 lakh Gram Panchayats** (~2.15 lakh GPs already connected under Phases I & II) plus **~3.8 lakh non-GP villages on demand**, deploying local Village Level Entrepreneurs as **BharatNet Udyamis (BNUs)** to deliver **1.5 crore rural FTTH connections** (minimum 25 Mbps) and saturate all rural government secondary schools and PHCs.
- **Governance Caution:** **CAG Audit Report No. 19 of 2026** flagged fund-utilization and diversion concerns in BharatNet implementation, underscoring the need to ring-fence DBN rural-broadband funds cleanly from BSNL's commercial revival capital.

### Wi-Fi 7, India's 6 GHz Spectrum Split, Li-Fi and Free-Space Optics

Short-range and optical-wireless links complement cellular and fiber networks across three distinct physical layers:

- **Wi-Fi 7 (IEEE 802.11be) & India's 2026 6 GHz Delicensing:** Wi-Fi 7 pushes unlicensed wireless LANs to theoretical peaks near **46 Gbps** through three leaps: **320 MHz ultra-wide channels** (double Wi-Fi 6E's 160 MHz), denser **4096-QAM** modulation, and **Multi-Link Operation (MLO)**—allowing a single device to transmit and receive concurrently across $2.4\text{ GHz}$, $5\text{ GHz}$, and $6\text{ GHz}$ bands for deterministic low latency. Resolving a fierce global spectrum tug-of-war between Wi-Fi advocates and cellular telcos, India struck a pragmatic split: DoT **delicensed the lower 6 GHz band (`5925–6425 MHz`, 500 MHz)** for indoor **Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7** (effective **January 2026**), while earmarking the **upper 6 GHz band (`6425–7125 MHz`, 700 MHz)** for licensed **5G-Advanced / 6G IMT** services under **NFAP 2025**—amplifying public Wi-Fi schemes like **PM-WANI**.
- **Li-Fi / Visible Light Communication (VLC — IEEE 802.11bb):** Uses imperceptibly fast intensity modulation of standard LEDs across the **$375\text{--}780\text{ nm}$ visible light spectrum** (10,000$\times$ wider unlicensed bandwidth than all RF combined) to transmit gigabit-class wireless data to photodiode receivers. Key physics properties (frequently tested by UPSC): **zero electromagnetic interference (EMI)**—making it safe inside hospital ICUs, nuclear control rooms, and aircraft cabins; **inherent physical security** because light cannot pass through opaque walls; and strictly **short-range, line-of-sight indoor operation** (not long-range wireless).
- **Free-Space Optics (FSO):** Projects narrow, invisible near-infrared laser beams ($850\text{ nm}$ or $1550\text{ nm}$) point-to-point through open atmosphere (typically $1\text{--}10\text{ km}$) for high-bandwidth, licence-free backhaul across rivers, valleys, or urban rooftops. Terrestrial FSO degrades severely under dense fog, heavy tropical rain, and thermal atmospheric scintillation—limitations that vanish completely in the vacuum of space, where FSO becomes the backbone of **Optical Inter-Satellite Links (OISLs)**.

## Non-Terrestrial Networks, Direct-to-Device Satcom, Subsea Cables and Geospatial Infrastructure

### GEO vs. MEO vs. LEO Mega-Constellations and India's Satcom Ecosystem

Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN) bypass rugged Himalayan terrain, island archipelagos, and maritime dead zones by beaming broadband from orbit. Because radio waves travel at finite speed $c$, orbital altitude imposes an unbreakable physics floor on two-way **propagation latency** ($t_{\text{RTT}} \approx 4h/c$ including user-to-satellite-to-gateway hops) alongside a direct trade-off against coverage footprint:

| Dimension | Terrestrial Fiber / 5G | GEO Satcom (Geostationary) | MEO Satcom (Medium Earth Orbit) | LEO Mega-Constellations (Low Earth Orbit) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Altitude** | Ground surface ($0\text{ km}$) | **$35,786\text{ km}$** (equatorial plane) | **$2,000\text{--}20,000\text{ km}$** (e.g., SES O3b at $\sim 8,000\text{ km}$) | **$340\text{--}1,200\text{ km}$** (typically $500\text{--}600\text{ km}$) |
| **Round-Trip Latency** | $1\text{--}15\text{ ms}$ | **$500\text{--}700\text{ ms}$** (unsuitable for real-time voice/gaming/HFT) | **$120\text{--}150\text{ ms}$** (suitable for enterprise cloud & cellular backhaul) | **$20\text{--}45\text{ ms}$** (rivaling terrestrial broadband) |
| **Constellation Scale & Coverage** | Dense towers / trenched fiber; uneconomic in sparse terrain | **3–4 satellites** cover nearly entire Earth (excluding extreme poles); stationary dish | **10–40 satellites** for global equatorial/mid-latitude band | **Hundreds to 10,000+ satellites** moving at $\sim 7.5\text{ km/s}$; requires electronically steered phased-array dishes & rapid beam handover |
| **Key Technical / Economic Trade-Off** | Highest aggregate capacity (Tbps/km²); vulnerable to floods, digging, low rural ARPU | Huge footprint, long 15-yr lifespan; severe latency & limited spot-beam throughput | Balanced throughput-to-constellation cost; lower debris burden than mega-LEO | Low latency + **Optical Inter-Satellite Links (OISLs)** routing laser data in vacuum faster than glass fiber ($c$ vs $c/1.47$); short 5-yr satellite life, high orbital debris & astronomy light-pollution risk |
| **Indian Deployment Status (Nov 2026)** | 42.36 lakh km OFC; 5.08 lakh 5G BTS | ISRO/NSIL HTS (**GSAT-11, GSAT-N2**); Hughes India (5,000+ NE/Ladakh GPs) | **JioSpaceFiber** (Reliance Jio + Luxembourg's **SES O3b mPOWER** — India's 1st gigabit satcom service) | **Eutelsat OneWeb** (Airtel-backed; GMPCS + IN-SPACe approved, commercial underway); **Starlink** (GMPCS Jun 2025 + IN-SPACe Gen1 Jul 2025; awaiting final security clearance & spectrum); **Amazon Kuiper** (applying) |

A key architectural leap inside modern LEO constellations (Starlink Gen2/3, Amazon Kuiper) is the deployment of **Optical Inter-Satellite Links (OISLs)**—vacuum laser relays routing packets directly from satellite to satellite across oceans without bouncing down to intermediate terrestrial gateways. Because light travels roughly **31% faster in vacuum than inside silica fiber** (refractive index $n \approx 1.47$), long-haul transoceanic OISL hops can theoretically beat subsea fiber latency for niche low-volume financial data—though satellites can never match subsea fiber's petabit-scale bulk throughput.

As confirmed in a **Lok Sabha reply (August 5, 2026)**, exactly **three companies** hold India's Unified Licence with **GMPCS (Global Mobile Personal Communication by Satellite)** authorization: **OneWeb India Communications**, **Jio Satellite Communications**, and **Starlink Satellite Communications** (which secured IN-SPACe Gen1 authorization on **July 8, 2025**, valid until July 2030, while awaiting final central security clearance and spectrum assignment as of October–November 2026). Alongside foreign constellations, Reliance Jio has proposed an indigenous 1,600-satellite broadband constellation to fortify sovereign resilience.

### 3GPP Direct-to-Device (D2D) Satellite Connectivity

Traditional satcom requires bulky external dish terminals. **Direct-to-Device (D2D)** technology links **unmodified, off-the-shelf smartphones and IoT sensors** straight to orbiting satellites in cellular dead zones, standardized by **3GPP Release 17** (baseline NB-IoT and NR NTN messaging), **Release 18** (5G-Advanced NTN mobility), **Release 19** (regenerative gNB payloads in space), and **Release 20** (native NB-NTN voice calling approved June 2025).

Two spectrum architectures compete globally:
- **Mobile Satellite Service (MSS) Spectrum (L-band / S-band):** Uses globally harmonized satellite bands requiring NTN-enabled smartphone modems (e.g., Apple–Globalstar Emergency SOS, Skylo NB-NTN, and Viasat/Inmarsat). Notably, at **IMC 2024 (October 2024)**, **BSNL and Viasat** demonstrated India's first **3GPP Release 17 two-way D2D satellite messaging** on a commercial Android smartphone over an Inmarsat I-4 **GEO L-band satellite**, proving that GEO links can support narrowband emergency/IoT D2D alongside LEO.
- **Terrestrial Cellular Spectrum Sharing / Supplemental Coverage from Space (SCS):** Uses partner MNOs' existing sub-3 GHz cellular frequencies to talk directly to unmodified 4G/5G handsets via massive orbiting phased-array antennas compensating for Doppler shifts and tiny handset radios (e.g., SpaceX **Starlink Direct-to-Cell** and **AST SpaceMobile BlueBird**).

Physics imposes clear realism: an ordinary smartphone carries a low-gain omnidirectional antenna and $< 0.2\text{ W}$ transmit power, preventing signals from penetrating urban roofs. D2D is therefore a life-saving outdoor **"not-spot" complement** (oceans, Himalayan border patrols, cyclones, rural highways), not a replacement for terrestrial 5G towers.

### Subsea Cables - Global Arteries, Landing Vulnerabilities and Sabotage Risks

While satellites capture public imagination, **over 95% of all intercontinental internet traffic, cloud AI data flows, and $10+ trillion in daily SWIFT financial settlements** traverse armoured fiber-optic **subsea cables** scarcely thicker than a garden hose laid across abyssal ocean floors. Satellite networks cannot even carry 1–2% of this global volume.

India's subsea ecosystem faces **four compounding strategic vulnerabilities** highlighted by TRAI and maritime security assessments in 2025–2026:

- **Extreme Geographic Landing Concentration:** India hosts **17 to 19 international subsea cables** across ~14–17 Cable Landing Stations (CLSs) clustered inside just **five coastal cities**—**Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Tuticorin, and Thiruvananthapuram**. **Mumbai alone handles roughly 95% of India's international bandwidth**, with at least **13 intercontinental cables converging along a single ~6 km stretch of Versova beach**, creating an acute single point of failure vulnerable to cyclones, ship anchors, or targeted sabotage.
- **Zero Indigenous Cable Repair Ships:** India currently possesses **zero Indian-flagged or Indian-owned submarine cable repair vessels**. Indian waters depend on two foreign maintenance consortia based in Singapore and Dubai (nearest cable depot at **Galle, Sri Lanka**), dragging repair turnarounds—worsened by multi-agency customs/Navy/Home clearances—to **four to five months**. Recognizing this gap, DoT opened inter-ministerial deliberations in **August 2026** with DG Shipping, the Shipping Ministry, and the Indian Navy to develop domestic repair vessels, alongside proposals to designate subsea cables as **Critical National Infrastructure (CNI)** and deploy **Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS)** along fibers to detect trawlers or underwater tampering in real time.
- **Maritime Chokepoints & Grey-Zone Sabotage:** Most Europe–India cables funnel through the narrow, shallow **Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea** and **Strait of Hormuz** corridors, exposed repeatedly by the 2024 *Rubymar* anchor cuts and 2026 West Asian maritime hostilities (which delayed final onshore joins for the **2Africa Pearls** Gulf branch). Parallel anchor-drag sabotage events in the Baltic Sea and Taiwan Strait have elevated subsea cable protection into a core Quad and Indo-Pacific naval priority.
- **Capacity Expansion & Route Diversification (2024–2026):** Alongside domestic island cables (**CANI** linking Chennai to Andaman & Nicobar and **KLI** linking Kochi to Lakshadweep), mega-capacity international systems are transforming India into an Indo-Pacific data hub: **2Africa Pearls**, **India–Asia–Xpress (IAX)**, **India–Europe–Xpress (IEX)**, **MIST**, **Blue-Raman**, **SEA-ME-WE 6**, Google's planned Chennai–Visakhapatnam subsea corridor (anchoring its $15 billion Visakhapatnam AI data centre), and **Meta's *Project Waterworth***—reaffirmed at the February 2026 India AI Impact Summit as a **50,000 km**, 24-fiber-pair deep-sea cable connecting India, the US, Brazil, and South Africa around southern oceans to bypass Red Sea and Hormuz geopolitical chokepoints.

### National Geospatial Policy, 2022 and Spatial Infrastructure

Notified by the **Department of Science and Technology (DST)** on **December 28, 2022** (replacing the restrictive National Map Policy, 2005), the **National Geospatial Policy (NGP) 2022** is a 13-year liberalizing blueprint consolidating India's 2021 deregulation that permitted Indian private entities to collect, generate, and sell high-resolution geospatial data freely without prior government clearance (retaining only a tight negative list for sensitive defence installations).

Institutionally, NGP 2022 establishes the **Geospatial Data Promotion and Development Committee (GDPDC)** as apex coordinator, supported by the **National Geospatial Data Registry (NGDR)**, a **Unified Geospatial Interface (UGI)**, Survey of India's **CORS (Continuously Operating Reference Stations)** network delivering real-time centimetre-level RTK positioning nationwide, **Operation Dronagiri** (pilot launched November 13, 2024 across five states demonstrating geospatial applications in agriculture, logistics, and livelihoods), and the **National Geospatial Mission (NGM)** launched in **Union Budget 2025–26** to modernize land records and PM Gati Shakti foundational layers.

Its phased national milestones establish crisp technical benchmarks:
- **By 2025:** Complete a **high-accuracy national Geoid model** (true mean sea-level reference surface essential for vertical height accuracy) alongside NGDR/UGI architecture.
- **By 2030:** Deliver nationwide high-resolution topographical mapping ($5\text{--}10\text{ cm}$ accuracy in urban/rural areas; $50\text{ cm}\text{--}1\text{ m}$ in forests/wastelands), a high-accuracy **Digital Elevation Model (DEM)** ($25\text{ cm}$ in plains, $1\text{--}3\text{ m}$ in hills/mountains), and a **Geospatial Knowledge Infrastructure (GKI)**.
- **By 2035:** Map high-resolution **bathymetric data** (inland waterways and shallow/deep-sea ocean floor), map **sub-surface urban utilities** (water, gas, fiber) across major cities, and build a **National Digital Twin** (3D interactive replicas) of India's major cities and towns.

## The Telecommunications Act 2023 - Statutory Overhaul, 2024-2026 Rules and Satcom Spectrum

### Legislative Consolidation, Authorization Regime and Digital Bharat Nidhi

Assented on **December 24, 2023** (Act No. 44 of 2023) and brought into force in phases beginning **June 26, 2024** and **July 5, 2024**, the **Telecommunications Act, 2023** repeals two colonial-era statutes—the **Indian Telegraph Act, 1885** and the **Indian Wireless Telegraphy Act, 1933** (note: the *Telegraph Wires (Unlawful Possession) Act, 1950* had already been repealed separately via the Repealing and Amending Act, 2023)—and amends the **TRAI Act, 1997** (notably opening TRAI Chairperson eligibility to senior private-sector corporate executives).

Its operational pillars modernize telecom governance across five dimensions:

- **Streamlined "Authorization" Framework (Section 3):** Replaces over 100 fragmented, legacy licensing regimes with unified **Authorizations** issued by the Central Government to provide telecom services, establish/operate telecom networks, or possess radio equipment. DoT operationalized this transition through the **Telecommunications (Authorisation for Provision of Principal Telecommunication Services) Rules, 2026** (notified **June 23, 2026**), collapsing legacy licences into five streamlined service categories.
- **Digital Bharat Nidhi (DBN) (Sections 24–26):** Renames the **Universal Service Obligation Fund (USOF, est. 2002)** into a **non-lapsable Digital Bharat Nidhi** (Rules notified August 30, 2024), critically expanding its legal mandate beyond rural tower subsidies to fund **telecom R&D, pilot projects, regulatory sandboxes, and emerging 6G/indigenous technologies** (such as TTDF).
- **Standardized Right of Way (RoW):** Establishes uniform, non-discriminatory, time-bound RoW procedures across public and private property (Rules notified September 17, 2024, effective January 1, 2025), clarifying that telecom infrastructure laid on private/public land does not confer property ownership rights to the operator.
- **Biometric SIM Verification, SIM Caps & Spam Protection:** Mandates **verifiable biometric identification (Section 3(7))** for issuing new telecom connections to curb mule SIMs and cyber fraud. Caps individual SIM holdings at **9 SIMs** generally (**6 SIMs** in Jammu & Kashmir, Assam, and the North-East; penalties of ₹50,000 for first offence and ₹2 lakh for subsequent offences, with up to 3 years imprisonment / ₹50 lakh fine for fraudulent SIM acquisition), and gives statutory force to **prior user consent** and **Do Not Disturb (DND)** registers against unsolicited commercial spam.
- **Emergency Interception, Suspension & Cybersecurity (Sections 20–22):** Empowers Central or State Governments (via Union/State Home Secretaries under the November 2024 Rules) to take temporary possession of networks, **intercept/disclose messages**, or order **temporary suspension of telecom services** (internet shutdowns) during a public emergency, public safety threat, or in the interest of sovereignty, defence, security of State, friendly relations, public order, or incitement to an offence—while expressly exempting press messages of accredited correspondents from interception unless prohibited under Section 20(2)(b). Companion **Telecom Cyber Security Rules, 2024** (amended October 2025 to introduce **Telecom Identifier User Entities [TIUEs]**, extending anti-fraud security rules to digital/OTT platforms that use mobile numbers as user identifiers, alongside DoT's November 2025 SIM-binding directive) and **Critical Telecommunication Infrastructure (CTI) Rules, 2024** tighten cyber resilience—though legal scholars continue flagging tension with *Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India* (2020) proportionality norms and potential weakening of end-to-end encryption.

### First Schedule Statutory Mandate - Administrative Allocation of Satellite Broadband Spectrum

Section 4 of the Telecommunications Act, 2023 establishes **auction as the default mode** of spectrum assignment for terrestrial commercial networks, while carving out a binding statutory exception under its **First Schedule** mandating **administrative assignment (non-auction)** for specified public-interest and technical categories—explicitly including **satellite-based services** (GMPCS, Commercial VSAT, MSS, FSS, In-Flight & Maritime Connectivity), national security/defence, radio astronomy, disaster management, and BSNL/MTNL/public broadcasting.

**Why Administrative Assignment Is Technically Necessary (Physics vs. Economics):**
Unlike terrestrial mobile bands—where exclusive geographic spectrum blocks are essential to prevent adjacent cell towers from jamming one another—**satellite spectrum is non-exclusive and globally shared** across overlapping orbital beams coordinated via the ITU. Auctioning exclusive national blocks to a single bidder would artificially lock out rival constellations, waste orbital spectral efficiency, and conflict with international ITU radio coordination rules.

**The 2025–2026 Policy Debate & TRAI Pricing Framework:**
Administrative allocation does *not* mean free spectrum. On **May 9, 2025**, **TRAI** recommended assigning satellite spectrum administratively for **5 years** (extendable by 2 years) at an annual spectrum usage charge of **4% of Adjusted Gross Revenue (AGR)** (minimum ₹3,500 per MHz), plus an additional **₹500 annual levy per urban NGSO subscriber** while exempting rural/remote users to protect rural affordability and avoid undercutting terrestrial telcos who paid thousands of crores upfront in spectrum auctions. Subsequent inter-ministerial deliberations (Digital Communications Commission / DoT in 2026) weighed raising the fee to 5% AGR (with remote-area relief to 4%) while dropping the ₹500 urban-user levy; final Cabinet pricing ratification and central security clearances for commercial Starlink/satcom launch remained pending as of late 2026. Balancing transparent price discovery against *2G Spectrum Judgment (2012)* Article 14 fairness, terrestrial level-playing-field concerns, and lawful interception sovereignty over foreign LEO gateways remains India's defining telecom-regulation challenge.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **LTE vs. VoLTE Trap (UPSC Prelims 2019 Direct Hit):** Neither LTE nor VoLTE is a 3G technology—both belong to **4G**. Nor is VoLTE "voice-only": **VoLTE carries voice natively as IP data packets over LTE's 4G data network**, enabling simultaneous HD voice and high-speed data.
> - **VLC / Li-Fi Range & EMI Trap (UPSC Prelims 2016 & 2020 Direct Hits):** Visible Light Communication (**Li-Fi**) uses **$375\text{--}780\text{ nm}$** wavelengths, transmits data far faster than Bluetooth/Wi-Fi, and causes **zero electromagnetic interference (EMI)**—but it is strictly **short-range indoor wireless**, NOT long-range optical wireless! (Long-range outdoor optical links use infrared laser **FSO**, not indoor Li-Fi).
> - **NFC vs. Short-Range Devices (UPSC Prelims 2015 & 2022 Direct Hits):** **NFC** uses contactless electromagnetic radio fields with encryption, but operates only across a **few centimetres (~4–10 cm)**—NOT a metre! By contrast, **CCTV, RFID, and WLAN (Wi-Fi)** are all classified as **Short-Range Devices (SRDs)**.
> - **5G NSA vs. SA Network Slicing Trap:** True end-to-end **Network Slicing** and sub-millisecond **uRLLC** require a **5G Standalone (SA)** cloud-native 5G Core—they cannot be delivered fully on **Non-Standalone (NSA)** networks anchored to 4G EPC cores.
> - **India's 6 GHz Split Trap (2025–2026):** India did **not** delicense the entire 6 GHz band for Wi-Fi nor auction all of it for 5G/6G—it **delicensed the lower 6 GHz (`5925–6425 MHz`)** for indoor **Wi-Fi 6E/7** (effective Jan 2026) while keeping **upper 6 GHz (`6425–7125 MHz`)** for licensed **IMT (5G/6G)** under NFAP 2025.
> - **BSNL Indigenous 4G Stack Attribution Trap:** Core network = **C-DOT**; Radio Access Network (RAN) = **Tejas Networks**; System Integration = **TCS**; Operator = **BSNL**. Do not swap C-DOT (Core) with Tejas Networks (RAN)!
> - **JioSpaceFiber Orbit & Satcom Spectrum Traps:** **JioSpaceFiber** (with SES O3b mPOWER) operates in **MEO (~8,000 km)**, NOT LEO (unlike Starlink and OneWeb). Under the **Telecommunications Act, 2023 First Schedule**, satellite broadband spectrum is assigned **administratively** (non-auction)—NOT auctioned—yet administrative allocation is **NOT free** (it carries statutory annual AGR spectrum charges).
> - **Telecom Act 2023 Repeal & DBN Traps:** The Act formally repealed the **Indian Telegraph Act, 1885** and **Indian Wireless Telegraphy Act, 1933** (the 1950 Telegraph Wires Act was already repealed separately by the Repealing & Amending Act, 2023). **Digital Bharat Nidhi** (replacing USOF) is **non-lapsable** and legally funds **telecom R&D and pilot projects**—not merely rural towers.
> - **Subsea Cable vs. Satellite Bandwidth Trap:** **Subsea fiber cables**—NOT LEO satellite mega-constellations—carry **>95% of intercontinental internet and financial traffic**. India currently owns **zero Indian-flagged subsea cable repair vessels**.

### Verified UPSC Mains PYQs (2014-2026) and Prelims Anchors

*Note on Mains vs. Prelims Distribution:* Between 2014 and 2026, UPSC has **not yet asked a standalone direct GS Paper 3 Mains question** specifically on 5G/6G architecture, LEO satellite broadband, subsea cables, or the Telecommunications Act, 2023 (the closest Mains touchpoint was **Mains 2026 GS1** *"Analyze the role of satellite-based technologies in achieving climate-smart agriculture and food security in India"* and broader digital-infrastructure/privacy themes). By contrast, UPSC **Prelims** tests wireless and communication physics almost cyclically—making the **Telecommunications Act 2023 (satcom administrative allocation)**, **subsea cable chokepoints**, and **Bharat 6G / BSNL indigenous stack** prime high-probability Mains GS3 targets for CSE 2027.

- **Prelims 2022 (Short-Range Devices PYQ):** *"Consider the following communication technologies: 1. Closed-circuit Television 2. Radio Frequency Identification 3. Wireless Local Area Network. Which of the above are considered Short-Range devices/technologies?"*
- **Prelims 2020 (VLC / Li-Fi Physics PYQ):** *"With reference to Visible Light Communication (VLC) technology, which of the following statements are correct? 1. VLC uses electromagnetic spectrum wavelengths 375 to 780 nm. 2. VLC is known as long-range optical wireless communication. 3. VLC can transmit large amounts of data faster than Bluetooth. 4. VLC has no electromagnetic interference."*
- **Prelims 2019 (LTE vs. VoLTE PYQ):** *"With reference to communication technologies, what is/are the difference/differences between LTE (Long-Term Evolution) and VoLTE (Voice over Long-Term Evolution)? 1. LTE is commonly marketed as 3G and VoLTE is commonly marketed as advanced 3G. 2. LTE is data-only technology and VoLTE is voice-only technology."*
- **Prelims 2016 (Li-Fi Fundamentals PYQ):** Tested whether **Li-Fi** uses light as a medium for high-speed data transmission and whether it is a wireless technology several times faster than Wi-Fi.
- **Prelims 2015 (NFC Physics PYQ):** Tested whether **Near Field Communication (NFC)** uses contactless electromagnetic radio fields, operates across distances of even a metre, and supports encryption for sensitive data.

### Prelims Trap Signature

- **Propagation Range & Medium Inversions:** Reclassifying short-range indoor technologies like VLC/Li-Fi as "long-range" (2020) or stretching centimetre-scale NFC to "a metre" (2015), and swapping RF interference properties with EMI-immune optical links.
- **Generation & Protocol Mislabels:** Labelling 4G LTE/VoLTE as 3G/advanced-3G or artificially splitting VoLTE into "voice-only" (2019), or assuming 5G NSA delivers full standalone Network Slicing.
- **Orbital & Spectrum-Law Swaps:** Confusing MEO (*JioSpaceFiber*/SES O3b) with LEO (*Starlink*/*OneWeb*), claiming the Telecom Act 2023 mandates *auctions* for satellite spectrum, or assuming administrative allocation means zero spectrum fee.
- **Component & Nodal Ministry Attribution Swaps:** Scrambling BSNL's indigenous stack partners (C-DOT Core vs. Tejas RAN vs. TCS integration), attributing National Geospatial Policy 2022 to MeitY/DoT instead of **DST**, or confusing **Digital Bharat Nidhi** (DoT statutory fund) with **BharatNet** (rural fiber project).

### Cross-Hub AIR-1 Provocations

1. **GS3 Economy + Science & Tech + GS2 Polity (Constitutional Fairness):** *"Administrative assignment of satellite broadband spectrum under the First Schedule of the Telecommunications Act, 2023 balances digital equity and international technical coordination against fiscal revenue maximization and level-playing-field competition with terrestrial telcos."* Critically examine this trade-off in light of the Supreme Court's 2G Spectrum jurisprudence and TRAI's 2025–2026 pricing framework.

Design an integrated maritime-digital resilience doctrine linking **subsea route diversification (*Project Waterworth*, east-coast hubs)**, **CNI legal protection**, **indigenous repair fleets**, and **sovereign LEO/MEO backup constellations**.
