# Chapter 11 - Intelligence Architectures, CAPF Operational Friction, Police Reforms & BNS/BNSS Criminal Justice Overhaul

Internal security is only as strong as its weakest institutional seam. Between raw intelligence collection, paramilitary border and counter-insurgency deployment, local station-house policing, and courtroom prosecution lies a complex federal chain governed by the **Seventh Schedule**: *Defence*, *Central Intelligence*, and *Armed Forces of the Union* (including CAPFs) reside in the **Union List (Entries 1, 2, 2A, 8)**, *Police* and *Public Order* sit exclusively in the **State List (Entries 1 & 2)**, while *Criminal Law and Criminal Procedure* occupy the **Concurrent List (Entries 1 & 2)**.

Over the last two years—between mid-2024 and late 2026—every link in this domestic security chain has undergone deep institutional stress or transformation. India brought the **BNS–BNSS–BSA criminal-code trilogy** into force (July 2024) backed by the **₹2,254.43 crore NFIES** forensic build-out, inaugurated an AI-enabled **upgraded Multi-Agency Centre (MAC)** (May 2025), unified Parliament security under **CISF** (May 2024), and ignited a fierce constitutional clash between judicial mandamus and parliamentary override via the **CAPF (General Administration) Act, 2026**.

## National Intelligence Architecture and Real-Time Fusion Grid

### India's Core Intelligence Agencies and the Oversight Debate

India distributes intelligence collection across functional silos—domestic human intelligence, external espionage, technical interception, military intelligence, and financial tracking—each answering to a distinct executive parent:

- **Intelligence Bureau (IB):** Founded in **1887** (making it the world's oldest surviving domestic intelligence agency), IB operates under the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)**. It handles domestic political intelligence, counter-terrorism, counter-intelligence, and border-belt internal monitoring. Crucially, IB is a **non-statutory executive body** carrying **no independent powers of arrest or criminal investigation**—it must transfer actionable intelligence to state police or central investigative agencies, while managing the **Multi-Agency Centre (MAC)**.
- **Research and Analysis Wing (R&AW):** Carved out of IB's external wing on **September 21, 1968** under founding chief **R. N. Kao** after the intelligence failures of the 1962 and 1965 wars. Administratively, R&AW functions as a **non-statutory wing of the Cabinet Secretariat**, headed by **Secretary (Research)** reporting directly to the **Prime Minister** (coordinated operationally by the National Security Adviser). Alongside foreign politico-military intelligence and counter-proliferation, R&AW houses specialized operational arms—most notably the **Aviation Research Centre (ARC)** (established in 1962 after the Sino-Indian War for high-altitude aerial reconnaissance and IMINT, later folded into R&AW), the Radio Research Centre (RRC), and Electronics & Technical Services (ETS).
- **National Technical Research Organisation (NTRO):** Raised in **2004** (originally as NTFO, following post-Kargil GoM recommendations) directly under the **Prime Minister's Office / NSA**. Functioning as India's premier **non-statutory technical intelligence (TECHINT)** super-feeder agency, NTRO executes **Signals Intelligence (SIGINT)**, **Imagery Intelligence (IMINT)**, **Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)**, satellite surveillance, and cryptology (via the National Institute of Cryptology Research and Development [NICRD]). NTRO also houses the statutory **NCIIPC** (Section 70A, IT Act).
- **Defence Intelligence Agency (DIA):** Operationalized in **March 2002** (post-Kargil GoM) under Headquarters Integrated Defence Staff (HQ IDS / CDS). DIA fuses tactical and strategic military intelligence across the Army's Directorate of Military Intelligence (MI), Naval Intelligence (DNI), and Air Intelligence (ACAS Intel), administering joint assets like the Defence Image Processing and Analysis Centre (DIPAC) and Directorate of Signal Intelligence.
- **Economic Intelligence Triad (Ministry of Finance — Dept. of Revenue):** Tracks illicit financial arteries sustaining terrorism and organized crime through three distinct institutions: the **Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI)** (apex anti-smuggling, customs evasion, narcotics, and FICN interdiction), the **Enforcement Directorate (ED)** (statutory enforcement of PMLA 2002, FEMA 1999, and FEOA 2018), and the **Financial Intelligence Unit–India (FIU-IND)** (independent central analytical reception hub processing Suspicious Transaction Reports [STRs] and Cash Transaction Reports [CTRs] without direct investigative powers).

**The Statutory Charter & Parliamentary Oversight Deficit (GS2–GS3 Tension):** Unlike peer democracies where intelligence agencies operate under parliamentary statutes and legislative oversight committees (the UK's *Security Service Act 1989* / Intelligence and Security Committee, or US Congressional Intelligence Committees), **IB, R&AW, and NTRO possess no enabling Act of Parliament**, are exempted from the RTI Act (Second Schedule), and undergo zero parliamentary scrutiny. Flagged as far back as the 1977 Shah Commission and echoed by the Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC) and the lapsed *Intelligence Services (Powers and Regulation) Bill, 2011*, this vacuum fuels recurring debates over democratic accountability, partisan misuse, and audit transparency—balanced against executive concerns that legislative exposure could compromise human sources and covert tradecraft.

### Real-Time Intelligence Fusion - MAC/SMACs, NIA and NATGRID

The 1999 Kargil intrusions and the 2008 Mumbai (26/11) terror attacks demonstrated that India's gravest failures stemmed less from lack of raw intelligence than from **stovepiped non-sharing** across agencies. Three institutional bridges address this fusion gap:

**1. Multi-Agency Centre (MAC) and Subsidiary MACs (SMACs):** Established in 2001 under IB following the Kargil Review/GoM reforms and strengthened post-26/11, MAC serves as the 24×7 operational intelligence-sharing hub linking central intelligence, military, CAPF, and state police agencies (via State-level **SMACs**). In **May 2025**, MHA inaugurated an **upgraded MAC in New Delhi** extending secure real-time digital connectivity down to the **district level**, powered by **AI/ML predictive analytics** across specialized Focus Groups covering violent extremism, cryptocurrency, narco-terrorism, terror financing, FICN, cyber threats, and bio-terrorism. According to MHA disclosures in Parliament (March 2025), MAC's counter-terrorism architecture has generated over **72,000 analytical reports**.

**2. National Investigation Agency (NIA — 2008 Act & 2019 Expansion):** Enacted on **December 31, 2008** in the wake of 26/11, the **NIA Act, 2008** created India's statutory federal counter-terrorism agency under MHA. Unlike the CBI (which requires state consent under Section 6 of the DSPE Act), **NIA exercises concurrent pan-India jurisdiction to investigate Scheduled Offences without prior State Government consent** (triggered via state request or *suo motu* Central Government direction).

The **NIA (Amendment) Act, 2019** dramatically widened its reach:
- **Extraterritorial Jurisdiction:** Empowered NIA to investigate Scheduled Offences committed **outside India** targeting Indian citizens or Indian strategic interests (subject to international treaties and host-country domestic laws).
- **Expanded Schedule:** Added **Cyber-Terrorism (IT Act Sec 66F)**, **Human Trafficking**, **Counterfeit Currency/Bank Notes**, **Prohibited Arms (Arms Act, 1959)**, and **Explosive Substances Act, 1908**.
- **Expedited Special Courts:** Empowered Central and State Governments to designate existing **Sessions Courts as NIA Special Courts** (expanding to **52 NIA Special Courts**, including 3 exclusive courts at Ranchi, Jammu, and Mumbai). Supported by 21 branch offices, 2 zonal offices (Guwahati and Jammu), and the **National Terror Data Fusion & Analysis Centre (NTDFAC)** for big-data analytics, NIA achieved an approximate **95% conviction rate** by 2025.

**3. National Intelligence Grid (NATGRID):** Conceptualized post-26/11 to eliminate manual paper requests across ministries, NATGRID provides a secure real-time **database-retrieval layer**—analytically distinct from MAC (**MAC** is the *inter-agency intelligence-sharing forum* for HUMINT/SIGINT inputs, whereas **NATGRID** is the *structured data-query engine* used to verify travel, banking, and identity trails). Originally anchored on **21 core databases**, MHA confirmed by March 2025 that NATGRID had integrated **over 35 data sources** (immigration, airlines, railways, banking/FIU, PAN/CBDT, telecom, VAHAN, NPR, CCTNS) connecting **11 Central User Agencies** (IB, R&AW, CBI, ED, DRI, FIU-IND, CBDT, CBIC, DGGI, NCB, NIA) AND **State/UT Police across all 28 States and 8 Union Territories**. By August 2026, MHA further converged **CCTNS, ICJS, NATGRID, and NAFIS** into an interoperable national investigative data bank.

## Central Armed Police Forces - Mandate Taxonomy and Structural Friction

### Functional Taxonomy Across Seven Central Forces

India's **Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs)**—comprising roughly **10 lakh personnel and ~13,000 Group 'A' officers**—operate under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** (reclassified from "Central Paramilitary Forces" in 2011). Functionally, India's central armed security forces divide into three operational tiers:

1. **Border Guarding Forces ("One Border, One Force" Doctrine):**
   - **Border Security Force (BSF — 1965):** Guards the **Pakistan (3,323 km)** and **Bangladesh (4,096 km)** international borders, while also undertaking anti-LWE and internal-security deployments.
   - **Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP — 1962):** Raised post-1962 war as a specialized high-altitude mountain force guarding the **China LAC (3,488 km)**.
   - **Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB — 1963):** Guards India's open, visa-free borders with **Nepal (1,751 km)** and **Bhutan (699 km)**.
   - **Assam Rifles (AR — 1835):** India's oldest paramilitary force ("Sentinels of the North-East"), guarding the **1,643 km Indo–Myanmar border** and executing counter-insurgency in the Northeast. Crucially, Assam Rifles operates under **Dual Control**: **administrative control, pay, and budget rest with MHA**, while **operational control and officer leadership rest with the Indian Army / MoD**—generating persistent inter-ministerial friction over unified command.
2. **Internal Security and Critical Infrastructure Forces:**
   - **Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF — 1939):** India's largest CAPF and primary internal-security backbone across Left-Wing Extremism (LWE) theatres, J&K counter-insurgency, elections, and law-and-order reinforcement. Houses two elite specialized wings: **CoBRA** (*Commando Battalion for Resolute Action*, 2008) for guerrilla/jungle warfare against Maoists, and the **Rapid Action Force (RAF)** (1992) for zero-response-time communal riot control.
   - **Central Industrial Security Force (CISF — 1969):** India's specialized critical-infrastructure shield protecting PSUs, nuclear installations, space centres, metro rails, over 68 civil airports, VIPs (Special Security Group, 2006), and **12 major seaports** (with a January 2026 mandate to expand security cover across **78 additional EXIM-handling non-major ports**). Following the December 13, 2023 Parliament security breach, MHA transferred comprehensive **Parliament House Complex security** to CISF on **May 20, 2024** (deploying ~3,300+ personnel), replacing the fragmented multi-agency setup of CRPF's Parliament Duty Group (PDG), Delhi Police, and Parliament Security Service with unified single-command access and counter-terror control.
3. **Federal Counter-Terrorism Contingency Force:**
   - **National Security Guard (NSG — raised 1984; statutory NSG Act, 1986):** Popularly known as the **"Black Cats,"** NSG is a specialized federal contingency force for high-stakes counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, and anti-hijacking, maintained with regional hubs at Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, and Gandhinagar. Uniquely, NSG has **no permanent direct-entry cadre**—it is a **100% deputation force** bifurcated into the **Special Action Group (SAG)** (drawn strictly from the **Indian Army** for kinetic counter-terror assault) and the **Special Ranger Group (SRG)** (drawn from **CAPFs and State Police** for perimeter security and VVIP cover).

### Operational Overstretch and the 2025–2026 IPS Deputation vs. OGAS Crisis

Two deep institutional pathologies strain India's CAPF ecosystem today:

**1. Overstretch, Mission Creep and Personnel Burnout:** Because state governments chronically under-invest in State Armed Police, they reflexively requisition central forces for routine elections, festivals, and municipal law-and-order duties—diverting specialized border-guarding units (BSF, ITBP, SSB) away from frontier readiness and denying battalions mandatory peace-station training rotations. The human toll is stark: data placed before Parliament revealed **749 CAPF suicides**, roughly **10,000 resignations**, and **~46,000 voluntary retirements** between 2021 and 2025—reflecting extended hardship tenures, denied leave, and family separation.

**2. IPS Deputation vs. OGAS Cadre Stagnation — The 2025 SC Verdict and *CAPF Act, 2026* Clash:** For two decades, direct-entry Group 'A' CAPF Executive Officers (recruited via the UPSC CAPF Assistant Commandant exam) have waged a bitter legal battle against chronic **cadre stagnation**—often waiting 18 to 25 years for promotions while lateral-entry **Indian Police Service (IPS)** officers occupy top command slots. CAPF cadre officers argue that reserving senior ranks for deputationists creates a morale-crushing glass ceiling and deprives specialized combat forces of lifelong domain leadership; conversely, the Union Government contends that IPS officers—as an **Article 312 All India Service**—provide indispensable operational synergy between central forces and state police/administrations.

Between May 2025 and November 2026, this administrative dispute escalated into a major constitutional showdown over **separation of powers**:
- **Supreme Court Watershed (*Sanjay Prakash & Ors. v. Union of India*, May 23, 2025 — 2025 INSC 779):** A Bench of Justices Abhay S. Oka and Ujjal Bhuyan—building upon *Union of India v. Harananda* (2019) and DoPT's July 12, 2019 Office Memorandum—unequivocally recognized Group 'A' executive officers of all five CAPFs (CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, SSB) as **Organised Group 'A' Services (OGAS) "for all purposes"** (securing Non-Functional Financial Upgradation [NFFU] and cadre parity). Crucially, the Court ordered: (1) completion of overdue **cadre reviews** and recruitment-rule amendments within **six months**, and (2) **progressive reduction and phasing down of IPS deputation posts up to the Senior Administrative Grade (SAG / Inspector General level) within two years**. On **October 28, 2025**, the Supreme Court **dismissed the Union's review petition**, rendering the ruling final.
- **Legislative Override — *Central Armed Police Forces (General Administration) Act, 2026* (Act No. 9 of 2026):** Rather than implementing the two-year phase-down, Parliament enacted an umbrella statute for the **five Scheduled CAPFs** (CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, SSB; Schedule amendable via notification) without repealing parent force Acts—introduced in Rajya Sabha on March 25, 2026, passed by Rajya Sabha (April 1) and Lok Sabha (April 2), given **Presidential Assent on April 9, 2026**, and **brought into force on October 8, 2026**. Invoking a sweeping **non-obstante override** in **Section 3** (*"notwithstanding any other law, any judgment, decree or order of any Court"*), the Act statutorily locks in IPS deputation quotas across all five forces:
  - **50%** of all **Inspector General (IG)** posts;
  - **Minimum 67%** of all **Additional Director General (ADG)** posts; and
  - **100%** (all posts) of **Special Director General (SDG)** and **Director General (DG)**.
- **Live Constitutional & Contempt Proceedings (Late 2026 Status):** Over 3,000 serving CAPF officers (lead petitioner: CRPF Assistant Commandant and Shaurya Chakra awardee Bibhor Kumar Singh) challenged Sections 3 and 4 before the Supreme Court as violating **Article 14** and impermissibly nullifying a judicial mandamus without curing its legal basis. Issuing notice in **August 2026**, a Special Bench (Justices Manoj Misra and Ujjal Bhuyan) heard constitutional challenges jointly with contempt petitions against Union Home Secretary Govind Mohan in **October 2026**—flagging *"stark non-compliance"* with the mandatory cadre-review deadline and setting a **November 30, 2026** affidavit compliance deadline. Final constitutional adjudication remains pending as of late November 2026.

## Police Modernization, Colonial Legacies and the Prakash Singh Impasse

### The Colonial Police Act of 1861 and Structural Capacity Deficits

India's frontline internal security relies on state police forces still largely anchored in the philosophy of the **Indian Police Act, 1861**—enacted immediately after the 1857 Revolt to build a cheap, militarized, regime-maintenance force accountable strictly to the colonial executive rather than to citizens or the rule of law. Over 85% of Indian police personnel belong to the lower constabulary, trapped in colonial hierarchies ("muscle over mind") with scarce career progression, 14-hour workdays, and punitive transfer regimes.

Empirical benchmarks from the **India Justice Report 2025** quantify this institutional fragility:
- **Severe Understaffing:** Actual police-to-population strength sits near **155 personnel per lakh population**, lagging far below the sanctioned norm of **~197 per lakh** and the **UN recommended benchmark of 222 per lakh**, leaving over **5.3 lakh vacancies** nationwide.
- **Neglected Training:** On average, states devote barely **1.25% of police budgets to training**, leaving station-house officers poorly equipped in cyber forensics, gender sensitization, and scientific investigation.
- **Politicization and Custodial Trust Deficit:** Arbitrary executive transfers create a **"transfer-posting industry"** rewarding political compliance over investigative independence, eroding citizen confidence and encouraging shortcut coercive interrogation.

### Soli Sorabjee Model Police Act (2006) and the Seven Prakash Singh Directives

Four decades of official diagnosis—from the **National Police Commission (1977–1981, Dharamvira — eight reports)** through the **Ribeiro Committee (1998)**, **Padmanabhaiah Committee (2000)**, and **Malimath Committee (2003)**—gathered dust because state political executives guarded discretionary control over policing. In 2005, MHA appointed the **Police Act Drafting Committee (PADC)** chaired by former Attorney General **Soli Sorabjee**, which submitted the **Model Police Act, 2006** replacing the 1861 mindset with citizen-centric service, human-rights obligations, and institutional accountability—circulated to states as advisory legislation given State List jurisdiction.

Confronting legislative paralysis, the Supreme Court invoked **Article 142** in ***Prakash Singh v. Union of India* (September 22, 2006)**—a landmark PIL filed by former UP/Assam DGP Prakash Singh—issuing **seven binding directives** pending state legislative compliance:

1. **State Security Commission (SSC):** Independent watchdog to shield police from extraneous political interference, set broad policy guidelines, and evaluate police performance.
2. **Merit-Based Selection & Fixed 2-Year Tenure for DGP:** Selected by the State Government strictly from among the three senior-most officers empanelled by the **UPSC**, assured a minimum **two-year tenure** irrespective of superannuation. *(In **July 2018**, the Supreme Court barred states from appointing **"acting DGPs"** to dodge UPSC vetting, requiring states to submit names to UPSC three months before a vacancy from officers holding at least six months of residual service).*
3. **Fixed 2-Year Tenure for Operational Field Officers:** Minimum two-year tenure for **IG (Zone), DIG (Range), District SP, and Station House Officer (SHO)** to dismantle arbitrary transfers.
4. **Separation of Investigation from Law and Order:** Dedicated investigative wings freed from VIP escort, crowd control, and riot duties—starting in urban cities with ≥10 lakh population.
5. **Police Establishment Board (PEB):** Internal professional board (DGP + four senior officers) governing transfers, postings, and promotions up to **Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP)** rank.
6. **Police Complaints Authorities (PCAs):** Independent external accountability bodies at the **State level** (chaired by a retired High Court/Supreme Court Judge probing SP-and-above misconduct and grave offences like custodial death/rape) and **District level** (chaired by a retired District Judge for DSP-and-below ranks).
7. **National Security Commission (NSC):** Union-level body overseeing merit-based selection and minimum tenure of chiefs of Central Police Organisations (CPOs).

**Two Decades of Chronic State Defiance (2006–2026):** Although roughly 17–18 states passed new Police Acts or amendments post-2006, successive audits—including the **Justice K. T. Thomas Monitoring Committee (2010)**, the **India Justice Report 2025**, and ongoing 2026 Supreme Court proceedings—confirm **not a single state has achieved faithful compliance**. States routinely circumvent the directives through legal loopholes: installing prolonged **"acting/officiating DGPs"** (still litigated before the SC in 2026), packing SSCs with ruling ministers while stripping recommendations of binding force, starving PCAs of independent investigators, and reducing investigation–L&O separation to paper circulars due to acute personnel deficits.

## SMART Policing and BNS-BNSS-BSA Security Overhaul

### The SMART Policing Paradigm

To reorient Indian policing beyond coercive colonial inertia, the Prime Minister unveiled the **SMART Policing** doctrine at the **49th Annual DGsP/IGsP Conference in Guwahati on November 30, 2014**—defining an aspirational ten-attribute operational framework:

- **S** — **Strict and Sensitive** (zero tolerance toward terror/crime paired with empathy toward vulnerable citizens);
- **M** — **Modern and Mobile** (modern weaponry, rapid-response patrol mobility, and emergency grids like **Dial 112 / ERSS**);
- **A** — **Alert and Accountable** (proactive threat anticipation backed by transparent citizen oversight);
- **R** — **Reliable and Responsive** (zero-friction FIR registration, Women Help Desks, and predictable public service);
- **T** — **Trained and Techno-savvy** (scientific forensics, digital evidence mastery, and continuous skill upgradation).

Supported financially through the Centre's umbrella **Modernisation of Police Forces (MPF)** scheme, SMART Policing laid the operational philosophy for India's historic 2024 statutory and digital criminal-justice overhaul.

### BNS, BNSS and BSA Security Overhaul (Effective July 1, 2024)

Exercising legislative competence under **Concurrent List Entries 1 and 2**, Parliament replaced India's three colonial criminal statutes—the Indian Penal Code (1860), Code of Criminal Procedure (1973), and Indian Evidence Act (1872)—with the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**, and **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**, effective nationwide from **July 1, 2024**.

From a national-security and internal-policing lens, the tri-code overhaul fundamentally redesigns substantive offences, investigative authority, and evidentiary science:

**1. Substantive Security Codification Under BNS, 2023:**
- **BNS Section 111 (Organised Crime) & Section 112 (Petty Organised Crime):** For the first time, general Indian penal law codifies **Organised Crime** uniformly nationwide—eliminating the patchwork reliance on disparate state statutes (such as Maharashtra's MCOCA 1999 or Gujarat's GUJCTOC). Section 111 targets continuing unlawful activity by crime syndicates (kidnapping, extortion, contract killing, land grabbing, human trafficking, cybercrimes, and serious economic frauds) executed via violence, coercion, or intimidation—prescribing **death or life imprisonment** (+ min ₹10 lakh fine) if death results, and minimum 5 years' imprisonment otherwise. **Section 112** separately penalizes **Petty Organised Crime** by groups (snatching, pickpocketing, card skimming, counterfeit goods, and public-exam paper leaks).
- **BNS Section 113 (Terrorist Act):** Integrates **"Terrorist Act"** directly inside general criminal law—broadly mirroring Section 15 of UAPA, 1967 (acts threatening India's unity, integrity, sovereignty, security, or economic security via explosives, CBRN/poisonous substances, CII disruption, FICN, or public-functionary abduction). Crucially, the **Explanation to Section 113** inserts an administrative gatekeeper: an officer not below **Superintendent of Police (SP)** must decide whether to register a prosecution under **BNS Section 113** or under **UAPA, 1967**. *Constitutional Critique:* Legal scholars caution that embedding terrorism inside ordinary criminal law—without UAPA's stringent central/state executive sanction safeguards—creates duplication risks unless SP-level discretion is strictly policed by courts.
- **BNS Section 152 (Acts Endangering Sovereignty, Unity and Integrity):** Replaces colonial Sedition (IPC Sec 124A) with a tailored national-security offence criminalizing secessionist, armed-rebellion, or subversive incitement—explicitly encompassing **electronic communication and financial means**—though critics urge narrow judicial construction aligned with *Kedar Nath Singh* (1962) to protect legitimate political dissent.

**2. Procedural and Scientific Investigation Overhaul Under BNSS, 2023 & BSA, 2023:**
- **Mandatory Crime-Scene Forensics (BNSS Section 176(3)):** Arguably the most transformative policing mandate in Indian statutory history—requiring a **forensic expert to physically visit the crime scene**, collect forensic evidence, and record the process via **videography** for **every offence punishable with imprisonment of 7 years or more**. This shifts policing from a confession-centric colonial model to objective scientific verification.
- **Search-and-Seizure Videography (BNSS Section 105):** Mandates audio-video recording of searches and seizures (and preparation of seizure lists), directly reducing evidence tampering and allegations of planted contraband.
- **Zero FIR & e-FIR (BNSS Section 173):** Statutorily codifies **Zero FIR** (compelling any police station to register cognizable offences irrespective of territorial jurisdiction) and **e-FIR** (electronic reporting authenticated via signature within 3 days).
- **Split Police Custody (BNSS Section 187):** While retaining the 15-day aggregate ceiling for police custody, BNSS breaks CrPC Section 167's rigid first-15-days-only window—allowing investigators to seek police custody **in parts across the initial 40 days** (for offences punishable ≤10 years) or **60 days** (for offences >10 years). While valuable for complex multi-state terror/cyber probes, civil-liberties jurists warn that staggered remand extensions heighten custodial coercion risks unless magistrates apply strict **Article 21** scrutiny.
- **Trial in Absentia (BNSS Section 356) & Proceeds Forfeiture (Section 107):** Empowers courts to try and convict **proclaimed offenders** in absentia after due-diligence notices—closing the impunity shelter exploited by fugitive terror handlers and underworld syndicates hiding abroad (complementing MHA's anti-fugitive drive that brought back **274 fugitives from abroad between 2019 and 2026**). Section 107 enables swift attachment and forfeiture of proceeds of crime for victim compensation.
- **Digital Records as Primary Evidence (BSA Sections 2(1)(d) & 61–63):** Elevates electronic and digital records (server logs, emails, CCTV footage, smartphone communications, *e-Sakshya* recordings) to **primary evidence** on equal footing with physical documents—backed by a mandatory **Section 63(4) authenticity certificate** capturing cryptographic **hash values** to lock chain-of-custody integrity.

### Forensic Infrastructure (NFIES & NFSU) and Digital Spine (CCTNS 2.0 & ICJS 2.0)

Mandating forensic crime-scene collection for every 7-year-plus offence without scaling laboratory capacity would risk gridlocking India's criminal justice system. New Delhi therefore paired statutory enactment with a synchronized physical and digital infrastructure push:

- **National Forensic Infrastructure Enhancement Scheme (NFIES — Approved June 19, 2024):** A **100% Central Sector Scheme** funded entirely from MHA's budget with a total outlay of **₹2,254.43 crore** across **FY 2024–25 to FY 2028–29**. NFIES executes three components: (1) establishing **new off-campuses of the National Forensic Sciences University (NFSU)** (an Institution of National Importance headquartered in Gandhinagar—with 9 new campuses planned nationwide), (2) establishing **new Central Forensic Science Laboratories (CFSLs)** (expanding beyond legacy hubs into states such as UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Kerala, and Jammu), and (3) upgrading existing infrastructure at the **NFSU Delhi Campus**—supported by the **Forensic Aptitude and Calibre Test (FACT)** to cure acute laboratory manpower shortages.
- **CCTNS 2.0 & ICJS 2.0 ("One Data, One Entry"):** **Crime and Criminal Tracking Network & Systems (CCTNS)** digitizes police-station FIRs and records nationwide, while **Inter-Operable Criminal Justice System (ICJS)** seamlessly links all **five criminal-justice pillars**—**Police (CCTNS), Courts (e-Courts), Prisons (e-Prisons), Prosecution (e-Prosecution), and Forensics (e-Forensics)**—eliminating manual paper relays and interlinking directly with **NAFIS** (National Automated Fingerprint Identification System) and **NATGRID**.
- **Flagship Digital Justice Applications (Launched August 4, 2024):** Developed by the National Informatics Centre (NIC) to operationalize the three new laws on the ground:
  - ***e-Sakshya*:** Mobile and web application enabling Investigating Officers to record real-time crime-scene videos, search-seizure footage, and witness statements stamped with **cryptographic hashing** directly into cloud evidence lockers linked to court charge sheets—operational across **15,899 police stations in 35 States/UTs** by February 2026.
  - ***NyayShruti*:** Enables courts to conduct virtual hearings and examine police officers, doctors, forensic scientists, and witnesses via video conferencing—slashing adjournments and police-escort wastage.
  - ***e-Forensics* & *Nyaya Setu*:** *e-Forensics* digitizes forensic sample chain-of-custody tracking across FSLs, while ***Nyaya Setu*** integrates Police, Medical, Forensic, Prosecution, and Prison dashboards onto a unified investigative interface.
  - ***e-Summons*:** Transmits judicial summons and warrants electronically from courts directly to police stations and recipients for verifiable mobile delivery.

### Systemic Imperatives - Beyond Statutory and Digital Upgrades

India's 2024–2026 security-justice reforms demonstrate a striking structural asymmetry: statutory codification (BNS/BNSS/BSA, NIA 2019, CAPF Act 2026) and digital plumbing (upgraded MAC, NATGRID, ICJS 2.0, *e-Sakshya*) have leaped decades ahead of **institutional autonomy and human-resource governance**. Technology alone cannot neutralize politicized policing or understaffed forensic labs.

Four foundational calibrations will determine whether this overhaul converts colonial force into constitutional *Nyaya*: (1) enforcing **statutory tenure insulation and independent PCAs** across states while closing the 5.3-lakh police vacancy gap and raising training budgets well beyond 1.25%; (2) placing **State and Central Forensic Science Laboratories under independent scientific directorates** detached from police hierarchy to eliminate institutional confirmation bias; (3) resolving the **CAPF–IPS leadership impasse** constructively—balancing All-India federal coordination with predictable, dignified promotional avenues and mandatory cadre reviews for combat CAPF officers; and (4) enacting a **statutory charter with calibrated parliamentary and judicial oversight** for India's core intelligence agencies (IB, R&AW, NTRO) so national security strengthens, rather than erodes, constitutional accountability.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Non-Statutory Intelligence Trio:** **IB** (MHA), **R&AW** (Cabinet Secretariat), and **NTRO** (PMO/NSA) are ALL **non-statutory executive bodies** (exempt under RTI Second Schedule). By contrast, **NIA** (NIA Act 2008), **NCIIPC** (Sec 70A IT Act), **ED** (PMLA/FEMA), and **NSG** (NSG Act 1986) are **statutory**. Further, **IB has NO arrest powers**, whereas **NIA has pan-India concurrent jurisdiction WITHOUT requiring prior State consent** (unlike CBI).
> - **MAC vs. NATGRID Functional Inversion:** **MAC** (housed inside IB, upgraded May 2025 down to district level) is a real-time **intelligence-sharing forum** for HUMINT/SIGINT inputs; **NATGRID** is a **structured database-retrieval engine** integrating 35+ data sources for 11 Central User Agencies and all 28 States/8 UTs.
> - **Assam Rifles & NSG Unique Traps:** **Assam Rifles** (1835, Indo–Myanmar border) is the *only* force under **Dual Control** (administrative control = **MHA**; operational control = **Indian Army / MoD**), and is **NOT** in the 5-force Schedule of the *CAPF Act, 2026*. **NSG ("Black Cats")** is a **100% deputation force** (**SAG** = Indian Army; **SRG** = CAPFs/State Police) with zero direct-entry cadre.
> - **CISF Unified Parliament Security:** Since **May 20, 2024**, **CISF** (not CRPF or Delhi Police) holds unified command of the entire Parliament House Complex.
> - **CAPF Act, 2026 Statutory Quotas & Scope:** Applies to **5 Scheduled CAPFs** (CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, SSB; Schedule amendable by Centre) and **does NOT repeal** their five parent Acts. Enforced **October 8, 2026**, it reserves **50% IG**, **minimum 67% ADG**, and **100% SDG/DG** posts for **IPS deputation**, overriding the May 23, 2025 SC *Sanjay Prakash* judgment—currently under live **Supreme Court constitutional challenge & contempt scrutiny**.
> - **NFIES Scheme Type Trap:** **National Forensic Infrastructure Enhancement Scheme (NFIES)** (approved June 19, 2024; ₹2,254.43 crore for 2024–25 to 2028–29) is a **Central Sector Scheme (100% MHA-funded)**—NOT a Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) shared with states.
> - **BNS / BNSS / BSA Atomic Clauses:**
>   - **BNS Sec 111** = Organised Crime; **Sec 112** = Petty Organised Crime; **Sec 113** = Terrorist Act (deciding officer choosing BNS vs. UAPA must be **SP rank or above**).
>   - **BNSS Sec 176(3)** mandates forensic crime-scene visit + videography for offences punishable with **7 years or more** (not 3 or 5 years).
>   - **BNSS Sec 187** retains the **15-day maximum police custody cap**, but allows split custody across the first **40 or 60 days** (unlike CrPC's first 15 days only).
>   - **BSA, 2023** elevates electronic records to **primary evidence** (not secondary evidence) backed by Sec 63(4) hash certification.
> - **Seventh Schedule Federal Split:** *Police* & *Public Order* = **State List (Entries 1 & 2)**; *Prisons* = **State List (Entry 4)**; *CAPFs / Armed Forces of the Union* = **Union List (Entries 2 & 2A)**; *Criminal Law & Criminal Procedure (BNS/BNSS/BSA)* = **Concurrent List (Entries 1 & 2)**.

### Prelims Trap Signature

- **Statutory Status & Consent Conflation:** Expect statements falsely claiming **IB** or **R&AW** are statutory bodies created by Acts of Parliament (**FALSE**), or claiming **NIA** requires State Government consent to investigate Scheduled Offences like CBI (**FALSE**), or asserting **CBI** is a statutory body under its own Act (**FALSE**—derives power from DSPE Act, 1946).
- **CAPF Act 2026 Scope & Quota Distortion:** Watch for traps claiming the *CAPF (General Administration) Act, 2026* applies to **Assam Rifles and NSG** (**FALSE**—Schedule lists only CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, SSB), *repealed* the five legacy force Acts (**FALSE**—runs alongside them), or *phased out* IPS deputation per the May 2025 SC order (**FALSE**—it legislatively overrode the judgment to lock in 50% IG / ≥67% ADG / 100% SDG-DG IPS quotas).
- **App-to-Function & Custody-Rule Inversion:** Distinguish ***e-Sakshya*** (crime-scene videography/evidence locker) from ***NyayShruti*** (virtual court hearings/witness depositions) and ***Nyaya Setu*** (unified 5-pillar investigation dashboard). On **BNSS Sec 187**, catch traps claiming the *total duration* of police custody increased from 15 days to 60 days (**FALSE**—maximum aggregate police custody remains **15 days**, newly splittable across the initial 40/60 days).
- **Prakash Singh vs. Model Police Act Confusion:** Remember the **Seven Directives** were issued by the **Supreme Court under Article 142** (*Prakash Singh*, Sept 2006), whereas the **Model Police Act, 2006** was drafted by the executive **Soli Sorabjee Committee** (non-binding advisory legislation circulated to states).
