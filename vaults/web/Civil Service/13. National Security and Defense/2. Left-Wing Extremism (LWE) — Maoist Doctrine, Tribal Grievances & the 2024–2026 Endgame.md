# Chapter 2 - Left-Wing Extremism (LWE) — Maoist Doctrine, Tribal Grievances & the 2024–2026 Endgame

For nearly six decades, Left-Wing Extremism (LWE)—popularly termed Naxalism or the Maoist insurgency—represented independent India’s most protracted internal challenge to constitutional authority, once described by Prime Minister Manmohan Singh in 2006 as the country’s "single biggest internal security threat". At its operational zenith around 2005–2010, the so-called "Red Corridor" stretched across mineral-rich forest belts from the Nepal border through Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh down to Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra, contesting state sovereignty across more than 126–200 districts.

Between 2024 and late 2026, however, India executed one of the most decisive counter-insurgency contractions in modern democratic history. Understanding how an ideological peasant uprising mutated into a sophisticated guerrilla war—and why its military apparatus collapsed in 2025–2026 while post-insurgency governance challenges endure—exposes the core mechanics of India’s security–development nexus.

---

## Genesis & Maoist Operational Doctrine

India’s far-left armed movement did not emerge as an imported conspiracy; it evolved through three distinct ideological and organizational phases that progressively fused radical agrarian politics with militarized insurgency.

### Historical Evolution - From Naxalbari (1967) to CPI (Maoist) (2004)

- **Phase I — Ideological Rupture (1967–1972):** In May 1967, a violent peasant-sharecropper uprising broke out in **Naxalbari** village in Darjeeling district, West Bengal, led by dissidents of the Communist Party of India (Marxist)—**Charu Majumdar**, **Kanu Sanyal**, and **Jangal Santhal**. Guided by Majumdar’s ***Historic Eight Documents***, which rejected parliamentary democracy as a bourgeois sham and advocated the "annihilation of class enemies," the faction formed the **CPI (Marxist–Leninist)** in 1969 before state counter-offensives (*Operation Steeplechase*, 1971) and internal splits shattered the first wave.
- **Phase II — Regional Fragmentation & Militarization (1980–2003):** Following the Emergency, splinter factions reorganized around regional agrarian and caste fault lines. Two dominant poles emerged: the **People’s War Group (PWG)**, founded by **Kondapalli Seetharamaiah** in Andhra Pradesh in 1980 (which pioneered jungle guerrilla squads in Dandakaranya), and the **Maoist Communist Centre (MCC)**—later **MCCI**—operating across feudal agrarian belts of Bihar and Jharkhand.
- **Phase III — Unified Insurgent Architecture (September 21, 2004):** Recognizing that inter-faction fratricide weakened revolutionary momentum, the PWG and MCCI merged on **September 21, 2004** to form the **Communist Party of India (Maoist)**, proscribed as a terrorist organization under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA) in June 2009. The merger united PWG’s military discipline and central-Indian forest sanctuaries with MCCI’s mass-mobilization networks across eastern India, triggering the bloodiest decade of Maoist violence (peaking in 2010).

### Mao’s *Protracted People’s War* & Tactical Architecture

The CPI (Maoist) adopted Mao Zedong’s three-stage doctrine of **Protracted People’s War (PPW)** aimed at overthrowing the Indian state by "encircling the cities from the countryside":

1. **Strategic Defensive:** Building clandestine political bases in remote, state-absent forest regions while conducting hit-and-run guerrilla raids to capture weapons and demoralize police.
2. **Strategic Stalemate (Equilibrium):** Establishing "liberated zones" (*Base Areas*) through **Area Denial**—blowing up roads, bridges, telecom towers, and school buildings to prevent security mobility and state service delivery—while escalating from squad ambushes to company- and battalion-level assaults.
3. **Strategic Offensive:** Converting guerrilla detachments into a conventional People’s Liberation Army capable of seizing urban industrial centers—a stage the Indian Maoists never approached.

To operationalize this doctrine, the CPI (Maoist) built four interlocking instruments:

- **People’s Liberation Guerrilla Army (PLGA):** Founded on December 2, 2000 (originally as PGA by PWG under leaders including Thippiri Tirupathi alias Devuji) and structured under the **Central Military Commission (CMC)**, the PLGA operated in three tiers: the **Main Force** (mobile combat units such as the dreaded **PLGA Battalion No. 1** in south Bastar), the **Secondary Force** (local guerrilla platoons/DALAMs), and the **Base Force** (village-level *Jan Militia* providing scouting, logistics, and IED emplacement).
- ***Janathana Sarkar* (Parallel Governance):** Inside deep interior pockets like Abujhmad, Maoists operated **Revolutionary People’s Committees (RPCs)** mimicking state departments—agriculture, forest management, education, health, and finance—enforced through brutal kangaroo courts (***Jan Adalats***) that executed alleged police informers to enforce village compliance through terror.
- **IED Ambushes & TCOC:** During dry pre-monsoon months (February–June), Maoists launched annual **Tactical Counter Offensive Campaigns (TCOC)** relying on command-wire and pressure-plate **Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs)**, booby-trapped spike holes, and multi-layered jungle ambushes—epitomized by the April 2010 Chintalnar/Tadmetla massacre (76 CRPF personnel killed) and the May 2013 Jhiram Ghati ambush in Sukma.
- **Extortion Economies & Urban Frontal Networks:** Financially, the movement sustained an informal parallel war chest by levying systematic "revolutionary taxes" on *tendu patta* (bidi leaf) contractors, bamboo traders, road/mining companies, and illicit cannabis cultivation. Politically, its 2004 strategy document ***Urban Perspective: Our Work in Urban Areas*** directed cadres to build legal mass fronts across industrial workers, students, and civil-society networks to secure ideological recruitment, safe houses, medical logistics, and legal defense—enabling influence far beyond forested districts.

## Structural Drivers - Tribal Alienation, Resource Extraction & Governance Vacuums

Military doctrine alone cannot sustain an insurgency for half a century; Maoists thrived precisely because they embedded themselves inside acute historical grievances across India’s mineral-rich Fifth Schedule heartland. The **Planning Commission’s D. Bandyopadhyay Expert Group Report (2008)** (*Development Challenges in Extremist Affected Areas*) famously reframed LWE not as a mere law-and-order aberration, but as a structural governance deficit where the state’s extractive face arrived decades before its welfare face.

Across central and eastern India, a profound spatial irony persists: the districts richest in iron ore, bauxite, coal, and forest wealth—Bastar, Dantewada, West Singhbhum, Malkangiri—historically exhibited the lowest human development indicators. Colonial forest statutes and post-Independence industrial projects triggered multi-generational **tribal land alienation** and involuntary displacement without commensurate rehabilitation, while poor enforcement of Fifth Schedule protections, PESA Gram Sabha consent norms, and Forest Rights titles left Adivasi communities vulnerable to predatory moneylenders, exploitative forest-produce middlemen, and administrative neglect. For a detailed statutory breakdown of constitutional tribal safeguards, community forest rights, and PVTG welfare saturation frameworks, see [[10. Marginalized Groups, Social Defense & Correctional Admin#Tribal Welfare FRA 2006 and PM-JANMAN|Tribal Welfare, FRA 2006 & PM-JANMAN]].

Maoists weaponized this legitimacy vacuum with ruthless pragmatism: initially protecting tribals from petty forest-guard harassment and forcing higher *tendu* leaf wages to earn local acceptance, before locking those same communities into perpetual isolation. Once embedded, insurgents deliberately sabotaged primary schools, health clinics, and roads because genuine state welfare and market integration destroy the very grievances upon which Maoist recruitment depends.

## India’s Counter-Insurgency Architecture

Under the **Seventh Schedule** of the Constitution, **Police** and **Public Order** belong to the **State List** (Entries 1 and 2), meaning State Police forces serve as the primary counter-insurgency responders while the Union Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) coordinates inter-state synergy, deploys Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs), shares intelligence, and funds capacity building. India’s strategic inflection arrived when fragmented, reactive police sweeps gave way to the institutionalized **National Policy and Action Plan to Address LWE (2015)**, uniting security dominance, accelerated development, rights/entitlements, and perception management under a whole-of-government architecture.

### The SAMADHAN Doctrine (May 8, 2017)

Articulated by Union Home Minister Rajnath Singh on **May 8, 2017** following the deadly Sukma CRPF ambush, the **SAMADHAN** doctrine operationalized India’s counter-LWE campaign into eight measurable, data-driven pillars:

1. **S — Smart Leadership:** Unified political resolve and seamless Centre–State operational command eliminating inter-state jurisdictional sanctuaries across tri-junctions (e.g., Chhattisgarh–Telangana–Maharashtra).
2. **A — Aggressive Strategy:** Transitioning from static camp defense to proactive, intelligence-led area domination and deep-forest penetration into Maoist sanctuaries.
3. **M — Motivation and Training:** Specialized jungle-warfare and counter-IED conditioning alongside hardened camp logistics, casualty evacuation helicopters, and troop welfare.
4. **A — Actionable Intelligence:** Building hyper-local human intelligence (HUMINT) via surrendered cadres and local tribal police, fused with Multi-Agency Centre (MAC) and State Intelligence Bureau (SIB) grids.
5. **D — Dashboard-Based KPIs & KRAs:** Quantifying operational, developmental, and surrender deliverables against time-bound Key Performance Indicators and Key Result Areas rather than open-ended deployments.
6. **H — Harnessing Technology:** Deploying UAVs/mini-drones, thermal imagery, satellite mapping, ground-penetrating IED detection, GIS tracking, and biometric/RFID-tagged weaponry.
7. **A — Action Plan for Each Theatre:** Rejecting one-size-fits-all templates in favor of tailored theatre plans (e.g., Abujhmad/South Bastar, Saranda–Kolhan, Odisha–Andhra Border, MMC tri-junction).
8. **N — No Access to Financing:** Choking the Maoist extortion economy through coordinated **NIA**, **Enforcement Directorate (ED)**, and State Police PMLA/UAPA asset attachments targeting levy collectors, contractors, and urban financial conduits.

### Fiscal & Developmental Architecture - SRE, SCA, SIS & Connectivity Saturation

Sustaining long-horizon counter-insurgency required separating operational police reimbursement from last-mile civic asset creation:

- **Security Related Expenditure (SRE) Scheme:** Reimburses LWE-affected states for operational costs, troop training, ex-gratia relief to martyred personnel and civilian victims, and **Surrender & Rehabilitation** incentives (offering ₹5 lakh for high-ranking cadres, ₹2.5 lakh for mid/low-ranking cadres, weapon bounties, and a ₹10,000 monthly vocational stipend for 36 months). Between FY 2014–15 and mid-2026, MHA released **₹3,805.04 crore** under SRE (including ₹1,453.95 crore to Chhattisgarh alone).
- **Special Central Assistance (SCA) Scheme (2017):** Provides untied, flexible capital grants specifically to plug critical public-infrastructure gaps in the worst-affected districts, with **₹4,289.20 crore** released since inception through July 2026.
- **Special Infrastructure Scheme (SIS):** Sanctioned **₹1,761 crore** to upgrade State Special Forces, Special Intelligence Branches (SIBs), and construct **597 Fortified Police Stations** (up nearly ninefold from just 66 prior to 2014).
- **Infrastructure, Telecom & Financial Saturation (PIB July 2026):** Under the **Road Requirement Plan (RRP-I)** and **Road Connectivity Project for LWE Affected Areas (RCPLWEA)**, **15,189 km of all-weather roads** were constructed; **9,497–9,600+ mobile towers** brought 4G/telecom coverage to **96% of villages** in erstwhile most-affected districts; **179 Eklavya Model Residential Schools (EMRS)**, **47 ITIs**, and **49 Skill Development Centres (SDCs)** expanded tribal human capital; **6,025 banking-enabled Post Offices**, **1,804 bank branches**, and **1,321 ATMs** eroded cash-based extortion; and **21,26,268 FRA title deeds** (20.20 lakh individual + 1.06 lakh community titles) were distributed.

### Specialized Counter-Insurgency Forces

Conventional policing fails in dense forest insurgencies; India broke the Maoist tactical advantage by combining elite central commandos with culturally fluent local state forces:

- **Andhra Pradesh Greyhounds (Raised 1989):** Conceptualized by IPS officer **K.S. Vyas**, Greyhounds set the gold standard for Indian jungle COIN—prioritizing small, stealthy, non-hierarchical commando teams operating without heavy logistics tails, relying on superior physical endurance, surprise night raids, and ruthless intelligence precision. Their success virtually eliminated Maoism from Andhra Pradesh and provided the institutional template adopted nationwide.
- **CRPF CoBRA (Commando Battalion for Resolute Action, Raised 2008):** Ten specialized jungle-warfare battalions embedded within the CRPF (India’s lead central force in the LWE theatre), equipped for multi-day deep-forest tracking, counter-ambush drills, and surgical inter-state raids.
- **Chhattisgarh District Reserve Guard (DRG):** Raised across Bastar districts (initially in Kanker and Narayanpur in 2008 and expanded aggressively across south Bastar post-2013), the **DRG** consists of regular salaried state police personnel recruited from **local Adivasi youth and vetted surrendered Maoists**. Because DRG troopers speak Gondi and Halbi, know forest trails instinctively, and understand PLGA tactics intimately, they eliminated the Maoists’ historic "home-terrain advantage" and spearheaded the 2024–2026 decapitation strikes.
- **CRPF Bastarriya Battalion (241st Battalion):** Sanctioned in 2016 and inducted in 2018 exclusively from Scheduled Tribe youth across four core Bastar districts (Bijapur, Dantewada, Narayanpur, Sukma)—notably maintaining **33% women representation**—combining central discipline with local linguistic trust, reinforced by Chhattisgarh’s locally recruited **Bastar Fighters**.

### Constitutional Guardrail - *Nandini Sundar* (2011) & Its May 2025 Epilogue

Counter-insurgency in a constitutional republic cannot outsource the state’s monopoly on violence to unaccountable militias. In 2005, Chhattisgarh backed ***Salwa Judum*** ("Purification Hunt" in Gondi), a civilian vigilante mobilization that recruited poorly trained, underpaid tribal youth (some barely 18 with Class 5 education) as **Special Police Officers (SPOs)**—popularly called *Koya Commandos*—under the Chhattisgarh Police Act, 2007, plunging Bastar into fratricidal displacement and severe human-rights violations.

On **July 5, 2011**, in ***Nandini Sundar & Ors. v. State of Chhattisgarh*** [(2011) 7 SCC 547], a Supreme Court bench of Justices B. Sudershan Reddy and S.S. Nijjar declared the deployment and arming of tribal SPOs in anti-Maoist combat **unconstitutional** under **Article 14** (arbitrary, unequal treatment) and **Article 21** (exposing untrained youth and local civilians to mortal danger without lawful training or safeguards). The Court ordered Chhattisgarh to immediately disarm all SPOs, cease backing *Salwa Judum* and *Koya Commandos*, and barred the Union Government from using central funds to finance SPO recruitment in COIN.

Crucially, the litigation reached a landmark constitutional epilogue on **May 15, 2025**: closing the 18-year case and a 2012 contempt petition challenging Chhattisgarh’s subsequent **Chhattisgarh Auxiliary Armed Police Force Act, 2011**, a bench of Justices B.V. Nagarathna and Satish Chandra Sharma held that **enacting a statute cannot constitute contempt of court**. Reaffirming the **Doctrine of Separation of Powers** (citing *Indian Aluminium Co. v. State of Kerala*, 1996), the Court clarified that a state legislature holds plenary power to remove the legal basis of a judgment via legislation, and any new law stands unless struck down as *ultra vires* on constitutional review. Operationally, the lesson endured: Chhattisgarh’s later success came not from unconstitutional vigilante mobs, but from absorbing local tribal youth into professionally trained, legally accountable regular police structures like the **DRG**.

## The 2024–2026 Decisive Contraction - FOBs, *Niyad Nellanar* & Operation Black Forest

If 2015–2023 steadily compressed the periphery of the Red Corridor, **2024–2026 shattered its Bastar core** through an aggressive **"Clear–Hold–Build"** campaign operationalized toward the Union Government’s declared **March 31, 2026** eradication deadline.

### Forward Operating Bases (FOBs) & *Niyad Nellanar* Saturation

The decisive tactical innovation was erasing the "security vacuum" inside hitherto impenetrable sanctuaries like **Abujhmad** (~4,000 sq km of unsurveyed forest across Narayanpur, Bijapur, Dantewada, and Gadchiroli) and south Sukma. Security forces established **408 new CAPF security camps / Forward Operating Bases (FOBs)** over seven years—supported by **68 night-landing helipads**—pushing permanent state presence deep into Maoist *Janathana Sarkar* strongholds, severing inter-district movement corridors, and choking PLGA logistics.

To convert military footprints into permanent political legitimacy, the Chhattisgarh Government launched the ***Niyad Nellanar Yojana*** ("Your Good Village" in the local Dandami/Gondi dialect) in **2024**. Designating new CRPF/police FOBs as **"Integrated Development Centres,"** the scheme mandates 100% saturation delivery of **50+ welfare schemes**—all-weather roads, electricity, PMAY houses, ration cards, Ayushman healthcare, Anganwadis, reopened schools (some shut for 40 years), mobile towers, FRA land titles, and revived weekly *haat bazaars*—across every village within a **5-km radius** (expanding outward), treating all resident households on par with Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs). When tribal villagers saw schools, rations, and health clinics arriving behind security camps, local recruitment dried up and intelligence tips surged.

### Operation Black Forest (April–May 2025) & Leadership Decapitation

Backed by sustained FOB encirclement and actionable HUMINT from surrendering cadres, joint forces executed the final dismantling of the CPI (Maoist) command apparatus across 2025–2026:

- **Operation Black Forest (April 21 – May 11, 2025):** The largest single anti-Maoist offensive in Indian history—a 21-day multi-jurisdictional joint campaign by CRPF, CoBRA, Chhattisgarh Police, DRG, STF, and Telangana/Maharashtra units across the treacherous **Karreguttalu Hills (KGH)** on the Chhattisgarh–Telangana border. Operating in extreme heat amid dense IED fields, forces cleared the hills, demolished Maoist training bunkers, technical/weapons dumps, and PLGA Battalion No. 1 staging hubs, neutralizing **31 Maoists**, recovering massive explosive caches, and triggering cascading arrests and surrenders (followed by *KGH-2* in February 2026).
- **Neutralization of General Secretary Basavaraju (May 21, 2025):** Days after Operation Black Forest concluded, DRG and joint units acting on intelligence from surrendered bodyguards engaged senior Maoist leadership inside **Abujhmad (Narayanpur district)** in a 50-hour encounter, neutralizing **27 Maoists**—including CPI (Maoist) General Secretary **Nambala Keshava Rao alias Basavaraju**. It marked the **first time in three decades** that a sitting General Secretary—the supreme military strategist behind the 2010 Dantewada and 2013 Jhiram Ghati attacks—was killed in combat.
- **Systemic Command Collapse (Late 2025 – Late 2026):** The Politburo and Central Committee—once numbering over 40 senior members and still 20 strong in January 2025—unraveled completely. Ideological chief **Mallojula Venugopal Rao alias Sonu** surrendered with 60 cadres in Gadchiroli (**October 2025**); dreaded south Bastar commander **Madvi Hidma** was neutralized in Andhra Pradesh (**November 2025**); Central Military Commission head **Thippiri Tirupathi alias Devuji** surrendered in Telangana (**early 2026**); and the last operational Politburo heavyweight in eastern India, **Misir Besra**, was arrested in Dhanbad, Jharkhand (**July 28, 2026**), followed by Central Committee member **Asim Mandal alias Akash** in Purba Medinipur, West Bengal (**September 17, 2026**—officially confirmed by Jharkhand Police/PTI as a negotiated surrender before the Kolkata Police STF, though described by state police/media as an arrest). In 2025 alone, joint operations saw over **2,391 surrenders** and **370 neutralizations**.

### Post-March 31, 2026 Geographic Contraction & Dual-Lens Assessment

A rigorous UPSC assessment must capture both the **official MHA statutory classification** and the **operational transition trajectory** post-March 31, 2026:

- **Progressive Spatial Shrinkage:** LWE-affected districts dropped from **126 (2014)** → **90 (April 2018)** → **70 (July 2021)** → **38 (April 2024)** → **18 (April 2025)** → **11 (October 2025, with only 3 "Most Affected" Bastar districts: Bijapur, Sukma, Narayanpur)** → **8 (December 2025)**.
- **Post-March 31, 2026 Status (Dual-Lens Precision):** Immediately following the March 31, 2026 deadline, interim security classifications isolated residual Maoist activity strictly to **2–4 micro-pockets** centered on Bastar (Bijapur, Sukma/Narayanpur fringes, Kanker) and Jharkhand’s Saranda forest (West Singhbhum). Subsequently, following mass surrenders and leadership neutralization, the MHA formally informed Parliament (**Rajya Sabha, July 29, 2026**) that **zero districts remain categorized as "LWE-affected"** nationwide for the first time in six decades—reclassifying **37 districts as "Legacy & Thrust" districts** (where active violence has ended but security and welfare saturation continue to block relapse) and retaining **1 "District of Concern"** (**West Singhbhum**, Jharkhand—tagged for Saranda remnants prior to Misir Besra’s arrest on July 28, 2026). Shortly thereafter (**Rajya Sabha reply, August 6, 2026** and subsequent MHA administrative summaries), following Besra’s capture, even that lone District of Concern was shifted into the **"Legacy & Thrust"** list—placing **all 38 SRE districts under "Legacy & Thrust"** with zero LWE-affected districts and zero Districts of Concern. Nationwide violent incidents plunged from **870 (310 fatalities) in 2014** to just **33 incidents (11 fatalities) in 2026 through July 29**, with police stations reporting violence dropping from 465 (2010) to 16.

### Sustaining Consolidation - Preventing Post-Insurgency Relapse

Operational victory over an armed wing does not automatically extinguish the underlying socio-political ecology. Sustaining a genuinely LWE-free heartland beyond 2026 hinges on resolving four post-conflict imperatives:

1. **Preventing Urban & Ideological Re-Incubation:** With jungle sanctuaries lost, residual sympathizers may pivot toward decentralized urban agitation, university campuses, labor disputes, and digital propaganda—requiring sharp intelligence discrimination that neutralizes clandestine violent mobilization without criminalizing lawful democratic dissent or tribal rights advocacy.
2. **Equitable Resource Governance over Extractive Rush:** Opening Abujhmad and Bastar’s vast iron-ore and mineral reserves post-pacification carries a severe risk: if rapid corporate mining bypasses **PESA Gram Sabha consent**, **FRA Community Forest Resource rights**, and local benefit-sharing via District Mineral Foundations (DMFs), state pacification will be perceived as "internal colonialism," seeding a future generation of grievances.
3. **Durable Surrender Rehabilitation & Demobilization:** Integrating over 3,000+ surrendered cadres into sustainable livelihoods (beyond short-term stipends) requires land/skill linkages, psychological counseling, protection from remnant retaliation, and ensuring surrendered youth do not drift into organized extortion or criminal syndicates.
4. **Calibrated Security Drawdown & Civil Policing Transition:** Over-militarized landscapes cannot normalize indefinitely. Retaining "Legacy & Thrust" CAPF deterrence while progressively transferring everyday order to responsive, culturally embedded civil police, local Panchayats, and accountable district administration—paired with transparent judicial inquiry into any contested encounter allegations—will cement democratic permanence.

## UPSC Interrogation Lens

### Verified UPSC Mains PYQs (2014–2026) & Prelims Anchors

### Prelims Trap Signature

1. **Central vs. State Force Attribution:** **CoBRA** and the **Bastarriya Battalion (241st Bn)** are wings of the **CRPF** (Union Ministry of Home Affairs), whereas **Greyhounds** (Andhra Pradesh/Telangana) and the **District Reserve Guard (DRG)** (Chhattisgarh) are **State Police** formations—NOT CAPFs.
2. ***Nandini Sundar* (2011) & May 2025 Separation of Powers Trap:** The July 2011 judgment barred Chhattisgarh from arming untrained civilian SPOs (*Salwa Judum*/*Koya Commandos*) under Articles 14 & 21; it did **not** declare state-raised regular tribal police units unconstitutional. Furthermore, in **May 2025**, the Supreme Court ruled that enacting the *Chhattisgarh Auxiliary Armed Police Force Act, 2011* did **not** amount to contempt of court, reaffirming legislative plenary power unless struck down via judicial review.
3. **SRE vs. SCA vs. *Niyad Nellanar* Distinction:** **SRE** reimburses *security/operational expenditure and surrender rehabilitation*, **SCA** funds *critical public infrastructure gaps* in erstwhile most LWE-affected districts, and ***Niyad Nellanar*** is a **Chhattisgarh State Government** saturation-welfare scheme centered around new FOBs—NOT a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.
4. **2026 MHA District Categorization Nuance:** Following March 31, 2026, MHA’s July 29, 2026 Rajya Sabha declaration classified **zero** districts as "LWE-affected," shifting **37 districts** into the **"Legacy & Thrust"** category (which *continues* to receive central security and developmental funds to prevent relapse) alongside **1 District of Concern** (West Singhbhum)—with MHA’s subsequent August 6, 2026 update shifting that lone District of Concern into "Legacy & Thrust" as well (**all 38 SRE districts as Legacy & Thrust**)—trapping candidates who assume "zero LWE-affected" means immediate termination of central funding or CAPF withdrawal.
