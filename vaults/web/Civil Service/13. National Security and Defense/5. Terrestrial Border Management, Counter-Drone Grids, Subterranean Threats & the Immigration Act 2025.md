# Terrestrial Border Management, Counter-Drone Grids, Subterranean Threats & the Immigration Act 2025

India’s terrestrial periphery is not a uniform Westphalian perimeter; it is a **15,106.7 km** mosaic of unresolved cartographic lines, hyper-arid salt marshes, shifting alluvial deltas, glaciated super-high-altitude deserts, and ethnically contiguous jungle ridges. Managing this frontier demands reconciling two competing state imperatives: **hard kinetic sealing** against state-sponsored terrorism, narco-arms trafficking, and grey-zone territorial encroachment, alongside **frictionless commercial and cultural connectivity** with friendly neighbors and trans-border tribal communities.

Over the last decade—and accelerating sharply across **2024–2026**—India’s border security paradigm has migrated from static barbed-wire fences and human foot-patrolling toward a **three-dimensional multi-domain grid**. Adversaries have weaponized both the airspace directly above the fence (low-signature hexacopters and drone swarms) and the soft alluvium beneath it (cross-border infiltration tunnels). India’s institutional counter-response fuses indigenous counter-UAS interceptors, electronic sensor walls, strategic Himalayan tunneling, saturation border-village development, and a unified statutory immigration regime under the **Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025**.

## The "One Border, One Force" Architecture and India's Land Frontiers

Following the 1999 Kargil conflict, the **Group of Ministers (GoM) on Reforming the National Security System (2001)** diagnosed fragmented command and overlapping jurisdictions as structural vulnerabilities along India’s frontiers. Two institutional pillars emerged from this review: the creation of a dedicated **Department of Border Management (DoBM)** inside the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) in **2004**, and the operational doctrine of **"One Border, One Border Guarding Force" (OBOBGF)**.

Under OBOBGF, a single dedicated Central Armed Police Force (CAPF) is assigned primary peacetime custodianship and lead-intelligence responsibility for each international border to build terrain mastery, uniform SOPs, and unambiguous accountability. In administrative practice, however, active military lines of dispute fracture this purity. Along the **Line of Control (LoC)** with Pakistan and the **Line of Actual Control (LAC)** with China, the Indian Army (Ministry of Defence) exercises operational primacy alongside or over CAPFs (MHA), creating persistent inter-ministerial coordination friction.

**Numeric Parity and the Seventh Neighbor Reconciliation:** Official MHA records fix India’s total international land border at **15,106.7 km** across **seven** neighboring countries (whereas legacy NCERT texts round this to *"over 15,100 km"* or *"15,200 km"*). The **six** actively administered and guarded frontiers sum to **15,000.7 km**; the remaining **106 km** is India’s de jure frontier with **Afghanistan** along the **Wakhan Corridor**, contiguous with Gilgit-Baltistan in Ladakh UT currently under Pakistan’s illegal occupation.

### Operational Nuances Across Key Frontiers

**Pakistan Frontier and the 50-km BSF Jurisdiction Debate:** While **93.25%** of the **2,289.66 km** International Border is physically fenced with floodlights and concertina coils, seasonal riverine gaps along the Ravi, Sutlej, and Chenab—plus shifting sand dunes in Rajasthan and tidal creeks in Sir Creek—create structural blind spots. Along the **~192 km** Jammu stretch (which Islamabad unilaterally calls the *"Working Boundary"*), BSF faces direct sniper/mortar harassment and subterranean tunneling.

In **October 2021**, MHA exercised powers under **Section 139 of the BSF Act, 1968** to standardize BSF’s operational search, seizure, and arrest belt to a uniform **50 km** inside the international border across **Punjab, West Bengal, and Assam** (up from 15 km), while narrowing Gujarat’s zone from 80 km to 50 km and retaining Rajasthan at 50 km. While operationally defended as vital to intercept long-range drone drops landing 10–25 km inland, border states challenged the move before the Supreme Court (original suit under Article 131 by Punjab) as an encroachment on **Entry 1 & 2 of the State List** (Public Order and Police).

**China Frontier and Grey-Zone Salami-Slicing:** Unlike the Pakistan border, the **3,488 km LAC** lacks mutually agreed ground demarcation and exchanges of maps in critical pockets (notably Depsang, Demchok, Hot Springs, and Tawang). China’s **Land Borders Law (effective January 2022)** legally institutionalizes civilian-military fusion along disputed lines, manifested through over 600 dual-use ***Xiaokang* (well-off) border villages** built along the Tibetan frontier to cement territorial claims without firing a shot.

Because **ITBP** operates under MHA while the **Indian Army** answers to MoD, dual command along forward LAC passes periodically generates turf friction over intelligence sharing, rules of engagement, and logistics. Successive strategic reviews—including the Naresh Chandra Task Force—have examined placing ITBP under Army operational control during peacetime, though MHA has resisted to retain paramilitary flexibility.

**Bangladesh Frontier and Riverine Porosity:** Despite **79.08%** physical fencing, nearly **857 km** remain unfenced due to meandering rivers (Brahmaputra, Teesta, Ichamati, Surma), ephemeral silt islands (*chars*), dense settlements lying directly on the **Zero Line**, and bilateral norms barring permanent defensive structures within **150 yards** of the international boundary. The **2015 Land Boundary Agreement (100th Constitutional Amendment)** eliminated 162 chaotic enclaves (*chhitmahals*), removing a major legal grey zone for smugglers.

However, political upheaval in Dhaka since **August 2024**, combined with lingering non-cooperation from select border states on land acquisition for pending West Bengal stretches, has forced BSF to rely on floating Border Out Posts (BOPs), riverine interceptor craft, and electronic laser-sensor walls.

**Nepal and Bhutan Open Frontiers:** Open borders under the **1950 Indo-Nepal** and **1949/2007 India-Bhutan** treaties treat mobility as a strategic trust asset rather than a vulnerability. Consequently, **Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)** relies on intelligence-led screening, biometric passenger checks at designated transit hubs, and civic goodwill rather than fences. Yet Nepal’s porous Terai plains remain a favored transit funnel for third-country intelligence operatives, fake Indian currency notes (FICN), and contraband narcotics, demanding frictionless coordination with Nepal’s Armed Police Force (APF).

## Indo-Myanmar Frontier Overhaul (2024-2026)

For over seven decades, the **1,643 km** India–Myanmar border functioned not as a Westphalian barrier but as a **negotiated ethno-ecological borderland**. Drawn arbitrarily by colonial cartographers along the Patkai–Chin–Arakan watershed, the boundary bisected living Naga, Kuki-Zo, Chin, Mizo, and Tangkhul families—even slicing straight through individual villages like **Longwa** in Nagaland’s Mon district, where the Angh’s (chief’s) house straddles both sovereign jurisdictions.

### Rise and Security Breakdown of the Free Movement Regime (FMR)

Originating in **1950 amendments to the Passport Rules** (initially allowing 40 km cross-border access, later trimmed to 16 km) and formalized under the **2018 India–Myanmar Land Border Crossing Agreement**, the **Free Movement Regime (FMR)** allowed hill tribes residing within **16 km** on either side to cross without a visa or passport for up to **14 days** using border passes.

Two cascading shocks between **2021 and 2023** shattered the equilibrium of this informal governance model:
- **Myanmar’s Post-Coup Civil War (February 2021 onward):** Collapse of the Myanmar military junta’s (*Tatmadaw*) territorial control across Sagaing Region and Chin State pushed over **78,000 Chin and Sagaing refugees** into Mizoram and Manipur. Anti-junta Ethnic Armed Organisations (EAOs) and People’s Defence Forces (PDFs) proliferated alongside Indian insurgent sanctuaries (PLA-Manipur, ULFA-I, NSCN-K factions) in Myanmar’s ungoverned Sagaing tracts.
- **Arms, Narcotics, and the Manipur Ethnic Conflict (May 2023 onward):** Myanmar’s borderlands form the western edge of the **Golden Triangle**, fueling massive cross-border trafficking of synthetic methamphetamine (*Yaba* tablets), Burmese opium, and military-grade small arms. During the protracted Meitei–Kuki-Zo conflict in Manipur, both state authorities and central security assessments flagged unregulated FMR corridors as vectors for cross-border militant reinforcement, demographic anxiety, and illicit weaponry.

### From Abolition to Regulated 10-km Biometric Containment (2024-2026)

Between 2024 and 2026, New Delhi executed its sharpest eastern frontier pivot since Independence—transitioning from *"negotiated informality"* to **securitized territorial hardening**:
- **February 8, 2024 — FMR Suspension Announcement:** MHA formally announced its decision to scrap the 16-km FMR and recommended immediate suspension to safeguard internal security and preserve the demographic equilibrium of Northeastern border states.
- **March 2024 — CCS ₹31,031.9 Crore Fencing Mandate:** The Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) granted in-principle approval for a **10-year, ₹31,031.9 crore** border infrastructure package. This covers **1,610.2 km of physical fencing and border roads** (₹20,264.4 crore, including 5-year maintenance) plus **64 feeder roads**—58 connecting **Assam Rifles Company Operating Bases (COBs)** and 6 approach roads (₹10,767.5 crore).
- **December 2024 — Calibrated 10-km Biometric Border-Pass Protocol:** Confronting fierce institutional pushback from Mizoram and Nagaland—both state assemblies passed resolutions opposing hard fencing and unilateral FMR abolition—MHA replaced the open 16-km regime with a **tightly regulated 10-km corridor**. Cross-border movement is now restricted exclusively to **designated biometric entry/exit gates** manned by Assam Rifles personnel who issue time-bound QR/biometric border passes (out of 43 planned crossing points, roughly **20–22 gates** became functional amid localized boycotts).

### The Policy-Execution Gap and Dual-Control Friction

Parliamentary disclosures between **December 2025 and March 2026** exposed a stark divergence between policy intent and engineering reality. Out of the 1,643-km frontier, physical fencing completed stood at barely **9.214 km to 43.75 km** (depending on whether pre-existing Moreh stretches and partially sanctioned segments are counted), with only **~11.5 km** of new border roads finished out of 3,194.8 km approved.

Three structural bottlenecks constrain execution: extreme monsoon-lashed Patkai terrain with fragile geology; acute **inter-state federal divergence** (Imphal valley groups demanding immediate sealing versus Mizoram, Nagaland, and Kuki-Zo/Naga bodies resisting kin separation); and the **dual-control anomaly of Assam Rifles**, where administrative budgets flow from MHA while operational deployment answers to the Indian Army (MoD), diluting single-chain border-guarding accountability.

## Asymmetric Border Technology Threats - Drones and Subterranean Tunnels

Physical fencing operates in two dimensions along the ground plane. Over the last five years, hostile non-state syndicates backed by state intelligence agencies have systematically outflanked India’s western border fence along the **vertical axis**—both overhead through low-cost unmanned aerial vehicles and underground through alluvial infiltration tunnels.

### The Cross-Border Drone Menace and Swarm Evolution

Following India’s 2019 suspension of cross-LoC trade and tightening of fence surveillance in J&K, Pakistan-based terror outfits (LeT, JeM, Khalistani modules) and narco-cartels pivoted decisively to **commercial and modified multi-rotor UAVs** (predominantly Chinese-made **DJI Matrice, DJI Mavic, and custom heavy-lift hexacopters**). Operating mostly during nocturnal windows (**22:00 to 04:00 hours**) across Punjab (Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Ferozepur, Gurdaspur), Jammu (Samba, Kathua, Poonch), and increasingly Rajasthan (Barmer, Sri Ganganagar), these drones drop 2–10 kg payloads of Afghan/Baloch heroin, pistols, AK-series rifles, grenades, magnetic sticky bombs, and RDX.

The empirical escalation is steep:
- **BSF Punjab Frontier Interceptions:** Surged from **21 drones (2022)** → **107 (2023)** → **294 (2024)** → **~251–287 BSF interceptions** (with **313 joint Punjab Police–BSF recoveries** out of **791 western-front drone intrusions** logged by MoD in **2025**).
- **Tactical Adaptation (2025–2026):** In the first 100 days of 2026 alone, BSF seized **331 kg of heroin** in Punjab (triple the 2025 daily rate) despite fewer physical drone recoveries—signaling a shift toward **higher-payload drop-and-return flights** and frequency-hopping, jam-resistant airframes. Simultaneously, **Operation Sindoor (May 2025)** validated the threat of coordinated tactical **drone swarms** targeting forward military bases and border infrastructure.

### Physics of Counter-UAS and India's Indigenous Anti-Drone Triad

Neutralizing small Group-1/Group-2 drones is a classic **signal-to-noise physics problem**. Conventional air-defense radars are calibrated to filter out slow, small objects (birds, foliage sway) as clutter. Commercial quadcopters feature tiny **Radar Cross Sections (RCS < 0.01 m²)** built from plastic or carbon-fiber composites, fly below radar horizon at 50–150 meters altitude, emit faint thermal signatures from electric motors, and increasingly navigate via pre-programmed **GNSS waypoints or inertial/optical navigation**—rendering radio-frequency (RF) control-link jammers ineffective.

Consequently, modern Counter-Unmanned Aircraft Systems (C-UAS) operate as a **multi-sensor detection-to-neutralization kill chain** fusing **3D micro-Doppler radar** (which identifies the unique electromagnetic modulation of spinning rotor blades), **RF spectrum analyzers**, **electro-optical/infrared (EO/IR) thermal cameras**, and **acoustic arrays**. Neutralization splits into two complementary layers:
- **Soft-Kill (Non-Kinetic):** Disrupts the command-and-control link via **RF jamming** or hijacks navigation via **GNSS/GPS spoofing** (injecting counterfeit satellite timing signals to force landing or diversion). Soft-kill carries minimal collateral hazard over populated border villages and preserves intact airframes for **BSF’s Drone Forensic Lab** (which decoded flight telemetry showing roughly **75%** of Amritsar-sector drones launched from Lahore precincts). Its limitation: autonomous pre-mapped or fiber-optic drones ignore RF jamming.
- **Hard-Kill (Kinetic / Directed Energy):** Physically destroys the airframe via **high-energy lasers**, **interceptor drones**, **smart air-burst guns**, or **low-cost micro-missiles**. Hard-kill resolves the fatal economic asymmetry of drone warfare—preventing defenders from firing ₹5–10 crore surface-to-air missiles at ₹2 lakh commercial UAVs.

India’s **2024–2026 indigenous counter-drone architecture** rests on three flagship platforms progressively networked into the IAF’s **IACCS** and Army’s **Akashteer** air-defense grids:
1. **DRDO–BEL D4 System (*Drone Detect, Deter and Destroy*) & IDDIS:** Designed by **DRDO** and manufactured/productionized by Navratna PSU **Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL)**. Provides 360° micro-Doppler radar, EO/IR, and RF detection up to **~4 km**, pairing **soft-kill** (RF/GNSS jamming) with a **hard-kill 1–2 kW directed-energy laser** neutralizer (~1–1.25 km lethal range). In **May 2025**, BEL signed a **₹572 crore** contract with Indian Army Air Defence for the upgraded **Integrated Drone Detection and Interdiction System (IDDIS)**.
2. ***Indrajaal* Autonomous Drone Dome (*Infra* & *Ranger*):** Developed by Hyderabad-based private defense firm **Grene Robotics** (often deployed alongside PSU/BEL sensor grids, though architected privately on Grene’s proprietary AI command platform, **SkyOS**). Unlike point-defense jammers, *Indrajaal* networks distributed radars, RF detectors, spoofers, and kinetic interceptors into a wide-area autonomous C5ISRT dome protecting **1,000–4,000 sq km**. Following live validation at naval ports during **Operation Sindoor (May 2025)**, Grene launched ***Indrajaal Infra*** (May 2025, for ports/refineries/border hubs), ***Indrajaal Ranger*** (November 2025, mobile anti-drone patrol vehicle), and won **~₹100 crore in MoD contracts (January 2026)** for western naval bases and eastern border sites.
3. ***Bhargavastra* Micro-Missile Counter-Swarm System:** Engineered by Nagpur-based private firm **Solar Defence and Aerospace Limited (SDAL)** alongside **Economic Explosives Ltd (EEL)**. Successfully field-tested in hard-kill mode at the **Seaward Firing Range, Gopalpur (Odisha) in May 2025**, *Bhargavastra* is India’s first indigenous low-cost counter-swarm micro-missile system. Its radar detects small incoming UAVs at **6–10 km**, and its mobile launcher fires up to **64 guided micro-missiles/micro-rockets in a single salvo** out to **>2.5 km**, neutralizing saturation swarms within seconds at a fraction of conventional missile cost.

### Subterranean Threats - Jammu Alluvial Infiltration Tunnels

While rocky Himalayan geology deters digging along the Kashmir LoC, the **~192 km Jammu International Border** spanning **Samba, Kathua, Hiranagar, R.S. Pura, and Arnia** rests atop deep, soft **quaternary alluvial loam** laced with seasonal rainwater *nullahs* (Basantar, Tarnah, Ujh) and dense 8-foot wild *sarkanda* elephant grass. Hostile handlers exploit this soil mechanics to excavate covert cross-border tunnels passing directly underneath BSF’s double-row concertina fence.

Since 2001, Indian forces have uncovered roughly **22 cross-border tunnels** (including renewed discoveries and heightened alerts through **2024–2025** following terror migration into the Jammu–Pir Panjal belt). Typically measuring **15–30 feet deep**, **150–400 meters long**, and **2–3 feet in diameter**, these tunnels are shored up with Pakistani-marked sandbags and emerge inside overgrown border shrubbery where over-ground workers (OGWs) extract infiltrating squads onto the Jammu–Pathankot National Highway (NH-44).

**Anti-Tunneling Physics and Countermeasures:** Detecting narrow air voids 20–30 feet underground presents severe geophysical constraints:
- **Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR):** Transmits high-frequency electromagnetic pulses into the subsurface and maps reflections caused by contrasts in **dielectric permittivity** between solid soil and hollow air cavities. *Physics limitation:* Wet clay and monsoon-saturated alluvium strongly attenuate high-frequency EM waves (skin-depth loss), limiting practical penetration and forcing operators to trade resolution for depth.
- **Geophones and Distributed Acoustic/Seismic Sensors (DAS):** Buried geophone arrays and fiber-optic acoustic cables continuously monitor low-frequency **seismic P-waves and S-waves** generated by manual digging, crowbars, or subterranean footsteps. Machine-learning filters separate clandestine excavation harmonics from background tractor, wind, and cattle noise.
- **Physical Interdiction Engineering:** Recognizing sensor limits in waterlogged fields, BSF pairs technical sweeps with systematic brute-force ground engineering. In **January 2025**, following the Jammu terror surge, BSF excavated a **25-km anti-tunnel intercept trench** (approximately **4 feet wide and 10 feet deep**) parallel behind vulnerable Samba–Kathua fence sectors, supplemented by mechanical clearing of *sarkanda* scrub, soil tilling up to the zero line, and bi-daily physical probing extending 400 meters inward.

## Border Infrastructure, Smart Grids, Strategic Tunnels and Border Ports

Securing 15,106.7 km of heterogeneous frontier requires synchronizing four institutional layers: **electronic smart-fencing**, **high-altitude military mobility**, **community-anchored demographic resilience**, and **digitized trade facilitation**.

### CIBMS, BOLD-QIT, and Committee-Led Reforms

**Madhukar Gupta Committee (2016) and CIBMS:** Constituted after the January 2016 Pathankot airbase attack exposed porous riverine gaps along the Punjab IB, the **Madhukar Gupta Committee** conceptualized the **Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System (CIBMS)**. CIBMS replaces labor-intensive linear patrolling with a **three-pillar electronic shield**: (i) multi-layered sensors (**LASER walls**, thermal imagers, uncooled infrared cameras, unattended seismic/acoustic ground sensors, aerostats, and micro-radars); (ii) an encrypted fiber-optic/microwave command-and-control network fusing live feeds into a single operational picture; and (iii) motorized **Quick Reaction Teams (QRTs)** dispatched only upon verified intrusion alerts.

Implemented operationally by BSF under MHA’s **Border Infrastructure and Management (BIM)** umbrella scheme—a **100% Central Sector Scheme** with a **₹13,020 crore outlay (FY 2021–22 to 2025–26)** funding fencing, floodlights, BOPs, helipads, and smart sensors—CIBMS debuted via two **5-km Jammu IB pilots** and its centerpiece riverine grid: **BOLD-QIT (*Border Electronically Dominated QRT Interception Technique*)**. Deployed across **61 km** of the shifting, un-fenceable Brahmaputra floodplain in **Dhubri district, Assam** (Indo-Bangladesh border), BOLD-QIT uses microwave links, day/night cameras, and underwater/ground sensors to seal riverine infiltration channels, with Phase-II expansion announced across sensitive West Bengal and Tripura stretches in **2024–2025**.

*Operational Critique:* Independent evaluations and parliamentary audits flag steep lifecycle costs of CIBMS (exceeding ₹10–15 crore per km in riverine stretches), frequent false-alarm fatigue triggered by fog, heavy monsoon foliage, and stray animals, erratic forward electricity grids, and heavy dependence on imported Israeli/European sensor spares.

**Lt. Gen. D.B. Shekatkar Committee (2016) Reforms on Border Infrastructure:** Addressing chronic delays in the 73 **Indo-China Border Roads (ICBRs)**—where CAG audits once highlighted multi-year cost and schedule overruns—the Ministry of Defence implemented three structural Shekatkar Committee reforms from **May 2020 onward**:
1. **Capacity Outsourcing & Mandatory EPC Mode:** Road works exceeding the Border Roads Organisation’s (BRO) optimal internal bandwidth are outsourced to private contractors, with mandatory **Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC)** contracting for all projects above **₹100 crore**.
2. **Pre-Award Statutory Clearance Mandate:** Works under EPC mode can be awarded only after **90% of land acquisition, forest, and environmental clearances** are bundled into Detailed Project Report (DPR) approval—eliminating mid-project litigation stalls.
3. **Delegated Mechanization Ceiling:** BRO’s financial authority for domestic and foreign procurement of heavy construction machinery (rock drills, F-90 snow-cutters, hot-mix plants, geotextiles) was raised over thirteen-fold from **₹7.5 crore to ₹100 crore**.

### Strategic Himalayan Tunnels - Breaking Winter Isolation

For decades, heavy snowfall severed India’s military road arteries into Ladakh and Tawang for four to six months annually, forcing exorbitant Indian Air Force winter air-maintenance sorties. Between 2020 and late 2026, four landmark Himalayan tunnels—built using the **New Austrian Tunnelling Method (NATM)** suited to fragile, seismically active Himalayan rock strata—have transformed India’s forward mobilization geometry:

- **Atal Tunnel (Rohtang, Himachal Pradesh — Executed by BRO, MoD):** Operational since October 2020; **9.02 km** single-tube bi-lane horseshoe tunnel beneath Rohtang Pass at **~10,000 ft (3,000–3,100 m)**. Cuts Manali–Leh transit by 46 km and grants year-round access to Lahaul–Spiti, forming the southern base of India’s interior Ladakh corridor.
- **Sela Tunnel (West Kameng, Arunachal Pradesh — Executed by BRO under Project Vartak, MoD):** Inaugurated **March 9, 2024** (₹825 crore); world’s **longest bi-lane road tunnel above 13,000 ft**. Built on the **Balipara–Chariduar–Tawang (BCT)** road (NH-13), the complex includes **Tunnel 1 (980 m single-tube)**, **Tunnel 2 (1,555 m twin-tube with parallel escape tunnel)**, and connecting approach roads (~12 km total alignment). Eliminates winter closure of the 13,700-ft Sela Pass and shields Indian Army **IV Corps** convoys moving toward the Tawang LAC from Chinese artillery observation.
- **Zojila Tunnel (J&K–Ladakh — Executed by NHIDCL under MoRTH, *NOT* BRO):** **13.153 km** main bi-directional single-tube horseshoe tunnel (often cited as **~14.15 km** with portals/structures; ₹6,808 crore) between **Baltal (Ganderbal, J&K)** and **Minamarg (Drass/Kargil, Ladakh)** on **NH-1** at **~11,578 ft (2,900–3,310 m)**. Achieved its **historic main-tunnel breakthrough on June 9, 2026** (85% overall work complete; target commissioning **February 2028**). Paired with the **6.4-km Sonamarg (Z-Morh) Tunnel** inaugurated in **January 2025**, it slashes Zoji La crossing times from 90+ minutes to 15 minutes, creating Asia’s longest bi-directional high-altitude tunnel and ending Srinagar–Leh winter isolation.
- **Shinkun La Tunnel (Himachal–Ladakh — Executed by BRO, MoD):** First blast witnessed by the Prime Minister on **July 26, 2024** (25th Kargil Vijay Diwas). A **4.1 km twin-tube tunnel** (₹1,681.5 crore) bored at **~15,800 ft** beneath the 16,580-ft Shinku-La pass along the **Nimmu–Padam–Darcha (NPD)** third axis linking Lahaul to Zanskar and Leh. Upon completion, it becomes the **world’s highest highway tunnel**—critically positioned deep inside the Himalayan interior beyond both Pakistani and Chinese long-range artillery observation.

### People-Centric Bordering - Vibrant Villages Programme I & II

Hard security infrastructure fails if border villages depopulate into ghost hamlets, forfeiting human intelligence (*HUMINT*) and territorial occupancy. Whereas the legacy **Border Area Development Programme (BADP, launched 1987)** operated as a scattered, gap-filling Centrally Sponsored Scheme often diluted across state budgets, New Delhi pivoted to targeted, saturation-convergence development designed to turn frontier citizens into the **"eyes and ears" of border guarding forces**:

- **Vibrant Villages Programme–I (VVP-I):** Approved **February 15, 2023** as a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS)** with a **₹4,800 crore outlay** (FY 2022–23 to 2025–26, including ₹2,500 crore earmarked for roads). Targets comprehensive saturation of **2,967 border villages (662 priority)** across **46 blocks in 19 districts** spanning **4 States and 1 UT** exclusively along the **northern (China) border**—Arunachal Pradesh (455 priority), Himachal Pradesh (75), Uttarakhand (51), Sikkim (46), and Ladakh UT (35). By mid-2026, over **2,906 projects** had been sanctioned via MHA and ministerial convergence (tourism circuits, cooperatives, medicinal herbs, renewable energy, 4G telecom, health, and education).
- **Vibrant Villages Programme–II (VVP-II):** Approved by the Union Cabinet on **April 2, 2025** as a **100% Central Sector Scheme**—eliminating state matching-grant delays—with a **₹6,839 crore outlay** running through **FY 2028–29**. Expands saturation development to **1,954 select strategic villages across 334 blocks** in **15 States and 2 UTs** abutting all International Land Borders *other than* the northern LAC stretches already covered under VVP-I (covering Assam, Bihar, Gujarat, J&K UT, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tripura, UP, WB, plus non-VVP-I international border blocks of Arunachal, Ladakh UT, Sikkim, and Uttarakhand). All-weather road connectivity is converged under **PMGSY-IV** (Ministry of Rural Development), while a **High-Powered Committee chaired by the Cabinet Secretary** holds explicit authority to relax rigid central schematic norms for difficult frontier geography.

### LPAI, Integrated Check Posts (ICPs), and Digital Land Ports

Choking illicit smuggling requires lowering the transaction cost of legal cross-border commerce. Created as a **statutory body** under the **Land Ports Authority of India Act, 2010** (operationalized March 2012), **LPAI** functions under MHA’s **Department of Border Management**—not the Ministry of Commerce or Ministry of Ports.

LPAI constructs and manages **Integrated Check Posts (ICPs)**—sanitized, single-window border complexes uniting Customs (CBIC), Immigration (BoI), Border Guarding Forces (BSF/SSB/AR), plant/animal quarantine, warehousing, and automated weighbridges within one secured perimeter. Beginning with **Attari (Punjab, 2012)**, LPAI expanded to **15 operational land ports by 2026**, including **Petrapole (West Bengal)**—South Asia’s busiest land port handling over 70% of Indo-Bangladesh overland trade—alongside Agartala, Sabroom, Srimantapur, Sutarkandi, Dawki, Moreh, Raxaul, Jogbani, Rupaidiha, and Darranga.

In **2026**, LPAI and CBIC operationalized a decisive digital layer: **ICEGATE–LPMS integration** (launched at ICP Agartala in January 2026) and the **Land Port Management System (LPMS) *'Vinimay'*** platform. By linking customs risk-management engines, e-Sanchit documentation, and security databases onto a single automated gate workflow, *Vinimay* cuts manual paperwork by nearly **90%** and reduces cross-border truck dwell times by **40–60%**—aligning trade facilitation with real-time security risk targeting.

## The Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 - Unified Entry and Expulsion Law

For over a century, India governed foreign entry, domestic movement, registration, and carrier accountability through a fragmented patchwork of **four colonial and legacy statutes**—creating statutory overlaps, light deterrence penalties, and weak legal footing for digital border screening. Exercising exclusive legislative competence under **Union List Entry 17** (*citizenship, naturalisation, and aliens*) and **Entry 19** (*admission into, emigration, and expulsion from India; passports and visas*), Parliament passed the **Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 (Act No. 13 of 2025)** on **April 2, 2025** (Presidential assent **April 4, 2025**).

Notified for enforcement on **September 1, 2025** alongside the *Immigration and Foreigners Rules, 2025* and *Exemption Order, 2025*, the Act repeals and consolidates **four legacy laws** in their entirety:
1. **Passport (Entry into India) Act, 1920** (which governed entry passports/visas; *note: the Passports Act, 1967 governing issuance of Indian passports to citizens remains untouched*).
2. **Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939**.
3. **Foreigners Act, 1946**.
4. **Immigration (Carriers' Liability) Act, 2000**.

### Key Statutory and Institutional Pillars

- **Statutory Foundation for the Bureau of Immigration (BoI):** Established in **1971** merely via executive order under the Intelligence Bureau, the BoI now receives explicit statutory authority headed by a **Commissioner**. Field enforcement operates through **Foreigners Regional Registration Offices (FRROs)**—expanded to **20 operational hubs by late 2026** (adding Jaipur, Chandigarh, Guwahati, Aizawl, Amaravati, etc.) with an MHA target to cover all state capitals by 2027—and district **Foreigners Registration Officers (FROs)** (typically the District Superintendent of Police).
- **Biometric Screening and Carrier Obligations:** Mandates valid passports/visas and biometric verification for all incoming and outgoing travelers across designated land, air, and sea immigration posts. International airlines, shipping lines, and land carriers must transmit **Advance Passenger Information (API)** and crew manifests prior to arrival, remain financially liable for transporting improperly documented passengers, and bear statutory responsibility for removing inadmissible entrants.
- **Ecosystem-Wide Institutional Reporting Duties:** Widens domestic surveillance of overstayers by imposing direct statutory reporting obligations on **accommodation keepers/hotels (Section 8)**, **universities and educational institutions**, and **hospitals/nursing homes providing indoor treatment (Section 10)**. Under **Rule 17 of the 2025 Rules**, details of every foreign national—**explicitly including Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholders**—must be logged electronically upon arrival and onward departure and retained for at least **one year** for inspection.
- **Escalated Deterrence and Control of Premises:** Enhances criminal penalties to **up to 5 years imprisonment and/or ₹5 lakh fine** for entry without valid travel documents or unauthorized entry into protected/restricted areas, and **2–7 years imprisonment plus ₹1–10 lakh fine** for knowingly using, supplying, or facilitating forged passports/visas. Empowers the Central Government to regulate, restrict access to, or shut down physical premises frequented by foreigners on national-security or public-order grounds.
- **Calibrated Exemptions (Section 33) vs. Constitutional Due-Process Tension:** The *Exemption Order, 2025* preserves visa-free treaty entry for Nepal/Bhutan nationals and retains targeted entry exemptions for specified persecuted minority communities from Afghanistan, Bangladesh, and Pakistan who entered up to **December 31, 2024** (requiring visa regularization for continued stay).

**Rule-of-Law Audit:** While the Act streamlines legitimate travel via e-FRRO digitization and modernizes counter-infiltration deterrence, legal scholars highlight three unresolved constitutional gaps. First, India still lacks a dedicated **statutory refugee or asylum framework** (remaining a non-signatory to the 1951 UN Refugee Convention), leaving persecuted asylum seekers—such as Chin or Rohingya refugees—administratively lumped with undocumented economic migrants. Second, expansive executive discretion over detention, blacklisting, and **Leave India Notices** offers no independent statutory appellate tribunal, forcing aggrieved foreigners or OCI holders to seek extraordinary **Article 226 High Court writ remedies**. Third, strict domestic deportation powers cannot resolve mass undocumented migration without formal **bilateral repatriation treaties** with Bangladesh and Myanmar.

> - **Passports Act Repeal Trap:** The **Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025** repeals the colonial **Passport (Entry into India) Act, 1920** (alongside the 1939, 1946, and 2000 Acts), **NOT** the **Passports Act, 1967** (which continues to govern issuance of Indian passports to Indian citizens).
