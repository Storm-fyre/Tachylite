# Chapter 7 - The Indian Ocean Littoral, Island Diplomacy & Regional Maritime Security Grids

Alfred Thayer Mahan's nineteenth-century axiom—that sea power shapes continental destiny—applies nowhere more acutely than in the **Indian Ocean Region (IOR)**. Bounded by over 40 littoral states housing ~40% of humanity, this third-largest ocean handles roughly **two-thirds of global seaborne oil shipments, one-third of bulk cargo, and half of all container traffic**, funneled through narrow maritime chokepoints from Bab-el-Mandeb and Hormuz to Malacca.

For India, whose peninsular geography projects 1,600 km deep into this central sea lane of communication (SLOC), the surrounding island states are neither peripheral backwaters nor mere tourist archipelagos. **Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Mauritius, Seychelles, Madagascar, and Comoros** act as unsinkable forward monitoring platforms astride India's energy lifelines—turning island diplomacy into the frontline contest against Chinese dual-use maritime encirclement.

## India - Sri Lanka - Debt Diplomacy, Tamil Devolution & Energy - Port Integration

### The 13th Amendment and Palk Bay Fishermen Frictions

Separated from Tamil Nadu by the ~30-km-wide **Palk Strait**, India–Sri Lanka relations remain uniquely conditioned by sub-national domestic politics on both shores. Two legacy fault lines continue to test bilateral equilibrium:

- **13th Amendment and Tamil Devolution:** Enacted following the **July 29, 1987 Indo–Lanka Accord** (signed by PM Rajiv Gandhi and President J.R. Jayewardene), the 13th Amendment recognized Tamil as an official language and created elected **Provincial Councils**. Successive Sinhala-majority governments, however, withheld full devolution of **land and police powers** and delayed Provincial Council elections for over a decade. While President **Anura Kumara Dissanayake's** National People's Power (NPP) administration initiated selective release of military-held private lands in northern Tamil provinces (November 2025), Tamil parties fear a proposed new unitary constitution could dilute existing protections. India has held a steady line—reiterating calls for early Provincial Council elections and full implementation of 13A at the **60th UNHRC session (September 2025)**, during **Vice President C.P. Radhakrishnan's visit (April 2026)**, and during **Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri's Colombo visit (August 5, 2026)**.
- **Palk Bay and the Katchatheevu Dispute:** Rooted in the **1974 and 1976 Indo–Sri Lankan Maritime Boundary Agreements**, India recognized Sri Lankan sovereignty over the uninhabited **285-acre Katchatheevu islet** while preserving traditional Indian access for resting, drying nets, and visiting St. Anthony's shrine without visas; the 1976 exchange of letters barred fishing in each other's Exclusive Economic Zones. Chronic conflict persists because mechanized Indian **bottom-trawlers**—having depleted Indian coastal stocks—cross the International Maritime Boundary Line (IMBL), damaging the livelihoods of northern Sri Lankan artisanal fishermen and prompting routine naval arrests and boat confiscations. During Supreme Court hearings on pending petitions challenging the 1974 ceding (*A.K. Selvaraj v. Union of India*, adjourned to February 2027), **Attorney General R. Venkataramani explicitly informed the Court on October 6–7, 2026 that India will not reopen the 1974/1976 agreements**, citing "excellent relations" with Colombo, while the Bench urged sensitizing Tamil Nadu fishermen against boundary violations.

### Countering Chinese Dual - Use Footprints and Spy Vessels

Sri Lanka's debt-driven embrace of Beijing's Belt and Road Initiative produced two acute security vulnerabilities immediately south of India's peninsula:

- **Hambantota Port & Colombo Port City:** Unable to service Chinese commercial loans, Colombo handed over a **70%–85% equity stake (85% in commercial leaseholder HIPG, reducible to 70% within 10 years)** in **Hambantota Port** to China Merchants Port Holdings (CMPort) on a **99-year lease in 2017**, alongside the **$1.4 billion Colombo Port City** reclamation Special Economic Zone—both sitting directly astride the main East–West SLOC.
- **Surveillance Vessels & The Moratorium:** Docking of PLA Navy tracking vessel ***Yuan Wang 5*** at Hambantota (August 2022) and oceanographic vessel ***Shi Yan 6*** at Colombo (October 2023) alarmed New Delhi over hydrographic mapping of submarine corridors and tracking of Indian missile tests from Odisha. Following strong Indian and US diplomatic pushback, Sri Lanka imposed a **one-year moratorium on all foreign research vessels effective January 1, 2024** (blocking China's *Xiang Yang Hong 3*). Although Colombo permitted non-research PLA Navy training ship *Po Lang* in October 2024, as of **November 17, 2026**, Sri Lanka's Foreign Minister reaffirmed that **no decision has been taken to lift the research-vessel moratorium**, and even a Chinese-donated unmanned surface vessel at the University of Ruhuna remained grounded for three years awaiting defense clearances.

### From the $4 Billion Lifeline to Energy - Port Integration under Dissanayake

When Sri Lanka declared sovereign default in April 2022 (amid >$83 billion debt), Beijing hesitated on bilateral haircuts while **India stepped in as First Responder with ~$4 billion in emergency assistance** (credit lines for fuel/food/medicines, currency swaps, and Asian Clearing Union deferrals). India subsequently co-chaired the **Official Creditor Committee (alongside Japan and France)** to unlock Sri Lanka's **$2.9 billion IMF Extended Fund Facility**, culminating in the signing of **Bilateral Amendatory Agreements on Debt Restructuring in April 2025**.

Fears that the September 2024 presidential election of Marxist-leaning JVP/NPP leader **Anura Kumara Dissanayake** (followed by a parliamentary supermajority in November 2024) would swing Colombo back toward Beijing proved unfounded. Dissanayake chose New Delhi for his **first foreign State Visit (December 16, 2024)**, guaranteed Sri Lankan territory and waters would never threaten Indian security, and returned to Delhi for the **India AI Impact Summit (February 20, 2026)**.

During **Prime Minister Narendra Modi's State Visit to Sri Lanka (April 4–6, 2025)**—where he was conferred the ***Sri Lanka Mitra Vibhushana***—both capitals locked in structural interdependence via **seven agreements**:

- **First-Ever Defence Cooperation MoU:** Institutionalized defense exchanges, training, and maritime security coordination.
- **Trincomalee Energy Hub (India–Sri Lanka–UAE Trilateral):** A three-way pact transforming the strategic natural deep-water harbour at **Trincomalee** (including its colonial oil tank farm) into a regional energy storage, multi-product pipeline, and refining hub.
- **Power Grid & Renewables:** MoU on **HVDC cross-border power grid interconnection** and virtual groundbreaking of the **120 MW Sampur Solar Power Project** (NTPC–Ceylon Electricity Board JV in Trincomalee), alongside Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) and upgraded **Maho–Omanthai rail infrastructure**.
- **Colombo West International Terminal (CWIT):** Operated under a 35-year BOT concession by **Adani Ports (APSEZ), John Keells Holdings, and SLPA** ($800 million investment; 20-m deep draft), CWIT became Colombo Port's first fully automated terminal in **April 2025**, emerged as its highest-efficiency berth in 2025, and inaugurated **Phase II expansion in October 2026**, doubling throughput capacity from 1.6 million to **3.2 million TEUs**—directly counterbalancing China's CICT terminal next door.
- **HADR First Responder Dividend:** Following **Cyclone Ditwah**, India deployed swift search-and-rescue and emergency relief under **Operation Sagar Bandhu**, backed by a **$450 million reconstruction package** (further anchored during VP Radhakrishnan's April 2026 visit, when Sri Lanka also acceded to the **International Big Cat Alliance**).

## India - Maldives - Chokepoint Geography and the 2024 - 2026 Pragmatic Reset

### Strategic Chokepoints and the "India Out" Rupture (2023 - 2024)

Spanning 1,192 coral islands across 90,000 sq km of the central Arabian Sea, the Maldives sits barely **70 nautical miles from India's Minicoy Island** and **300 nautical miles from India's west coast**, astride the critical **Eight Degree Channel** (separating Minicoy from the northern Maldives) and **One-and-a-Half Degree Channel**—with India's **Nine Degree Channel** directly north between Minicoy and the main Lakshadweep archipelago.

Following former President Ibrahim Solih's staunchly pro-New Delhi **"India First"** tenure, bilateral relations hit a historic trough after **Dr. Mohamed Muizzu** won the November 2023 presidential election riding an anti-incumbency **"India Out"** campaign. Breaking tradition by choosing Türkiye and China for his earliest foreign visits, Muizzu demanded the withdrawal of ~88 Indian military personnel operating two gifted Dhruv helicopters and a Dornier surveillance aircraft used for medical evacuation and SAR, declined to renew the 2019 bilateral hydrography agreement, inked a military assistance pact with Beijing (March 2024), and procured Turkish Bayraktar TB2 drones. Rather than escalating punitively, New Delhi exercised strategic patience—seamlessly replacing uniformed military crew with **civilian technical personnel from Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) by May 2024** to keep aviation platforms operational without losing maritime leverage.

### Debt Distress and Structural Recalibration (2024 - 2026)

Macroeconomic gravity swiftly reversed Malé's ideological posture. Facing severe foreign-exchange reserve depletion, soaring Chinese debt servicing, and looming sovereign sukuk maturities in 2025–2026, Muizzu sought financial stabilization from New Delhi, executing a textbook **pragmatic reset** across three consecutive milestones:

1. **October 2024 State Visit & Vision Document:** Visiting New Delhi in October 2024, President Muizzu and PM Modi adopted the Joint Vision for a **"Comprehensive Economic and Maritime Security Partnership"**. India rolled over **$100 million in State Bank of India-subscribed Treasury Bills** and finalized a bilateral **Currency Swap Agreement** under the RBI's **2024–27 SAARC Currency Swap Framework**, granting Malé access to **$400 million** (USD/Euro window) and **₹3,000 crore / ₹30 billion** (INR swap window) on concessional terms.
2. **PM Modi's July 25–26, 2025 State Visit (60th Independence Day):** Serving as Guest of Honour in Malé marking 60 years of Maldivian independence and diplomatic ties, PM Modi extended a fresh **₹4,850 crore (~$565 million) Line of Credit**, signed an **Amendatory Agreement reducing annual Maldivian debt-repayment obligations on GoI LoCs**, launched **India–Maldives Free Trade Agreement (IMFTA)** Terms of Reference, handed over 3,300 Hulhumalé social housing units, and jointly inaugurated the Indian-built **Maldives Ministry of Defence headquarters**.
3. **October 2026 Financial Backstop & Maritime Continuity:** On **October 16, 2026**, India approved the **first rollover of the ₹3,000 crore (₹30 billion) INR currency swap facility** effective **October 23, 2026** (following initial drawdown in April 2026)—bringing cumulative RBI swap assistance since 2012 above **$1.1 billion**, alongside **$350 million in long-term T-bonds** ($250M + $100M) and **$150 million in T-bills** (with India absorbing $42.8 million in interest costs). Simultaneously, flagship strategic infrastructure returned to fast-track execution: the **$500 million Greater Malé Connectivity Project (GMCP — $100M grant + $400M LoC)** linking Malé to Villingili, Gulhifalhu, and Thilafushi; **Hanimaadhoo International Airport** (runway inaugurated October 2024); **Addu City** drainage/roads; and the Indian-assisted MNDF **'Ekatha' Coast Guard Harbour at Uthuru Thila Falhu (UTF)**—confirming that geography and fiscal solvency ultimately anchor Malé to New Delhi.

## Western Indian Ocean Islands and France - Securing the Southwestern Flank

Extending far beyond South Asia, India's maritime perimeter hinges on the **South-Western Indian Ocean (SWIO)**—the **Vanilla Islands** (*Mauritius, Seychelles, Madagascar, Comoros, and French Réunion*)—which guard the **Mozambique Channel** and the Cape of Good Hope energy corridor.

### Mauritius - Agaléga, Enhanced Partnership & the Chagos Sovereignty Deadlock

Bound by deep Girmitiya diaspora kinship (~68% of Mauritians are of Indian origin), Mauritius was the **first African state to sign a trade agreement with India** via the **Comprehensive Economic Cooperation and Partnership Agreement (CECPA, 2021)**. Strategic defense integration crossed a decisive threshold on **February 29, 2024**, when both Prime Ministers jointly inaugurated Indian-financed dual-use infrastructure on **Agaléga Island** (~1,100 km north of Port Louis): a **3,000-metre airstrip** capable of operating P-8I maritime patrol aircraft, the **St. James Jetty**, and a secure communications centre—vastly expanding Indian Maritime Domain Awareness across the western IOR.

During **PM Modi's State Visit to Port Louis (March 11–12, 2025)** as Chief Guest at Mauritius's National Day, India and Prime Minister **Navinchandra Ramgoolam** elevated bilateral ties to an **Enhanced Strategic Partnership**, inking eight pacts spanning local-currency trade settlement, maritime data sharing, and MSMEs, while Modi unveiled **Vision MAHASAGAR**.

Equally consequential is the **Chagos Archipelago Sovereignty Transfer**:

- **Legal Foundation:** Unlawfully detached by Britain from colonial Mauritius in **1965** to create the **British Indian Ocean Territory (BIOT)** and lease its largest atoll, **Diego Garcia**, to the United States (forcibly expelling ~1,500–1,750 Chagossians), British rule was ruled legally untenable across three landmark proceedings—the **2015 PCA Chagos Marine Protected Area Award**, the **2019 ICJ Advisory Opinion**, and the **2023 ITLOS Special Chamber delimitation ruling** (Mauritius vs. Maldives).
- **May 22, 2025 Treaty, 2026 Prorogation Lapse & Intra-IOR Rift:** Consistently backed by India on anti-colonial sovereignty grounds, the UK and Mauritius signed the **Chagos Archipelago Treaty on May 22, 2025**, transferring sovereignty of the entire archipelago to Mauritius while retaining an extendable **99-year lease for the UK–US military base at Diego Garcia**—an outcome serving India's dual goal of honoring Mauritian sovereignty while keeping a stabilizing US–UK deterrent against Chinese submarine expansion in the central IOR. However, as of **November 2026**, the treaty has **not yet entered into force**: following fierce criticism and security demands from US President Donald Trump regarding Diego Garcia and Washington's refusal to clear the updated US–UK exchange of notes, the UK implementing Bill (*Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill*) **formally lapsed when the UK Parliament was prorogued on April 29, 2026**. On **November 17, 2026**, PM Ramgoolam formally set a **December 2026 deadline** for London to resume implementation, warning of renewed international legal action. Compounding the deadlock is a sharp **intra-IOR rift**: in **February 2026**, Maldivian President Mohamed Muizzu formally objected to the UK–Mauritius treaty, rejected the 2023 ITLOS maritime delimitation ruling, and asserted a competing Maldivian historical claim to Chagos (*Foalhavahi*)—prompting **Mauritius to suspend diplomatic ties with the Maldives on February 27, 2026** and **PM Ramgoolam to block Malé's bid to host CHOGM 2028** in **November 2026**.

### Seychelles - Beyond Assumption Island to the 2026 "SESEL" Partnership

Comprising barely 455 sq km of land yet commanding a **1.4 million sq km EEZ** astride western tanker lanes, Seychelles represents an asymmetric maritime force multiplier. India learned an instructive diplomatic lesson when its **2015 agreement to jointly build naval facilities on Assumption Island** (~1,100 km southwest of Mahé) collapsed in **2018** amid Seychellois parliamentary resistance against foreign military bases.

Pivoting cleanly from intrusive basing toward sovereign capacity building, New Delhi installed and continues upgrading **six Coastal Surveillance Radar Systems (CSRS)** across Seychelles (linked into regional MDA networks), gifted Fast Patrol Vessel ***SCG PS Zoroaster*** (2021) and replacement vessel ***PB Boudeuse*** (February 2025), and supported Seychelles' **November 2025 accession as the 6th full member of the Colombo Security Conclave**. Following **Dr. Patrick Herminie's October 2025 presidential election victory**, his inaugural overseas **State Visit to India (February 5–10, 2026)** adopted the **2026 Joint Vision "SESEL"** (*Sustainability, Economic Growth and Security through Enhanced Linkages*), elevating relations to a **Comprehensive Strategic Partnership** backed by a **$175 million Special Economic Package** ($125M Rupee LoC + $50M grant). PM Modi reinforced this western pivot via a **State Visit to Victoria, Mahé (June 27–29, 2026)** for Seychelles' 50th Independence Golden Jubilee.

### Madagascar, Comoros, the IOC and France (Réunion)

- **Madagascar & Comoros:** India established its **first overseas maritime listening post and coastal radar station in northern Madagascar (2007)**, delivered rapid cyclone relief via **Operation Vanilla (2020)**, and expanded diplomatic and Line-of-Credit engagement with **Comoros**—strategically perched at the northern entrance of the **Mozambique Channel**. Both island republics sit alongside Mauritius, Seychelles, and France (Réunion) in the **Indian Ocean Commission (IOC)**, where **India gained Observer status in March 2020** (linking IOC initiatives to India's CDRI–IRIS and ISA).
- **France as a Resident Indian Ocean Power:** Through its overseas departments of **Réunion Island and Mayotte** (~1.6 million citizens and a vast SWIO EEZ defended by FAZSOI forces), France is India's deepest extra-regional maritime security partner. Built on the **2018 Joint Strategic Vision of Cooperation in the IOR** and a **2018 Reciprocal Logistics Support Agreement**, India and France pioneered **joint P-8I surveillance patrols from Réunion Island (from 2020 onward)**—the first partner with whom New Delhi conducted true joint maritime patrols—alongside *Varuna* naval exercises, France posting the **very first International Liaison Officer at IFC-IOR**, the **8th India–France Maritime Cooperation Dialogue (Paris, May 20, 2026)**, and **India–France–UAE** / **India–France–Australia** trilaterals. Realist diplomacy nonetheless requires careful calibration: Paris maintains sensitive sovereignty disputes with Comoros (over **Mayotte**), Madagascar (over the **Scattered Islands**), and Mauritius (over **Tromelin**), obliging New Delhi to separate operational naval interoperability from postcolonial territorial quarrels.

## IOR Doctrinal & Institutional Architecture - From SAGAR to Vision MAHASAGAR

### Doctrinal Evolution - SAGAR (2015) to Vision MAHASAGAR (2025)

Exactly ten years after unveiling **SAGAR** (*"Security and Growth for All in the Region"*) in Port Louis on **March 12, 2015** while commissioning the Indian-built offshore patrol vessel MCGS *Barracuda*, Prime Minister Modi announced its strategic successor in Mauritius on **March 12, 2025**: **Vision MAHASAGAR** (*"Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions"*).

The semantic shift from *Sagar* (sea) to *Mahasagar* (great ocean) encodes three deliberate strategic upgrades:

1. **Geographic Widening:** Moves beyond the immediate IOR littoral to integrate Africa, the wider Indo-Pacific, and the **Global South** into a single maritime-developmental continuum.
2. **Identity Reframing:** Evolves India's posture from an assertive *"net security provider"* (which smaller capitals occasionally misread as hegemonic) toward a collaborative **"Preferred Security Partner and First Responder"** anchored in local agency.
3. **Functional Fusion:** Ties security directly to **trade for development, capacity building (technology sharing, concessional loans/grants, Digital Public Infrastructure), Blue Economy sustainability, and climate resilience**—operationalized via deployments such as the Indian Ocean Ship **IOS Sagar** (crewed jointly by personnel from nine IOR partner navies) and the **AIKEYME** Africa–India naval exercise.

### Regional Security and Governance Grids - CSC, IORA, IONS & IFC - IOR

India operationalizes Vision MAHASAGAR through four complementary, layered maritime institutions:

- **Colombo Security Conclave (CSC):** Born in **2011** as an India–Sri Lanka–Maldives National Security Adviser (NSA) trilateral, lapsed during Malé's Yameen era, and revived in **November 2020**, the CSC has rapidly matured into India's premier operational minilateral in the central-western IOR. It inducted **Mauritius (2022)**, **Bangladesh (5th full member, July 2024)**, signed its **Founding Charter and Permanent Secretariat documents in Colombo (August 30, 2024)** with an Indian-appointed first Secretary-General, and welcomed **Seychelles as its 6th Full Member** at the **7th NSA-level Meeting in New Delhi (November 20, 2025)** (where **Malaysia** attended for the first time as a Guest). Structured around **five non-traditional security pillars**—*(1) Maritime Safety & Security, (2) Countering Terrorism & Radicalisation, (3) Combating Trafficking & Transnational Organised Crime, (4) Cyber Security & Critical Infrastructure Protection, and (5) HADR*—the CSC conducts operational drills including *Exercise Dosti* and the **September 2026 CSC Table Top Exercise at Visakhapatnam**.
- **Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA):** Conceived during Nelson Mandela's 1995 Delhi visit and established on **March 6–7, 1997** (formerly IOR-ARC) with its Secretariat at **Ebene, Mauritius**, IORA is the sole pan-oceanic ministerial intergovernmental organization uniting **23 Member States** across Asia, Africa, Oceania, and Europe (**France** joined via Réunion) alongside **12 Dialogue Partners** (including the US, China, EU, Japan, UK, Russia, and Saudi Arabia). Governed by consensus under an annual Council of Foreign Ministers (COM) while explicitly excluding bilateral disputes, IORA spans **six priority areas** (*Maritime Safety & Security, Trade & Investment Facilitation, Fisheries Management, Disaster Risk Management, Tourism & Cultural Exchanges, Academic/S&T Cooperation*) plus **two cross-cutting themes** (*Blue Economy* and *Women's Economic Empowerment*). **India assumed the two-year IORA Chairmanship (2025–2027) from Sri Lanka on November 12, 2025** under the theme *"Innovation, Openness, Resilience & Adaptability in the Indian Ocean Region"*, hosting the 10th Indian Ocean Dialogue (May 2026), the 28th CSO (June 2026), an India–Australia SAR exercise in Chennai (June 2026), and preparing to host the IORA Council of Ministers in New Delhi in late November 2026.
- **Indian Ocean Naval Symposium (IONS):** Conceived by the **Indian Navy in 2008**, IONS is a voluntary, inclusive biennial forum of regional naval chiefs comprising **25 Member States and 9 Observers** (34 nations total), organized into working groups on Maritime Security, HADR, and Information Sharing/Interoperability. Crucially, **India assumed the IONS Chairmanship from Thailand for the 2025–2027 term in February 2026**, granting New Delhi simultaneous leadership of **both IORA (diplomatic-economic) and IONS (naval-operational)** across 2025–2027.
- **Information Fusion Centre – Indian Ocean Region (IFC-IOR, Gurugram):** Established by the Government of India on **December 22, 2018**, hosted by the Indian Navy and co-located with the joint Navy–Coast Guard **Information Management and Analysis Centre (IMAC)** at INS Aravali, Gurugram, IFC-IOR serves as the operational nerve center of India's **Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA)**. Designated by the International Maritime Organization (IMO) as a voluntary reporting centre, it tracks White Shipping, Vessels of Interest (VoI), piracy, IUU fishing, contraband, Irregular Human Migration, and Red Sea/GPS-jamming hybrid threats. By late 2026, IFC-IOR maintained **76 international linkages across 28 partner countries and 54 organizations**, hosting **20 International Liaison Officers (ILOs)** (scaling upward from 15 resident nations in 2025 toward a Navy-announced goal of ~50 ILOs by 2028 to turn *"information asymmetry into information equity"*), while seamlessly linking with the Quad's **IPMDA**, EU CRIMARIO, and the Bahrain-based **Combined Maritime Forces (CTF 154)**.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **IORA vs. IONS Membership Trap:** **Pakistan is NOT a member of IORA** (23 members), yet **Pakistan IS a full member of IONS** (25 members), while **China** is *neither* a full member of IORA nor IONS—holding **Dialogue Partner** status in IORA and **Observer** status in IONS. **France** (via Réunion Island) is a **full member** of *both* IORA and IONS, as well as the Indian Ocean Commission (IOC).
> - **IORA Origin & Mandate (Tested in Prelims 2015):** IORA (formerly IOR-ARC) was **NOT** created recently for piracy or solely for maritime security; it was founded in **March 1997** primarily for multi-sectoral socio-economic and trade cooperation (now covering 6 priority areas + 2 cross-cutting themes), headquartered in **Ebene, Mauritius** (not Colombo or New Delhi).
> - **CSC Membership & Secretariat:** Do not treat **Seychelles** as merely an "Observer" anymore—it formally acceded as the **6th Full Member of the Colombo Security Conclave in November 2025** (after Mauritius in 2022 and Bangladesh in July 2024). The CSC Permanent Secretariat is in **Colombo** (NOT Gurugram), whereas **IFC-IOR** is in **Gurugram** co-located with IMAC.
> - **Degree Channels & Island Precision:** The **Eight Degree Channel** separates India's **Minicoy Island** from the **Maldives**; the **Nine Degree Channel** separates **Minicoy** from the rest of the **Lakshadweep (Laccadive) archipelago** (NOT from Maldives); and the **Ten Degree Channel** separates Little Andaman from Car Nicobar. Also note: **Agaléga** belongs to **Mauritius**, **Assumption Island** belongs to **Seychelles**, and **Diego Garcia** lies in the **Chagos Archipelago** (treaty signed May 2025; UK implementing Bill lapsed upon parliamentary prorogation in April 2026).

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **Doctrinal Acronym & Launch Site Precision:** Both **SAGAR (March 2015)** and **Vision MAHASAGAR (March 2025)** were unveiled by PM Modi in **Mauritius** (not Seychelles or Maldives), and MAHASAGAR is a **foreign-policy doctrine**, not a legally binding multilateral treaty.
- **Chagos Legal Status Nuance (2026):** Although the UK and Mauritius **signed** the Chagos Archipelago sovereignty treaty on **May 22, 2025**, it had **not yet entered into force** as of November 2026 because the UK implementing Bill **lapsed upon parliamentary prorogation on April 29, 2026** amid US objections over Diego Garcia (further complicated by Maldives' competing February 2026 claim)—watch out for statements asserting sovereignty transfer has already been legally completed.
- **First ILO & Co-Location of IFC-IOR:** **France** (not the US or Australia) posted the very first International Liaison Officer to IFC-IOR; IFC-IOR operates in **Gurugram** (co-located with IMAC), while the **IORA Secretariat** sits in **Ebene, Mauritius** and the **CSC Secretariat** sits in **Colombo**.
- **Simultaneous 2025–2027 Chairmanships:** India holds the rotating Chairmanship of **both IORA (from Sri Lanka, Nov 2025–2027)** and **IONS (from Thailand, Feb 2026 / 2025–2027)** concurrently—UPSC frequently swaps previous Chairs or conflates IORA's civilian Foreign Ministers council with IONS's naval chiefs symposium.
