# Chapter 13 - Central Asia, Africa, Latin America & Global South Leadership

Beyond India's immediate neighborhood and great-power axes lies a vast strategic arc stretching from the **Eurasian Heartland** through **Sub-Saharan Africa** to **Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC)**. Historically sidelined by geographical distance or ideological Non-Alignment, these three regions now anchor India's **uranium and critical mineral security**, **food and energy diversification**, **maritime domain depth**, and diplomatic claim to represent the **Global South**.

The central structural tension across this arc is **asymmetric competition**. New Delhi cannot out-lend China's state banks or match Russia's mercenary-security interventions in fragile regimes. Instead, Indian statecraft leverages a differentiated toolkit—concessional demand-driven credit, generic pharmaceuticals, naval capacity building, and open-source **Digital Public Infrastructure (India Stack)**—to translate moral goodwill into durable geopolitical equity.

## 1. Central Asia & the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

### 1.1 Strategic Calculus & Institutionalizing *Connect Central Asia*
Occupying Mackinder's **Eurasian Heartland**, the five **Central Asian Republics (CARs)**—**Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan**—sit at the strategic intersection of Russia, China, Iran, and Afghanistan. Because Pakistan denies India direct overland transit, New Delhi's continental reach into its "extended northern neighborhood" remains structurally handicapped, leaving India–Central Asia bilateral trade hovering around **$2 billion** against China's $89 billion+ footprint.

To overcome this geographic isolation, India launched the **Connect Central Asia Policy (2012, Bishkek)** anchored in the **4Cs** (*Commerce, Connectivity, Consular, Community*). Over the past decade, New Delhi institutionalized engagement across three dedicated tiers:
- **Summit Level:** The inaugural **India–Central Asia Summit (January 27, 2022, virtual)** marked 30 years of diplomatic ties, establishing a permanent **India–Central Asia Centre** in New Delhi and a Joint Working Group on Chabahar.
- **Foreign Ministerial Track:** The **India–Central Asia Dialogue** (launched January 2019 in Samarkand) convened its **4th Meeting in June 2025**, prioritizing INSTC Eastern-branch optimization, **TIR Carnet** customs harmonization, rare earths, and a **$1 billion Indian Line of Credit** for priority infrastructure.
- **Security Track:** Institutionalized meetings of **National Security Advisers (NSAs) / Secretaries of Security Councils** (3rd meeting held in **Bishkek, October 16, 2025**) coordinate counter-terrorism intelligence, border security, and shared Afghan contingencies.

### 1.2 Nuclear Fuel, Critical Minerals & the Secular Buffer
India's hard material stakes in Central Asia converge on three axes:

- **Uranium & Energy Diversification:** **Kazakhstan**—the world's largest uranium producer—is India's **single largest supplier of nuclear fuel** through long-term KazAtomProm contracts powering India's safeguarded civil reactors. New Delhi is actively broadening nuclear fuel sourcing to **Uzbekistan** (targeting **$5 billion** bilateral trade by 2030 during PM Modi's August 2026 Tashkent talks) while exploring Central Asian lithium, tungsten, and rare earth reserves.
- **Secular Buffer Against Afghan Extremism:** Post-2021 Taliban rule in Kabul exposed both India and the secular CARs to common transnational threats: **Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP)**, the **Islamic Movement of Uzbekistan (IMU)**, the **East Turkestan Islamic Movement (ETIM)**, and the **Northern Route** Afghan narcotics corridor that launders opium and methamphetamine profits into terror financing.
- **Defense Diplomacy & the Ayni Airbase Exit:** India sustains bilateral military exercises across the region—**Kazind** (Kazakhstan), **Khanjar** (Kyrgyzstan), and **Dustlik** (Uzbekistan)—and historically operated a military hospital at **Farkhor (Tajikistan)**. However, India suffered a notable strategic setback publicly confirmed in **October–November 2025**: New Delhi quietly vacated the **Ayni Airbase (Gissar Military Aerodrome)** near Dushanbe—India's sole overseas military airbase, renovated via BRO with ~$70–80 million investment (3.2 km runway) under a 2002 bilateral agreement. Following the lease's 2022 expiration, Tajikistan declined renewal amid Russian and Chinese resistance and diminishing Indian tactical need post-Taliban takeover, shrinking India's direct airlift reach near the Wakhan Corridor.

### 1.3 Connectivity Bottlenecks - Ashgabat Agreement, INSTC & TAPI
Without Pakistani transit, India relies on three interlocking—yet geopolitically constrained—Eurasian corridors:

- **Ashgabat Agreement:** Signed on **April 25, 2011** (in force **April 2016**; **Turkmenistan** is the depository state) to build a multimodal transport and transit corridor linking Central Asia to Persian Gulf ports. Originally signed by **Iran, Oman, Qatar, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan**, **Qatar withdrew in 2013**, while **Kazakhstan (2015)**, **Pakistan (2016)**, and **India (February 2018)** acceded—creating **7 active members**. The corridor leverages the **Iran–Turkmenistan–Kazakhstan (ITK) railway** and synchronizes directly with **INSTC's Eastern branch**.
- **INSTC & Chabahar Vulnerability:** Central Asian states repeatedly endorsed utilizing Chabahar's Shahid Beheshti terminal and INSTC's Trans-Caspian and Eastern branches (India backs Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan joining INSTC). Yet the **April 2026 expiration of the US Chabahar sanctions waiver** and the 2026 Iran War severely impaired commercial scalability, leaving China's **$8 billion China–Kyrgyzstan–Uzbekistan (CKU) railway** and BRI land corridors largely uncontested.
- **TAPI Gas Pipeline:** Proposed **1,814-km** natural gas pipeline from Turkmenistan's giant **Galkynysh field** through Herat and Kandahar (Afghanistan) and Quetta/Multan (Pakistan) to **Fazilka (Punjab, India)**. Although Ashgabat and the Taliban initiated localized pipeline work on the Serhetabat–Herat Afghan spur, persistent Afghan insecurity, Western financial blacklisting, and acute India–Pakistan hostility (post-Pahalgam 2025) keep Indian gas off-take practically frozen.

### 1.4 The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) - Plural Eurasian Platform vs. Sino–Russian Bloc
Originating as the **Shanghai Five (1996)** (China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan) to demilitarize post-Soviet borders, the **SCO** was formally established on **June 15, 2001** in Shanghai upon Uzbekistan's accession (Charter signed June 2002; in force September 2003). Its supreme decision-making organ is the **Heads of State Council (CHS)**, supported by the **Heads of Government Council (CHG)** and two permanent bodies: the **SCO Secretariat (Beijing)** and the **Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS, Tashkent)** targeting the **"Three Evils"** (*terrorism, separatism, and extremism*).

- **Expansion to 10 Full Members:** Following the **Astana Summit (June 2017)** induction of **India and Pakistan** (driven by Russian balancing of Chinese dominance), the SCO admitted **Iran (July 2023, New Delhi virtual summit)** and **Belarus (July 2024, Astana)**—bringing full membership to **10 states**, alongside **2 Observers (Afghanistan, Mongolia)** and **15 Dialogue Partners**.
- **India's Strategic Navigation (2023–2026):** During its 2023 Presidency, India promoted the **SECURE** framework (*Security, Economic development, Connectivity, Unity, Respect for sovereignty, Environmental protection*), later distilled at the **25th SCO Summit (Tianjin, September 2025)** into **S-C-O (*Security, Connectivity, Opportunity*)**. At the **26th SCO Summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan (August 31 – September 1, 2026)**—marking the SCO's 25th anniversary and attended in person by PM Modi—India secured three tangible diplomatic outcomes:
  1. **Counter-Terrorism & Connectivity Red Lines:** The **Bishkek Declaration** rejected "double standards" on terrorism and endorsed sovereignty-respecting connectivity—while India remained the **sole SCO member refusing to endorse China's BRI** in joint communiqués because CPEC violates Indian territorial sovereignty in PoK.
  2. **Linguistic & Civilizational Soft Power:** **English was formally adopted in 2026** alongside Russian and Chinese as an official and working language, widening Indian corporate and academic access; the Bishkek Declaration also institutionalized India's **SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum** (inaugural edition hosted at **Purana Qila, New Delhi, November 2026**).
  3. **Institutional Horizon & Diplomatic Friction:** Kyrgyzstan pushed to operationalize the Tashkent Universal Centre for Countering Security Threats, the Dushanbe Anti-Drug Centre, and an SCO Development Bank, before handing the **2026–27 rotating Chairmanship to Pakistan** (host of the 2027 Islamabad Summit)—creating an immediate diplomatic calibration test for New Delhi.

## 2. Africa - Capacity-First Development, Maritime Security & Great-Power Rivalry

### 2.1 Normative Spine - The 10 Kampala Principles & AU Agenda 2063
Unlike Western conditional aid or Chinese resource-backed mega-loans, India frames its African outreach around anti-colonial solidarity and demand-driven co-development aligned with the **African Union's Agenda 2063**. Codified by PM Modi before the Ugandan Parliament in **July 2018**, the **10 Kampala Principles** establish India's operational doctrine: keeping Africa at the top of Indian priorities; letting African partners define project priorities; keeping Indian markets open; harnessing India's digital revolution; boosting African agriculture; jointly confronting climate change; strengthening counter-terrorism; keeping the Indian Ocean open and free; ensuring Africa does not revert to great-power rivalries; and jointly reforming global governance.

### 2.2 Development Architecture - Lines of Credit, e-VBAB, Pharma & Campuses
India translates the Kampala Principles into four operational pillars, while confronting measurable execution gaps:

- **Trade, Investments & Lines of Credit (Dual-Standard Parity):** Bilateral India–Africa trade crossed the **$100 billion milestone in FY 2024–25** (making India Africa's **third-largest trading partner**, targeting $200 billion by 2030), alongside **over $80 billion in cumulative Indian investments** (led by telecom, energy, and agribusiness). Under its **Duty-Free Tariff Preference (DFTP)** scheme, India grants duty-free entry on **98.2% of tariff lines**, benefiting **33 African LDCs**. On development finance, **MEA/Exim Bank accounting** records **190–196 Lines of Credit (LoCs) worth $10–12+ billion** across **41–42 African nations** (over one-third of India's $32 billion global LoC portfolio under the IDEAS scheme), plus **$700 million+ in grants**—financing metro rails (Mauritius), hydropower (Zambia, Rwanda), solar parks, and water grids. *Structural friction:* India's **75% tied-procurement mandate** (goods/services sourced from India) and Exim Bank bureaucratic approvals frequently cause multi-year project delays compared to Chinese turnkey delivery.
- **Digital & Educational Footprint:** India expanded its diplomatic footprint by opening **18 new resident African embassies** since 2018 and providing **80,000+ scholarships/training slots** over eleven years (ITEC, ICCR, CV Raman Fellowships). The pioneering **Pan-African e-Network Project (2009)** evolved in 2019 into **e-VBAB (*e-VidyaBharati and e-AarogyaBharati*)**, delivering free tele-education (**15,116+ scholarships**) and tele-medicine across partner countries. Marking a qualitative leap into permanent institutional presence, India established **IIT Madras Zanzibar (Tanzania, 2023)**—the first overseas IIT campus—and Africa's first **National Forensic Sciences University (NFSU) campus in Uganda**.
- **"Pharmacy of Africa":** Indian generic drugmakers supply roughly **20% of Africa's pharmaceutical imports**, providing low-cost antiretrovirals (HIV/AIDS), anti-malarials, TB therapeutics, and vaccines (*Vaccine Maitri*). New Delhi is increasingly pairing pharmaceutical exports with **Jan Aushadhi Kendras** and local vaccine-manufacturing hubs—exemplified by PM Modi's **July 2025 visit to Ghana** (elevating ties to a Comprehensive Partnership and advancing a West African vaccine hub alongside bauxite and manganese critical-mineral exploration) and **Namibia** (first Indian PM visit in 27 years, covering UPI adoption, cobalt/rare earths, and defense ties).
- **Summit Cadence:** Following three **India–Africa Forum Summits (IAFS: 2008 New Delhi, 2011 Addis Ababa, 2015 New Delhi)**, **IAFS-IV** was scheduled for May 31, 2026 in New Delhi before India and the African Union jointly postponed it due to a regional Ebola outbreak (confirmed by EAM S. Jaishankar on August 5, 2026).

### 2.3 Maritime & Defense Leap - AIKEYME 2025 and Vision MAHASAGAR
Historically confined to peacekeeping (UNMISS, MONUSCO) and officer training at NDA/DSSC, India's African defense engagement achieved a major maritime inflection following the **Lucknow Declaration (2020)**, the biennial **AFINDEX** army exercise, and PM Modi's **March 2025 Vision MAHASAGAR** (*Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions*).

From **April 13 to 18, 2025**, India and Tanzania co-hosted the inaugural **AIKEYME 2025 (*Africa India Key Maritime Engagement* — meaning "Unity" in Sanskrit)** off **Dar-es-Salaam**. Involving **10 nations**—co-hosts **India and Tanzania** alongside **Comoros, Djibouti, Kenya, Madagascar, Mauritius, Mozambique, Seychelles, and South Africa**—the exercise paired Indian warships (*INS Chennai*, *INS Kesari*) with **INS Sunayna** deployed as **Indian Ocean Ship (IOS) SAGAR** carrying a multinational African-IOR crew. Harbour tabletops and sea-phase Visit, Board, Search, and Seizure (VBSS), anti-piracy, and IUU-fishing drills positioned India as East Africa's preferred maritime security partner along the Mozambique Channel and Western IOR chokepoints.

### 2.4 Great-Power Competition - China's FOCAC, Russia's Africa Corps & AfCFTA
India operates inside Africa's most crowded great-power arena since decolonization:
- **China:** Africa's largest trading partner (**~$295.6 billion** in 2024, nearly triple India's volume). At the **9th Forum on China–Africa Cooperation (FOCAC, September 2024)**, Xi Jinping pledged **$50–51 billion (360 billion yuan)** in financing over three years, atop Chinese equity across **46 African ports** and the PLA's overseas naval base in **Djibouti**. Yet debt-distress defaults (Zambia, Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya's SGR) have triggered mounting African pushback against Beijing's opaque lending.
- **Russia's Africa Corps:** Following Yevgeny Prigozhin's 2023 death, Moscow subordinated Wagner Group remnants directly under the Russian Ministry of Defence as the **Africa Corps**. Exploiting post-colonial resentment against France across the coup-ridden **Alliance of Sahel States (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger)**, Central African Republic, and Libya, Russia trades praetorian regime security and disinformation campaigns for **gold and uranium mining concessions**—though failing to halt jihadist advances (underscored in April 2026 when Mali's Defence Minister was killed alongside Russian Africa Corps troops in a northern ambush).
- **Engaging African Agency (AfCFTA):** Rather than mimicking Beijing's debt-heavy scale or Moscow's kinetic footprint, India's durable lever lies in negotiating preferential trade and digital-payment linkages directly with the **African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)**—converting Africa's demographic dividend into an integrated Indo-African manufacturing and DPI market.

## 3. Latin America & the Caribbean (LAC) - Energy, Critical Minerals & Diplomatic Revival

### 3.1 Breaking "Geographical Fatalism" & Trade Blocs
Long relegated to the periphery of Indian diplomacy by 15,000-km maritime logistics, absence of direct flights, and Washington's hemispheric shadow, Latin America and the Caribbean have surged into India's strategic calculus as a twin hedge for **food/hydrocarbon security** and **green-transition critical minerals**. Two back-to-back Prime Ministerial outings cemented this pivot: **November 2024 (Guyana & 2nd India–CARICOM Summit)** and **July 2–9, 2025 (Trinidad & Tobago, Argentina, and Brazil)**—marking the first bilateral visit by an Indian PM to Argentina in 57 years and Guyana in 56 years.

India navigates LAC's fragmented regional trade architecture through targeted bilaterals and sub-regional pacts:
- **MERCOSUR PTA:** Signed in 2004 and operational since **June 2009**, the India–MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement remains narrow, covering roughly **452 Indian tariff lines and 450 MERCOSUR lines** with modest 10–20% duty margins. **Crucial 2024–2026 Institutional Shift:** **Bolivia acceded as a full MERCOSUR member in 2024**, expanding active full membership to **five states** (**Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay, Bolivia**; **Venezuela** remains suspended since December 2016; headquarters **Montevideo**). During his July 2025 visits to Buenos Aires and Brasília (as Brazil assumed the H2 2025 MERCOSUR Chair), PM Modi pressed for substantial PTA expansion toward broader tariff liberalization.
- **Bilaterals & Regional Platforms:** India holds a **PTA with Chile (2007)** now under upgrade negotiations toward a **CEPA**, conducts active **India–Peru FTA negotiations**, holds **Observer status in the Pacific Alliance** (Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru), and sustains structured dialogues with **CELAC** (all 33 LAC nations) and **SICA** (Central America).

### 3.2 Hydrocarbon Diversification, Food Security & KABIL's Lithium Triangle Play
- **Energy & Edible Oils:** LAC supplies crucial non-OPEC crude diversification via **Brazil** (Petrobras offshore oil, co-leadership in the **Global Biofuels Alliance**, Embraer defense/aviation ties, and a February 2026 bilateral critical minerals framework), **Guyana** (whose massive offshore **Stabroek Block** oil boom pairs with a ~40% Indo-Guyanese diaspora), **Mexico**, **Colombia**, and sanction-contingent **Venezuela**. Simultaneously, **Argentina and Brazil** anchor India's food security as premier suppliers of **crude soybean and sunflower oil** alongside pulses.
- **KABIL & the Lithium Triangle:** Over half of global lithium resources reside in the Andean **Lithium Triangle** (**Argentina, Bolivia, and Chile**). On **January 15, 2024**, **Khanij Bidesh India Limited (KABIL)**—a state-owned joint venture of **NALCO, Hindustan Copper Ltd (HCL), and MECL**—signed a ₹200 crore agreement with Argentina's Catamarca provincial PSU **CAMYEN** for exploration and development rights across **5 lithium brine blocks (15,703 hectares)**. Coal India Limited and private Indian entities (e.g., Greenco) have subsequently pursued additional Argentine concessions, while Indian firms eye equity stakes in Chilean lithium leader SQM. *Epistemic caveat:* As of late 2026, KABIL's Catamarca blocks remain strictly in the **exploratory drilling and appraisal stage**; commercial extraction, refining, and delivery to Indian gigafactories have not yet commenced.

### 3.3 IBSA & India–CARICOM - Democratic Minilateralism
- **IBSA Dialogue Forum:** Established via the **Brasília Declaration (June 2003)** by **India, Brazil, and South Africa**, IBSA unites three major pluralistic democracies across Asia, South America, and Africa—explicitly distinct from BRICS by excluding China and Russia. Alongside the trilateral **IBSAMAR** naval exercise and the UN-administered **IBSA Fund for Poverty and Hunger Alleviation** (funding grassroots projects in LDCs), IBSA enjoyed a diplomatic revival when PM Modi joined Brazilian and South African leaders at the **IBSA Leaders' Meeting in Johannesburg (November 2025)** on the margins of the G20 Summit.
- **India–CARICOM:** Representing India's sole summit-level institutional mechanism in the Western Hemisphere, the **2nd India–CARICOM Summit (November 20, 2024, Georgetown, Guyana)**—co-chaired by India and Grenada—unveiled PM Modi's **7-Pillar C-A-R-I-C-O-M roadmap**: **C**apacity building, **A**griculture & food security, **R**enewable energy & climate change, **I**nnovation/tech/trade, **C**ricket & culture, **O**cean economy/maritime security, and **M**edicine & healthcare. In July 2025, PM Modi further deepened Caribbean diaspora capital in **Trinidad & Tobago** (marking 180 years of Indian arrival) by extending **OCI card eligibility up to the 6th generation** of Indian-origin citizens and operationalizing UPI acceptance.

## 4. Institutionalizing Global South Leadership - G21, VOGSS, DAKSHIN & India Stack

### 4.1 Reclaiming the Global South - From Ideological NAM to Bridging Statecraft
First coined by activist **Carl Oglesby in 1969**, the **"Global South"** is not a strict latitudinal divide (Australia and New Zealand sit in the Southern Hemisphere yet belong to the Global North; India and China sit in the Northern Hemisphere). It denotes a **structural political-economic coalition** of ~125–130 post-colonial developing nations united by sovereign debt vulnerability, climate-finance inequity, and under-representation across Bretton Woods institutions and the UN Security Council.

Where Cold War **Non-Alignment (NAM)** often tilted defensively anti-Western, India's contemporary Global South statecraft operates as a **"Non-Western, Not Anti-Western" bridge**. New Delhi converts its simultaneous membership in the **Quad/G7 outreach** and **BRICS+/SCO** into agenda-setting leverage—translating developing-world priorities into actionable G20 and multilateral outcomes without forcing binary Cold War alignments.

### 4.2 Voice of Global South Summits (VOGSS) & DAKSHIN
To prevent Global South leadership from remaining rhetorical, India engineered permanent institutional machinery:
- **Voice of Global South Summits (VOGSS):** India convened **three virtual summits**—**1st VOGSS (January 12–13, 2023; 125 countries)** to crowdsource developing-world inputs ahead of the G20; **2nd VOGSS (November 17, 2023; 130 countries)** to consolidate post-G20 follow-through; and **3rd VOGSS (August 17, 2024; 123 countries)** themed *"An Empowered Global South for a Sustainable Future."*
- **DAKSHIN (Development and Knowledge Sharing Initiative):** Announced at the 1st VOGSS and inaugurated at the **2nd VOGSS (November 2023)** in New Delhi, **DAKSHIN** functions as the **Global South Centre of Excellence** dedicated to research, skilling, and replicating scalable Indian development solutions (digital health, disaster resilience, renewable grids) across partner states.
- **Targeted Funds & Global Public Goods:** India backed VOGSS with concrete financing instruments—an initial **$25 million contribution to the Social Impact Fund (SIF)** to accelerate DPI adoption in the Global South (3rd VOGSS), a **$2.5 million fund** for trade policy capacity building, the **Global South Young Diplomat Forum**, **Arogya Maitri** (portable BHISHM trauma hospital cubes and Jan Aushadhi supplies), a proposed **Global South Weather & Climate Monitoring Satellite**, and the **$150 million India–UN Development Partnership Fund** managed through UNOSSC.

### 4.3 The G20 African Union Milestone (G21) & the 2025 Johannesburg Continuum
The diplomatic centerpiece of India's **2023 New Delhi G20 Presidency** was securing consensus on **September 9, 2023** to induct the 55-member **African Union (AU)** as a **permanent G20 member** on par with the European Union—effectively transforming the forum into **G21** (19 countries + EU + AU).

Crucially, this anchored an unprecedented **four-year Global South G20 Presidency streak**: **Indonesia (2022) → India (2023) → Brazil (2024) → South Africa (2025)**. At the **20th G20 Leaders' Summit in Johannesburg (November 22–23, 2025)**—the first G20 Summit ever held on African soil, themed *"Solidarity, Equality, Sustainability"*—an unprecedented **US boycott** alongside absences by Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin threatened multilateral collapse. India stepped up alongside South Africa and Brazil to preserve a **122-paragraph Johannesburg Leaders' Declaration**, locking in Global South priorities on **debt sustainability**, **disaster resilience**, **just energy transition**, and the **G20 Critical Minerals Framework** (advocating local value-addition rather than raw ore extraction)—before carrying that momentum directly into **India's 2026 BRICS Presidency** and the **February 2026 India AI Impact Summit** in New Delhi.

### 4.4 Exporting India Stack / UPI via the Global DPI Repository (GDPIR)
India's sharpest asymmetric geopolitical instrument across Africa, LAC, and Central Asia is **Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)**—open-source, interoperable digital rails built as public goods (**Identity:** Aadhaar/MOSIP; **Payments:** UPI; **Data Exchange:** DigiLocker/Account Aggregator). Developing nations seeking digital sovereignty face an unattractive duopoly: proprietary **US Big Tech** walled gardens (commercial rent extraction) or **Chinese vendor ecosystems** (Huawei/Alibaba surveillance and data-governance lock-in). **India Stack** breaks this binary by enabling low-cost sovereign ownership with open APIs.

- **Multilateral Architecture:** Championed under India's 2023 G20 Presidency and hosted by MeitY, the **Global DPI Repository (GDPIR)** established the first multilateral consensus on DPI, featuring **54 DPI solutions from 16 countries** (MEA data, December 2025) alongside the **One Future Alliance**.
- **Global South Adoption & UPI Internationalization:** Representing **~49% of global real-time payment volume (2025)**, UPI has become India's flagship fintech export via **NPCI International (NIPL)** alongside India Stack MoUs with **12+ Global South partners**:
  - **Africa:** **Namibia** became the first African nation to sign a UPI-like Fast Payment System (FPS) deployment pact (May 2024; operationalized during PM Modi's July 2025 visit), alongside **Ghana** (payment interlinking) and **Mauritius** (RuPay & UPI live), while IIIT-Bangalore's open-source **MOSIP** digital ID architecture has been adopted by **Morocco, Ethiopia, Togo, Uganda, Sierra Leone, and Guinea**.
  - **Latin America & Caribbean:** **Peru** became the first South American country to sign an NIPL pact to build a UPI-like instant payment system (June 2024), while **Trinidad & Tobago** became the first Caribbean nation to adopt UPI (September 2024; accelerated July 2025).

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **SCO RATS vs. Secretariat Trap:** UPSC repeatedly tests location swaps—the **SCO Secretariat** is in **Beijing**, whereas **RATS (Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure)** is headquartered in **Tashkent (Uzbekistan)**, NOT Beijing or Astana. Also note **Turkmenistan is NOT a member of SCO** (maintaining permanent neutrality), whereas **Belarus** became the **10th full member in July 2024** and **English** became an official working language in **2026**.
> - **Ashgabat Agreement Membership Trap:** **Qatar** was an original 2011 signatory but **withdrew in 2013**; **Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan, and Russia are NOT members**; **Pakistan IS a member** (joined 2016 before India's Feb 2018 accession).
> - **MERCOSUR & Lithium Triangle Overlaps:** Do not confuse **MERCOSUR** with the **Lithium Triangle**. **Bolivia** graduated from associate to **full MERCOSUR member in 2024** (alongside Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay; Venezuela suspended), while **Chile** is only an *Associate* member of MERCOSUR (and a full member of the **Pacific Alliance**). The **Lithium Triangle** comprises **Argentina, Bolivia, and Chile**—*NOT* Brazil or Peru!
> - **AIKEYME 2025 vs. AFINDEX vs. IBSAMAR:** **AIKEYME 2025** is India's inaugural multilateral *naval* exercise co-hosted with **Tanzania** (10 nations, April 2025); **AFINDEX** is the India–Africa *Army* field training exercise; **IBSAMAR** is the trilateral *naval* exercise exclusively among **India, Brazil, and South Africa**.
> - **Voice of Global South Dates & Global South Geography:** All three VOGSS editions were **virtual** (**Jan 2023, Nov 2023, Aug 2024**—none in Jan 2024). The "Global South" is an economic-structural designation, NOT a geographic hemisphere (Australia and New Zealand are Global North).

- **Membership Inclusions & Exclusions:** UPSC systematically injects **Turkmenistan** into SCO (false—permanently neutral), **Qatar** into active Ashgabat Agreement members (false—withdrew 2013), **Brazil** into the Andean Lithium Triangle (false—Argentina, Bolivia, Chile), or omits **Bolivia** as a full MERCOSUR member (true since 2024).
