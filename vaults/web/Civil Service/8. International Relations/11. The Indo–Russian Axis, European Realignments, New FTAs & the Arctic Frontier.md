# Chapter 11 - The Indo-Russian Axis, European Realignments, New FTAs & the Arctic Frontier

## The Indo-Russian Special and Privileged Strategic Partnership

Few bilateral relationships in modern diplomacy illustrate **structural realism** more cleanly than New Delhi’s enduring partnership with Moscow. Across the Cold War, post-Soviet collapse, and the post-2022 Ukraine rupture, India has preserved ties with Russia for three cold material imperatives: **legacy defense sustenance, continental Eurasia balancing against China, and UNSC veto insurance**.

The institutional bedrock remains the **August 9, 1971 Indo–Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation**, whose **Article IX** mutual-consultation clause deterred American and Chinese military intervention during the 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War. Replaced by the 1993 Friendship Treaty, elevated to a Strategic Partnership in 2000, and upgraded in **2010 to a "Special and Privileged Strategic Partnership,"** ties operate through Annual Leaders’ Summits (**22nd in Moscow, July 2024; 23rd in New Delhi during President Vladimir Putin’s state visit, December 4–5, 2025**) and the **2+2 Foreign and Defence Ministerial Dialogue** (launched 2021).

In **February 2025**, both governments signed the **Reciprocal Exchange of Logistics Agreement (RELOS)** (ratified by Moscow in December 2025)—mirroring India’s LEMOA with the US—granting the Indian Navy streamlined access to Russian Arctic and Pacific naval facilities while allowing Russian logistics port calls in India.

### Defense Sustenance and Civil Nuclear Interdependency

While India deliberately diversified new weapons procurement toward France, the United States, and Israel—cutting Russia’s share of India’s *new* arms imports from ~70–75% (2009–13) to roughly **36% (2020–24)**—approximately **55–60% of India’s active frontline combat fleet** remains Soviet/Russian-origin. Severing Moscow abruptly would cripple Indian readiness across the Himalayan LAC:

- **Core Combat Fleet & Joint Production:** The IAF’s mainstay **~260 Su-30MKI** air-superiority fighters, the Army’s **T-90S Bhishma** main battle tanks, the Navy’s aircraft carrier **INS Vikramaditya**, and the **BrahMos** supersonic cruise missile JV (**50.5% India DRDO / 49.5% Russia NPOM**) anchor India’s deterrent backbone. Under the **Indo-Russian Rifles Private Limited (IRRPL)** JV at Korwa (Amethi), production of **~6.1 lakh AK-203 assault rifles** has crossed 65,000 deliveries while progressing toward 100% domestic indigenization.
- **S-400 Triumf Air Defense:** Contracted in October 2018 for **$5.43 billion** (5 squadrons) despite US CAATSA sanction threats, three squadrons are operational and proved battle-effective during *Operation Sindoor* (May 2025). Ukraine-war industrial strain delayed the final two squadrons, which Moscow rescheduled for delivery across **mid-2026 to March 2027**.
- **Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant (Tamil Nadu):** Russia’s Rosatom remains the only foreign partner actively building commercial nuclear reactors in India—constructing **six 1,000 MW VVER-1000 units** (Units 1 & 2 operational; Unit 3 advancing through fuel loading and commissioning in 2026; Units 4–6 under construction).

### Discounted Crude, the $50B+ Rupee-Ruble Imbalance & 2025–2026 Recalibration

Following Western sanctions after February 2022, Moscow redirected stranded Ural/Sokol crude eastward at steep discounts, propelling Russia’s share of India’s oil imports from under **2% (2021)** to a peak of **35–40% across FY 2023–25**. Bilateral trade exploded to **~$65–68.7 billion in FY 2024–25**, exposing two severe structural strains:

- **Extreme Trade Deficit & Trapped-Rupee Friction:** With Indian exports stagnant at ~$4.3–4.9 billion against ~$60–64 billion in Russian oil, fertilizers, and coal imports, India’s bilateral trade deficit crossed **$55–64 billion** in FY25. Early attempts to settle oil via **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVAs)** stalled because Moscow accumulated billions in unconvertible rupees with insufficient Indian goods to buy—forcing traders to pivot to **UAE Dirhams, Hong Kong Dollars**, and Russian reinvestment into Indian government securities and equities.
- **2025–2026 Secondary-Sanction Squeeze:** Facing Trump 2.0’s **Executive Order 14329 (August 2025)** 25% penalty tariff on Russian oil and **October 2025 US Treasury sanctions on Rosneft and Lukoil**, Indian refiners recalibrated sharply—reducing Russia’s crude share toward **~19–24% across late 2025–2026**. Total FY 2025–26 trade eased to **~$60–62 billion** (leaving a deficit still exceeding **$50 billion**). To rebalance trade toward the December 2025 summit’s **$100 billion (2030) target**, India is pushing pharmaceuticals, auto components, electronics, and an **India–Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU) FTA** (third negotiating round completed August 2026).

### Hedging Sino-Russian Convergence - Act Far East & Chennai-Vladivostok Corridor

India’s deepest strategic anxiety regarding Moscow is no longer Ukraine itself, but Russia’s accelerating structural subordination to Beijing under their **"No Limits" partnership**. If Moscow becomes a junior economic vassal of China, India risks spare-parts vulnerability or diplomatic neutrality erosion during future LAC crises.

New Delhi hedges this risk by giving Moscow an independent Indo-Pacific economic outlet through two interlocking initiatives:

- **Act Far East Policy (2019):** Announced by PM Modi at the Vladivostok Eastern Economic Forum (September 2019) backed by a **$1 billion Line of Credit** and renewed via the **2024–2029 Programme of Cooperation**, targeting Indian equity in Russian Far East coking coal, LNG, timber, diamonds, and Arctic logistics.
- **Chennai–Vladivostok Eastern Maritime Corridor (EMC):** Spanning **~5,600 nautical miles (~10,300 km)** through the Sea of Japan, South China Sea, and Strait of Malacca, the EMC slashes shipping distance by **~40%** and transit time from ~40 days (via Suez/St. Petersburg) to roughly **~24 days**. Operationalized for coking coal, crude, and LNG shipments since November 2024, the corridor directly integrates southern India’s industrial ports into Russia’s Pacific Rim, counterbalancing Chinese monopoly over Siberia and the Far East.

## European Realignment and India’s Historic 2025–2026 FTA Wave

For nearly two decades, Europe and India operated as mismatched partners—separated by European normative lecturing, stalled trade talks (BTIA collapsed in 2013), and Indian protectionism. Two twin geopolitical shocks—Russia’s war in Ukraine and Trump 2.0’s aggressive tariff unilateralism across 2025–2026—catalyzed an unprecedented **strategic and commercial realignment**, driving India to lock in **three landmark European trade pacts within sixteen months**.

### India-EFTA TEPA (In Force October 1, 2025)

Signed on **March 10, 2024** and entering into force on **October 1, 2025**, the **Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA)** with the four **non-EU** European Free Trade Association states (**Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein**) rewrote global FTA jurisprudence:

- **Legally Binding $100B FDI & 1 Million Jobs Pledge:** TEPA is the **first trade agreement in global history** containing a binding investment-and-employment obligation—committing EFTA economies to invest **$100 billion in FDI** into India ($50B within 10 years + $50B in the subsequent 5 years, excluding FPI) and create **1 million direct jobs** over 15 years. Crucially, if EFTA fails to meet these benchmarks, India holds a legal **"rebalancing" snap-back mechanism** to partially suspend tariff concessions.
- **Asymmetric Tariff Bargain:** EFTA granted zero-duty access across **92.2% of tariff lines** covering **99.6% of India’s exports**; India offered phased concessions on 82.7% of lines (~95.3% of EFTA exports, largely Swiss watches, precision machinery, pharma, and gold—while strictly insulating sensitive dairy, soya, coal, and PLI sectors).

### India-UK CETA and Double Contributions Convention (In Force July 15, 2026)

Concluded on May 6, 2025, signed in London on **July 24, 2025** by PM Modi and UK PM Keir Starmer, and **entering into force on July 15, 2026**, the **India–UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA)** anchors post-Brexit Britain’s Indo-Pacific tilt:

- **Near-Total Market Access for Indian Exports:** The UK eliminated tariffs across **99% of tariff lines (~100% of India’s export value)**—delivering immediate zero-duty gains for labor-intensive textiles, apparel, leather, footwear, marine products, gems, and engineering goods. India offered phased cuts on **90% of UK lines**: Scotch whisky tariffs dropped immediately from **150% to 75%** (phasing to **40% by Year 10**), and luxury automobiles dropped from >100% to **10% under strict quotas**, while dairy, apples, oats, and edible oils remained completely excluded.
- **Double Contributions Convention (DCC):** Negotiated alongside CETA as India’s flagship services breakthrough, the **DCC** social-security pact exempts Indian IT/corporate professionals temporarily posted in the UK—and their employers—for up to **three years** from paying dual UK National Insurance contributions, saving Indian workers and firms an estimated **~₹4,000 crore annually** while doubling bilateral trade ambitions from ~$56B toward $120B by 2030.

### India-EU Free Trade Agreement (Concluded January 26–27, 2026), TTC & Green Trade Friction

After nearly nineteen years of intermittent negotiations (relaunched June 2022), India and the 27-member European Union **formally concluded negotiations on the India–EU Free Trade Agreement at the 16th India–EU Summit in New Delhi on January 26–27, 2026** (PM Modi with European Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen)—creating a **$27 trillion combined economic space** of two billion consumers (undergoing legal scrubbing and European Parliament ratification toward **2027 entry into force**):

- **Tariff Architecture (PIB Benchmarks):** The EU committed to liberalize **96.8% of tariff lines** covering **99.5% of India’s export value** (with **70.4% of lines / 90.7% export value** moving to zero duty on Day 1—textiles, garments, leather, footwear, marine products, gems, chemicals, machinery, pharma). India committed to gradual cuts across **96.6% of EU export value** over up to 10 years—including reducing European car tariffs from 110% to **10% under a 250,000-unit annual quota**, wines from 150% to 20–30%, and spirits to 40%, while shielding dairy, beef, poultry, cereals, and sugar. Parallel tracks include a **Mobility Framework** for skilled professionals/students, standalone **Investment Protection** and **GI** negotiations, and an **EU–India Security and Defence Partnership**.
- **India–EU Trade and Technology Council (TTC):** Operationalized in **February 2023** (2nd Ministerial in New Delhi, February 28, 2025)—the EU’s **only second TTC globally after the United States**—coordinating Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI), semiconductor supply chains, 6G, and green tech.
- **Unresolved Green Protectionism — CBAM & EUDR:** Even after the historic FTA conclusion, structural friction persists over Europe’s unilateral climate-trade walls. The EU’s **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** transitioned from reporting to its **definitive financial levy on January 1, 2026**, taxing carbon-intensive Indian steel, aluminium, cement, and fertilizers (Brussels declined a blanket FTA exemption, offering only a technical verification dialogue, carbon-price recognition for India’s domestic carbon market, and €500M decarbonization finance). Concurrently, the **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)**—requiring strict plot-level geolocation proof of zero deforestation (delayed to **December 30, 2026** for large operators and June 2027 for SMEs)—threatens smallholder Indian exports of coffee, rubber, cocoa, wood products, and leather.

## Key European Bilateral Anchors - France and Germany

Within Europe, India avoids treating the EU as a monolith—anchoring hard-security and industrial ties in Europe’s two continental pillars, **France** and **Germany**.

### France - India’s Most Autonomous Western Partner ("Special Global Strategic Partnership")

France holds a singular trust premium in New Delhi: Paris refused to sanction India after **Pokhran-II (1998)**, pioneered India’s first Western Strategic Partnership (1998), and shares India’s instinct for **strategic autonomy** independent of Washington. Anchored by the **Horizon 2047 Roadmap** (July 2023), President **Emmanuel Macron’s state visit to India (February 17–19, 2026)** elevated bilateral ties to a **"Special Global Strategic Partnership"**, launched the **India–France Year of Innovation 2026**, and inaugurated the **Tata–Airbus H125 helicopter Final Assembly Line** in Karnataka:

- **Aerospace, Naval & Jet-Engine Sovereignty:** Alongside 36 operational IAF Rafales, India signed the **₹63,000–64,000 crore contract for 26 Rafale-M** carrier-borne naval fighters on **April 28, 2025** (to operate off *INS Vikrant*), complementing six Mazagon Dock-built **Scorpène (*Kalvari*-class)** submarines (with three follow-on boats in advanced negotiation) and a **February 2026 BEL–Safran HAMMER** smart-munition JV. The crown jewel is the proposed **DRDO (GTRE)–Safran 120 kN fifth-generation jet engine co-development** for India’s **AMCA Mk-2** stealth fighter—offering **100% Intellectual Property (IP) transfer** (agreed in principle August 2025, advanced toward CCS clearance in February–late 2026), surpassing the US F414’s ~80% transfer ceiling.
- **Nuclear Energy & Resident Indo-Pacific Partnership:** While the mega **Jaitapur Nuclear Power Project (Maharashtra — 6 × 1,650 MW = 9,900 MW EPR reactors)** remains bogged down in tariff-viability and financing disputes despite India’s SHANTI Act (2025), Paris and New Delhi signed a fresh **February 2026 SMR/AMR civil nuclear agreement**. Geographically, France is a **resident Indian Ocean power** via **La Réunion** (1.6 million French citizens and ~9 million sq km Indo-Pacific EEZ), executing **Varuna** (navy), **Garuda** (air), and **Shakti** (army) drills, joint western IOR patrols, and **India–France–Australia** and **India–France–UAE** minilateral trilaterals.

### Germany - Green Capital, Skilled Mobility & the P-75(I) Defense Pivot

Historically constrained by post-WWII pacifist arms-export restrictions and China-centric industrial dependence, Germany underwent a decisive strategic pivot (*Zeitenwende*) toward India across 2024–2026—crystallized during the **7th Intergovernmental Consultations (Oct 2024)** and **Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s first official visit to India (January 12–13, 2026)**:

- **Green Development & Talent Bridge:** Under the **Green and Sustainable Development Partnership (GSDP, May 2022)**, Berlin committed **€10 billion** through 2030 for Indian renewable grids, green hydrogen, and urban mobility. Simultaneously, Germany expanded skilled-worker visas for Indians to **90,000 annually** and granted **visa-free airport transit** for Indian passport holders in January 2026.
- **Defense Industrial Breakthrough — Project-75(I) Submarines:** Ending decades of export hesitation, Berlin and New Delhi signed a **Joint Declaration of Intent on Defence Industrial Cooperation (January 2026)**. Strategically, Germany’s **ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems (TKMS)**—offering its fuel-cell Air Independent Propulsion (**AIP**) Type-214NG submarine partnered with **Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders (MDL)**—emerged as the **sole technically compliant bidder** for India’s **Project-75(I)** (6 conventional stealth submarines) in January 2025 after Spain’s Navantia failed sea trials. Commercial negotiations progressed through 2026 on the **~₹70,000–99,000 crore** deal toward final CCS approval, positioning Germany alongside France as a Tier-1 Indian maritime industrial partner.

## The Arctic Geopolitical Frontier - Melting Ice, Corridor Wars & India’s Polar Strategy

Warming roughly **three to four times faster** than the global mean (*Arctic Amplification*), the Arctic has shifted from a frozen scientific commons into an acute theater of resource, shipping, and military competition. Beneath its retreating sea ice lie an estimated **13% of the world’s undiscovered oil**, **30% of undiscovered natural gas**, critical rare-earth minerals, and emerging trans-polar sea lanes—whose melting directly teleconnects with **Indian Southwest Monsoon variability** and global sea-level rise.

### The Arctic Council Freeze and NATO’s Polar Encirclement

Established by the **September 19, 1996 Ottawa Declaration**, the **Arctic Council** is the premier consensus-based intergovernmental forum governing Arctic environmental protection and sustainable development (involving six Indigenous "Permanent Participants"). Crucially, its founding mandate **explicitly excludes military security**.

Membership is strictly restricted to the **eight Arctic States** with territory above the Arctic Circle: **Canada, Denmark (via Greenland), Finland, Iceland, Norway, Russia, Sweden, and the United States (via Alaska)**. Following **Finland’s (2023)** and **Sweden’s (2024)** accession to NATO, **seven of the eight Arctic States ("Arctic 7") are now NATO allies**, geopolitically isolating Russia. After Moscow’s February 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the A7 suspended high-level political cooperation with Russia; although Norway’s (2023–25) and **Denmark/Greenland’s (2025–27)** chairmanships cautiously revived limited virtual technical Working Groups, the Council remains diplomatically fractured. **India has held permanent Observer status since the May 2013 Kiruna Ministerial** (alongside 12 other non-Arctic states including China, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, UK, France, and Germany).

### Russia’s Northern Sea Route (NSR) vs. China’s "Polar Silk Road"

- **Russia’s Northern Sea Route (NSR):** Controlling **~53% of the Arctic coastline** and operating the world’s sole **nuclear-powered icebreaker fleet** (*Arktika*-class), Russia treats the **NSR** along its Siberian coast as a sovereign strategic lifeline. Navigable increasingly across summer/autumn months, the NSR cuts East Asia–Europe transit distances by **30–40%** compared to the Suez Canal and carries sanctioned Yamal/Arctic LNG-2 hydrocarbons eastward to Asia.
- **China’s "Polar Silk Road":** Despite lying ~1,500 km south of the Arctic Circle, China declared itself a **"Near-Arctic State"** in its **2018 Arctic White Paper**, folding the NSR into its BRI **"Polar Silk Road."** Exploiting Russia’s Western isolation across 2024–2026, Beijing expanded equity in Russian Arctic LNG, deployed its own polar icebreakers (*Xue Long 2*), and initiated joint Sino–Russian Arctic coast-guard patrols—raising alarms in both Washington and New Delhi over Chinese dual-use polar dominance.

### India’s 2022 Arctic Policy, Svalbard Rights & Himadri Year-Round Footprint

India’s legal footprint in the High North predates independence: British India signed the **1920 Svalbard Treaty** in Paris, granting India non-discriminatory rights to conduct scientific research and peaceful commercial activities in Norway’s Svalbard archipelago. Guided today by the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) and the **National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR, Goa)**, India unveiled its formal policy—**"India and the Arctic: Building a Partnership for Sustainable Development"**—on **March 17, 2022**, anchored on **six pillars**: (1) Science and Research; (2) Climate and Environmental Protection; (3) Economic and Human Development; (4) Transportation and Connectivity; (5) Governance and International Cooperation; and (6) National Capacity Building.

- **Himadri Station & Scientific Assets:** Established in **July 2008** at **Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard (Norway)**, **Himadri** is India’s flagship Arctic research station. Previously seasonal (summer-only), India launched its **first-ever Winter Arctic Expedition in December 2023**, transforming **Himadri into a year-round polar observatory**—complemented by **IndARC** (India’s first underwater moored observatory in Kongsfjorden, deployed 2014) and the **Gruvebadet** atmospheric laboratory.
- **Strategic Balancing Through Polar Connectivity:** India is acquiring an indigenous ice-class **Polar Research Vessel (PRV)** (partnering Garden Reach Shipbuilders with Norway’s Kongsberg) and engaging Moscow on **NSR commercial navigation** linked to the **Chennai–Vladivostok corridor** and **RELOS**—seeking both energy diversification and a non-Chinese multipolar presence across the thawing High North.

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions

Do **NOT** include the UK (left EU in 2020, did not rejoin EFTA), Austria, Sweden, or Finland (all EU members). **TEPA** entered into force **October 1, 2025** and is the **first FTA globally** with a legally binding **$100B FDI + 1M jobs** clause and tariff **snap-back mechanism**.

Also remember: **CBAM** definitive financial levy took effect **January 1, 2026**—India did **NOT** receive a blanket CBAM exemption inside the EU FTA!

>   1. Exactly **8 Arctic States** (Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Russia, Sweden, USA) — **Denmark** qualifies via **Greenland**, **USA** via **Alaska**; **UK, Japan, China, and India** are **Observers** (India since **2013**), NOT members.
>   2. The Arctic Council was formed by the **1996 Ottawa Declaration** (NOT a binding treaty) and **explicitly excludes military security** from its mandate.
>   3. **7 of 8 Arctic Council members** are now **NATO members** (after Finland 2023 & Sweden 2024)—Russia is the sole non-NATO member.

> [!info] Mains Arsenal
> **1. The European Hedge Against Bilateral Vulnerability (2025–2026 Grand Strategy):**
> - By simultaneously activating **EFTA TEPA (Oct 2025)**, **UK CETA + DCC (July 2026)**, and concluding the **India–EU FTA (Jan 2026)**—covering 32 advanced European economies—India executed a masterstroke of **geo-economic diversification**. These pacts directly cushion Indian exporters against **Trump 2.0 tariff volatility** while reducing excessive dependence on Chinese intermediate inputs.
>
> **2. Managing the Russia–China–West Trilemma:**
> - India’s post-2022 Russia posture exemplifies **pragmatic multi-alignment**: refusing Western moral coercion on Ukraine while simultaneously trimming Russian oil share (~40% → ~19–24%) when US secondary tariffs threatened wider national exports, diversifying arms toward **France (Rafale-M, 100% IP Safran engine)** and **Germany (P-75I)**, and deploying **Act Far East / Chennai–Vladivostok / RELOS** to prevent Beijing from monopolizing Moscow’s eastern and Arctic pivot.
>
> **3. Arctic Policy as Climate + Geopolitical Insurance:**
> - Arctic warming is not a distant polar curiosity; it alters the **Indian Monsoon**, opens the **Northern Sea Route** (30–40% shorter than Suez), and risks a **Sino–Russian Polar Silk Road monopoly**. India’s **six-pillar 2022 Arctic Policy**, year-round **Himadri** presence (via the 1920 Svalbard Treaty), and planned **Polar Research Vessel** position New Delhi as a responsible scientific and connectivity stakeholder in the thawing High North.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature

- **The "Svalbard Treaty & India" Oversight:** Aspirants often assume India’s Arctic presence began in 2008 (*Himadri*) or 2013 (Observer status). **Exact fact:** British India was an original **1920 signatory to the Svalbard Treaty**, giving sovereign India century-old legal standing in Norway’s Svalbard archipelago.
- **FTA Specific-Clause Mix-Up:** Do NOT cross-wire the three European pacts—**$100B binding FDI / 1M jobs** belongs strictly to **EFTA TEPA**; the **3-year Double Contributions Convention (DCC)** belongs to **UK CETA**; and **96.8% tariff lines / 99.5% export value + 250,000 car quota** belongs to the **India–EU FTA**.
- **Safran (France) vs. GE (USA) Fighter Engine Trap:** **GE F414** (~80% ToT, USA) is for **LCA Tejas Mk-2**; **Safran 120 kN** (100% IP co-development, France) is proposed for the 5th-generation **AMCA Mk-2**. Similarly, **Project-75** (*Kalvari*/Scorpène) is **French**, whereas **Project-75(I)** (AIP Type-214NG sole compliant bidder) is **German (TKMS–MDL)**.
- **Arctic Council Institutional Misstatement:** Watch for Prelims options claiming the Arctic Council was created by a *"UN treaty"* or handles *"Arctic military demilitarization"*—both are **false** (created by the non-treaty **1996 Ottawa Declaration**; military security is explicitly excluded).
