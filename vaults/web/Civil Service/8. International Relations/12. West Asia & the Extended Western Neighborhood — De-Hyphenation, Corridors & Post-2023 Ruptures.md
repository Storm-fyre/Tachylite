# Chapter 12 - West Asia & the Extended Western Neighborhood — De-Hyphenation, Corridors & Post-2023 Ruptures

West Asia is not a distant geopolitical theater for New Delhi; it is India's **extended western neighborhood**, separated from the Gujarat and Konkan coasts by less than a thousand nautical miles of the Arabian Sea. No other region fuses India's domestic macroeconomic stability, diaspora welfare, maritime supply lines, and counter-terrorism architecture into a single volatile equation.

Between October 2023 and late 2026, that equation suffered its severest structural shock since the 1973 oil embargo. The Gaza conflict metastasized into the collapse of the Assad regime in Syria (December 2024), the June 2025 Twelve-Day War, and the direct US–Israeli–Iranian war beginning February 28, 2026. Navigating these ruptures without sacrificing energy flows, connectivity corridors, or nearly ten million Indian citizens has become the ultimate stress test of Indian **strategic autonomy**.

## 1. Strategic Architecture - From "Look West" to "Think West" & the De-Hyphenation Doctrine

### 1.1 Evolution of India's West Asia Grand Strategy
For four decades after Independence, India approached West Asia through an ideological, defensive prism. Non-Aligned solidarity with secular Arab nationalism (Nasser's Egypt, Ba'athist Iraq), dependence on Gulf oil, and anxiety over Pakistan rallying the **Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC)** on Jammu & Kashmir locked New Delhi into a rigid pro-Arab posture that delayed full diplomatic ties with Israel until January 1992.

The 1991 collapse of the Soviet Union and economic liberalization triggered a gradual transition—formalized as **"Look West"** (2005) and accelerated post-2014 into **"Link West / Think West."** Under this realist doctrine, West Asia ceased to be viewed merely as a hydrocarbon filling station and labour sink. Instead, New Delhi reframed the Gulf monarchies, Israel, and Iran as co-investors in Indian infrastructure, defense co-production partners, counter-terror collaborators, and multimodal bridges to Europe and Eurasia.

### 1.2 Hard Material Imperatives (Diaspora, Remittances & Energy Parity)
Three structural dependencies anchor India's stakes in the region, each exhibiting important shifts in 2024–2026 official datasets:

- **The Gulf Diaspora (Dual-Standard Parity):** Legacy textbooks cite **~9 million** Indians in the Gulf. Per **MEA parliamentary disclosures (2025–2026)**, the figure has climbed to **~9.68–10 million** across the six **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)** states—led by the **UAE (4.33 million)**, **Saudi Arabia (2.75 million)**, **Kuwait (1.04 million)**, **Qatar (0.83 million)**, **Oman (0.68 million)**, and **Bahrain (0.32 million)**. Crucially, **95.77 lakh** of these are **Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)** holding Indian passports—accounting for **55.8% of all NRIs worldwide**—with barely 1 lakh Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs). Because Gulf monarchies rarely grant citizenship, any regional war immediately converts this population into a massive Indian consular and non-combatant evacuation liability.
- **Remittances & Structural Shift (RBI 6th Survey Parity):** India remained the world's top remittance recipient, drawing **$118.7 billion in FY 2023–24** and **$135.4 billion (provisional) in FY 2024–25** (Economic Survey 2025–26). While GCC states still generate **~$52.44 billion annually** (FY 2024–25 MEA data), the **RBI's Sixth Round of India's Remittances Survey (March 2025)** documented a historic inflection: the GCC's *percentage share* fell from **47% (FY17) to 38% (FY24)**, overtaken for the first time by **Advanced Economies (>50%)**, with the **United States (27.7%)** surpassing the **UAE (19.2%)** as India's top single source. Gulf nationalization policies (*Nitaqat/Saudization*, *Emiratization*) and *Kafala* sponsorship vulnerabilities—only partially mitigated by India's *e-Migrate* portal, bilateral labour pacts, and voluntary, **non-binding** multilateral mobility platforms such as the **Abu Dhabi Dialogue (2008)** (bridging Asian labour-origin states with GCC destinations on contractual labour mobility), the **Colombo Process (2003)** (12 Asian labour-origin states), and the **Global Forum on Migration and Development (GFMD)** (proposed by former UNSG Kofi Annan)—drive this relative shift.
- **Hydrocarbons & Price-Shock Exposure:** Historically, West Asia supplied **60–65%** of India's crude oil. Following post-2022 discounted Russian crude purchases, Middle Eastern crude share dipped to **46.9% (April–January FY26, PPAC)** before rebounding to **~54% in September 2026** as refiners balanced sanctions pressure and spot cargoes. Gas dependency remains far steeper: West Asia supplies **over 80% of India's LPG** and the bulk of its **LNG** (anchored by Qatar and the UAE). The 2026 Iran War exposed this raw macroeconomic nerve: the Indian crude basket nearly doubled from **$65.08/barrel (October 2025) to $119.91/barrel (October 2026)**, pushing India's April–October FY27 crude import bill to **$111.0 billion**—nearly matching the entire FY26 bill ($125.8 billion) in just seven months.
- **Counter-Terrorism & Neutralizing the OIC:** Deepening intelligence sharing, extradition cooperation, and anti-terror financing coordination with Riyadh and Abu Dhabi systematically dismantled Pakistan's diplomatic monopoly over the Islamic world. Symbolic validation arrived in March 2019, when the UAE invited India as **Guest of Honour** to the OIC Foreign Ministers' Conclave despite the Balakot airstrikes days earlier; subsequent OIC condemnations of Article 370 abrogation failed to derail Gulf sovereign investments in Jammu & Kashmir.

### 1.3 The De-Hyphenation Doctrine - Logic and Limits
**De-hyphenation** is the operational doctrine of decoupling India's bilateral relationship with one West Asian actor from its hostility toward another, engaging each strictly on independent strategic merit. Structurally, it adapts **multi-alignment** to a region polarized along three overlapping fault lines: **Israel vs. Palestine**, **Sunni Arab Gulf vs. Shia Iran**, and **US/Israel vs. Iran's Axis of Resistance**.

New Delhi formalized Israel–Palestine de-hyphenation in **July 2017**, when PM Narendra Modi became the first sitting Indian Prime Minister to visit Israel *without* a balancing stop in Ramallah, followed by a standalone visit to **Ramallah in February 2018**—treating Tel Aviv as a high-tech security partner and Palestine as a political-humanitarian commitment anchored in a negotiated **Two-State Solution**. Simultaneously, India cultivated **Saudi Arabia and the UAE** for capital and energy while engaging **Iran** for Eurasian access via Chabahar.

The 2023–2026 wars revealed the **structural ceiling** of de-hyphenation. De-hyphenation flourishes under cold rivalries (proxy competition and rhetorical hostility), where partners tolerate hedging. It fractures under **kinetic great-power warfare**: US secondary sanctions forced India's operational retreat at Chabahar in April 2026, Iranian maritime interdictions threatened India-bound tankers in Hormuz, and Gaza's civilian toll forced delicate recalibrations in India's UN General Assembly voting to preserve Global South and Arab diplomatic capital.

## 2. Bilateral & Regional Pillars - GCC, Israel & Iran

### 2.1 The Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) - From Hydrocarbon Vendors to Strategic Investors
India's economic architecture with the six-member GCC (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain) has pivoted from ad hoc energy procurement toward institutional trade pacts, sovereign wealth integration, and defense interoperability. On the collective plane, India and the GCC operationalized their **Joint Action Plan (2024–2028)** and formally launched **India–GCC Free Trade Agreement (FTA) negotiations in February 2026**, building on landmark bilateral CEPAs with the UAE and Oman.

#### United Arab Emirates (UAE) - The Anchor Partner
Upgraded to a **Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**, the UAE represents India's deepest operational footprint in West Asia across trade, finance, energy buffers, and soft power:

- **CEPA & Trade Surge:** Signed in February 2022 (in force May 2022), the **India–UAE CEPA** was India's first trade accord in the MENA region. Bilateral trade climbed to **$101.25 billion in FY 2025–26**, cementing the UAE as India's **third-largest trading partner** and **second-largest export destination** ($37.35 billion). During UAE President **Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan's** visit to New Delhi on **January 19, 2026**, both sides set a target of **$200 billion bilateral trade by 2032**. Structural frictions persist, however, notably a ~$26 billion Indian trade deficit and Indian customs scrutiny over third-party gold and silver re-routing through Dubai.
- **Financial Plumbing & Local Currency Settlement:** Under the July 2023 RBI–Central Bank of the UAE framework, both sides operationalized **Rupee–Dirham Local Currency Settlement (LCS)** for bilateral trade (including crude settlements) and moved to interlink India's **UPI** with the UAE's **Aani** instant payment platform, alongside RuPay–Jaywan domestic card integration.
- **Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPRs) & Hormuz Bypass:** The UAE's **ADNOC** became the first—and so far only—foreign energy company to store crude in India's underground **Indian Strategic Petroleum Reserves Limited (ISPRL)** caverns, holding **5.86 million barrels at Mangalore** (Karnataka), with India retaining first right of refusal during emergencies. During PM Modi's **May 15, 2026 visit to the UAE**, ISPRL and ADNOC signed a fresh strategic MoU to expand ADNOC's storage up to **30 million barrels** across **Visakhapatnam** and Phase-II caverns at **Chandikhol (Odisha)**, alongside exploring **Indian SPR crude storage at Fujairah**—a deep-water UAE port on the Gulf of Oman located *outside* the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint.
- **LNG, Tech, Infrastructure & Soft Power:** In January 2026, **HPCL and ADNOC Gas** signed a 10-year, $3 billion Sales & Purchase Agreement for **0.5 MMTPA LNG** starting 2028, positioning the UAE as India's second-largest LNG supplier after Qatar. Commercial nodes include **Bharat Mart** (a 2.7 million sq. ft. Indian MSME hub in Dubai's Jebel Ali Free Zone), the **MAITRI Virtual Trade Corridor**, the **2024 Bilateral Investment Treaty (BIT)**, Dholera SIR investments, **C-DAC–G42** AI supercomputing collaboration, sovereign **Digital Embassies**, and a **Framework for Strategic Defence Partnership (May 2026)**. Institutionally, **IIT Delhi–Abu Dhabi** (inaugurated 2024, scaling to 400 students with specialized AI Master's programmes by late 2026) and the **BAPS Hindu Mandir (2024)** anchor cultural and knowledge diplomacy. Minilaterally, India, France, and the UAE operate a Western Indian Ocean **trilateral** covering maritime security, solar energy, and defense.

#### Oman - The Maritime Sentinel & June 2026 CEPA Breakthrough
Geographically commanding the southern shore of the Strait of Hormuz and opening directly onto the Arabian Sea, Oman is India's oldest and quietest strategic partner in the Gulf. India conducts tri-service exercises with Muscat (*Al Najah*, *Naseem-Al-Bahr*, *Eastern Bridge*) and enjoys vital logistical and maintenance access at **Duqm Port**, giving the Indian Navy an operational fulcrum overlooking the Western Indian Ocean and Horn of Africa.

Commercially, bilateral ties achieved a step-change with the **India–Oman CEPA**, signed on **December 18, 2025** in Muscat during PM Modi's visit and **entering into force on June 1, 2026** (Oman's first bilateral trade pact in twenty years). Key architecture includes:
- **Goods:** Immediate **100% duty-free access** on **98.08% of Oman's tariff lines**, covering **99.38% of India's export value** (textiles, gems, engineering goods, pharmaceuticals, automobiles), while India safeguarded domestic farmers by excluding sensitive lines (dairy, cereals, spices, edible oils, fruits, vegetables).
- **Services & Regulatory Fast-Track:** Commitments across **127 services sub-sectors**, raising Oman's **Intra-Corporate Transferee (ICT) ceiling from 20% to 50%** for Indian engineers, doctors, and IT consultants, plus **90-day fast-track marketing authorization** for Indian pharmaceuticals approved by USFDA, EMA, UK MHRA, or TGA.
- **Strategic Bypass Proof-of-Concept:** During the early 2026 Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption, regional logistics chains rerouted Gulf cargo overland to Oman's Arabian Sea deep-water ports—**Salalah, Duqm, and Sohar**—validating Oman's irreplaceable role as an energy and trade bypass corridor.

#### Saudi Arabia - Vision 2030, SPC & Defense Institutionalization
Anchored by the **Delhi Declaration (2006)** and **Riyadh Declaration (2010)**, India–Saudi ties were institutionalized via the **Strategic Partnership Council (SPC)** in 2019, co-chaired by the Indian Prime Minister and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman across two pillars: (i) **Political, Security, Social and Cultural (PSSC)** and (ii) **Economy and Investment**.

Following the inaugural SPC Leaders' Meeting in New Delhi (September 2023), both leaders co-chaired the **Second SPC Leaders' Meeting in Jeddah on April 22, 2025** (curtailed when PM Modi returned urgently following the Pahalgam terror attack). Major outcomes included establishing a new **Ministerial Committee on Defence Cooperation** under the SPC—building on bilateral exercises (*Al-Mohed Al-Hindi* naval, *Sada Tanseeq* army)—and advancing Saudi Arabia's **$100 billion investment commitment** via an understanding to co-develop **two oil refineries in India**. With Riyadh supplying **~13.6% of India's crude and ~15% of its LPG (FY25)**, cooperation now extends into Saudi **Vision 2030** megaprojects, cybersecurity for critical infrastructure, green hydrogen, and the Northern Corridor of IMEC.

#### Qatar & Kuwait - Gas Security and Quiet Diplomacy
- **Qatar:** Supplies over 40% of India's LNG. In February 2024, **Petronet LNG and QatarEnergy** renewed their long-term supply contract for **20 years (2028–2048)** at **7.5 MMTPA** on substantially improved delivered terms. Equally revealing was India's discreet, high-level diplomatic intervention in February 2024 that secured the release and repatriation of **eight former Indian Navy personnel** initially sentenced to death in Doha—demonstrating quiet statecraft over megaphone diplomacy.
- **Kuwait:** Elevated bilateral ties to a **Strategic Partnership**, remains a major crude supplier and host to over one million Indian workers, and requires continuous consular engagement (underscored by the June 2024 Mangaf labour-camp fire tragedy).

### 2.2 Israel - From Covert Supplier to "Special Strategic Partnership"
Since establishing full diplomatic ties in **January 1992**, India–Israel relations moved from discreet security cooperation—catalyzed during the **1999 Kargil War**, when Israel airlifted laser-guided munitions, Heron UAVs, and artillery ammunition without political conditions—to a **Strategic Partnership (2017)** and, during PM Modi's **February 25–26, 2026 State Visit to Israel** (including an address to the Knesset), to a **"Special Strategic Partnership for Peace, Innovation, and Prosperity."**

- **Defense Co-Production & Naval Modernization:** India remains Israel's largest single defense market, historically absorbing roughly 40% of Israeli arms exports. Flagship platforms span **Barak-8 / MR-SAM** (jointly developed by DRDO and IAI), **Phalcon AWACS**, **Spyder** air-defense systems, **Heron / Searcher UAVs**, and **Hermes-900 (Drishti-10)** drones co-manufactured in Hyderabad by the Adani–Elbit joint venture. On **November 18, 2026**, the Ministry of Defence inked a **₹1,976.40 crore ($208 million)** contract with Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) to modernize naval **Barak Point Defence Missile Systems** across Indian warships. With India raising domestic content thresholds under *Buy (Indian-IDDM)* from 50% to 60% in 2026, Israeli firms are increasingly pivoting to *Make in India* joint ventures.
- **Agriculture, Water & Frontier Tech:** Under the **Indo-Israel Agricultural Project (IIAP)** led by **MASHAV**, **35 Centres of Excellence (CoEs)** are operational across India (with 8 more underway), having trained **over one million Indian farmers** in micro-irrigation, protected cultivation, and arid-zone horticulture. The February 2026 visit launched the **ICAR–MASHAV India–Israel Innovation Centre for Agriculture (IINCA)**, alongside expanded cooperation in desalination, Ganga river rejuvenation, AI, quantum computing, semiconductors, cybersecurity, and the **$40 million I4F** industrial R&D fund.
- **Trade, Haifa Port & Labour Mobility:** Non-defense merchandise trade peaked at **$10.77 billion (FY23)** before regional wars and Red Sea disruptions compressed it to **$6.53 billion (FY24)** and **$3.75 billion (FY25)**. Both countries signed a **Bilateral Investment Agreement in September 2025** and concluded the **first round of India–Israel FTA negotiations in February 2026**. Strategically, an **Adani Ports-led consortium acquired Israel's Haifa Port ($1.18 billion, 2022–23)**—anchoring India's physical stake in IMEC's Mediterranean gateway—while bilateral mobility agreements dispatched tens of thousands of Indian construction and caregiving workers to Israel post-2023.
- **Abraham Accords & I2U2 ("West Asian Quad"):** The US-brokered **Abraham Accords (2020)**—normalizing Israeli ties with the **UAE, Bahrain, Sudan, and Morocco**—dissolved India's longstanding diplomatic friction between Gulf capitals and Tel Aviv. Capitalizing on this opening, **India, Israel, the UAE, and the USA** launched **I2U2** (October 2021 Foreign Ministers; **July 14, 2022 first Leaders' Summit**) as a non-military geo-economic minilateral spanning **six focus areas: water, energy, transportation, space, health, and food security**. Signature initiatives include a **$2 billion UAE-funded network of climate-smart integrated food parks in India** (leveraging US/Israeli agri-tech), a **300 MW hybrid wind-solar renewable energy project with battery storage in Gujarat**, and a joint Space Venture—reaffirmed by Modi and Netanyahu in February 2026 despite regional hostilities.
- **Calibrating Palestine & UNGA Votes:** India's post-October 2023 stance reflects disciplined de-hyphenation rather than ideological reversal. New Delhi unequivocally condemned Hamas's October 7 terrorist attack, abstained on an October 2023 UNGA ceasefire draft that omitted Hamas, then **voted in favour** of the December 2023 humanitarian ceasefire resolution and the **May 2024 UNGA resolution backing full UN membership for Palestine**, while delivering humanitarian aid to Gaza and reiterating support for a sovereign **Two-State Solution**. In parallel, security convergences remained intact: on **November 3, 2026**, India and Israel were the *only two states* in the 193-member UNGA First Committee to vote against a Pakistan–Belarus draft resolution on regional conventional arms control.

### 2.3 Iran - Civilizational Tether, Chabahar Port & the 2026 Sanctions Rupture
Geographically, Iran is India's sole overland gateway to Afghanistan, Central Asia, and Russia bypassing Pakistan. Historically, Iran was also a major crude supplier to Indian refineries until US re-imposition of secondary sanctions following Washington's 2018 exit from the **JCPOA (Iran Nuclear Deal)** reduced Indian oil imports from Tehran to zero, compressing bilateral trade to roughly **$2.3 billion** (largely Indian basmati rice, tea, and pharma against Iranian methanol, bitumen, and dry fruits).

#### Chabahar Port - Strategic Rise and the April 2026 Waiver Collapse
Located in Iran's **Sistan-Baluchistan** province on the Makran coast along the **Gulf of Oman**, **Chabahar** is Iran's only deep-water oceanic port outside the Strait of Hormuz, sitting roughly **72 nautical miles (~170 km overland)** west of China's **Gwadar Port** in Pakistan. It comprises two terminals: **Shahid Kalantari** and **Shahid Beheshti**—with India developing and operating the latter via **India Ports Global Limited (IPGL)**.

- **2003–2024 Trajectory:** Conceived in 2003 and operationalized through the **2016 India–Iran–Afghanistan Trilateral Transit Agreement** (backed by a 2018 US sanctions carve-out for Afghan reconstruction), Chabahar bypassed Pakistan to ship Indian wheat and humanitarian aid to Afghanistan. However, US steel and financial sanctions stalled India's participation in the **Chabahar–Zahedan railway**, prompting Tehran to proceed independently. On **May 13, 2024**, IPGL signed a landmark **10-year long-term contract** with Iran's Ports and Maritime Organisation (PMO) committing **$120 million** for port equipment and a **$250 million credit window** for port infrastructure.
- **The 2025–2026 Trump 2.0 Sanctions Hammer:** Within days of returning to office, President Donald Trump issued **National Security Presidential Memorandum-2 (NSPM-2) on February 4, 2025**, restoring "maximum pressure" and directing the State Department to revoke Iran sanctions waivers—explicitly naming Chabahar. Washington formally revoked the 2018 exemption in **September 2025**, granted India a temporary **six-month conditional waiver in October 2025**, and allowed that waiver to **expire on April 26, 2026** amid the US–Iran war.
- **India's Damage-Control Maneuver:** Facing direct exposure of Indian banks and shipping firms to US Treasury (OFAC) secondary sanctions, New Delhi confirmed in Parliament (March 2026) that it had fully discharged its **$120 million equipment commitment** with no further GoI financial liability, withdrew Indian operational personnel from Chabahar, and entered talks with Tehran on an interim mechanism transferring Shahid Beheshti operations to a local Iranian entity with a **built-in revert-back clause** should sanctions lift. Chabahar thus remains physically operational for regional trade but commercially unscalable under Washington's financial veto.

Connectivity corridors in West Asia are not neutral engineering projects; they are **geopolitical wagers** over who controls Eurasian trade arteries, bypasses hostile chokepoints, and sets digital and energy standards. India uniquely straddles *both* competing north-south and east-west architectures—**INSTC** (Sanction-Resistant Eurasian Axis) and **IMEC** (US–Gulf–European Indo-Abrahamic Axis)—neither of which has escaped the kinetic turbulence of 2023–2026.

#### Competing West Asian Corridor Architectures
Two rival regional proposals directly challenge IMEC's Northern Corridor monopoly. First, the **$17 billion Iraq–Turkey "Development Road"** (supported by Qatar and the UAE) seeks to connect Iraq's **Al-Faw Grand Port** on the Persian Gulf via 1,200 km of rail and highway straight into Turkey and Europe—explicitly bypassing Israel and addressing Ankara's resentment at being excluded from IMEC. Second, following the Assad regime's fall, Saudi-backed concepts for a northward land corridor through **post-Assad Syria** toward the Mediterranean have resurfaced, though frozen Gulf capital flows amid the 2026 Iran War keep both alternatives nascent.

## 4. Post-2023 Geopolitical Ruptures & India's Crisis Statecraft

### 4.1 The Beijing-Brokered Saudi–Iran Détente (March 2023) - Promise and Rupture
On **March 10, 2023**, China stunned Washington and New Delhi by brokering the restoration of full diplomatic relations between **Saudi Arabia and Iran** in Beijing—signaling China's graduation from a purely mercantilist oil buyer into a diplomatic power-broker in West Asia. For India, the détente initially yielded a welcome **stability dividend**: reduced Houthi attacks on Saudi Aramco facilities and calmer Gulf waters lowered energy risk premiums.

Yet the **2026 Iran War** exposed the hard limits of Beijing's diplomatic paper. China possessed neither the naval architecture nor the security guarantees to prevent US–Israeli strikes or Iranian retaliation against Gulf energy installations and Hormuz shipping. When Iranian missiles and drones struck Gulf infrastructure in early 2026—forcing Arab capitals to freeze regional investments and tighten security against Iranian assets—Beijing remained a risk-averse bystander, underscoring that **economic leverage cannot substitute for hard security deterrence**.

### 4.2 From October 7, 2023 to the 2026 Iran War - Escalation Ladder & India's Response
The regional security order unraveled across three distinct kinetic phases between late 2023 and late 2026:

1. **Phase I - Gaza, Lebanon & Breaking the "Shadow War" Taboo (Oct 2023 – Dec 2024):** Hamas's October 7, 2023 cross-border assault triggered Israel's devastating campaign in Gaza and a northern front against Hezbollah in Lebanon that systematically decapitated Hezbollah's senior leadership. In **April and October 2024**, Iran and Israel exchanged direct ballistic missile, drone, and air strikes on each other's sovereign territory—permanently shattering decades of proxy-only deterrence. India executed **Operation Ajay (October 2023)** to evacuate **1,343 nationals** (including OCIs and foreign spouses) from Israel.
2. **Phase II - The Twelve-Day War & Operation Sindhu (June 2025):** On **June 13, 2025**, Israel struck Iranian nuclear and military facilities, followed by direct US bombing of three Iranian nuclear sites, igniting the **Twelve-Day War**. India mounted **Operation Sindhu (June 18–27, 2025)**—a textbook display of multi-aligned consular agility. Using **19 special flights** (including three IAF C-17s), New Delhi evacuated **4,415 Indian nationals** (**3,597 from Iran** via Mashhad after Tehran specially opened its airspace, and overland through **Armenia's 44-km Nurduz–Agarak border** and Turkmenistan; **818 from Israel** overland via Jordan and Egypt), alongside Nepali and Sri Lankan citizens. Armenia's emergence as India's primary northern exit corridor underscored the dividend of India's defense exports (Pinaka, Akash-1S) to Yerevan, as Pakistan, Azerbaijan, and Turkey were geopolitically closed.
3. **Phase III - The 2026 Iran War & Strait of Hormuz Crisis (Feb 28, 2026 – Ongoing):** On **February 28, 2026**, joint US–Israeli forces launched **Operation Epic Fury**, striking Iranian air defenses, missile networks, and leadership sites—killing Supreme Leader **Ayatollah Ali Khamenei**. Iran's Assembly of Experts moved swiftly on **March 8, 2026** to select **Mojtaba Khamenei** as the new Supreme Leader. Following the collapse of a Pakistan-mediated temporary truce (April 8 – July 8, 2026), Washington imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports while IRGC naval forces periodically shut or mined approaches to the **Strait of Hormuz**, targeting merchant tankers and Gulf energy infrastructure.

India's diplomatic posture during the 2026 Iran War crystallized the mechanics of **hedged neutrality under fire**:
- **Balanced Restraint:** New Delhi avoided condemning either Washington/Tel Aviv's strikes or Tehran's regime publicly—Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri signed the condolence book in New Delhi and India dispatched two consignments of medical aid to Iran, even as PM Modi consulted Gulf monarchs and PM Netanyahu and condemned attacks on sovereign territory and maritime navigation.
- **Defending Maritime Red Lines:** When IRGC forces fired upon or interdicted India-bound tankers in April, July, and October 2026 (resulting in Indian seafarer casualties and missing crew on *MT Petro Dolphin*), the MEA summoned the Iranian ambassador and issued pointed condemnations demanding unimpeded transit. Simultaneously, discreet humanitarian gestures—such as extending port refuge—and sustained back-channel engagement paid tangible dividends when Iranian lawmakers announced selective **Hormuz transit exemptions** in November 2026 for vessels linked to **India, China, Iraq, and Qatar**, while India facilitated cross-border evacuation of an additional **2,557 nationals from Iran via Armenia**.

### 4.3 December 2024 Collapse of the Assad Regime in Syria
On **December 8, 2024**, a lightning offensive led by *Hayat Tahrir al-Sham (HTS)* ended 54 years of Ba'athist Assad family rule in Damascus—a geopolitical earthquake with four systemic consequences:

- **Severing Iran's "Shia Crescent" Land Bridge:** Syria was the indispensable territorial artery linking Tehran through Iraq to Hezbollah in Lebanon. Its loss stripped Iran of strategic depth against Israel, severely degrading Hezbollah's resupply architecture even as Hezbollah continued covert spoiler plots inside Syria through late 2026.
- **Erosion of Russian Mediterranean Projection:** Moscow's preoccupation in Ukraine prevented a repeat of its 2015 rescue of Damascus, jeopardizing Russia's sole warm-water Mediterranean naval base (**Tartus**) and airbase (**Khmeimim**).
- **Turkish Ascendancy & Gulf Recalibration:** Ankara emerged as the dominant northern broker in Syria, while transitional President **Ahmed al-Sharaa** pursued pragmatic internal stabilization, winning tentative US backing and maintaining Syrian **neutrality during the 2026 Iran War**—though Iranian strikes on Gulf infrastructure froze vital Saudi, Qatari, and Emirati reconstruction investments.
- **India's Pragmatic Engagement:** India historically valued the secular Ba'athist regime for backing New Delhi's position on Kashmir at the OIC, and evacuated 86 nationals during the 2024–25 transition. Post-Assad, New Delhi pivoted from ideological attachment to calibrated diplomatic engagement with Damascus, monitoring counter-terror spillovers (ISIS resurgence and foreign fighters) while safeguarding long-term INSTC and Levant equities.

### 4.4 Red Sea / Bab-el-Mandeb Crisis & Indian Navy's Operation Sankalp
Beginning with the November 19, 2023 seizure of the car carrier *Galaxy Leader*, Yemen's Iran-backed **Houthi (*Ansar Allah*)** movement waged a sustained anti-ship ballistic missile, cruise missile, and unmanned surface vessel campaign across the **Southern Red Sea and Bab-el-Mandeb Strait**—targeting over **100 merchant vessels**, sinking four, and forcing global shipping lines to reroute around the **Cape of Good Hope** (adding **10–14 days** and steep war-risk insurance surcharges). Renewed Yemen hostilities in July–August 2026 saw the sinking of the Indian-flagged vessel *MSV Faize Noore Oliya* off Hodeidah (with all 14 crew rescued).

The crisis exposed an acute Indian trade chokepoint: the Bab-el-Mandeb–Suez corridor carries roughly **50% of India's merchandise exports (~₹18 lakh crore)** and **30% of its imports (~₹17 lakh crore)**—India's primary commercial artery to Europe, North Africa, and the US East Coast. Moreover, Somali piracy opportunistically resurged in the Gulf of Aden as Western navies focused on Houthi missiles.

New Delhi responded through **Operation Sankalp**, demonstrating hard maritime autonomy:
- **Evolution & Autonomy:** Originally launched on **June 19, 2019** to escort Indian-flagged tankers through the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman, Operation Sankalp was massively expanded post-December 2023 across the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, and Arabian Sea. Crucially, India **declined to formally join** the US-led *Operation Prosperity Guardian* or launch kinetic strikes inside Yemen—preserving diplomatic channels with Tehran and avoiding entanglement in Western coalitions while deploying one of the largest independent naval task forces (up to a dozen destroyers, frigates, and OPVs) in the Western IOR.
- **Kinetic & Legal Teeth:** Backed by the **Maritime Anti-Piracy Act, 2022** (which grants Indian forces statutory jurisdiction to arrest and prosecute high-seas pirates), Indian warships executed high-profile operations—including the **March 16, 2024 recapture of *MV Ruen*** (involving an IAF C-17 paradropping **MARCOS** commandos and combat boats to join *INS Kolkata*, rescuing 17 crew and capturing 35 pirates), *INS Sumitra*'s rescue of hijacked fishing vessels (including 19 Pakistani nationals on *FV Al Naeemi*), and firefighting/escort missions for missile-struck tankers like *MSC Sky II*.
- **Domain Awareness & Strategic Dividend:** Coordinated with the **Information Fusion Centre – Indian Ocean Region (IFC-IOR)** in Gurugram—which tracked 37 hybrid maritime incidents in October 2026 alone across Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman—Operation Sankalp elevated the Indian Navy from a coastal defender to the Western IOR's **Preferred Security Partner and First Responder**, standing in stark contrast to the PLA Navy's reluctance to escort non-Chinese commercial hulls under fire.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature
- **IMEC Signatories vs. Transit Nodes Trap:** UPSC frequently slips *Israel, Jordan, or Greece* into the list of **original September 2023 IMEC MoU signatories**. The original eight signatories are strictly **India, USA, Saudi Arabia, UAE, EU, France, Germany, and Italy**; Israel, Jordan, and Greece are planned transit geographies (with Greece, Cyprus, Oman, Egypt, and Lebanon exploring late-2026 "IMEC+" entry).
- **Hormuz Bypass Port Inversion:** Examiners test whether **Chabahar (Iran)**, **Fujairah (UAE)**, and **Duqm/Salalah/Sohar (Oman)** lie *inside* the Persian Gulf. All five sit on the **Gulf of Oman / Arabian Sea** *outside* the Strait of Hormuz; conversely, **Bandar Abbas (Iran)**, **Jebel Ali (UAE)**, **Al-Faw (Iraq)**, **Bahrain**, and **Qatar** lie *inside* the Persian Gulf.
- **Remittances Source-Share Reversal:** A classic outdated-stat trap claims *"GCC countries account for over 50% of India's total inward remittances."* Per the **RBI's 6th Remittances Survey (March 2025)**, **Advanced Economies (>50%, led by the US at 27.7%)** have overtaken the **GCC (38%; UAE #2 at 19.2%)** in share—even though GCC remittances remain ~$52B+ in absolute terms.
- **INSTC vs. Ashgabat vs. Evacuation Ops Confusion:** **INSTC** has **13 members** (including Oman, Syria, Turkey, Ukraine, Armenia, Azerbaijan; *Bulgaria* is Observer; **Pakistan and China are NOT members**). Distinguish **Operation Ajay (Oct 2023, Israel)** from **Operation Sindhu (June 2025, Iran & Israel via Armenia/Turkmenistan/Jordan/Egypt)** and **Operation Sankalp (June 2019–present, maritime security in Hormuz/Gulf of Aden/Red Sea)**.
