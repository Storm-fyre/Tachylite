# Chapter 5 — Aeolian, Glacial, Periglacial, Coastal Landforms & Polar Cryosphere Dynamics

Beyond humid river basins, three specialized exogenic systems—wind in hyper-arid interiors, flowing ice across polar and high-alpine belts, and refracting waves along continental rims—carve Earth's starkest topography. Today, these geomorphic arenas have converged onto the front line of planetary climate risk, where Arctic amplification, West Antarctic marine-ice instability, and Himalayan glacier retreat actively redraw coastlines and Asian water security.

---

## 1. Aeolian Arid Geomorphology — Wind Physics & Desert Landforms

### Aeolian Transport & Erosional Physics
Because air possesses barely **1/800th the density of water**, wind exerts modest direct lifting force and transports sediment primarily via three size-sorted mechanisms:

- **Suspension (<0.07 mm fine silt/clay):** Lifted kilometers into the troposphere and carried across entire oceans (Saharan dust fertilizing Amazon rainforests; Central Asian dust building China's Loess Plateau).
- **Saltation (0.1–0.5 mm sand grains):** Ballistic hopping in parabolic arcs **0.5–2 meters above the ground**, carrying **~75% of all aeolian sand flux**. Because sandblasting concentrates strictly inside this lowest 2-meter saltation curtain, wind erosion preferentially undercuts rock bases—carving top-heavy **Mushroom Rocks (*Gara / Pedestal Rocks*)**.
- **Surface Creep (>0.5 mm coarse grains):** Rolling and sliding along the ground propelled by high-speed impacts from landing saltating grains.

Wind sculpts arid basins through **Deflation** (sweeping away loose unconsolidated fines), **Abrasion / Corrasion** (quartz grains sandblasting bedrock), and **Attrition** (windborne grains colliding into frosted, spherical *millet-seed* quartz sand).

### Erosional Arid Landforms
- **Deflation Hollows (Blowouts):** Shallow wind-scoured depressions excavated in loose desert sand or silt until deflation reaches the moist capillary fringe of the groundwater table—exposing verdant **Oases** or vast structural–deflation pits like Egypt's **Qattara Depression** ($-133\text{ m}$ below sea level).
- **Desert Pavements (*Reg* / *Serir* / *Gibber*):** Tightly packed, wind-varnished pebble armor left behind after deflation strips away all underlying silt and fine sand, shielding the desert floor from further scour. *(Distinguish strictly from sandy dune seas = **Erg** and bare swept bedrock plateaus = **Hamada**.)*
- **Ventifacts & *Dreikanter*:** Loose desert cobbles planed flat and pitted on their windward sides by abrasive saltation; periodic shifts in prevailing wind direction facet three sharp intersecting bevels, producing classic pyramidal ***Dreikanter*** ("three-edged").
- **Zeugens vs. Yardangs (Structural Bedding Contrast):**
  - **Zeugens:** Flat-topped, tabular **inkpot or ridge-and-furrow** pedestals carved in **horizontally layered** alternating resistant caprock and soft lower strata; dew and thermal cracking breach the upper crust, letting saltating sand hollow out the softer underlying bed.
  - **Yardangs:** Aerodynamic, keel-like **whale-back ridges** separated by deep wind-scoured corridors carved in **vertically or steeply inclined** alternating hard and soft strata aligned **parallel** to the prevailing wind (**Lut Desert**, Iran; **Lop Nur**, China).
- **Pediments & Inselbergs:** Flanking arid mountain fronts, episodic desert **sheetfloods** and parallel scarp retreat plane a gently sloping ($1^\circ\text{–}7^\circ$), concave **bare-bedrock erosional ramp** called a **Pediment** (often thinly veneered with transient gravel). Isolated, steep-sided residual bedrock monoliths left standing abruptly above flat coalesced pediplains are **Inselbergs** ("island mountains", e.g., **Uluru / Ayers Rock**, Australia).

### Depositional & Fluvio-Aeolian Arid Landforms
**Sand Dunes** self-organize into five diagnostic planforms controlled strictly by three variables—**wind directional variability, sand supply, and anchoring vegetation**:

1. **Barchans:** Classic crescentic mobile dunes featuring a gentle windward slope ($10^\circ\text{–}15^\circ$), a steep leeward **slip face at the angle of repose ($32^\circ\text{–}34^\circ$)**, and twin **horns pointing *downwind***. They form under **unidirectional wind**, **scarce sand supply**, and flat vegetation-free ground (Thar Desert, Sahara).
2. **Parabolic Dunes:** U-shaped, reversed-crescent dunes whose **horns point *upwind***—pinned in place by sparse scrub or coastal moisture while the bare sandy central nose migrates downwind.
3. **Transverse Dunes:** Long, sinuous asymmetric sand waves aligned **perpendicular** to a steady unidirectional wind where **abundant sand** fuses individual barchans into continuous ridges.
4. **Seif (Longitudinal / Linear) Dunes:** Razor-crested ("sword") sand ridges stretching tens of kilometers **parallel** to the net wind direction, sculpted under **bimodal (two-direction converging) wind regimes** and moderate sand supply (western Thar, Sahara, Australia's Simpson Desert).
5. **Star Dunes:** Towering pyramidal peaks (up to 300 m tall) with three or more radiating sinuous arms built by **multidirectional seasonal winds** (Grand Erg Oriental, Sahara; Badain Jaran, China).

**Loess, Bajadas & Playas:**

- **Loess:** Unstratified, porous, buff-colored windblown **silt** (20–50 $\mu\text{m}$) deposited far downwind of deserts or glacial outwash plains—forming sheer vertical cliffs and fertile agricultural soils across **China's Loess Plateau** (>300 m thick, swept from the Gobi and Taklamakan), Central Europe, the US Midwest, Argentina's Pampas, and Kashmir's *Karewa* caps.
- **Bajadas & Playas (Fluvio-Arid Dominance):** Despite scarce annual rainfall, violent cloudburst flash floods along dry desert canyons (**wadis**) move more coarse rock mass than wind. Coalescing alluvial fans dumped at mountain mouths merge into continuous gently sloping **depositional aprons called Bajadas** (sitting directly downslope of the erosional bedrock *Pediment*). Inside closed interior-drainage (**endorheic**) basins (**bolsons**), fleeting floodwaters pool centrally and evaporate into salt-crusted ephemeral lakes called **Playas** (*salars / sabkhas / alkali flats* — **Sambhar, Didwana, and Pachpadra** in Rajasthan; **Salar de Uyuni**, Bolivia).

## 2. Glacial & Periglacial Geomorphology

### Glacier Mass Balance & Basal Mechanics
Accumulated alpine or polar snow compacts under overburden pressure via recrystallization into dense granular **Firn (Névé)** and eventually impermeable blue **Glacial Ice** ($\rho \approx 0.9\text{ g/cm}^3$). Every glacier is partitioned by the **Equilibrium Line Altitude (ELA / Snowline)** into an upper **Zone of Accumulation** (net snow gain) and a lower **Zone of Ablation** (net loss via melting, sublimation, or iceberg calving).

Below ~50 m depth, confining stress forces crystalline ice to flow ductilely via **Internal Plastic Deformation** (while the brittle top 50 m stretches and cracks into deep **Crevasses**). In **warm (temperate) alpine glaciers**, pressure-melting lubricates the sole, adding rapid **Basal Sliding** that powers intense erosion via **Plucking / Quarrying** (refreezing meltwater rips fractured bedrock blocks loose) and **Abrasion** (rock-studded basal ice grinds bedrock into parallel **striations** and milky **glacial rock flour**).

### Glacial Erosional Landforms
- **Cirques (*Corries / Cwms*) & Tarns:** Steep-headwalled, armchair-shaped rock amphitheaters hollowed out at glacier heads by rotational basal scouring and bergschrund frost wedging. After deglaciation, meltwater filling the over-deepened basin creates a crystalline alpine **Tarn Lake**.
- **Arêtes & Pyramidal Horns:** Two cirques cutting headward back-to-back leave a razor-sharp serrated knife-edge ridge (**Arête**); three or more converging cirques isolate a sheer, multi-faceted **Pyramidal Peak / Horn** (**Matterhorn**, Swiss Alps; **Shivling & Nilkanth**, Garhwal Himalayas).
- **U-Shaped Glacial Troughs & Hanging Valleys:** Valley glaciers bevel off interlocking fluvial spurs (**Truncated Spurs**) and excavate steep-walled, flat-floored **U-shaped Troughs** (**Yosemite Valley**, upper **Nubra & Bhagirathi valleys**). Lighter tributary glaciers incise shallower floors that terminate abruptly high on the main trough cliff—forming **Hanging Valleys** from which post-glacial streams leap as lofty waterfalls (**Bridalveil Fall**, Yosemite).
- **Fjords:** Deep, steep-walled U-shaped glacial troughs drowned by post-glacial sea-level rise, distinguished diagnostically by a **shallow submerged bedrock sill (threshold)** near their seaward exit where thinning glacier ice lost vertical cutting power (**Norway**, **Southern Chile**, **Milford Sound [NZ]**, Alaska, Greenland).
- ***Roches Moutonnées* ("Sheepbacks"):** Asymmetric glaciated bedrock bosses displaying a gently sloped, **smoothed and striated *stoss* (up-ice) side** shaped by abrasion and a **steep, jagged, fractured *lee* (down-ice) side** torn away by plucking.

### Glacial & Glacio-Fluvial Depositional Landforms
Glacial deposits (**Glacial Drift**) split sharply between **Unsorted Till** (dumped directly by melting ice with zero grain-size sorting) and **Stratified Outwash** (sorted and layered by meltwater rivers):

- **Moraines (Unsorted Till):** Linear or hummocky ridges of angular till—**Lateral** (along glacier flanks), **Medial** (running down-center where two tributaries merge), **Ground** (subglacial sheet), **Terminal / End** (marking farthest ice advance, often damming hazardous proglacial lakes), and **Recessional** (halting stages during retreat).
- **Drumlins (Unsorted Subglacial Till):** Smooth, elongated, inverted-spoon-shaped hills occurring in swarms (**"Basket of Eggs" topography**). Unlike *roches moutonnées*, a Drumlin's **steep, blunt nose faces *up-ice*** (direction the glacier arrived from) while its **long, streamlined tail tapers *down-ice*** (direction of ice advance).
- **Outwash Plains (*Sandur*), Eskers, Kames & Kettles (Stratified Glacio-Fluvial Drift):**
  - **Outwash Plains (*Sandur*):** Broad, gently sloping braided aprons of sorted gravel and sand fanning down-valley beyond terminal moraines (Iceland's vast *Skeiðarársandur*).
  - **Eskers:** Long, narrow, **sinuous winding ridges** of stratified sand and gravel marking the cast of **subglacial meltwater rivers** that flowed through tunnels beneath stagnant ice sheets.
  - **Kames:** Steep, irregular mounds or terraces of water-sorted sand and gravel deposited inside depressions on or along stagnant ice margins.
  - **Kettles & Kettle Lakes:** Circular collapse depressions pitting outwash plains wherever detached blocks of buried dead ice melted slowly beneath sediment blankets (**Walden Pond**, USA; Minnesota lake districts).

### Periglacial Geomorphology — Permafrost, Pingos & Thermokarst
Surrounding ice sheets and crowning high alpine plateaus lie **Periglacial environments** underlain by **Permafrost**—ground remaining continuously at or below $0^\circ\text{C}$ for **at least two consecutive years** (~15–20% of Northern Hemisphere land area), capped by a thin seasonally thawing **Active Layer** (0.3–3 m deep).

- **Pingos:** Conical, ice-cored periglacial hills (up to 50–70 m tall) heaved upward when confined pressurized groundwater freezes into a massive lens beneath permafrost (Mackenzie Delta, Canada; Siberia).
- **Patterned Ground & Ice-Wedge Polygons:** Intense winter thermal contraction splits frozen tundra soil into geometric polygonal networks; summer meltwater refreezes inside the cracks each year, growing vertical **ice wedges**.
- **Thermokarst:** Chaotic, subsided terrain dotted with collapsing mud slumps, tilted **"drunken forests,"** and shallow **thaw lakes (*alas*)** created when warming melts ice-rich permafrost—physically mimicking karst collapse through solid-to-liquid **phase change** rather than rock dissolution.

## 3. Polar Geography & Cryosphere Instability

### Arctic Amplification & Polar Feedback Physics
The planetary cryosphere—Earth's frozen water stores (~69% of all global freshwater, holding ~1.7–2% of total Earth water)—acts as a high-reflectivity planetary thermostat. Between 1979 and 2025, however, polar warming has raced far ahead of the planetary mean:

> **Dual-Standard Numeric Parity (Arctic Amplification):** Legacy textbooks cite Arctic warming at roughly **~2× the global average**. Modern observational syntheses (Rantanen et al., *Communications Earth & Environment* 2022; NOAA Arctic Report Cards 2024–2025) demonstrate that since 1979, the Arctic has actually warmed nearly **~3.8–4 times faster** than the global mean.

Four reinforcing physical mechanisms power Arctic Amplification:

1. **Ice–Albedo Feedback:** Fresh snow and sea ice reflect **70–85%** of incoming sunlight (albedo $0.70\text{–}0.85$); melting exposes dark open Arctic seawater that absorbs **90–94%** (albedo $0.06\text{–}0.10$), storing summer heat that radiates back into the Arctic atmosphere throughout autumn and winter.
2. **Lapse-Rate Feedback:** Persistent polar surface temperature inversions suppress vertical atmospheric convection, confining greenhouse warming tightly near the surface where outgoing longwave cooling to space is far less efficient than in the convecting tropics.
3. **Poleward Moisture Convergence:** Warmer mid-latitude air carries more latent heat and water vapor poleward (+7% per °C via Clausius–Clapeyron), amplifying winter cloud greenhouse trapping.
4. **Permafrost Carbon Feedback:** According to NOAA's 2024–2025 Arctic assessments, rapid permafrost thaw coupled with extreme boreal wildfires has flipped the Arctic tundra region from a millennia-long carbon sink into a **net source of atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{CH}_4$**.

### Arctic vs. Antarctic Asymmetry (2021 GS1 Mains Anchor)
Melting polar ice yields starkly divergent global impacts because the two poles are geographic opposites:

- **The Arctic (An Ocean Surrounded by Continents):** Floating **Arctic sea ice** (1–3 m thick) displaces its own mass of seawater; by **Archimedes' principle**, melting sea ice **does NOT directly raise global sea level** (ignoring minor salinity-driven steric expansion). Instead, Arctic sea-ice collapse slashes planetary albedo, shrinks the **equator-to-pole temperature gradient**, weakens and destabilizes the **Polar Front Jet Stream** (inducing slow meridional Rossby meanders, mid-latitude heat domes, and winter Polar Vortex outbreaks), unlocks trans-polar maritime trade (**Northern Sea Route**), and exposes shelf oil, gas, and critical minerals *(Arctic Council governance, shipping geopolitics, and India's Arctic Policy 2022 [Himadri / IndARC] are detailed in International Relations)*.
- **Antarctica (A High Frozen Continent Surrounded by Ocean):** Anchoring **~90% of Earth's grounded land ice** (~2.1 km mean thickness, **~58 m global sea-level equivalent**), Antarctica is thermally isolated by the **Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC)**. Unlike floating sea ice, discharging grounded Antarctic (and Greenland) land ice into the ocean **directly raises global sea level**, while massive cold freshwater plumes freshen abyssal waters and slow global thermohaline overturning.

### Greenland & West Antarctic (Thwaites Glacier) Marine Ice Instability
- **Greenland Ice Sheet (~7.4 m Sea-Level Equivalent):** Losing ~270 Gt of ice annually via surface melt ponds (which cut ice albedo and drain through vertical *moulins* to lubricate basal sliding) and marine outlet calving (*Jakobshavn Isbræ*). Fresh meltwater pouring into the Labrador and Irminger Seas directly threatens **Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC)** stability (Chapter 11).
- **West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) & Thwaites Glacier ("Doomsday Glacier"):** Spanning ~192,000 $\text{km}^2$ in the Amundsen Sea sector, **Thwaites Glacier** currently generates ~4% of global sea-level rise, holds **~65 cm** of direct sea-level rise, and mechanically buttresses the wider **West Antarctic Ice Sheet (~3.3 m total sea-level rise)**. Its existential risk lies in **Marine Ice Sheet Instability (MISI)**: Thwaites rests on a **retrograde (inland-sloping) bedrock basin** plunging >1–2 km below sea level. Warm **Circumpolar Deep Water (CDW)** melts its floating ice shelf from beneath; as the **grounding line** retreats backward into deeper bedrock, the thicker vertical ice face accelerates seaward discharge in a self-reinforcing runaway loop.
- **2024–2026 Scientific Refinements:** High-resolution satellite radar interferometry (*PNAS*, May 2024, Rignot et al.) uncovered **kilometers-wide daily tidal intrusions of warm seawater** pulsing directly beneath Thwaites's grounding zone, dramatically speeding basal melt. Concurrently, the **International Thwaites Glacier Collaboration (ITGC, Sept 2024 / 2025 syntheses)** found that rapid vertical cliff failure (*Marine Ice Cliff Instability, MICI*) appears less imminent within this century than worst-case early models projected—yet confirmed that irreversible MISI-driven collapse of Thwaites and much of WAIS over the 21st–23rd centuries remains a high-probability tipping hazard.

### Himalayan "Third Pole" Glacial Retreat
The **Hindu Kush Himalaya (HKH)** holds the largest volume of snow and glacial ice outside the polar circles—serving as the **"Third Pole"** and Water Tower of Asia feeding ten major river basins (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra, Yangtze, Yellow, Mekong, Salween, Irrawaddy, Amu Darya, Tarim) sustaining ~2 billion people:

- **Accelerating Mass Loss & Black Carbon:** Per **ICIMOD (HI-WISE 2023 & Snow Update 2024–2025)**, HKH glaciers shed ice **65% faster** in 2011–2020 than in the preceding decade, facing **30–50% volume loss** (and up to **75–80%** under high emissions) by 2100. Beyond **Elevation-Dependent Warming**, heavy **Black Carbon (soot)** aerosols transported upward from Indo-Gangetic fossil-fuel and biomass burning darken glacier snowpacks, slashing albedo and accelerating melt.
- **The Karakoram Anomaly:** Unlike rapidly shrinking Eastern and Central Himalayan glaciers (e.g., Gangotri, Zemu), Karakoram glaciers (Siachen, Biafo, Baltoro) long exhibited stability or slight advance—attributed to stronger winter **Western Disturbance** snowfall and thick insulating supraglacial rock debris—though 2020s extreme heatwaves are beginning to erode this anomaly.
- **"Peak Water" Paradox & GLOF Escalation:** Rapid retreat initially *inflates* meltwater discharges while swelling unstable moraine-dammed glacial lakes—**ISRO (April 2024)** documented **27% of 2,431 large Himalayan glacial lakes (>10 ha)** expanding noticeably since 1984 (130 within India), starkly demonstrated by Sikkim's **October 2023 South Lhonak Lake GLOF**. Beyond **"Peak Water"** (~2040–2060), shrinking glacier area permanently slashes dry-season spring/summer lean flows across the Indus and Ganga plains *(cryospheric tipping governance and NDMA GLOF risk-reduction protocols are expanded in Ecology & Disaster Management)*.

## 4. Coastal Geomorphology — Wave Mechanics & Coastline Taxonomies

### Wave Refraction, Erosion & Longshore Drift
Ocean waves perform coastal geomorphic work through **Hydraulic Action** (wave shock compressing air explosively inside rock fissures), **Abrasion / Corrasion** (hurled sand and pebbles scouring cliffs), **Attrition**, and **Solution**. Along irregular rocky coasts, **Wave Refraction** bends approaching wave crests parallel to submerged contours—focusing destructive wave energy tightly onto protruding **Headlands** while fanning weak energy into sheltered **Bays**, progressively straightening the coastline over millennia.

Along beaches, waves arriving at an oblique angle drive sand diagonally up the foreshore (**Swash**), while gravity pulls returning water straight downslope (**Backwash**). This continuous sawtooth conveyor—**Longshore Drift (Littoral Transport)**—transports millions of tonnes of coastal sand parallel to shorelines, building beaches, spits, and barrier islands. *(Low-energy **Constructive Waves** with strong swash build wide beaches; steep storm **Destructive Waves** with fierce backwash strip sand offshore.)*

### Erosional vs. Depositional Coastal Architecture
- **Rocky Headland Erosion Sequence:** Concentrated wave pounding cuts a basal **Wave-Cut Notch** at high-water mark; repeated overhang collapse drives the **Wave-Cut Cliff** landward, leaving a gently sloping intertidal bedrock **Wave-Cut Platform** at its base. Simultaneously, refraction excavating fractures on opposing headland flanks carves **Sea Caves** $\to$ punches through to form a **Sea Arch** $\to$ collapses into a freestanding **Sea Stack** (*Old Man of Hoy*, Scotland; *Twelve Apostles*, Australia) $\to$ erodes down to a submerged **Sea Stump**. *(Vertical ceiling vents jetting sea spray = **Blowholes / Gloups**; narrow collapsed roof inlets = **Geos**.)*
- **Depositional Longshore Landforms:**
  - **Spits & Hooked Spits:** Narrow sand/shingle tongues anchored at one end to the mainland and extending across a bay or estuary mouth via longshore drift; cross-waves bend the free tip landward into a **Recurve / Hooked Spit** (**Kakinada Hope Island Spit**, Andhra Pradesh; **Chilika Outer Spit**).
  - **Bars & Lagoons:** Submerged or emergent **Offshore Bars** run parallel to shallow coasts; when a spit seals off a bay entrance completely, it becomes a **Baymouth Bar**, trapping a brackish coastal **Lagoon** behind it (**Chilika** [Odisha], **Pulicat** [AP–TN], **Vembanad / Ashtamudi *Kayals*** [Kerala]).
  - **Tombolos:** Narrow wave-deposited sand/shingle causeways welding a **nearshore island directly to the mainland** inside the island's calm wave shadow (**Chesil Beach–Isle of Portland**, UK; the **Rama Setu / Adam's Bridge** shoal alignment linking Rameswaram toward Mannar; Mumbai's coalesced island archipelago).
  - **Barrier Islands:** Elongated, wave-built sandy islands paralleling low-gradient coastal plains separated from the mainland by shallow tidal lagoons—exemplified by North Carolina's **Outer Banks** and India's **Sriharikota Barrier Island** enclosing Pulicat Lake (site of ISRO's Satish Dhawan Space Centre).

### Submergent vs. Emergent Coastlines (Genetic Taxonomy)
Changes in relative sea level—via crustal tectonics, glacial isostasy (GIA), or glacio-eustatic ocean shifts—divide world coastlines into two master classes:

1. **Submergent Coastlines (Land Sinks OR Sea Level Rises):** Deep, highly indented, irregular margins dotted with offshore islands, drowned valleys, and prime **natural deep-water harbors**:
   - **Ria Coast:** **Drowned unglaciated river valleys** creating branching V-shaped funnel inlets that widen and deepen smoothly seaward with *no* shallow entrance sill (**NW Spain [Galicia]**, **SW Ireland**, India's **Konkan Coast**).
   - **Fjord Coast:** **Drowned glaciated U-shaped troughs** with sheer canyon walls and a diagnostic **shallow seaward rock sill** (**Norway, Southern Chile, Alaska, NZ Milford Sound**).
   - **Dalmatian (Concordant / Longitudinal) Coast:** Submergence of folded coastal mountain chains running **parallel** to the shoreline, transforming outer anticlines into strings of elongated offshore islands separated by narrow flooded sounds (**Croatia's Adriatic Dalmatian Coast**). *(Contrast with **Discordant / Atlantic coasts**, where structural mountain trends intersect the shoreline obliquely or perpendicularly.)*
2. **Emergent Coastlines (Land Uplifts OR Sea Level Drops):** Straight, smooth, shallow-water shores exposing former seabed plains, **raised beaches and elevated marine wave-cut terraces**, inland fossil sea cliffs, long sandy bars, spits, and lagoons—offering **poor natural deep-water ports** that require artificial breakwaters and continuous dredging (**India's Eastern Coromandel & Northern Circars Coasts** [Chennai, Pulicat, Chilika], **Kathiawar raised beaches**, **Kerala's emergent sandy barrier flats** flanking *Kayals*, and **GIA-uplifting Scandinavian shores**).

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Barchan vs. Parabolic Dune Horns:** **Barchan** horns point **downwind** (free sand, unidirectional wind); **Parabolic** horns point **upwind** (anchored by desert scrub/moisture while the central nose migrates downwind).
> - **Zeugen vs. Yardang Bedding:** **Zeugens** develop on **horizontal** alternating hard/soft strata; **Yardangs** develop on **vertical or steeply tilted** alternating beds parallel to prevailing winds.
> - **Pediment vs. Bajada:** A **Pediment** is an **erosional** bare-bedrock ramp along the upper mountain foot; a **Bajada** is a **depositional** sheet of coalesced alluvial fans downslope of the pediment.
> - **Drumlin vs. *Roche Moutonnée* Direction Flip:** A **Drumlin** (depositional till) has its **steep blunt end facing UP-ICE** and long tapered tail pointing **down-ice**; a ***Roche Moutonnée*** (erosional bedrock) has its **gentle smoothed stoss face UP-ICE** and steep jagged plucked face **down-ice**!
> - **Unsorted Till vs. Stratified Drift:** **Moraines and Drumlins** are **unsorted, unstratified till** dumped directly by ice; **Eskers, Kames, Outwash Plains, and Kettles** are **sorted, stratified glacio-fluvial** meltwater sediments.
> - **Ria vs. Fjord vs. Dalmatian Coast:** **Ria** = drowned *fluvial* river valley (deepens smoothly seaward, no sill); **Fjord** = drowned *glacial* trough (shallow seaward bedrock *sill*); **Dalmatian** = submerged *parallel* coastal mountain ridges forming linear island chains.
> - **Arctic Sea Ice vs. Land Ice Sea-Level Trap:** Melting floating **Arctic sea ice** does **not** directly raise sea level (Archimedes' principle), though its albedo collapse drives global warming; melting grounded **land ice** (Greenland, West Antarctica/Thwaites, Himalayan glaciers) directly elevates sea level.
> - **Arctic Amplification Dual Parity:** Modern post-1979 observations show Arctic warming at **~3.8–4×** the global average (not merely legacy ~2×).
> - **Thermokarst $\neq$ Karst:** **Thermokarst** results purely from physical **thawing of subsurface permafrost ice**, involving zero limestone chemical dissolution.

### 2. Prelims Trap Signature
- **Erosional vs. Depositional Swaps:** Grouping **pediments, yardangs, zeugens, cirques, or *roches moutonnées*** with depositional features, or misclassifying **drumlins, eskers, bajadas, tombolos, or loess** as erosional.
- **Directional & Structural Inversions:** Reversing wind/ice orientation on **barchans vs. parabolic dunes** and **drumlins vs. *roches moutonnées***, or swapping horizontal (**zeugen**) versus vertical (**yardang**) rock bedding.
- **Glacier–River & Coastline Misclassifications:** Pairing **Milam** with Mandakini (feeds Gori Ganga) or **Zemu** with Manas (feeds Teesta), and confusing **ria** (drowned river) with **fjord** (drowned glacier with shallow sill) or **emergent** eastern Indian coasts with **submergent** Konkan shores.
- **Cryosphere Physics Fallacies:** Claiming melting floating Arctic sea ice directly elevates sea level, ignoring **lapse-rate feedback** alongside albedo in Arctic Amplification, or assuming Himalayan glacier retreat reduces river discharge immediately rather than after passing "Peak Water."

2. *"Analyze the dual hydrological paradox of the Himalayan 'Third Pole'—accelerating glacial lake expansion and GLOF disasters in the short run versus post-'Peak Water' lean-season scarcity in the medium run—and assess how Indo-Gangetic black-carbon mitigation intersects with India's hydropower and transboundary river security."*
