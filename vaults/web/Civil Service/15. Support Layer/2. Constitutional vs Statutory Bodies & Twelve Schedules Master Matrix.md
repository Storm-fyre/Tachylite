# Compendium 2 - Constitutional vs. Statutory Bodies & Twelve Schedules Master Matrix

Indian public institutions divide cleanly along a three-tier legal hierarchy based on their **source of creation** and **amendability threshold**:

1. **Constitutional Bodies:** Derive existence directly from specific Articles of the Constitution. Their structural mandate can be altered only by a Constitutional Amendment under **Article 368**.
2. **Statutory Bodies:** Created by an ordinary Act of Parliament or a State Legislature. They can be restructured, merged, or abolished by ordinary legislation (simple majority).
3. **Non-Statutory / Executive Bodies:** Created by a Union Cabinet Resolution or Ministerial Notification without dedicated enabling legislation. They can be dissolved or reconstituted by executive fiat—though some (such as CBI) borrow investigative authority from a pre-existing statute.

**Live November 2026 Baseline:** This matrix incorporates the **CEC & Other ECs Act, 2023** (split SC verdict on reference in *Jaya Thakur*, Sept 2026), the **Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025** (in force April 9, 2025), the **SHANTI Act, 2025** (assent Dec 20, 2025, granting AERB statutory status), the **16th Finance Commission Report** (tabled Feb 1, 2026), the **23rd Law Commission** (Justice Dinesh Maheshwari, Apr 2025), and the **Tribunals Reforms Act, 2026** (assent Aug 13, 2026, establishing the National Tribunals Commission post-*Madras Bar Association*, Nov 2025).

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Asymmetric Appointment vs. Removal Pairs:** Both **SPSC Chairman/Members** (Art 317) and **State Election Commissioners** (Art 243K) are appointed by the **Governor** but can be removed **only by the President** (SPSC via binding SC inquiry; SEC via HC Judge impeachment route).
> - **Binding vs. Advisory Supreme Court Opinion:** Under **Art 143** (Presidential Reference), SC advice is *not binding* on the President; under **Art 317** (removal of UPSC/SPSC Chairman/Member), SC inquiry advice is **strictly binding** on the President.
> - **Re-Employment Asymmetry:** **CAG** (Art 148(4)) and **UPSC Chairman** (Art 319) face a total constitutional ban on *any* further Union/State government office. By contrast, **CEC / ECs** have **zero constitutional bar** on post-retirement government appointments (e.g., Governors, Ministers, Advisors), though the 2023 Act bars reappointment to the ECI itself.
> - **What the Constitution Leaves Out:** The Constitution does **NOT** fix tenure, age limit, or qualifications for **ECI**, **NCSC/NCST/NCBC**, **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities**, **AG**, or **AdvG**—nor does it fix CAG's 6-year/65-year term (that sits in the 1971 Act) or FC qualifications (1951 Act).
> - **Surviving Anglo-Indian Mandate (Art 338(10)):** Even though the 102nd CAA (2018) shifted OBCs out of NCSC to NCBC and the 104th CAA (2019) ended Anglo-Indian legislative nomination (Art 334), **NCSC** still retains the constitutional mandate to investigate safeguards for the **Anglo-Indian community**.
> - **GST Council Veto Calculus:** Because every decision requires **≥75% weighted votes** and the **Centre holds 33.33%**, the Centre enjoys a unilateral veto. Because **States collectively hold 66.67%**, the Centre cannot pass any resolution without backing from roughly **more than half of the States present and voting**.
> - **AG vs. Solicitor General (SG):** Only the **Attorney General (Art 76)** is a constitutional office. The Solicitor General of India and Additional Solicitors General are **non-constitutional** posts created under the *Law Officers (Conditions of Service) Rules, 1987*.

## The Twelve Schedules - Complete Atomic Anatomy

The original Constitution (1950) contained **8 Schedules**; Schedules 9 through 12 were added by the **1st, 52nd, 73rd, and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts**.

| **First** | **Arts 1 & 4** | Names & territorial extent of **28 States** and **8 Union Territories**. | Modified by **Simple Majority** (Art 4) — not treated as an Art 368 amendment (*except* when ceding Indian territory to a foreign state, requiring Art 368 per *Berubari Union, 1960* & 100th CAA 2015). | **Second** | **Arts 59, 65, 75, 97, 125, 148, 158, 164, 186, 221** | Emoluments, allowances, and privileges of **9 exact offices**: (1) President, (2) Governors, (3) Speaker & Deputy Speaker of LS, (4) Chairman & Deputy Chairman of RS, (5) Speaker & Deputy Speaker of LAs, (6) Chairman & Deputy Chairman of LCs, (7) SC Judges, (8) HC Judges, (9) CAG. | **Classic Exclusions:** **Vice-President** (draws salary as RS Chairman, not separately as VP), **PM, Union/State Ministers, MPs, MLAs, CEC/ECs, UPSC, AG** are **NOT** in Second Schedule. **54th CAA (1986)** allowed Parliamentary alteration of SC/HC Judges' salaries without constitutional amendment. |

## Four Parliamentary Majority Types - Mathematical & Constitutional Cheat Sheet

To eliminate ambiguity, every formula below is benchmarked against an **illustrative Lok Sabha** with: **Total Strength = `543`**, **Vacancies = `3`** *(Effective Strength = `540`)*, and **Present & Voting = `400`** *(140 absent/abstaining)*.

| Majority Type | Exact Mathematical Definition | Benchmark Seat Count (`543` / `540` / `400`) | Exhaustive Constitutional & Parliamentary Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Simple Majority** *(Functional / Working Majority)* | **`> 50%`** of members **present and voting** *(abstentions excluded from denominator)*. | `> 200` → **`201`** | • Ordinary Bills, **Money Bills (Art 110)**, Financial Bills (Art 117)<br>• **No-Confidence, Confidence, Censure, & Adjournment Motions**; Vote on Account<br>• Election of **Speaker & Deputy Speaker (LS/LA)** and **Deputy Chairman (RS)**<br>• Approval/Continuation of **President's Rule (Art 356)** & **Financial Emergency (Art 360)**<br>• **Revocation/Disapproval of National Emergency by Lok Sabha (Art 352(7))**<br>• **Constitutional alterations outside Art 368:** Creation/reorganisation of States (**Arts 2, 3, 4**); Creation/abolition of State Legislative Councils in **Parliament (Art 169)**; 2nd, 5th, 6th Schedules; Parliamentary quorum/privileges; Citizenship (**Art 11**); Delimitation; SC puisne judge strength<br>• **State Legislature ratification** of federal Art 368 amendments (≥50% of States)<br>• **Lok Sabha agreement** to Rajya Sabha resolution removing the Vice-President (**Art 67(b)**) |
| **2. Absolute Majority** | **`> 50%`** of **total membership** of the House *(vacancies NOT deducted)*. | `> 271.5` → **`272`** | • **Never used standalone** in the Constitution to pass a bill or motion.<br>• Operates constitutionally as the **mandatory lower bound (floor)** embedded inside **Art 368 Special Majority (Type II & III)**, and politically as the practical benchmark for forming a stable government at the Centre/States. |
| **3. Effective Majority** *(Constitutional text: "majority of all the then members")* | **`> 50%`** of **effective strength** = `(Total Strength − Vacancies) / 2 + 1` *(absentees & abstentions still stay in denominator!)*. | `(543 − 3) = 540` → **`271`** | **Exact 5 Constitutional Uses (Removal of Presiding Officers Only):**<br>1. Removal of **Vice-President in Rajya Sabha** (**Art 67(b)** — requires RS Effective Majority + LS Simple Majority; 14 days' notice)<br>2. Removal of **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha** (**Art 90(c)**)<br>3. Removal of **Speaker & Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha** (**Art 94(c)**)<br>4. Removal of **Speaker & Deputy Speaker of State Legislative Assembly** (**Art 179(c)**)<br>5. Removal of **Chairman & Deputy Chairman of State Legislative Council** (**Art 183(c)**) |
| **4A. Special Majority — Type I** *(Art 249 / Art 312)* | **`≥ 2/3`** of members **present and voting** *(NO absolute-majority floor!)*. | `2/3 × 400 = 266.67` → **`267`** | **Exclusive Rajya Sabha Federal Powers:**<br>• **Art 249:** RS resolution empowering Parliament to legislate on a **State List** subject in national interest (valid up to 1 yr at a time).<br>• **Art 312:** RS resolution authorising Parliament to create one or more **All India Services** common to Union & States. |
| **4B. Special Majority — Type II** *(Art 368 Pure & Parallel Removals)* | **`≥ 2/3`** of members **present and voting** **AND** **`> 50%`** of **total membership** (Absolute Majority floor). | `max(267, 272)` → **`272`** *(if 450 voted → `300`)* | • **Constitutional Amendments under Art 368** (Fundamental Rights, DPSPs, and all non-federal provisions — **no Joint Sitting** under Art 108; **no "same session" restriction** across the two Houses)<br>• Removal of **SC Judges (Art 124(4))**, **HC Judges (Art 217)**, **CAG (Art 148)**, **CEC (Art 324(5))**, and **State Election Commissioner (Art 243K)** — *address must be passed by both Houses in the **SAME SESSION**!*<br>• Approval & 6-month continuation of **National Emergency (Art 352)** *(upgraded from Simple to Special by **44th CAA, 1978**)*<br>• **State Legislative Assembly resolution** recommending creation/abolition of a Legislative Council (**Art 169(1)**) *(note: Parliament then enacts the law by **Simple Majority**!)* |
| **4C. Special Majority — Type III** *(Art 368 + Federal Ratification)* | **Type II Special Majority** in both Houses of Parliament **+** Ratification by **at least half (≥50%) of State Legislatures** by **Simple Majority**. | `max(267, 272)` + **`≥14 States`** *(no time limit for States)* | **Amendments Affecting Entrenched Federal Provisions (Art 368(2) proviso):**<br>• Election of President & manner (**Arts 54 & 55**)<br>• Extent of Union & State executive power (**Arts 73 & 162**); UT High Courts (**Art 241**)<br>• **GST Council (Art 279A)**<br>• Union Judiciary (Pt V Ch IV), State High Courts (Pt VI Ch V), Union–State Legislative Relations (Pt XI Ch I)<br>• **Any of the 3 Lists in Seventh Schedule**<br>• Representation of States in Parliament (**Fourth Schedule**)<br>• **Article 368 itself** |
| **4D. Special Majority — Type IV** *(Art 61 Impeachment)* | **`≥ 2/3`** of the **total membership of each House** *(strictest threshold in the Constitution; vacancies & absences NOT deducted!)*. | `2/3 × 543 = 362` → **`362`** *(RS: `2/3 × 245 = 164`)* | • **Sole Constitutional Use:** **Impeachment of the President of India (Art 61)** for "violation of the Constitution" *(14 days' notice signed by ≥1/4th of total members of the initiating House; nominated MPs participate in impeachment though barred from Presidential election; State MLAs do NOT participate)*. |

> [!warning] Prelims Traps & Atomic Distinctions
> - **Emergency Majority Split:** **National Emergency (Art 352)** requires **Type II Special Majority** in *both* Houses for **approval/extension** (within 1 month), yet needs only **Simple Majority in Lok Sabha alone** for **mandatory revocation** (Art 352(7)). Both **President's Rule (Art 356)** and **Financial Emergency (Art 360)** need only **Simple Majority** in both Houses (within 2 months).
> - **Asymmetric Bicameral & Centre–State Splits:**
>   - **VP Removal (Art 67(b)):** Rajya Sabha = **Effective Majority**; Lok Sabha = **Simple Majority** *(must originate only in RS)*.
>   - **State Legislative Council (Art 169):** State Legislative Assembly = **Type II Special Majority**; Parliament = **Simple Majority** *(and Parliament is NOT bound to accept the State Assembly's resolution!)*.
> - **"Same Session" Constraint (Art 124(4) vs. Art 368):** Although both share the exact same **Type II Special Majority** threshold, an **Art 124(4) removal address** (SC/HC Judges, CAG, CEC, SEC) must be passed by both Houses in the **same session**, whereas an **Art 368 Constitutional Amendment Bill** has no "same session" restriction and can be passed by the second House in a subsequent session.
> - **Effective vs. Absolute Majority Trap:** If `543` seats have `3` vacancies (`540` effective) and `300` members attend, **Effective Majority** (`271`) is **harder** than Simple Majority (`151`), and **easier** than Absolute Majority (`272`) by only 1 seat — because **absentees are NOT deducted** when computing Effective Majority!
> - **Presidential Assent & Joint Sitting Lockouts:** Under **24th CAA (1971)**, the President **must** give assent to an Art 368 Constitutional Amendment Bill (no suspensive or pocket veto). **Joint Sitting (Art 108)** is strictly barred for both **Constitutional Amendment Bills (Art 368)** and **Money Bills (Art 110)**.
> - **Fifth vs. Sixth Schedule Amendment Parity:** Despite altering tribal governance, amendments to the **Fifth & Sixth Schedules** are explicitly deemed **outside Art 368** (Para 7 of 5th Schedule; Para 21 of 6th Schedule) — Parliament can amend them by **Simple Majority**.
